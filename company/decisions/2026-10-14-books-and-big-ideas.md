## Why two categories, and not a course or a vertical
- **Books are not Courses.** The Courses org owns one work end to end, mapping every chapter. Book Summaries teaches 2 to 4 ideas from each of many books and judges whether each holds up. A book that needs more than 4 ideas goes to courses-lead (charter rule).
- **Big Ideas is not a vertical.** A vertical (principal-pm-verticals) is a cross-category portfolio for one audience. Big Ideas owns its own concepts: the shift in view, with the subject mechanism as a prerequisite. This keeps one owner for "is this idea stated as the evidence supports".
- **Strand managers where it makes sense.** Both categories have three natural strands with distinct sources and checks. Business books are checked against management research, psychology against replication records, and science and history against primary data. One manager per strand matches the other subject categories. Fewer strands would put about 30 concepts under one manager. More would make roles that have nothing to do until the category leaves incubation, which is this office's own failure mode, "adding roles to fix problems that are really about process".

## Objections (each with what happened to it)
1. **Copyright risk (Books).** Summaries of in-copyright books can become derivative works if they follow the book's text, structure or figures. *Answer:* the charter's copyright rules are binding, and changing them needs a decision record. No reproduced text. At most one short attributed line. Our own words, and original stages and models. No figures, covers or exercises. Books published in the last 12 months are refused. The fact-checker treats a breach as a **dispute**. Lessons teach one idea, never a chapter-by-chapter retelling. *What remains:* nobody in the company is a lawyer, and "short quote with attribution" is a policy, not legal advice. **Open for the founder:** whether to get a legal read before the first Books lesson is published. This is on the board, and it does not block building.
2. **Pop-science accuracy (both categories).** Popular books and big-idea retellings state findings more strongly than the evidence does: 10,000 hours, growth-mindset effect sizes, Dunning–Kruger, the decoy effect, disruption. *Answer:* in Books, every idea carries two separate claims, "the book argues X" (with a chapter) and "does X hold up" (sourced to research, with three allowed verdicts). Big Ideas sources the strong form or flags it, ends every reveal with where the idea stops working, and refuses replication failures stated as settled. Of the first 24 briefs, about a third have a "contested" or "mostly not" verdict (habits-one-percent, cheap-upstart-catches-up, noise-draws-dunning-kruger, checklist-steps-compound in part). That is by design. *What remains:* the "holds up" verdicts in the briefs are the drafter's reading of the literature, and the fact-checker may overturn any of them.
3. **Chapter citations can't be checked from here.** This host often can't open publisher, library or journal sites (fact-check records from W-369 onward). *Answer:* every chapter is marked **(unconfirmed)**, and a claim citing an unconfirmed chapter can't be verified. That is the same rule as the Courses org's unconfirmed sections. *What remains:* Books lessons may sit in review until someone with access confirms the chapters. **Open for the founder:** allow access to a book source, or accept a slower verification path.
4. **Independence: briefs written outside the owning role.** The founder's request had the curricula and the first 24 briefs written by this office, whose scope excludes lesson content. *Answer:* every brief says who drafted it. The owning strand manager re-checks each brief's answer (`node company/domains/briefs/checks/…`) and its claims before any builder picks it up. A board item is filed for each category lead. Strand managers may reorder or rewrite. The drafter does not review their re-check.
5. **Overlap with subject categories (Big Ideas especially).** Many big ideas are already subject lessons: comparative advantage, the tragedy of the commons, public goods, the Coase theorem, Goodhart's law, regression to the mean. *Answer:* those are left out or used as prerequisites. Each brief names its nearest lesson and what it adds. The charter's "subject first" rule sends anything that adds nothing to the subject's manager.
6. **Domain launch process skipped.** `company/domains/README.md` asks for a scout proposal and a Domain Launch Review. *Answer:* the founder decided, as with Life Hacks. Both categories enter as `incubating`. Each stays there until 5 lessons pass Domain Launch Review. For Books, each lesson's copyright check is recorded at that review.
7. **The kit type list.** `src/kit/types.ts` `DomainId` does not yet include `books` or `big-ideas`. *Answer:* the platform engineer owns that change (board). This office does not edit kit code. Builders should not start until it lands, or the first build adds it under an engineering review.
8. **Slots and the 2,000 target.** Going from 879 to 2,000 lessons is a volume goal, and volume is not Surface Value. *Answer:* following Priya's Courses ruling (her change 2), these categories get no reserved lane. Their briefs compete for the cycle's lesson slots at cycle-planning. The demand ratings are estimates until learner data exist.

## Would be wrong if
- The fact-checker can't confirm chapters for months, so Books lessons pile up in review (objection 3).
