## What the founder decided, and what is left to the owners

- **Decided by the founder (whether):** the purpose of management structure is scale, not control. The org is built from independent units, each keeping its own subdomain history, with separation of concerns between them. Coordination cost is to be minimised: a unit that needs no coordination gets none.
- **Decided by the founder (why the layers exist):** hierarchy here is not a simulation of a human company. It exists because each agent has limited memory and attention (a bounded context). Managers and heads hold the big picture and the strategy of their scope in compressed form, so no single worker needs the whole company in context, and innovation can happen at scale. Light coordination and strategic oversight are both required; cutting manager rounds must not cut the strategy layer.
- **Left to the owners (how):**
  - What a unit is (category, strand or course) and what it owns: its queue, its memory and history, its acceptance conditions.
  - How units meet: only through mechanical contracts (the handoff shape in company/ceo-office/handoff-map.md §7, the claims ledger, the readiness gate), not manager rounds.
  - Roles stay defined but run only when their unit's queue has work ("lazy roles"); rituals run on anomalies, not timers, where the owner can show nothing is lost.
  - The strategy layer: each head or manager keeps one short, current strategy-and-state page for its scope (goals, bets, what it is not doing, open questions, what changed), written for a reader with no other context, and kept small enough to read at the start of every run. Workers read their unit's page and their parent's, not the raw history. Heads summarise up; strategy changes flow down as briefs. Strategy upkeep is the manager's standing job even when its queue is idle.
  - How lightness is measured: coordination tokens per accepted outcome, manager token share, founder interventions, queue latency. The test is the lean-lane proposal (company/reviews/org/2026-10-17-lean-lane-proposal.md).

## Objections

- **chief-of-staff:** independent units can drift apart on shared standards (lesson standard, claims, gates) and duplicate effort across subdomains. **Answer:** shared standards stay mechanical and central (protected gates, readiness, kit); units are independent in what they work on and how, not in what counts as done. Held to a check at the first lean-lane review.

- **chief-of-staff:** a strategy page that is not refreshed turns into confident stale context, which is worse than none. **Answer:** each page carries an as-of date and the evidence it rests on; the unit design (W-1685) sets a staleness limit and who refreshes it. Held to the first lean-lane review.
