## Decision
Behavioral economics and irrational behavior becomes its own **category**, not a strand of Economics. It is set up by the category onboarding playbook (company/product/verticals/category-onboarding-playbook.md) in its order. It uses the category variant specified in company/product/verticals/2026-10-15-behavioral-economics.md, at the same size: 40 counted lessons, a 5-lesson pilot, then 30 on triggers.

## What follows from the playbook
- Step 2: domain-scout writes the DLR proposal for the category variant (W-896).
- Step 3: head-of-product decides the slots and the four boundary splits with Economics, Probability, Books and Big Ideas (W-897). F-22, the slot budget, is still the founder's.
- Step 4: portfolio-manager budgets it at Project Review 1 (W-898, W-905).
- Step 5: the CEO office creates **one category manager** after step 3, and strand managers only after the DLR five pass (W-899).
- Step 6: HQ floor 6, row 2 (W-900).

## Objections (from principal-pm-verticals, carried)
- About 30 of the subject's concepts already sit in Economics, Probability, Books and Big Ideas; only about 15-18 are collision-free. A category must not take lessons from its neighbours: the boundary table is signed by the neighbouring managers (playbook step 7).
- Its checking rule needs a replication rule: priming, ego depletion and power posing are never taught as claims, and contested effects are labelled contested (research-lead sourcing note, W-902).
- Many of the most famous effects stay in Books (anchoring, loss aversion, defaults). The category must stand on its own without them.
