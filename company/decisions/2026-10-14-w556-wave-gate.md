## The decision

**The gate is adopted.** No builder claims a Wave K brief until all three hold:

1. **Review queue ≤ 160**, counted as every lesson in `draft` or `review` in every category, **including Life Hacks**, minus only Life Hacks lessons that a written triage record has parked (no review work being spent on them). *Amendment 1:* the board title said "non-lifehacks"; the plan's table said "excluding held Life Hacks". I rule the table's version. The 200 draw on the same learner reviewers and fact-checkers (883 claims). A gate that leaves them out hides the load it exists to watch.
2. **Wave J ≥ 60% published**, measured against a fixed slug list. *Amendment 2:* `company/ops/waves.json` has no Wave J entry. The chief of staff records the Wave J slug list there (144 today, from the "## Wave J" headings) so the denominator cannot drift.
3. **0 disputes older than one wave.**

How it runs:
- Category managers **may write** Wave K briefs. Each one goes under a "## Wave K" heading and gets a line in `company/ops/brief-skips.json`: "Wave K gate (W-556)". That is the existing mechanism, so no tool changes.
- The gate opens when the chief of staff writes the three measurements in a dated line under "## Gate checks" below and all three pass. Then the managers remove their Wave K lines.
- **Not gated:** Wave J briefs (including Life Hacks ranks 6-14), revisions, fixes, merges, and re-reviews. The gate holds new builds only.
- **Wave order accepted as proposed:** J, then D (drain), then K. L and M stay as written but depend on founder asks 1 and 3. N to Q are re-allocated on learner data. K's 36 exam-prep slots depend on ask 1. If the founder declines, they go to the subject categories' unmet demand-3 concepts, pro rata, and nothing is added to cs or geography (both saturating).

## Three questions only the founder can answer (on the board, owner founder)

**Ask 1. Exam prep: a new vertical, as a two-syllabus pilot?** (intro physics and intro statistics; AP Physics 1 and AP Statistics are candidates, unconfirmed from here)
- Yes: CMs map the syllabi to existing lessons now (no new lessons), and K takes 36 gap lessons from that map. The vertical doc comes to me and to you before L.
- No, or not yet: K's 36 slots go to unmet demand-3 concepts. Physics, probability and math then grow by CM-chosen concepts, which is demand we made up.
- What it costs: 36 of K's ~150 slots, and topic codes stay "(unconfirmed)" until a host can open a syllabus. Kill rule: under 15% path completion, or recall no better than the feed median, 60 days after deploy.
- My recommendation: yes to the mapping now. Decide the build slots after the mapping shows how many gaps are real.

**Ask 2. One web-capable fact-check session per wave: who runs it, and is it allowed?**
- Today no source host can be opened. Held claims include W-571 (Life Hacks Wave J, two load-bearing), W-420 (NCM sections), W-541 (Books), F-10, and `spaced-beats-cram`'s recall claim, which by itself holds the Life Hacks wave-1 exit gate.
- Yes: about 19 held claims per wave get cleared (~12% of subject lessons), and Books becomes possible.
- No: source sentences are cut wherever a derivation cannot carry them, Books stays blocked (~100% of its lessons held), and Life Hacks wave 2 waits.
- Options: you run it yourself, a connected session you approve, or an allow-list for a fact-check host. My recommendation: one session per wave, at the end of the drain wave first.

**Ask 3. A first deploy, so that EXP-002 and metrics part B can start.** (This links F-04 "Deploy to Vercel" and F-11 / W-007 "recruit 5 learners". It does not replace them.)
- It is M's entry gate, and the only route to the data that re-allocates waves N to Q. Without it, every allocation after M is still judgement.
- What you would approve: one specific deploy, under the runbook, once W-477 and W-534 (the /spike S1 hold) are cleared. CLAUDE.md requires your approval of that deploy itself, not of the plan.
- My recommendation: approve a first deploy during the drain wave, with drafts unreachable (W-480).

## Objections, and what happened to each

| Objection | Raised by | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "Hold builders and we lose the pace." | anticipated in the plan | **Declined.** Published count is the pace. 0 of 144 Wave J lessons are published. More builds raise the queue, not the count. |
| Counting Life Hacks makes the gate hard to reach while the 200 sit in review. | head-of-product, against the plan's wording | **Accepted as the intended effect.** The plan's own drain wave triages the 200 to their cap. Parked lessons stop counting. Unparked ones are real load. |
| The plan's ≤ 1 wave contradicts the board's healthy line "< 2 waves". | head-of-product | **Accepted as a deliberate tightening for Wave K only.** Two waves is the alarm line, and one wave is the entry line. They answer different questions. |
| "Exam prep is just another vertical." | anticipated in the plan | **Not mine to settle.** Framed as ask 1, with the plan's answer (syllabus-defined demand, kill rule) attached. |
| "Discounting Life Hacks to ~75 is too harsh." | anticipated in the plan | **Declined.** The ~75 comes from the PR #2 decision's cap and survivor estimate. If triage keeps more, K's Life Hacks slots shrink. |
| The gate is enforced by managers adding skip lines, which relies on them remembering to. | head-of-product | **Accepted, with a check.** The chief of staff's gate line counts Wave K briefs without a skip line. A tool gate would be an edit to the claim queue, and that work belongs to the tools owner, not to me. |

## This would be wrong if

- Wave J stalls at under 60% for reasons that more builds would not affect (for example, held claims only). Then the publish condition is measuring web access, not review load, and I revisit it with ask 2's answer.
