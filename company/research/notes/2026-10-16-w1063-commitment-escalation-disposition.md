## What this means for a lesson (evidence for cm-behavioral, not a decision)

- **Commitment devices.** A claim like "some people choose to lock their own money away, and those people saved more" has opened support (secondary for SEED, primary for Beshears 2020). The safe scope words are "some" and "a minority", with the place named. "Commitment works" without limits is not supported: two opened primaries (Uganda, Malawi) show strict commitments doing worse or failing.
- **Automatic escalation.** The headline number (3.5% → 13.6%) is secondary only, and three secondary papers describe the design three different ways. A reveal that states it should name it as "as reported by" and say it is from one US programme. One opened primary (Choi 2017) shows a reversal at a different firm. That is a good honest limit for the reveal, and it fits B6 (no default-effect size).
- **Disposition effect.** The behaviour itself is the best-sourced of the three. The open question is whether it is a mistake, and that depends on the asset. A lesson that says "people sell winners sooner than losers" is supported. A lesson that says "and that is always a mistake" is not (Guenther & Lordan). This sits well with E10's "no sell/hold advice".
