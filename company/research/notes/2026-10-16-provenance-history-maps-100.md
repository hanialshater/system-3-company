## 1. Whose record is it, and who made it (all 25 snapshots)

- **A modern, hand-made GIS layer, not a record from any of the years shown.** The README's Credits: "a collection of basemaps collected, adapted and converted from diverse sources, sometimes only available through the wayback machine. Among these sources, anonymous students from the 'ThinkQuest Team C006628'" (opened, high). Maintainer: André Ourednik. Licence GPL-3.0 (opened, high; the licence question is W-1557, not this note).
- **No polygon carries a citation.** Every feature has exactly five properties, NAME, ABBREVN, SUBJECTO, BORDERPRECISION, PARTOF (opened, high). No source, author, date of edit or confidence per feature exists. So "who drew this border" cannot be answered for any single polygon. **We don't know.**
- **The maker's own warnings** (README; opened, high): "work in progress: verify the maps by comparison to other sources before using in academic work"; "historical boundaries are even more disputed than contemporary ones"; territory and national boundary "becomes meaningful, in Europe, only since the Peace of Westphalia (1648)"; "areas of civilizations actually overlap"; overlaying these maps on modern coastlines "can be misleading". CONTRIBUTING.md: "Some show a 20 km shift. This must yet be corrected", and files are kept "generalized enough to achieve file size below 3 MB" (opened, high).
- **The maker's own view of disputed borders** (CONTRIBUTING.md, "Deontology"; opened, high): he suggests drawing each country "as perceived / recognized by" its own government, so that overlaps reveal disputes. The pinned files do not do this at the places these lessons show (section 3): the disputed places I tested sit in one polygon each.

### Field meanings and what the numbers in them are worth

- `SUBJECTO` = "the name of the colonial power exercising authority on the country or region; the name of the region otherwise". `PARTOF` = a larger cultural area. `BORDERPRECISION` 1 approximate, 2 moderately precise, 3 "determined by international law" (README; high).
- **BORDERPRECISION is an era code, not a per-border judgment** (counted, high). Every polygon in the 13 snapshots from 100 to 1500 is 1, except one in 1492. From 1700 on almost every polygon is 3: 1700 774 of 782, 1800 626 of 627, 1815 435 of 436, 1880 234 of 236, 1900 to 1994 all. So "Pampas cultures" (1700), "central Asian khanates" (1800), "Eora" (1800) and the 1960 "Israel" and "Tibet" polygons are all coded "determined by international law". That cannot be true of most of them (low, my inference). **The stage ignores the field** and titles every shape "approximate reconstruction", with the caption "Approximate reconstruction" (`src/lib/history-maps/HistoryMapStage.tsx` lines 44, 55; high). That is the safer reading. No lesson may quote the field.
- **NAME is often null.** Unnamed polygons: 1500 148 of 291, 1700 191 of 782, 1880 63 of 236, 1900 48 of 270 (counted, high). The generator drops features without a NAME, so a lesson's "coverage missing" often means **the source drew a polygon there but did not name it**, not that no polygon exists (Addis Ababa 1880, Harare 1900, Mombasa 1700 and Naha 1700 and 1900 all sit in unnamed polygons; point tests, high). The reveal's "missing coverage in this reconstruction" is accurate only if read as "no name".
- **Different snapshots follow different coding schemes** (counted, high). The 1492 file has 1,946 features, most of them peoples (the Andes as "Quechua", "Wari", "Kichwa", "Kitu Kara"; Australia as several hundred named Aboriginal nations, which reappear in the Sydney lesson's 1800 frame); 1500 has 291 and 1530 has 494, mostly states. `map-cusco-quito-1492-1530` sees 294 labels in 1492 and 4 in 1530. A label change between such files can be a **change of coding scheme**, not a change on the ground (low, my inference, but it follows from the counts).
- **Coding is copied forward between years** (high for what the files contain). Like the khanates note's 1279/1300 finding, several snapshots carry names that look stale for their year: the 1400 file has no Ming or Joseon polygon, and Hangzhou, Beijing, Seoul and Guangzhou are inside "Great Khanate" (SUBJECTO "Mongol Empire"); the 1200 file has no Ayyubid or Jin polygon (Cairo and Damascus are "Fatimid Caliphate", Beijing is "Liao"); 700 still has "Sui Empire"; 1800 has "Quebec" with SUBJECTO France; 1900 has "Kingdom of Brazil" with SUBJECTO Portugal. That these names are wrong for those years is unverified (memory): Yuan rule ended in 1368, Joseon from 1392, Fatimids ended 1171, Liao ended 1125, Tang from 618, Quebec British from 1763, Brazil independent 1822 and a republic from 1889.

**What this means for the limit sentence (F5).** Every reveal says: "These approximate snapshots do not date a transition, establish continuous control, explain causes, or settle present-day claims. An unlabelled dot means missing coverage in this reconstruction, not empty land." That matches section 1 on borders. It does **not** say that a **label itself can be wrong or out of date for its year**, which is the larger limit here. Proposed addition, for cm-history to decide: "Some labels are carried over from other years or are simply wrong; the map is one project's reading, not a record from the time." The `limits` claim in each `meta.json` should say the same.

## 2. What every lesson shares (one engine, so one finding applies 100 times)

1. **The learner sees source names verbatim**, in the map key, the trace and the SVG titles (`HistoryMapStage.tsx`; `map.json` `shapes[].label`). So every naming issue in the source is a learner-visible label (checklist P5, L4). The trace uses ABBREVN, and **ABBREVN sometimes strips the colonial marker or changes the meaning** (high, from `map.json` `shortLabels`): "Mesopotamia (GB)" → "Mesopotamia"; "Syria (France)" → "Syria"; "Senegal (FR)" → "Senegal"; "Angola (Portugal)" → "Angola"; "Dutch East Indies" → "East Indies"; "Netherlands Indies" → "Netherlands" (Makassar and Ternate shown as "Netherlands"); "New South Wales (UK)" → "United Kingdom" (Sydney shown as "United Kingdom"); "Denmark-Norway" → "Norway" (Copenhagen shown as "Norway"); "Emirate of the White Sheep Turks" → "Turks" (Tehran, Shiraz and Tabriz shown as "Turks"); "Austrian Empire" → "Austria-Hung" (Split and Venice in 1815); "Manchu Empire" → "Manchu China". Checklist P1 asks the lesson to say whose record it is; dropping "(GB)" hides the coloniser in the very place the source named it.
2. **The answer key compares NAME only.** "Same label" and "different labels" are set equality of NAME (`generate.mjs`). The README and the recall bank say NAME is not SUBJECTO. That is honest, but in 1920 Mosul and Baghdad are "same label" ("Mesopotamia (GB)") while the ruling power (SUBJECTO "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland") is visible only in the source, not on the stage.
3. **Recalls rotate by roster position, not by fit** (`generate.mjs`, `recalls[index % 10]`; high). Recall 9 asks what moved when a label changed, with the keyed answer "Nothing necessarily; the map classification changed". It lands on 10 lessons, among them **amman-beirut-1920-1960, dhaka-kolkata-1900-1994, sydney-wellington-1800-1900, dili-kupang-1800-1960, dali-lhasa-1200-1700 and addis-ababa-massawa-1880-1960**: places where, in the years shown, very large numbers of people were displaced, killed or dispossessed (unverified, memory: the 1947 Partition, 1948 and the Palestinian displacement, 1971, the colonisation of Australia and Aotearoa New Zealand, Timor). The sentence is true as map logic. On those six stages it reads as minimisation (checklist L2, R5 adjacent). Recall 8 ("does a label settle a present-day claim? No") is the right one for contested places, and it now falls on venice-split, sanaa-aden, harare-maputo, n-djamena-kano, baku-yerevan, karachi-delhi, taipei-beijing, vientiane-luang-prabang, riga-tallinn and new-york-boston: chance, not choice.
4. **Modern place names as orientation** (`cases.mjs` comment; the `positions` claim). Honest, and the title shows only modern names ("Istanbul" in 500, "Kinshasa" in 1880, "Addis Ababa" in 1880, before the city was founded in 1886, unverified memory). L4 asks for the source-era name too where it differs. Several titles pair a modern name with a period the name did not exist in: Istanbul, Ankara, Harare, Kinshasa, Maputo, Nouakchott, Jakarta, Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru, Kolkata, Yangon, Ulaanbaatar, Almaty, Ashgabat, Dili, Wellington (unverified, memory, for each rename date). This is a reviewer call, not an error.
5. **Mercator, crops computed per lesson.** Lesson-level only; the stage says "Mercator" and compares labels, not areas (I3 on projection is satisfied). One crop is broken: see sydney-wellington in section 4.10.
6. **Words the checklist names (L1).** Source labels in view include "Berber Tribes", "Tuareg Nomadic Tribes", "Savanna hunter-gatherers", "Amazon hunter-gatherers", "Andean hunter-gatherers", "Australian aboriginal hunter-gatherers" (lower-case "aboriginal"), "Pampas cultures" (high). "Tribe" is on the L1 list. "Hunter-gatherers" names a way of life, not a people, and is applied at Potosí in 1530 and at Buenos Aires in 1700 (section 4). "Caliphate" is used where the rulers were sultans or emirs: "Seljuk Caliphate", "Wattasid Caliphate", "Tukular Caliphate" (unverified, memory, as to the titles used). That is a religious title, so L6 is touched as well as accuracy.
7. **One feature in the source is an artefact, not data.** Raw point tests in 1960 and 1994 put every place inside "Chile" as well: a ring with the wrong winding (high). The generator normalises winding (`tools/history-maps/geometry.mjs`), so no lesson shows it (checked: no lesson's matches include Chile). No action; recorded so that nobody re-running a raw test is misled.
