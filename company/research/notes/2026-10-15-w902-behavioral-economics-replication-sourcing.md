## How this was done, and its limits

- **Hosts.** `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` answered. Its search page works with plain curl. **Its HTML article pages throw a reCAPTCHA after a few requests** (other sessions share the egress). **The PMC OAI-PMH service on the same host** (`/api/oai/v1/mh/?verb=GetRecord&identifier=oai:pubmedcentral.nih.gov:<id>&metadataPrefix=pmc`, or `pmc_fm` for front matter only) returned full JATS text for every open-access paper I asked for, with no captcha. The fact-checker should use it. `eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` and `www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` did not answer. Publisher sites and osf.io were not tried (blocked per the task).
- **"Opened"** means I fetched the paper from PMC and read the abstract and the quoted passages. **"Secondary"** means an opened paper reports another paper's result; the original was not opened. **"Not opened"** means I have only my memory or a citation, and nothing here rests on it.
- No search snippet is used as evidence. Page content was treated as data.
- Verdict words: **robust · with limits · contested · failed** (the task's scale). They map onto the shared words: holds up · holds with limits · mixed or contested · did not replicate.

## Verdict table

| Effect | Verdict | Opened source(s) (PMC id) | What it says | Limits and notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Allais / certainty effect** (and reflection) | **robust** (smaller than in the 1979 original) | Cicerale, Blanzieri, Sacco & Soltani 2022, *PLoS ONE*, PMC9337702 (opened) · List & Haigh 2005, *PNAS*, PMC545552 (abstract) · Seak et al. 2025, *JEBO*, PMC13576738 (abstract) | Cicerale: Ruggeri et al. 2020 "substantially confirmed the original findings, albeit with attenuated effects, in a study enrolling 4098 participants from 19 countries" (secondary). Their own three Italian/French samples (N = 134, 176, 140) showed more risk aversion in crises, and the reflection effect was "largely lost". List & Haigh: students and Chicago Board of Trade traders both show Allais-type choices; traders less often. Seak: independence-axiom violations in humans "in a broad range of reward probabilities" | Ruggeri 2020 not opened. Effect size depends on population (experts less) and on circumstances. The derivation on the learner's own pair is checkable without any source |
| **Framing** (Asian disease / risky-choice) | **robust** | Rachev et al. 2021, *PLoS ONE*, PMC8432807 (opened in full) | 88,181 participants in 47 countries (March-May 2020): framing d = 0.69, against Many Labs 1's d = 0.60 (36 samples, N = 6,344, more than 70% from the US; secondary). The effect "varied substantially across nations" | Many Labs 1 not opened. The within-learner design weakens framing (the vertical's own rule); no source opened for that |
| **Endowment effect** | **with limits** (procedure- and context-dependent) | McGranaghan & Otto 2022, *J Risk Uncertain*, PMC9200560 · Humphrey et al. 2017, *J Econ Sci Assoc*, PMC6956941 · Baratgin, Godin & Jamet 2022, *Front Psychol*, PMC8822236 (all opened) | McGranaghan & Otto call it "the robust empirical finding", but reducing choice uncertainty (tasting the goods) "eliminates under-trading" and shrinks the WTA-WTP gap. Humphrey et al. found no endowment effect in their baseline. Baratgin et al.: Kanak participants show it in a French setting but not in a Kanak one | Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler 1990 and Plott & Zeiler 2005 not opened (cited in McGranaghan & Otto) |
| **Present bias** | **with limits** (clearer for effort than for money) | Cohen, Ericson, Laibson & White 2020, *J Econ Lit*, PMC10489299 (opened) · Ruggeri et al. 2022, *Nat Hum Behav*, PMC9584811 (opened) | Cohen et al.: Augenblick, Niederle & Sprenger (2015) found dynamic inconsistency with real effort; with money, "much weaker (but still statistically significant)" (secondary); money-earlier-or-later choices "are driven in part by some factors that are distinct from underlying time preferences". Ruggeri 2022: 61 countries, N = 13,629, monetary choices; present bias appears in every country, and 54% of participants show at least one anomaly | The reversal itself is a derivation. "People do it" is a type d claim with these limits attached |
| **Mental accounting** | **with limits** | Li & Feldman 2025, Registered Report, *R Soc Open Sci*, PMC12445221 (opened in full) | Replicated 17 problems from Thaler 1999 with US MTurk samples (about 500 per problem, N = 1,007): support for 11, mixed for 3, none for 3. The theatre ticket replicated (90% would buy after losing a $10 bill vs 77% after losing the ticket). **The jacket-and-calculator problem did not** (11% vs 7%, p = 0.066) | Choose the problem by its result. One online US sample. Hastings & Shapiro 2013 not opened |
| **Beauty contest / level-k** | **robust** | Pantelis & Kennedy 2017, *Cognition*, PMC5303671 (opened) · Arruñada, Casari & Pancotto 2015, *Front Behav Neurosci*, PMC4446529 (opened) · Coricelli & Nagel 2009, *PNAS*, PMC2685737 (abstract) | First-round means far above the equilibrium of 0: 36.4 (250 online players, 2/3 of the mean; winning number 24); 39.0-44.6 by group (195 students, groups of three). Level-k / cognitive-hierarchy models fit the spread | Nagel 1995's own means not opened. The equilibrium 0 is a derivation. Means vary with population and group size, so quote a sample, not "people guess 33" |
| **Overconfidence** (90% intervals) | **robust** (depends on the elicitation format) | Haran, Moore & Morewedge 2010, *Judgm Decis Mak*, PMC5386407 (opened) · Prims & Moore 2017, *Judgm Decis Mak*, PMC5978695 (opened) | Haran et al.: "Overprecision is the most robust type of overconfidence"; 90% intervals contained the answer 73.8% of the time (N = 103), which they note is higher than typically found before; a different format (SPIES) removed the bias | The size depends on the format, so the learner's own intervals (type b) are the safest claim |
| **Money illusion** | **with limits** (thinly sourced) | Weber, Rangel, Wibral & Falk 2009, *PNAS*, PMC2664018 (abstract) · Yu, Huang & Bruce 2013, *PLoS ONE*, PMC3559431 (opened) | Weber: behavioural money illusion in the evaluation of transactions, correlated with vmPFC activity. Yu: self-reported pleasantness tracked face value as well as real value | **No replication project or meta-analysis found.** Both are small lab or neuroimaging samples. Shafir, Diamond & Tversky 1997 not opened. Teach the nominal-to-real conversion as a derivation; keep any "people do it" sentence held |
| **Dictator giving** | **with limits** (robust in labs; varies by population and stakes) | Barreda-Tarrazona et al. 2025, *PLOS ONE*, PMC11819478 (opened) · Raihani et al. 2013, *PLoS ONE*, PMC3741194 (opened) | Both report Engel's (2011) meta-analysis: dictators give about 28% on average, "based on laboratory studies using predominantly Western undergraduate samples" (secondary). 1,161 lab subjects gave about a third (women 35%, men 25%). Online, stake size changed giving among Indian but not US players | Engel 2011, Henrich et al. 2001 and List 2007 not opened. "In labs" and "varies across populations" must be in the same reveal |
| **Social and behavioural priming** | **failed** (iconic studies); the wider literature is **contested** | Doyen, Klein, Pichon & Cleeremans 2012, *PLoS ONE*, PMC3261136 (opened in full) · Vadillo, Hardwicke & Shanks 2016, *J Exp Psychol Gen*, PMC4831299 (opened) · Weingarten et al. 2016, *Psychol Bull*, PMC5783538 · Dai et al. 2023, *Psychol Bull*, PMC12421318 (both opened) · Shanks & Vadillo 2021, PMC8522447 (opened) | Doyen: the elderly-walking effect failed with automated timing; it appeared "only when experimenters believed participants would indeed walk slower". Vadillo et al.: in Many Labs 1, "flag priming" and "currency priming" "could not be reproduced in the majority of direct replication attempts conducted by 36 independent laboratories" (secondary); the money-priming literature shows "selection bias, reporting biases, or p-hacking". **Against:** two meta-analyses report average behavioural priming effects of d ≈ 0.35 and d = 0.37 (351 studies), with bias corrections the authors judged minor | The vertical's rule ("never as a claim; failure lesson only") stands for social and money priming. Do not write "priming doesn't exist": the meta-analyses disagree, and the honest verdict for the field as a whole is contested |
| **Ego depletion** | **failed** | Vohs et al. 2021, *Psychol Sci*, PMC12422598 (opened) · Vadillo, Gold & Osman 2018, *R Soc Open Sci*, PMC6124090 (opened) · Dang 2016, *Front Psychol*, PMC4971805 (opened) | Vohs et al. (36 labs, N = 3,531, preregistered): d = 0.06, not significant; the data were "4 times more likely under the null". Many Labs 3 reanalysis (N > 2,000): no depletion effect. Dang: Hagger et al. 2016 (23 labs, N = 2,141) did not replicate it (secondary) | Defenders argue the manipulations did not deplete (Dang; and a 2021 opinion, PMC8134656, not opened). That does not rescue it as a claim |
| **Power posing** | **failed** (hormones; felt power not shown in the opened replications) | Metzler, Grèzes & Vonk 2019, *PeerJ*, PMC6585898 (opened) · Barel et al. 2024, *BMC Psychol*, PMC11619626 (opened) | Metzler et al.: four high-powered studies (Ranehill et al. 2015 and three others, secondary) "could not replicate" the hormone effects; their own repeated-posture study (82 men) found none. Barel et al. (N = 92): no effect on testosterone or felt power; partial support for cortisol and risk-taking | Carney, Cuddy & Yap 2010 and the 2018 p-curve defence not opened. **Not in this pass** (in §6 but not in the task's list): anchoring, sunk cost, defaults, loss-aversion size, commitment devices, decoy, choice overload, scarcity, the honesty-pledge retraction. One extra was opened along the way: **nudges at scale.** DellaVigna, Kim & Linos 2024 (PMC11449432, opened) restate DellaVigna & Linos 2022: 73 trials in 30 US cities with a national nudge unit raised outcomes by 1.9 percentage points on average, "a 13% increase relative to the baseline average of 15%". The comparison with academic-journal effects, the basis of the "much smaller" prior, is in the 2022 paper, which was not opened. Verdict: with limits (small at scale); "contested" stays the prior for the comparison until it is sourced.

## What changes from Selin's priors (§6)

- **Agree:** framing, certainty/Allais, beauty contest and overconfidence as robust; endowment, present bias, mental accounting and dictator giving with limits; ego depletion and power posing failed.
- **Sharper:** mental accounting's limits are specific: pick problems that replicated (the theatre ticket, yes; the jacket and calculator, no).
- **Weaker than assumed:** money illusion. Its verdict stays "with limits", but the only opened evidence is two small studies. I would hold every empirical sentence about it.
- **More nuanced:** priming. "Did not replicate" is right for the iconic social- and money-priming studies, which are what the vertical refuses. Two large meta-analyses still report positive average effects, so the failure lesson must say "contested" about the field and "did not replicate" about the named studies.
