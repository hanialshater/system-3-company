# Zoology and deep-time lessons: interaction directions

Status: draft design directions for W-2055, 2026-10-28. These are prompts for individual lesson briefs, not implemented lessons, verified science, or a new approved lesson format. The 500-item curriculum is a proposal; review overlap and scientific claims before production. Preserve the existing predict → explore → reveal → later recall contract.

## Visual principles

- Show the relevant evidence in the stage. An animal picture alone does not explain an adaptation, phylogeny or behavior.
- Label whether an illustration represents a living animal, a specimen, a fossil, a reconstruction or a simplified model. Show uncertainty in reconstructions where the evidence does not resolve a feature; do not imply that inferred coloration or soft tissue is fossil evidence.
- Use original SVG for diagrams that must carry precise labels and interactive states. When naturalistic image assets add useful recognition or ecological context, generate or commission them only from a reviewed, species-specific brief; fact-check anatomy and visual provenance, and provide meaningful alternatives for screen readers. A plausible image is not evidence that the anatomy is accurate.
- Do not encode the correct answer only in color, relative size or an initially highlighted region. Hide the deciding evidence until prediction. Keep essential labels legible, provide keyboard controls and test the 390 × 844 reveal with the stage.
- Name a model's scope and assumptions. Do not treat an invented path, distance, phenotype, speed or population as observed measurements.

## Candidate interactions

| Pattern | Learner action | What the stage can show | Key design risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fossil evidence table | Uncover or sort evidence | Body and trace evidence, with a specific question about what each supports | A footprint does not identify every feature of its maker. |
| Deep-time placement | Place a taxon or event against labeled time intervals | Overlap and separation, with an explicit timescale | Avoid presenting an interval as an exact first or last appearance; check dates and units. |
| Branching relationships | Reveal nested branches | Shared ancestry and the position of named groups | A simplified tree is not a ladder; a modern species is not shown as the direct ancestor of another. |
| Compare form and function | Select a structure and observe an illustrated mechanism | How the named structure interacts with a modeled environment | Similarity alone cannot establish adaptation or causal effectiveness. |
| Feeding apparatus | Match a food item to the depicted mouthpart or feeding step | Contact and movement of the pictured structure | Avoid implying that every species in a broad group eats the same diet. |
| Habitat constraints | Change a stated environmental condition | Qualitative response in a declared model | The modeled outcome is not an observed individual or an absolute prediction. |
| Life-cycle sequence | Arrange known stages | A verified sequence, with where development occurs | Differences between species must not be collapsed into a universal cycle. |
| Behavior and evidence | Reveal observations before choosing an inference | What an observation supports and what it does not | Avoid assigning human intent to behavior without evidence. |
| Sensory world | Toggle the stimulus and visible receptor | A comparison of a sensory pathway under the stated conditions | Sensory ranges and experiences are species-specific; avoid pretending to show subjective perception. |
| Motion mechanics | Change one physical input and observe the stated toy model | How a modeled force or geometry affects a visible outcome | Do not advertise a toy model as measured speed or animal performance. |
| Ecological network | Add or remove a stipulated interaction | Direct and indirect links in a small declared network | Model consequences are conditional; they do not predict a real ecosystem without evidence. |
| Conservation decision | Compare two clearly specified scenarios | Trade-offs, uncertainty and evidence available to a decision | Avoid unsupported forecasts or prescribing intervention outside the lesson's evidence. |

## Per-lesson handoff

A brief should specify the focal organism or taxon, prior knowledge, misconception, prediction, what the learner manipulates, the particular evidence shown at reveal, a genuinely new recall scenario, the diagram's conventions and the source for every factual claim. For generated imagery, add the taxonomic identity, distinctive anatomy, pose, environment, reconstruction uncertainty, reference material, alt text and independent review status. Store the image only when it improves that lesson's question.

Before a draft can be advanced, obtain independent domain and source review, a walkthrough and keyboard/screen-reader review, strict phone fit, and a learner review. Do not infer learning outcomes from a mechanical browser pass. Record any scientifically unresolved identification or source coverage as a blocker, not as a verified claim.
