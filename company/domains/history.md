## Audience
Curious adults and older students who enjoy history but distrust it as a list of dates, and people who like charts and maps and want to know where the numbers come from. Many have heard "people died at 30", "the winter beat Napoleon" or "the past was static" and never tested them. They want to look at the record themselves, not be told a story.

## What a history lesson here is
A History lesson is **predict, explore, reveal on real data or a real map**. The learner makes a prediction about the past that a common view gets wrong. They then work the actual record on the stage, with preset buttons that recompute the picture from the data, and the reveal quotes what their own presses showed.

- **The stage is the record.** Every number and every line on the stage comes from a data file named in the brief and recomputed by its check script. Inventing illustrative numbers is reserved for subject categories' toy models. Here a model is allowed only when it is labelled as ours and sits beside the real series, never in place of it.
- **Not a narrative.** A lesson does not retell events in order. If a lesson needs more than one sentence of story to make sense, it is the wrong lesson. The one sentence of context goes in the stage question.
- **Not dates to memorise.** Dates appear only as axes, labels and the case in the question. The recall item asks about the mechanism or the shape of the data ("most of the losses came before the cold"), never a date, a name or a place.
- **Every reveal ends with where the record stops.** That means who was counted and who was not, how the numbers were rebuilt, and what the data cannot tell us. That sentence is a claim like any other.
- **The past's own charts are fair game.** Playfair, Nightingale, Minard and van Langren made some of the first data graphics. When a lesson uses one, the stage lets the learner re-draw it (divide wages by price, compare areas, re-read the scale). It does not trace the original image, and it says what the original chart hid.

## What we refuse to publish (the refuse list; cm-history owns it, changes need a decision record)
- **Numbers nobody here opened.** A figure quoted from memory, a textbook or a web page this host cannot open is not a claim. It is either cut or held for the fact-checker as a citation, and a held citation cannot carry the lesson (see "How claims are sourced").
- **Rankings of peoples, cultures, religions or "civilisations"** as better, more advanced or more deserving. Comparisons of countries are allowed only as a measured quantity (income per person, emissions, area), with its uncertainty, and never as a verdict on the people.
- **Moral or political conclusions stated as facts**: who was right in a war, whether an empire was good, what a country owes today, what policy follows. A lesson may show the numbers that such arguments use. It never makes the argument.
- **Atrocity as spectacle.** No death tolls used as a surprise for its own sake, and no gamified stage over victims (no "how many can you save" sliders on real massacres). Genocide, slavery and famine lessons are about the record and how it was made, and only after the sensitive-topics route.
- **"Great man" history**: one person credited with a collective outcome. "Napoleon lost his army" is a phrase; the lesson is about the column's numbers.
- **Counterfactual history stated as fact** ("without X, Y would have happened"). A counterfactual may be a labelled model with its assumptions on the stage, or nothing.
- **Maps that present contested borders as settled.** Any border that is disputed today, or approximate in the source, is drawn and labelled as such (historical-basemaps marks every 1279 border "approximate").
- **Reconstructed images of real people, and archival photographs of the dead.** Illustrations are original and schematic.

## Sensitive topics (the review route)
**Classes that trigger the route:** war and violence (including war deaths as numbers), colonialism and empire, slavery and forced labour, genocide and mass killing, borders and territory (anything disputed today), religion, famine, epidemics that are tied to a named group, migration and population by group or country, and comparisons of groups. **Added 2026-10-15 (W-889), under the founder's decision `company/decisions/2026-10-15-founder-sensitive-route-and-history-tranches.md`, which adopted the route design `company/decisions/2026-10-14-w626-sensitive-topics-review-route.md` (Decision 1):** race and ethnicity, comparisons of countries as well as groups, and political figures (any named leader, ruler, party or movement). The full table is section 0 of `company/international/sensitive-topics-checklist.md`; where it and this charter differ (it says epidemics tied to a named group *or place*), this charter applies until localization-lead rules (W-889 section of the briefs file). Each concept's route is recorded as `reviewRoute` in `content/curriculum/history.json`.

This is the same route Big Ideas uses for its society topics (`company/domains/big-ideas-strategy.md` section 6). Its missing piece, a cultural-reviewer scope for source-language lessons (W-626), was decided yes by the founder on 2026-10-15 (W-764) and the scope patch is applied. History shares that route and adds no second one.

1. **Brief.** The strand manager marks "Review route: sensitive (class)" and writes a **care line**: what the lesson will not say, whose count is missing from the record, and the wording to avoid. No policy conclusion, no people ranked, and no victim group used as a surprise.
2. **research-lead**, *before build*: a short literature or provenance note in `company/research/` for each sensitive brief. It covers what the dataset counts, who made it and when, the main published alternatives to its numbers, and "we don't know" where that is true. The brief's limit sentence must match the note.
3. **cultural-reviewer**, *before build* (on the brief) and *before publish* (on the walkthrough), recorded as `company/reviews/<slug>/<date>-culture-en-brief.md` (brief) and `company/reviews/<slug>/<date>-culture-en.md` (publish). The role's scope has covered this since 2026-10-15 (W-764). A sensitive brief stays unclaimed (its skip line says so) until the research-lead note and a brief verdict of go exist. Sensitive concepts beyond the written briefs are tranche 2 (founder F-16) and are not briefed until its four conditions hold.
4. **fact-checker** as usual. A sensitive claim whose number is overturned goes back to the strand manager and research-lead, not to the builder.
5. Nothing on the route is "noted". Every finding is answered in the brief or the review record.

## How claims are sourced (binding; changes need a decision record)
**Claims are split three ways**, and every brief lists them under these headings:
- **Data**: a number read from a named file, with its row or column ("Nightingale.csv, sum of Disease over 24 months = 14,476"). Support `source`, with the source string naming the file *and* its pin (`history-data.mjs: nightingale`). The fact-checker verifies a data claim by re-running the check script, which is a check they can actually do from this host.
- **Derivation**: arithmetic on data claims (shares, ratios, averages, km per degree). Support `derivation`, checked in the same script.
- **Source**: anything the data file does not contain, such as why something happened, who drew a chart, what an author argued, or what historians dispute. These are **held** until the fact-checker opens the source. The reveal carries **at most one** held source sentence and names its cut fallback. Every other held sentence is marked "cut if not checkable", and the lesson must still teach without them.

**Data must be openable.** A dataset qualifies only if someone on this host can fetch it and re-run the check:
- **Reachable today** (checked 2026-10-14 with curl from this host): `raw.githubusercontent.com`, `registry.npmjs.org` (npm tarballs) and `pypi.org`. Natural Earth is already in `node_modules/world-atlas` (2.0.2).
- **Blocked today**: `ourworldindata.org`, `rug.nl` (the Maddison Project's home), `cran.r-project.org`, `census.gov`, `naturalearthdata.com`, the GitHub API, and most publisher and journal sites (fact-check records from W-369 onwards).
- **Pins.** `company/domains/briefs/checks/history-data.mjs` fetches every file into `artifacts/history-data/` (gitignored: third-party data keep their own licences) and verifies each one by SHA-256. A brief may use a file only after it has a pin there. If upstream changes a file, the pin fails and the check script refuses to run. Re-pin only after re-running the numbers.
- **Never cite unopened numbers.** If a number came from a data file's documentation, the claim says so ("as the HistData documentation states"), because the documentation was opened but the primary source behind it was not.

## Boundaries with other categories
Shared verdict words are used wherever a History lesson tests a famous claim about the past (Playfair's "never so well off", "the winter beat Napoleon", a book's thesis): **holds up · holds with limits · mixed or contested · untested · did not replicate**. These are the words Books and Big Ideas use (`company/domains/big-ideas-strategy.md` section 8.2). "Did not replicate" rarely fits history. "Mixed or contested" with both sources named is the usual honest verdict for a disputed reconstruction.

The placement rule follows Books section 8: one idea, one owner, and engines are shared property. Disputes go to the two category leads, then to head-of-product.

## Portfolio rules
- **Strands.** Each concept carries one strand in `subarea`: `population-economy` (Population & Economy over Time: growth, cities, the demographic record, trade, wages, energy), `maps-borders` (Maps & Borders: empires, migrations, how maps changed, projections in history) or `events-data` (Events through Data: epidemics, wars as numbers, technology diffusion, famine, told with care). Each strand manager owns that strand's concepts and the briefs file `company/domains/briefs/history.<strand>.md`. cm-history owns the cross-strand ranked list `company/domains/briefs/history.md`.
- **Data first.** A concept may be briefed only when its dataset is reachable and pinned. Concepts whose data are not yet found are kept in the map with `"data": "not found yet"`, which keeps the map honest about them.
- **Balance.** Each ranked list covers all three strands, and no strand has more than half of the briefs.
- **One lesson per idea first.** No concept gets a second lesson until every demand-3 concept has one.
- **Ranking.** Demand first. Then how far the record overturns a common view. Then how well it shows on one phone screen. Then verification cost (data and derivation only rank above held sources) and the review route (standard ranks above sensitive, because sensitive briefs wait for W-626).
- **Demand ratings** in `content/curriculum/history.json` are the drafter's estimates, not learner data. cm-history confirms or changes them at the first cycle planning.
- **Lesson shape** (every brief): a format line from `company/design/formats.md`; preset buttons listed in DOM order, each setting the full state, with no Reset button, so QA pressing every control in order reaches every milestone; every meaning-carrying label at least 10 px at 390 px wide (author at 11 px); a stage question of at most 25 words; a reveal of at most 90 words, counted by the check script with the numbers filled in; and a transfer recall on a different case, with its answer in the check script.
- **Target and gate.** The founder set a target of 100 History lessons, built now (`company/decisions/2026-10-14-founder-history-100.md`), and lifted the W-556 wave gate for this category. Every brief drafted outside the owning role has a line in `company/ops/brief-skips.json` ("awaiting cm-history re-check") until its strand manager re-checks it. Sensitive briefs keep a second hold for W-626. A brief whose data cannot be opened is not written: it goes on the W-583 list for the web-capable fact-check session.
- **Launch gate.** The category stays incubating until 5 lessons pass Domain Launch Review.
