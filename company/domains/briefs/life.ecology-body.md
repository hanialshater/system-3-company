### 1. steady-catch-from-a-lake · concept: maximum-sustainable-yield · stretch · demand 2 · rank 3 · format: Single card (quota slider + Run, one fish-count-over-time chart)
- Why this one next: `maximum-sustainable-yield` is the coverage "next" uncovered concept in Life (demand 2, prerequisite logistic-growth). Population harvest sits inside the category focus (populations through simulation).
- Audience / knowledge boundary: has seen `fastest-at-the-bend` (logistic growth, fastest at half full). Does not know that this peak growth sets a hard ceiling on any fixed yearly catch, or that a catch just above it empties a full lake slowly and then fast.
- Misconception: "A lake of 1,000 fish can supply 130 a year forever. That is only 13%, and it is full to start with."
- Prediction (stage text, 25 words): "A lake holds at most 1,000 fish and starts full. A few fish multiply fast; a crowded lake barely grows. You catch 130 every year." Question: "After 60 years, how many fish are left?" options: A. About 870 / B. About 600 / C. 0 (answer: C, empty in year 55). All three options are numbers. 600 is the real outcome for a catch of 120, so it is a fair distractor.
- Model: logistic with harvest, dN/dt = 0.5·N·(1 − N/1000) − H, N(0) = 1000, Euler step 0.001 year. Without the catch the lake neither grows nor shrinks at 1,000.
- What the stage shows: one chart, fish (0 to 1,000) over years 0 to 60, with a dot field of the lake above it (1 dot = 10 fish) drawn in the same panel. Controls (2): quota slider 80 to 150 a year (step 10, starts at 130) and Run (draws the curve over about 2 s, ≤ 400 ms when replayed). Readout: "fish left: N" at the cursor end. The locked stage shows the full lake and an empty chart: no target line and no 500 or 125 marks before the commit.
- Key state: the learner has run 130 to the end (lake empty) and at least one quota of 120 or less (settles), so both endings are on screen. Reveal unlocks after both.
- Check (node, re-run 2026-10-14): fish left by year (10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60): quota 120 → 665, 620, 607, 602, 601, 600; quota 130 → 619, 539, 487, 428, 290, empty at year 54.9; quota 140 empty at 28.6; 150 empty at 20.6; 80 → 800; 100 → 724; 110 → 673; 125 → 531 at year 60 (heading slowly to 500). Settling level for H ≤ 125: N* = 500 + √(250,000 − 2,000·H). Largest yearly growth 0.5·N·(1 − N/1000) is 125 at N = 500.
- Reveal draft (≤ 90 words; 80 words): "In your run, a catch of 130 drained the lake: about 540 fish at year 20, 290 at year 50, empty in year 55. This lake can add at most 125 fish a year, when it is half full. A catch of 130 takes more than it can ever replace, so it empties: slowly, then fast. A catch of 120 settled near 600. Ten fish a year changed the ending. Toy model: real stocks also swing with weather and luck."
- Phone fit (390×844): the reveal points only at the curve the learner drew and its end readout. The chart stays at the top of the stage; the dot field shrinks into the chart header if the reveal would crop the stage top. The quota row sits below the chart. One representation: the fish-count timeline.
- Planned claims:
  1. In the lesson's model, a full lake of 1,000 fish with a fixed catch of 130 a year is empty in year 55 (54.9); with 120 a year it settles at 600. support: simulation; source: node Euler run above, rerunnable (dN/dt = 0.5N(1 − N/1000) − H, dt 0.001).
  2. The most the model lake can add in a year is 125 fish, at 500 fish: growth 0.5·N·(1 − N/1000) peaks at N = K/2 with value rK/4 = 0.5 × 1000 / 4. support: derivation; source: calculus on the stated model.
  3. Any fixed catch above 125 a year has no level where growth matches it, so the lake empties from any start. support: derivation; source: 0.5N(1 − N/1000) ≤ 125 for all N.
  4. The model is a toy: fixed growth rate and limit, no weather, age structure or chance (convention, stated on the card). Optional context for the fact-checker, not on the card: the logistic surplus-production model is the textbook origin of "maximum sustainable yield" (Schaefer 1954; Hilborn and Walters 1992, Quantitative Fisheries Stock Assessment). Unverified, needs a human source check, and not needed for the lesson.
