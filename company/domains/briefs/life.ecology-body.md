### 1. steady-catch-from-a-lake · concept: maximum-sustainable-yield · stretch · demand 2 · rank 3 · format: Single card (quota slider + Run, one fish-count-over-time chart)
- Why this one next: `maximum-sustainable-yield` is the coverage "next" uncovered concept in Life (demand 2, prerequisite logistic-growth). Population harvest sits inside the category focus (populations through simulation).
- Audience / knowledge boundary: has seen `fastest-at-the-bend` (logistic growth, fastest at half full). Does not know that this peak growth sets a hard ceiling on any fixed yearly catch, or that a catch just above it empties a full lake slowly and then fast.
- Misconception: "A lake of 1,000 fish can supply 130 a year forever. That is only 13%, and it is full to start with."
- Prediction (stage text, 25 words): "A lake holds at most 1,000 fish and starts full. A few fish multiply fast; a crowded lake barely grows. You catch 130 every year." Question: "After 60 years, how many fish are left?" options: A. About 870 / B. About 600 / C. 0 (answer: C, empty in year 55). All three options are numbers. 600 is the real outcome for a catch of 120, so it is a fair distractor.
- Model: logistic with harvest, dN/dt = 0.5·N·(1 − N/1000) − H, N(0) = 1000, Euler step 0.001 year. Without the catch the lake neither grows nor shrinks at 1,000.
- What the stage shows: one chart, fish (0 to 1,000) over years 0 to 60, with a dot field of the lake above it (1 dot = 10 fish) drawn in the same panel. Controls (2): quota slider 80 to 150 a year (step 10, starts at 130) and Run (draws the curve over about 2 s, ≤ 400 ms when replayed). Readout: "fish left: N" at the cursor end. The locked stage shows the full lake and an empty chart: no target line and no 500 or 125 marks before the commit.
- Key state: the learner has run 130 to the end (lake empty) and at least one quota of 120 or less (settles), so both endings are on screen. Reveal unlocks after both.
- Check (node, re-run 2026-10-14): fish left by year (10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60): quota 120 → 665, 620, 607, 602, 601, 600; quota 130 → 619, 539, 487, 428, 290, empty at year 54.9; quota 140 empty at 28.6; 150 empty at 20.6; 80 → 800; 100 → 724; 110 → 673; 125 → 531 at year 60 (heading slowly to 500). Settling level for H ≤ 125: N* = 500 + √(250,000 − 2,000·H). Largest yearly growth 0.5·N·(1 − N/1000) is 125 at N = 500.
- Reveal draft (≤ 90 words; 80 words): "In your run, a catch of 130 drained the lake: about 540 fish at year 20, 290 at year 50, empty in year 55. This lake can add at most 125 fish a year, when it is half full. A catch of 130 takes more than it can ever replace, so it empties: slowly, then fast. A catch of 120 settled near 600. Ten fish a year changed the ending. Toy model: real stocks also swing with weather and luck."
- Phone fit (390×844): the reveal points only at the curve the learner drew and its end readout. The chart stays at the top of the stage; the dot field shrinks into the chart header if the reveal would crop the stage top. The quota row sits below the chart. One representation: the fish-count timeline.
- Planned claims:
  1. In the lesson's model, a full lake of 1,000 fish with a fixed catch of 130 a year is empty in year 55 (54.9); with 120 a year it settles at 600. support: simulation; source: node Euler run above, rerunnable (dN/dt = 0.5N(1 − N/1000) − H, dt 0.001).
  2. The most the model lake can add in a year is 125 fish, at 500 fish: growth 0.5·N·(1 − N/1000) peaks at N = K/2 with value rK/4 = 0.5 × 1000 / 4. support: derivation; source: calculus on the stated model.
  3. Any fixed catch above 125 a year has no level where growth matches it, so the lake empties from any start. support: derivation; source: 0.5N(1 − N/1000) ≤ 125 for all N.
  4. The model is a toy: fixed growth rate and limit, no weather, age structure or chance (convention, stated on the card). Optional context for the fact-checker, not on the card: the logistic surplus-production model is the textbook origin of "maximum sustainable yield" (Schaefer 1954; Hilborn and Walters 1992, Quantitative Fisheries Stock Assessment). Unverified, needs a human source check, and not needed for the lesson.
- curriculum: `{"primary":"life:maximum-sustainable-yield","also":["life:logistic-growth"],"prerequisites":["life:logistic-growth"],"level":"stretch","strand":"ecology-body","tags":["harvest","logistic growth","tipping point"]}`
- Needs interaction-designer spec: no (Single card, two controls).
- Differs from nearest: `fastest-at-the-bend` asks where growth peaks (K/2), so this card does not ask that again: it asks what a fixed catch does over decades, and the peak appears only as the reason in the reveal. `overshoot-from-delay` is a delay oscillation with no harvest. `below-the-threshold-crash` (Allee) is collapse from small numbers with no catch. Economics `stock-vs-flow` is the bathtub idea without growth that depends on the stock.

### 3. empty-ponds-still-left · concept: life:metapopulation-extinction · stretch · demand 2 · rank 7 · format: Simulation run (100-pond grid, drain preset buttons, seed shown)
- Why this one next: habitat loss is the category focus (populations through simulation), and the surprise is a threshold the learner can watch: a species can vanish while good habitat is still there.
- Audience / knowledge boundary: has seen `cut-the-island` (less area, fewer species). Does not know that a species living as many small populations needs empty patches to be refilled faster than occupied ones die out, so it is lost before the last good patch goes.
- Misconception: "As long as some good habitat is left, the species that lives there will hang on in it."
- Prediction (stage text, 23 words): "100 ponds. Each year frogs die out in some ponds, and frogs from occupied ponds settle empty ones. Now 60 ponds have frogs." Question: "We drain 70 ponds. 30 good ponds are left, 19 with frogs. After 100 years, how many ponds have frogs?" options: A. 19 or more / B. About 10 / C. None (answer: C). The locked stage shows the 10×10 grid with 60 frog ponds and no drained ponds.
- Model (on the card): each year, frogs in an occupied pond die out with chance 0.2; an empty good pond gains frogs with chance 1 − 0.995^n, where n is the number of occupied ponds. Ponds 0 to 59 start occupied; the drained ponds are those with (index × 37) mod 100 below the drain count, so drains mix occupied and empty ponds. Seeded mulberry32; seed 1 is the start, shown on the stage.
- What the stage shows: one 10×10 grid of ponds (drained: grey with a cross; empty: outline; frogs: filled; shape plus fill, not hue alone), a year counter and the readout "year 100: k ponds with frogs". Controls in DOM order (4 buttons): "Drain 30", "Drain 50", "Drain 70", "New seed". Each drain button runs 100 years on the current seed and replays the grid within 400 ms; "New seed" re-runs the current drain with the next seed.
- Milestones (minInteractions 2): m1 "a smaller drain seen" (Drain 30 or 50); m2 "Drain 70 seen", the question's case. QA path: 30, 50, 70, New seed; the last state (drain 70, seed 2) still shows an empty grid. Values quoted are recorded at the milestone.
- Check (E3): seed 1, year 100: drain 30 → 33 ponds (start 42); drain 50 → none (died out in year 67); drain 70 → none (year 23). Seeds 1 to 1,000: drain 70 died out by year 100 in 996 runs; drain 50 in 496; drain 30 in 0 (mean 25.8 ponds). Levins version (the deterministic average): occupied share p* = 1 − D − e/c with e/c = 0.4, so 0.6 now and 0 once the drained share D reaches 0.6.
- Reveal draft (83 words, counted by script): "With 70 ponds drained, the frogs were gone by year 23, though 30 good ponds were still there. Draining 30 left frogs in 33 ponds. Each pond's frogs die out now and then, and only frogs from other ponds refill it. With few occupied ponds, refills come too slowly to keep up. In the simplest version of this model, the species is lost once the drained share reaches the share that had frogs before: 60% here. Habitat can outlast its species. Toy model."
- Phone fit (390×844): the grid (10 cells of ≥ 28 px) with the readout above it fits the stage top; the four buttons fit one row (or two rows of two). The reveal points at the grid and the readout.
- Planned claims:
  1. In the lesson's model (seed 1), after 70 of 100 ponds are drained the frogs die out in year 23; across 1,000 seeds they are gone by year 100 in 996. support: simulation; source: check script E3, seeds disclosed.
  2. After 30 ponds are drained, frogs persist (33 ponds at year 100, seed 1; no seed of 1,000 died out by year 100). support: simulation; source: E3.
  3. In the deterministic Levins model dp/dt = c·p·(1 − D − p) − e·p, the occupied share settles at 1 − D − e/c, so the species is lost when the destroyed share D reaches 1 − e/c, the share occupied before any loss. support: derivation; source: set dp/dt = 0. Optional context, not on the card: Levins 1969 and Nee and May 1992 ("Dynamics of metapopulations: habitat destruction and competitive coexistence", J. Anim. Ecol. 61). **Unverified, needs a human source check.**
  4. Rates, pond count and the drain pattern are invented (convention, on the card).

### 4. opposite-flows-swap-more · concept: life:countercurrent-exchange · core · demand 2 · rank 10 · format: Before / after toggle (flow-direction buttons and section presets, one exchanger diagram)

- Audience / knowledge boundary: knows things move from high to low concentration (`slow-across-a-room`). Does not know that two side-by-side flows level off at the halfway mark when they run the same way, but keep exchanging along the whole length when they run opposite ways.
- Misconception: "Which way the water flows past the blood makes no difference. Same pipes, same speed, same exchange."
- Prediction (stage text, 24 words): "Water with oxygen level 100 flows past blood starting at 0, through 10 sections. In each section the two level off halfway between them." Question: "Flowing the same way, blood leaves at 50. Flowing opposite ways, blood leaves at about:" options: A. 50 / B. 70 / C. 90 (answer: C, 90.9). All options are numbers. The locked stage shows both pipes with no arrows and no levels.
- Model (on the card): 10 sections; each tick, in every section blood and water move to the average of the two; then blood moves one section along and water moves one section along its own direction; fresh blood enters at 0 and fresh water at 100. Run to steady state (2,000 ticks, computed instantly). Deterministic.
- What the stage shows: two horizontal pipes (water above, blood below) split into sections, each section labelled with its level (≥ 10 px), arrows for each flow, and the readout "blood leaves at N". Controls in DOM order (5 buttons): "Same way" (start, active and disabled), "Opposite way", "1 section", "4 sections", "10 sections" (start, active and disabled). ### 5. same-dose-levels-off · concept: life:dose-accumulation-plateau · core · demand 2 · rank 13 · format: Single card (dosing-interval preset buttons, one sawtooth chart)
