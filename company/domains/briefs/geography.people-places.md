# People & Places briefs (cm-geography-people-places)

Ranked briefs for this strand, in the same format as `geography.md` (`### N. slug · concept: <id> · level · demand N`). `tools/claim.mjs next geography` hands these out after the domain's own list. Check every brief's answer in node before adding it.

Added 2026-10-13 at the founder's request. Sensitive topic: race and housing. Cultural review required (cultural-reviewer) before build and before publish. Language follows the sources: "groups" (the Census and Brown University use race and Hispanic-origin categories); no stereotypes, no claim about why any person lives anywhere. Overlap: nearest existing lesson is `population-distribution` (where people live is uneven); this one is about sorting, not density. Prerequisite lesson: `mild-preferences-sharp-divides` (Economics, markets), which teaches the mechanism.

### 1. schelling-on-new-york · concept: segregation-model-nyc · core · demand 3 · cultural review required · sensitive topic: race and housing
- Status 2026-10-14: node check DONE (coarse hand-drawn mask, see Check). The original question ("above or below the real city's measured level") was NOT fair and is redesigned below. Real-city figures are unverified (egress blocked). Not final until the cultural-reviewer answers the list at the end. Reference added: Easley and Kleinberg ch. 4 (section 4.5 unconfirmed), S2-17.
- Learner knows: the Schelling mechanism (mild preferences, sorted grid) from the Economics lesson / does not know: that a segregation index depends on the size of the unit it is measured on, and what a model like this can and cannot say about a real city.
- Misconception: "If a simple model reproduces a city's pattern, then that is why the city looks that way." Second: "A mixed-at-random city would score a segregation index near zero."
- Prediction (redesigned): "Run the Economics rule on a grid shaped like New York's five boroughs, two groups placed at random in NYC-like proportions, then everyone with a 30% threshold moves until settled. Before anyone moves, how segregated does the random start already look on D, measured on 2 x 2-cell blocks?" options: A) Near 0 (nothing is sorted yet) / B) About 0.5 / C) Near 1. **Answer: B.** Random start D = 0.47 to 0.50 on seeds 1 to 5 (0.43 to 0.54 over seeds 1 to 50). After settling the same blocks give 0.78 to 0.84 (seeds 1 to 5). Why: a block holds only a handful of agents, so chance alone makes blocks uneven; D is high on small units even with no sorting. Knowledge boundary: a grid of squares is not tracts; one cell is one agent, a tract holds thousands; the model has no income, no prices, no jobs, no history; two groups, not four.
- Why redesigned: the model's D moves from 1.00 (1 x 1 cells) to 0.38 (8 x 8) on the same settled world, and the random start alone spans 0.14 to 0.49 across block sizes. Any published tract D (about 0.7 to 0.8 for White and Black residents of New York, from memory, UNVERIFIED) lies inside that range, so "model above or below the city" is decided by choosing the block size, not by the model. The card must therefore not draw the real D as a target line on the model's axis. It says "same order, same direction, different unit", and the threshold is not tuned. The real D appears only in the final toggle, labelled with its source, year, group pair and unit (tract), and flagged as not comparable number-for-number.
- Check (done in node 22; script `company/domains/briefs/checks/schelling-nyc-check.mjs`; run `node ... selftest|draw|check`):
  - Rule: identical to abm.ts (8 neighbours, no wrap, unhappy iff like*100 < pct*occupied, 10% of cells empty, mulberry32, Fisher-Yates, per-tick shuffle of unhappy, move to a random empty cell, CAP 60). Cells outside the mask are walls: not agents, not empty, not neighbours. Self-test: with an all-land mask and equal shares the script reproduces abm.ts `run()` final frames exactly (seeds 1 to 3: 13, 15, 12 ticks, frames identical).
  - Mask: HAND-DRAWN, not from NYC Open Data (tqmj-j8zm and the Brown site returned proxy 403). Rough borough polygons in lon/lat typed from memory of public geography, 32 x 32 over lon -74.27 to -73.69, lat 40.48 to 40.93, a cell is land if its centre is inside a polygon. 404 land cells: Queens 145, Brooklyn 91, Staten Island 82, Bronx 58, Manhattan 28 (Manhattan is about 1 cell wide, and the Bronx, Queens and Brooklyn borders are crude). The shipped lesson must use the rasterised official file; this mask only supports the check.
  - Shares: model uses two groups. Equal 50/50 (engine default) and 31:20 normalised to 61/39 (the White non-Hispanic and Black non-Hispanic shares from memory, UNVERIFIED; Hispanic and Asian residents, about half the city, are not in a two-group model). The engine's `place()` splits equally, so the lesson needs a `share1` option and a wall value (engine change for the builder; the rule is unchanged).
  - D = 0.5 * sum over blocks |a_i/A - b_i/B| on aligned k x k blocks, seeds 1 to 5, threshold 30:

    | blocks (cells) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | 50/50 start | 1.00 | 0.45 | 0.30 | 0.23 | 0.16 | 0.12 |
    | 50/50 settled | 1.00 | 0.75 | 0.60 | 0.51 | 0.37 | 0.31 |
    | 61/39 start | 1.00 | 0.49 | 0.32 | 0.26 | 0.18 | 0.14 |
    | 61/39 settled | 1.00 | 0.80 | 0.66 | 0.57 | 0.46 | 0.38 |

  - Like-neighbour share (the engine's metric): 50/50 start 0.48 to 0.51, settled 0.73 to 0.78. 61/39 start 0.50 to 0.53, settled 0.75 to 0.81.
  - Settling: 50/50 settles in 6 to 11 ticks (5 of 5). 61/39 takes 20 to 60 ticks and seed 3 had not settled at CAP 60; over seeds 1 to 50, 29 of 50 had not settled by 60 ticks. The card must not say "settles" for this setup; use "after 60 ticks or when nobody is unhappy" and show the count, or prefer 50/50.
  - Threshold sweep (50/50, mean D, blocks 2 / 4 / 8): 0%: .45/.23/.13; 20%: .53/.30/.18; 30%: .75/.51/.31; 40%: .83/.60/.39; 60%: .96/.83/.66. D rises with the threshold at every block size; the direction is robust, the level is not.
  - What this does not show: a match to any published D. Not computed, because the unit differs (see "Why redesigned").
- Interaction: threshold slider, Run / Step / Reset, seeded RNG disclosed. Stage: the NYC-shaped grid, live share happy, and D (blocks of 2 x 2 cells, size stated on the card) over ticks, starting from the random-start value so the first frame already shows about 0.5. Final step (toggle, not a second card) reveals the real-world figure with source, year, groups, unit.
- Reveal (written to fit 90 words): a random mix already scores about 0.5 on small blocks, so a segregation number means nothing without its unit. Mild preferences push this model to about 0.8, so preferences alone can produce a divided pattern. That does not show they did. Documented causes include the 1938 HOLC maps, housing and lending discrimination, and income.
