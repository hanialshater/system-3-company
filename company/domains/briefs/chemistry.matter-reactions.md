### 1. ethanol-and-its-twin · concept: hydrogen-bonding · core · demand 2 · format: Before / after toggle
- Replaces brief 33 (`lighter-yet-boils-later`, skipped in `company/ops/brief-skips.json`, board W-069): that question asked for water's boiling point, which every learner knows, and its key state leaned on an unsourced "2 times the energy" meter. This version asks about a molecule nobody has memorised and uses no energy meter.
- Learner knows: that a liquid boils when its molecules escape into gas; that oxygen pulls electrons harder than hydrogen (bond-polarity, `who-grabs-the-electrons`) / does not know: that an H on an O (or N, F) lets molecules cling to each other with hydrogen bonds, and that this, not mass, can decide a boiling point.
- Misconception: molecules made of the same atoms, with the same mass, boil at about the same temperature (boiling point follows weight).
- Prediction (stage text <= 25 words): "Ethanol and dimethyl ether are built from the same nine atoms (C2H6O, 46 u). Ethanol boils at about 78 °C. Dimethyl ether boils at about..." options: A. about 78 °C / B. about 35 °C / C. about −24 °C (answer: C).
- Check: both C2H6O, 2(12.011) + 6(1.008) + 15.999 = 46.07 u (node). Ethanol CH3-CH2-O-H has one H on O; dimethyl ether CH3-O-CH3 has none, so ether molecules cannot donate a hydrogen bond to each other. Boiling points at 1 atm: ethanol about 78.3 °C, dimethyl ether about −24.8 °C, a gap of about 100 °C (CRC).
- Locked stage: a cluster of 6 ethanol molecules (ball-and-stick, O filled, H on O marked) with dashed hydrogen bonds O-H···O, readout "Ethanol · boils about 78 °C". No ether drawn, no ether temperature shown (No answer before the commit).
- What the stage shows (explore): one toggle "Move the O to the middle" redraws every molecule as CH3-O-CH3: the H on O disappears, every dash vanishes, a readout "dashes in cluster: N" falls to 0 and the boiling readout changes to "Dimethyl ether · boils about −24 °C". Toggle back restores ethanol. Second control: "Pull one out" drags the centre molecule away; for ethanol the dashes it held stretch and break (count shown), for ether nothing breaks. Milestones: (1) ether shown, (2) one molecule pulled out in each form. The dash count is a schematic of one moment, disclosed as illustrative; there is no energy number on the stage.
- Key state: both forms seen, with the ethanol cluster's dash count recorded at milestone 1 and the ether count 0.
- Reveal draft (80 words): "Same nine atoms, same 46 u, yet the boiling points sit about 100 °C apart: ethanol about 78 °C, dimethyl ether about −24 °C. In your ethanol cluster, {n} dashes linked an O–H to a neighbour's O. Moving the O to the middle left no H on oxygen, and every dash vanished. Boiling pulls molecules apart, so the grip between them, not their weight, sets the temperature. Water, at only 18 u, boils at 100 °C for the same reason."
- Phone fit: the reveal names the dash count and the two boiling readouts; the cluster (both forms' last state) and both readouts must be on screen with stage-top crop 0. Keep the stage to one picture, one readout row, two controls.
- Planned claims:
  1. Ethanol and dimethyl ether share the formula C2H6O and a molar mass of about 46.07 g/mol. support: derivation; source: structure count and standard atomic weights (IUPAC 2021: C 12.011, H 1.008, O 15.999).
  2. Ethanol boils at about 78 °C and dimethyl ether at about −24 °C at 1 atm. support: citation; source: CRC, "Physical Constants of Organic Compounds" (ethanol 78.29 °C, dimethyl ether −24.8 °C); cross-check PubChem CID 702 and CID 8254 if reachable.
  3. A hydrogen bond needs an H bonded to a strongly electronegative atom (N, O or F) on the donor; dimethyl ether has no such H, so its molecules cannot hydrogen-bond to each other. support: derivation (structure) plus citation; source: Arunan et al., "Definition of the hydrogen bond (IUPAC Recommendations 2011)", Pure Appl. Chem. 83(8), 1637-1641; Atkins, intermolecular interactions.
  4. Water (18.02 u) boils at 100 °C at 1 atm (99.97 °C on ITS-90). support: citation; source: CRC; molar mass by derivation.
  5. The dashes on the stage are a schematic of one instant, not a measured count. support: model disclosure; source: the lesson's own drawing.
- Metadata for meta.json: `also`: ["chemistry:bond-polarity", "chemistry:states-of-matter"]; references: none.
- curriculum: concept `hydrogen-bonding` exists (core, demand 2, prerequisite bond-polarity); renamed "Hydrogen bonds, not mass, set boiling points" to match this brief.
- Needs interaction-designer spec: no (before/after toggle with the "after" persisting into the reveal, per formats.md).
- Differs from: `who-grabs-the-electrons` (bond-polarity) shows electrons shifting inside one bond, not attraction between molecules; `which-dissolves-in-oil` (like-dissolves-like) is about mixing, not boiling; `eight-percent-showing` (ice-floats) uses water's H-bond network for density, not boiling point. No lesson in any category compares isomers.

### 2. one-gram-two-fires · concept: nuclear-vs-chemical-energy · core · demand 3 · format: Single card
- Learner knows: that burning releases energy stored in bonds (pay-first-then-profit); that a nucleus is made of protons and neutrons (which-particle-names-it, no-atom-weighs-35-45) / does not know: how large the gap between chemical and nuclear energy is, and that it comes from what is rearranged: electrons in burning, the nucleus in fission.
- Misconception: nuclear fuel is "stronger" by a factor of tens or hundreds, like a better fuel; the scale gap is invisible because both are just "energy from fuel".
- Prediction (stage text <= 25 words): "Burn 1 g of carbon completely. Split all the atoms in 1 g of uranium-235. To the nearest power of ten, the uranium releases..." options: A. about 100 times more / B. about 10,000 times more / C. about 1 million times more / D. about 1 billion times more (answer: C, the ratio is about 2.5 million).
- Check (node, 2026-10-14): carbon: 393.51 kJ/mol / 12.011 g/mol = 32.8 kJ/g. Uranium: (1 / 235.04) mol x 6.022e23 x 200 MeV x 1.602e-13 J/MeV = 8.21e10 J/g. Ratio 2.51e6; log10 = 6.4, nearest power of ten 10^6. Per event: one C + O2 -> CO2 releases 393.51e3 / 96,485 = 4.08 eV; one fission about 200 MeV; ratio 4.9e7. Atoms per gram: carbon has 235.04 / 12.011 = 19.6 times more, which takes back only a factor of about 20. Equivalent: 1 g of U-235 matches about 2.5 tonnes of carbon.
- Locked stage: one horizontal log axis of energy (1 kJ to 100 GJ, a gridline per power of ten), a single bar "1 g carbon burned: about 33 kJ" and an empty, unlabelled slot for uranium. No uranium value or marker on the locked stage.
- What the stage shows (explore): control 1 "Split 1 g U-235" grows the uranium bar along the log axis to about 82 GJ (readout); control 2, a stepper "carbon burned: x10" multiplies the carbon mass by ten per tap (1 g, 10 g, ... ) with its bar climbing, until it passes the uranium bar between 1 t and 10 t (readout shows the mass and the number of taps, 6 or 7). Control 3 "Per event" switches both bars to one event (4.08 eV against 200 MeV) on the same kind of axis. Milestones: (1) uranium bar shown, (2) carbon bar reaches the uranium bar, (3) per-event view seen. Every value is computed from the constants above, no simulation.
- Key state: carbon stepped up to about 2.5 t to match 1 g of uranium, with the tap count recorded at milestone 2.
- Reveal draft (84 words): "Burning 1 g of carbon gave about 33 kJ; splitting 1 g of uranium-235 gave about 82 billion joules, about 2.5 million times more. You needed {n} tenfold steps, about 2.5 tonnes of carbon, to match one gram. Per event the gap is bigger: forming one CO2 frees about 4 eV, one fission about 200 MeV. Burning only moves electrons between atoms; fission rearranges the nucleus itself. Carbon packs 20 times more atoms into a gram, which closes only a little of the gap."
- Phone fit: the reveal names the two per-gram bars, the tap count and the per-event values; the axis with both bars and the per-event readout row must be on screen with stage-top crop 0. One axis, the bars on it, one readout row, three controls; per-event replaces the per-gram labels rather than adding a second chart (One representation on a phone).
- Planned claims:
  1. Burning carbon to CO2 releases about 393.5 kJ/mol, about 33 kJ per gram. support: derivation from a cited constant; source: standard enthalpy of formation of CO2(g), −393.51 kJ/mol (CODATA Key Values for Thermodynamics, 1989; CRC); arithmetic in node.
  2. Fission of one U-235 nucleus releases about 200 MeV. support: derivation plus citation; source: check from binding energies (about 7.6 MeV per nucleon at A = 235 against about 8.5 MeV per nucleon for the fragments near A = 90-140, about 0.9 x 235 = about 210 MeV; AME2020 tables or any nuclear physics text); citation Krane, *Introductory Nuclear Physics*, fission chapter (about 200 MeV total, about 190-200 MeV recoverable).
  3. Fully fissioning 1 g of U-235 releases about 8.2 x 10^10 J, about 2.5 million times the energy of burning 1 g of carbon, equal to burning about 2.5 tonnes of carbon. support: derivation; source: node arithmetic with NA = 6.02214076e23 /mol and 1 eV = 1.602176634e-19 J (SI 2019 exact).
  4. Forming one CO2 molecule from carbon and oxygen releases about 4.1 eV. support: derivation; source: 393.51e3 J/mol / 96,485 C/mol (Faraday constant, SI exact via NA and e).
  5. Assumption (stage caption or claim, not the reveal): complete fission of every atom is an idealisation; real reactor fuel is mostly U-238 and only a few per cent fissions before refuelling. Pure carbon stands in for coal, which gives less per gram (about 24-35 MJ/kg). support: citation; source: World Nuclear Association or IAEA fuel-burnup pages if reachable, else keep it as a stated assumption without numbers for burnup.
