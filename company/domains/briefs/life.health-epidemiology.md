### 1. contact-order-matters · concept: contact-timing-concurrency (new, core, demand 2; prerequisite herd-immunity) · core · demand 2
- curriculum.references: easley-kleinberg-2010 ch. 21 §21.6 (§ unconfirmed)
- Learner knows: that infection spreads along contacts; a "who is connected to whom" diagram / does not know: that infection can only move forward in time, so the same contacts in another order reach different people.
- Misconception: "If you can trace a chain of contacts from the first case to someone, that person is at risk." (A contact diagram ignores when each contact happened.)
- Prediction (stage shows only the four contacts as a diagram with no dates, A infected): "A is infected. Four contacts happen one at a time: A-B, B-C, C-D, D-E. If they happen in the order D-E, C-D, B-C, A-B, how many people other than A can get infected?" options: A. 0 / B. 1 / C. 4 (answer: B). Check (node, rule below): reversed order reaches only B; the order A-B, B-C, C-D, D-E reaches B, C, D, E (4). Rule: contacts happen one at a time; a person can pass the infection on only at a contact that comes after they were infected; every such contact transmits (a deliberately certain toy rule). The answer is not visible before the learner commits: the stage shows an undated diagram, and the order is in the question.
- Interaction (storyboard / scrub; matches `company/design/specs/contact-order-matters.md`): a dated contact timeline, rows A to E, days 0 to 4, four contacts as links. Controls in DOM order (7): Day slider, "Day 4" button, order chips "A→E", "E→A" (start, the question's own order), "Overlap", and person buttons "A" and "E" that choose the first case (start A) and keep the day and the order. People turn from healthy to infected only as the cursor passes a contact (on a day after the carrier was infected). The reveal quotes only what the learner can reach: E→A from A reaches 1 (B, day 4); A→E from A reaches 4; Overlap reaches 4 from A and 4 from E (checked: from E, D, C, B, A, one per day). From E, A→E reaches only D, quotable only if reached. The 1/k! result and "12 of 24 orders reach one person" are NOT reachable on the stage and are not in the reveal as seen. One representation: the dated contact timeline. Neutral "contacts", no disease named and no health advice.
- Payoff: The same contacts can protect or expose someone depending on their order; overlapping contacts open paths in both directions. A diagram of who knows whom overstates who can be reached (Morris and Kretzschmar 1997 on concurrency; unverified).
- Node check (re-run 2026-10-14 by me, own function `reach`, earliest-infection-time relaxation): (a) 3 people, A infected, A-B in March then B-C in February: reaches B only; B-C moved to April: reaches B and C (matches the ask). (b) 4 contacts on the path A-B-C-D-E, all 24 orders, A infected: reach counts are 1 person in 12 orders, 2 in 8, 3 in 3, 4 in 1 (mean 1.708). Closed form: P(reach at least k) = 1/k!, giving 24/24, 12/24, 4/24, 1/24, which agrees. (c) Overlap, all four contacts spanning the same period: reach 4 from A and 4 from E. (d) Serial A-B (months 1-2) then B-C (3-4): from A reaches B, C; from C reaches B only. Reversed serial: from A reaches B only; from C reaches A and B. Order alone makes the paths one-way; overlap makes them two-way (B infected in month 1 can still meet C in month 2-3). Only the answer (reversed = 1) is used in the prediction; the 1/k! result is a note only: the stage cannot reach it, so it is not a learner-facing claim unless told as arithmetic with its own claim, never as something the learner saw.
- Planned claims: (1) In the toy rule, infection reaches a person only through contacts that come after the carrier was infected (convention, the lesson's rule). (2) In the forward order all four people are reached; in the reversed order only B is (simulation / arithmetic from the rule, rerunnable). (3) Overlap rule (spec): days 1 to 4, the first case is infected on day 0, and every pair meets on each of days 1 to 4; on day d a contact infects the other person only if one of the pair was infected on an earlier day (no relay on the same day). Under this rule infection reaches all four from either end, one person per day (simulation, rerunnable; re-run 2026-10-14: from A days 1,2,3,4 for B..E; from E days 1,2,3,4 for D..A). (4) Concurrent partnerships can raise reach compared with the same partnerships in sequence (Morris and Kretzschmar 1997, "Concurrent partnerships and the spread of HIV", AIDS 11(5); **unverified, needs a human source check by the fact-checker, including the citation details**). (5) Real transmission is not certain at each contact and contacts are not known in advance (convention).
- Format: Storyboard / scrub (`company/design/formats.md`; by hand with a slider today; a kit `<Timeline>` is proposed). Spec written (interaction designer, draft, in design review): chips, scrubber and two person buttons on a 390 px phone (labels not clipping, tap targets 44 px). Illustrations: none.
- Overlap: nearest lessons are `herd-immunity`, `pockets-below-threshold` and `spreaders-are-rare` (who gets infected, random mixing or clusters, with no time order) and `waves-return` (time, but population-level waves). Differs: this is about the order of individual contacts on a fixed set of links. `node tools/overlap.mjs` scans lesson metadata only; this brief is not yet in it.
