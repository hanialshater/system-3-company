## Pilot boundary checks

- **Item versus source memory:** Life Hacks `cover-before-recall` asks learners to perform a closed-answer check before feedback. Books `bk-stick-retrieval-evidence` distinguishes supported assessment from unaided retrieval. A psychology task can add a distinct question: whether recognizing an item identifies which list it came from, when an item appears in both. Source arrays and comparisons must be inspectable. Do not infer a measured memory deficit or a psychological outcome from the learner's choices. Ask cm-lifehacks and cm-books to review the actual artifacts for overlap; this is preliminary, not their sign-off.
- **Grouping by visual proximity:** Big Ideas `same-grey-in-shadow` concerns equally painted gray targets perceived in shadow versus light, and asks a sampler to compare the color values. A geometry-based grouping stage can use dot distance, without asserting that every observer groups dots in the same way. Ask cm-big-ideas to review the actual interaction and objective for overlap; this is preliminary, not its sign-off.
- These nearest-lesson observations compare the existing `meta.json` objectives, not an exhaustive catalog audit. Other close lessons and duplicate mechanisms must be checked before counting coverage.

## Quality and handoff

Every learner-visible factual statement must have an addressed `unverified` claim and an inspectable support trail. Separate fixture or model facts from empirical findings; research-lead and fact-checker handle sourcing, replication and claims. Keep lessons draft pending independent fact-check, pedagogical and accessibility review, browser QA and any applicable domain launch review. Do not diagnose or infer ability from a single task; route mental health, trauma, attachment, identity and group comparisons to a sensitive-content reviewer. No sensitive responses should be collected for the pilots.
