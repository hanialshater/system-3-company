## 3. The blinding issue: "IN REVIEW" badge and claim counter

**What happened.** The step screenshots and `sheet.png` show the card chrome. That includes an "In review" status tag (identical for all 17) and the footer "Why trust this? (k/n claims checked)". The redacted transcripts carry neither (the trust-sheet appendix was removed). The counter differed: 15 lessons showed n/n, two-said-blue@v5 showed 8/9 and news-comes-from-acquaintances@v3 showed 4/5. judge-1 named the counts in trust reasons `round-1-42` (pair-07: "its footer reads 8/9 claims checked") and `round-1-108` (pair-18: "its footer shows 4/5 claims checked"). In both, the count is a trailing clause after a content reason (a missing toy-model disclaimer, an unhedged hook). In the three pairs that set a full-count lesson against one of these two (05, 07, 18), the full-count lesson won trust each time. The plan had kept both elements on purpose as "learner-facing". That was my call, and it was wrong for `trust`.

**Why `blind` stays true.** Rule 3 defines blind as no authorship, no recency and no rank. The counter is none of those directly. It is fact-check **process state**, which can correlate with recency (a recent revision may have unverified claims). It is not what the card states, and the trust criterion asks what the card states. So this is a contaminant of one criterion, not a broken blind.

**Decisions.**
1. **Flag, do not exclude, do not delete.** All 144 lines stay in the file and in the fit. Round-1 trust is labelled **"trust: possibly anchored on claim counts (round 1)"** here, in `company/evaluation/README.md` (Round flags) and in the WBR note. The two citing lines are not excluded. Each gives a sufficient content reason on its own. Excluding them would also hide the issue rather than show it, and the anchoring may have worked silently in lines that do not cite it.
2. **Sensitivity (reported, not adopted).** Refitting trust without `round-1-42` and `round-1-108` moves two-said-blue@v5 from 13th (878) to 10th (989) and news-comes-from-acquaintances@v3 from 17th (686) to 15th (788). The top 5 set is unchanged. Every move stays inside the intervals. So the anchoring, if it acted, changed nothing that the intervals can resolve.
3. **Fix for future packets (from round 2):** blank the status badge **and** the claim counter in every screenshot and `sheet.png`, and strip them from transcripts if they ever appear there. The badge is uniform today but will not be once some lessons are published, so it becomes a status/recency leak. A packet check fails a packet if "claims checked", "In review" or a status tag appears in its text, and a reviewer eyeballs one sheet per packet. The duration tag (for example "75s") stays: it is a property of the lesson that a learner sees and judges can weigh. Task **E1** below, owner evaluation-engineer.
4. **Re-judge for calibration (round 2):** re-run trust only on pairs 05, 07 and 18 with blanked packets and fresh judges. If the verdicts hold, the flag can be dropped for those lines in the round-2 record. The round-1 lines stay as recorded either way.
5. **Question for the evaluation steward (not an amendment yet):** should "blind" in `company/reviews/README.md` name fact-check or process-status signals explicitly, next to authorship, recency and rank? The packet fix makes this moot for pairwise rounds, but walkthrough-first reviews see the same counter.

| scope | median width | range |

| clarity | 371 | 273–632 |

| trust | 314 | 209–497 |
| overall | 376 | 274–518 (understated, see section 6) |

On this scale a width of 400 points (±200) runs from "beats the average lesson about 1 time in 4" to "about 3 times in 4". With **2–4 comparisons per lesson per criterion**, the intervals are very wide. Most of the 17 lessons overlap most others on every criterion. The ordering inside the middle 10 or so of each criterion is **noise** and should not be read, quoted or shown as a ranked list without its bars. Per the fit's own header, the bootstrap intervals are rough. Lessons with 2 comparisons (the 6 non-chord, non-repeat lessons) are the least certain. Halving these widths needs roughly 4× the comparisons per lesson, so on the order of 4 more rounds of this size. The leaderboard measures **model preference** (judges are the builders' model family), not learning.

## 6. Decision: HQ does not show "Overall"

The CPO's open question (decision record `2026-10-14-evaluation-org`, objection 4) gives this call to the head of evaluation. **Decision: HQ does not show an Overall rank, as a default or as a filter, from now until a defined aggregate is approved.** `bt.mjs` may keep computing the scope as a diagnostic in the generated JSON, but the page must not render it.

Why:
1. **False precision.** Overall pools the 6 lines a judge wrote about one pair as if they were 6 independent comparisons ("n=18" for a lesson seen 3 times). The bootstrap resamples lines, not judgements, so the overall intervals are narrower than the evidence (overall shows prices-sort-the-buyers as [398, 672] from 3 judgements).
2. **It is an unapproved criterion.** Pooling weighs the six criteria equally. That is a definition of "best", and the criteria change only with CPO approval and a decision record.
3. **It hides the disagreement the criteria exist to show** (penalty-kick-mix: first on prediction, last on learning). And this round it carries the flagged trust results into every lesson's number.
4. **It is the number most likely to become a gate** (Leonie's objection). A rank must never be a gate, and the safest way to keep it from becoming one is not to publish a single one.

What HQ shows instead: per-criterion leaderboards with intervals, defaulting to `learning` (the first criterion), and optionally a per-lesson profile of its six criterion ratings side by side. Tasks H1 (hq-engineer) and E2 (evaluation-engineer). Revisit if the CPO approves a defined aggregate in a decision record (for example "learning only", or explicit weights) **and** the fit resamples by judgement. Recorded in `company/evaluation/README.md` under "Overall".
