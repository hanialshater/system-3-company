**Same terms for a person or an agent.** The contract does not depend on who holds the role. A **brief comes in** as a dated file in Meta's inbox folder (§3), with an acceptance condition, a budget and a due date. A **record goes out** as a dated file in Meta's records, linked from the strategy page. **Someone else checks it**, as the brief names. At the end of every working session the holder writes an offboarding entry of at most 12 lines in Meta's role memory. Context reaches the holder the same way in both cases: the onboarding doc, then the strategy page, then the role memory, then the brief, and never "ask X".

What changes when a person holds the role:
- **Inbox.** The same folder, plus a notice to the person by the channel the founder sets. Founder decision D6; the default is email from the founder.
- **SLA.** Acknowledge a brief within 2 working days. Deliver by the due date, or write a dated delay note on the strategy page before the date passes.
- **When the holder is late.** The founder hears about it through the founder inbox. Nobody does the role's work as a general-purpose stand-in (org-change-process step 0). The precedent is F-26, the First Light moderator, where nobody covered the missing human role.
- **Identity.** A person commits under their own git identity. An agent commits under a role label. Records are written by the holder, never by a coordinator writing in the holder's name (the retro objection W-1203).
- **Starting work.** An agent holder needs a session started for it, in Meta's checkout. The founder starts it: one action per brief. A person starts their own work.

## 2. The strategy layer: Meta's strategy-and-state page

- **What it is.** One file, `strategy.md`, in Meta's home, at most one page (about 60 lines). It is written for a reader with no other context and read at the start of every Meta run.
- **What it contains:**
  - `as-of:` date and the evidence it rests on, with file links
  - the mission in two lines
  - the current bets and briefs, each with a due date and a budget line (spent against limit)
  - what Meta is **not** doing
  - open questions
  - decisions waiting on the founder
  - what changed since the last version
- **Who refreshes it.** The VP. It is refreshed at the end of any run that changes a bet, a brief, a budget or a founder ask, and at least every **7 days** while a brief is open.
- **Staleness limit: 14 days.** After that, a reader treats the page as stale and says so in their record. The CEO office's WBR health section flags it as a finding, from the date alone. When no brief is open, the page reads `dormant since <date>` and the limit does not apply (lazy roles, light-management decision).
- **Who reads it.** The founder, as their one regular view of Meta, with no rounds or reports. The CEO office reads it at the WBR, for the date and the budget line only. It is the template for W-1686 (the strategy layer for every head). It does not wait on W-1686.

## 3. Home, independence and lightness

**Home: its own repository** (default, D2; the founder names it, for example hanialshater/system3-meta). This follows the Press. Reasons:
1. The product is the kernel taken out of this company. A kernel that only runs inside this repository has not been shown to be a kernel, so a separate repository is the first portability test.
2. This company's protected paths, lesson gates and single-writer files should not govern Meta, and Meta should not be able to break them.
3. Isolation is designed in. The shared tree, index and host incidents of 2026-10-16 are listed in brief §3.
4. Repository access is the natural boundary for a human holder (and later, tenants).

**Meta's org.** Meta's org lives as data in its own repository, and its HQ floor comes from a generated `hq-snapshot.json`, as the Press does. The CEO office bootstraps the VP role there once (step 5), by copying `tools/org/` (the generator, roles and memory policy). That copy is itself the first data point for the kernel map. This repository gets only `company/sibling/system3-meta/`: the brief, this design, a copy of the strategy page, the kernel map's copy for readers here, and Meta's requests to this company.

**Fallback (if D2 is declined or deferred).** The VP role is defined in this company's `tools/org/roles.mjs` (team `sibling-meta`). It writes only `company/sibling/system3-meta/` and moves out when the repository exists.

**Founder span.** The founder's direct reports would go from 9 (this company's 8 plus vp-books) to **10**. The added load is capped by design:
- one page to read per week, at most
- the gate decisions in §8
- one session start per brief

Meta has no rounds, no reviews by the founder and no standing meetings. Measure: founder messages about Meta, classified with the tools/lib/founder-leverage.mjs classes (decision, correction, routing). If they exceed 2 a week outside gates for two weeks running, the CEO office reports at the WBR that the design is wrong.

## 4. Interfaces with this company and the Press

- **Meta may read** both repositories in full: code, records, events, ledger, board and role memories. These are its case studies.
- **Meta may not read** production data, learner or account data, environment files or secrets.
- **Meta may not change** anything in either company. That includes:
  - protected paths and gates
  - the board, progress.md, events.jsonl and the founder inbox (the chief of staff writes those)
  - roles, and Press files
- **Meta writes here** only in `company/sibling/system3-meta/`, through a commit that the chief of staff merges.
- **How a request travels.** Meta writes `requests/<date>-<slug>.md`, naming the role it needs and the record it wants. The chief of staff turns it into a board item. The owning role answers in its own records.

**How a learning moves between companies.** A learning is a dated record: what happened, the evidence (file links), the mechanism it suggests, and "what would make this wrong". The source company publishes it in its own records. The receiving company's owner decides by its own process: a decision record with objections, or an amendment if it touches a gate. No company edits another company's files to "apply" a learning. Kernel changes that Meta proposes for this company go to tools-lead (tools), the evaluation steward (gates, by amendment) or the CEO office (org), as proposals. Whether this company ever runs on Meta's extracted kernel is a later founder decision, outside this design.

## 6. The first brief: the kernel map

**Ask.** Map this company's operating system so that a second company can be built from it. Classify every part as one of:
- **(a) generic kernel:**
  - the company CLI and gates (tools/company.mjs, readiness in tools/lib/readiness.mjs, publish-ready)
  - the org generator (tools/org/)
  - memory (tools/memory.mjs)
  - the mailbox (tools/mailbox.mjs, tools/lib/mailbox.mjs)
  - dispatch-check and slots
  - the ledger
  - rituals (company/rituals/, tools/rituals-due.mjs)
  - events (tools/events.mjs, the event spool)
  - HQ (src/app/hq)
  - readiness records
- **(b) lesson-specific parts:** the kit, meta.json claims, lesson gates, domains and curriculum.
- **(c) coupled to Claude Code or this host,** needing an abstraction: the model binding per role, `.claude/` hooks and settings, agent files, worktrees, ports and build directories, the Agent tool held only by the chief of staff.
- **(d) needed by a second company but not existing:** human role holders, multi-model checkers, an external success measure, and anything else the VP finds.

For each row, the map records:
- the path(s)
- what the part does
- its class
- **whether it runs:** enforced, advisory or dormant, with evidence (an event, a test or a CI job). The external assessment found the gap between written rules and execution to be the main weakness, for example preflight is not a mandatory boundary, and the snapshot shows zero `hk-` hook events.
- what it assumes: lessons, Claude Code, this host
- extraction size (S, M or L)
- its dependencies on other rows

**Not in scope:**
- extracting or rewriting code
- editing this company
- proposing roles
- choosing the first use case: that is the next brief, and it carries the external measure

**Acceptance (checked by principal-engineer, on a different model from the VP where possible):**
1. Every named part in (a) to (c), and every file in `tools/` and `tools/lib/`, appears in exactly one row, with a path.
2. The checker picks 10 rows at random and verifies each against the code and the event log. The map passes if at least 9 are right. A failed row is a finding the VP fixes, or a disagreement the VP records as an objection.
3. Every (d) item cites the gap's source: brief §2 or §3, or the assessment.
4. The checker writes its findings before reading the VP's notes.

**Budget:** 600k tokens, tagged `[project:meta-kernel-map]`, with a hard stop. Unspent tokens return.
**Due:** 14 days after approval, and no later than **2026-11-13**, before First Light's go/no-go week (2026-11-23).
**Output:** `kernel-map.md` in Meta's home, a copy in `company/sibling/system3-meta/`, a strategy-page update and a memory entry.
