# Style sheet: Solar System Quiz Pack (ages 8-10)

> **Heading rule: the first line of your file is `# Round n: <topic>`, with ONE `#`.**
> Copy the topic exactly as it is written in the Rounds list below. Example: `# Round 2: The rocky planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars)`.

Each writer writes one round of exactly 4 questions. Keep sentences short and words simple.
Every fact must be true and checkable in a children's encyclopedia or on NASA's kids pages.

## Format

Write each question as three lines, then a blank line. The example below shows the layout only.
It is not about space: do not reuse it or its answer.

```
**Q1.** What colour do you get when you mix blue paint and yellow paint?
**Answer:** Green
**Why:** Blue and yellow paint each soak up some colours of light, and the colour left over for our eyes is green.
```

- `**Qn.**` where n is 1 to 4 within your round. One question, ending in `?`, at most 20 words.
- `**Answer:**` a single word, name or number. No multiple-choice lists.
- `**Why:**` exactly one sentence, at most 25 words, that explains the answer.
- Start your file with `# Round n: <topic>` (one `#`, see the heading rule at the top).
- Friendly, curious tone. Keep every word kind and gentle: no insults, no violent or gory words, nothing scary.
- Banned words. None of these may appear anywhere in your round, in any form (for example plurals or "-ed"/"-ing" endings): `stupid`, `dumb`, `kill`, `dead`, `blood`, `hate`. This list is the minimum; other unkind or scary words are also out.
- No repeated answers. No answer may appear twice in the pack. Before you submit, read the Answer lines of the other four rounds and change your question if any answer matches yours.

## Rounds

- Round 1: The Sun
- Round 2: The rocky planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars)
- Round 3: The giant planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune)
- Round 4: Earth's Moon
- Round 5: Space exploration (rockets, astronauts, rovers)

Stay inside your topic. Who owns what:

- **Round 1** owns the Sun, and also day and night, sunrise and sunset, and the seasons (how the Sun
  looks from Earth and why it seems to move). A Why may mention Earth spinning or tilting to explain these.
- **Round 2** owns Earth as a planet (its oceans, air, rocks, mountains, size and place among the planets),
  and the places Mercury, Venus and Mars themselves (their surfaces, weather and features).
- **Round 3** owns Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune themselves.
- **Round 4** owns the Moon as a place: its phases, craters, seas, surface and its trip around Earth.
- **Round 5** owns every spacecraft, rocket, rover, astronaut and mission, wherever it went. That includes
  the Apollo Moon landings and the people who walked on the Moon, and all rovers and landers on Mars.

Rule of thumb: a question about a place belongs to the round that owns that place; a question about the
people or machines that went there belongs to Round 5. Rounds 2 and 3 do not ask about any planet outside
their own list. Dwarf planets, comets and asteroids are out of scope for every round.

## Difficulty

Each round climbs in difficulty: Q1 and Q2 easy (most 8-year-olds could answer), Q3 medium
(a 9-year-old who has read about space), Q4 harder (a keen 10-year-old). No question may need
arithmetic, unit conversion or a number longer than two digits, unless that number is the answer
itself and the Why explains it.
