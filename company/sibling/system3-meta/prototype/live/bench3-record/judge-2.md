# Judge 2: Solar System Quiz Packs (A vs B)

| Criterion | Pick | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Accuracy | Tie | I found no factual errors in either pack, and the simplifications are fair in both (A: Olympus Mons "more than twice Everest", Pluto "shares its path"; B: Olympus Mons "about as wide as Arizona", the Sun holds "almost all" the solar system's stuff). |
| 2. Fun / wow | B | B has more vivid, surprising images (hot enough to melt lead, methane lakes on Titan, spacecraft flying through the asteroid belt without hitting anything, the comet tail pointing away from the Sun), while A spends a slot on recalling a date (1969). |
| 3. Difficulty | B | A breaks the climb in Round 2 (Earth comes after Mercury but is easier) and Round 4 (opens with a hard date), and A R1 Q3 nearly answers itself ("dark spots on the Sun" leads to "sunspots"); B's only weak spots are a near-freebie in R4 Q1 (whose wording "change shape" hints at Q2 "phases"), and each B round ends on a real reasoning question (the comet tail). |
| 4. Why lines | B | Almost every B line adds a new fact (88-day year, rust, 165-year orbit, solar wind, light reflected off gray rock), whereas several A lines just restate the answer (8 minutes, Mercury's "smallest path", "most asteroids travel in this band", comets as ice and dust). |
| 5. Coverage | B | B covers all four giant planets including Neptune, moons of two planets (Ganymede, Titan) and meteors; A has no Neptune and is heavy on Jupiter (Jupiter, the Great Red Spot, Ganymede, Io, Europa), plus two comet questions. |
| 6. Publish | B | B is the one I would publish as is; A would need its question order and the sunspots giveaway fixed first, though it is still acceptable. |

**Publish at all?**
- A: Yes (accurate and child-friendly; small ordering and giveaway flaws)
- B: Yes

**Overall pick: B**. The two packs are equally accurate, and B wins on wow, why lines, coverage and the difficulty climb.
