- Round 1, Q4 (Why): "the Sun squeezes hydrogen together to make helium, which gives off light and heat." Read literally, this says helium gives off the light and heat. What is true: the fusion of hydrogen into helium releases the energy; the helium does not glow on its own. Suggested wording: "...to make helium, and this gives off light and heat."
- Round 2, Q3 (Why): "Venus has thick clouds and heavy air that trap the heat like a blanket." What is true: the thick carbon dioxide atmosphere causes the runaway greenhouse effect. Venus's sulfuric-acid clouds actually reflect most sunlight away. "Heavy air" is the right reason; the clouds are a minor part of it.
- Round 2, Q4 (Why): "Mount Everest, the tallest mountain on Earth." What is true: Everest is the highest above sea level. Mauna Kea is taller measured from its base on the sea floor (about 10 km). "Highest mountain" avoids the problem. "More than twice as tall" is correct either way: Olympus Mons is about 22 to 26 km, Everest 8.8 km.
- Round 4, Q4 (question): "covered in a thick shell of ice with a big salty ocean under it." This states the ocean as fact. What is true: there is strong evidence for it (Galileo magnetometer data, surface salts), but it has not been directly confirmed. The Why line rightly says "scientists think"; the question could say "scientists think has a big salty ocean."
- Round 5, Q2 (Why): "it shares its path with many other icy worlds." What is true: the 2006 rule is that a planet must have "cleared its neighbourhood" of other objects. Pluto has not, because it orbits within the crowded Kuiper Belt. This is acceptable for ages 8-10, but a pedant would say most Kuiper Belt objects do not share Pluto's exact orbit. "Lives in a crowded zone with many other icy worlds" is closer.
- Round 5, Q4 (answer key): "Ice and dust." Many children's books teach "ice and rock" (the "dirty snowball"), and the pack's own Why line mentions "bits of rock." An answer key that accepts only "ice and dust" would mark a reasonable answer wrong; it should also accept "ice and rock" or "ice and dirt." Also, some recent comet missions (for example Rosetta) found comets may contain more dust than ice, so they are sometimes called "icy dirtballs." "Mostly ice" is still the standard simplification.
