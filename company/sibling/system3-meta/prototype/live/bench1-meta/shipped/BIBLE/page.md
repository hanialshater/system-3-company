# Pip and the Night: Story Bible

A picture book for ages 4-6. Five pages, one beat each.

## Characters

- **Pip** (the hero): a little fox, she/her, who is afraid of the dark at bedtime; brave in small steps, never far from home.
- **Old Hoo the owl**: slow, low and kind; she shows rather than tells. Her sound: "Hoo is there? Only me."
- **Glim the firefly** (with her fireflies): tiny, giggly and rhyming; they light a little path. Their sound: "blink, wink, follow the light."
- **The cricket choir**: a sing-song chorus hidden in the grass. Their sound: "chirr-chirr."
- **Bristle the hedgehog**: shy and snuffly, a little afraid of Pip too, so she mirrors Pip's own feelings.

## Setting

Pip's den at the edge of a quiet wood, at bedtime. The den door opens onto a small clearing with a big oak by the den door. Pip never goes past the big oak and is always in sight of home. As the light goes, the stars slowly come out; there is no moon character. No villain, no real danger, and every worry is soothed on the page where it appears.

Tone: hushed, warm, sleepy read-aloud; short lines, simple words, soft sound words children can join in on. Friends show, they do not lecture, and nobody tells Pip her fear is silly.

Refrain on pages 1-4: "What is that in the night?" Page 5 closes on "Goodnight, night."

## Page plan

- Page 1: Bedtime in the den; the shadows look huge, and Pip pulls her tail over her eyes because the dark is too big. Refrain: "What is that in the night?"
- Page 2: A soft hoo-hoo; Pip peeks out the den door and meets Old Hoo the owl ("Hoo is there? Only me."), who says wait and look, and slowly the stars appear. Refrain.
- Page 3: Glim and the fireflies blink a little path just outside the den ("blink, wink, follow the light"); Pip takes three brave steps, no further than the big oak by the den door, always in sight of home. Refrain.
- Page 4: The night sounds get names: the rustle is Bristle the hedgehog, shy and a little afraid of Pip too, and the chirring is the cricket choir ("chirr-chirr"). Refrain, now asked with a smile.
- Page 5: Back in bed with the den door open a crack, Pip says goodnight to each friend and falls asleep, not afraid; last line: "Goodnight, night."
