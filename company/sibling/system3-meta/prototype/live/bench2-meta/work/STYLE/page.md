# Solar System Quiz Pack: style sheet

For ages 8-10. Five rounds of 4 questions each, 20 questions in all. Source of every rule here: kickoff meeting MTG-1 (items 1 and 2, both decided with no objections).

## Format

Each question is exactly three lines, with one blank line between questions:

```
**Q1.** Which planet is the hottest?
**Answer:** Venus
**Why:** Its thick clouds trap heat like a blanket, so it is even hotter than Mercury.
```

- `**Qn.**` is one short, open question ending in "?". Number the questions Q1 to Q4 in every round. No multiple choice, no trick questions.
- `**Answer:**` is 1-4 words: a name or a short phrase. One clear answer only.
- `**Why:**` is one sentence of at most 20 words (see the voice rules below).
- Each round, title included, is 100-300 words and reads at reading ease 60 or higher: short sentences and everyday words.

## Rounds

Topics are split by kind of object, so every answer has exactly one home round.

- Round 1: The Sun and the solar system as a whole. Owns: the Sun is a star; it is the biggest thing in the solar system; it is mostly hydrogen and helium; its light takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth; there are eight planets. No planet, moon or dwarf-planet name may be an answer here.
- Round 2: Rocky planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, as planets). Owns: Mercury is closest to the Sun; Venus is the hottest planet because thick clouds trap heat; Mars is red from rusty dust; Olympus Mons on Mars. Their moons belong to Round 4.
- Round 3: Giant planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, as planets, with rings and storms). Owns: Jupiter is the biggest planet; the Great Red Spot; Saturn's bright rings; Uranus spins on its side; Neptune is the farthest planet. Their moons belong to Round 4.
- Round 4: Moons (every moon in the solar system). Owns: our Moon and its phases; the first Moon landing (1969); Ganymede is the biggest moon; Titan's thick air; Io's volcanoes; Europa's ice; Phobos.
- Round 5: Small worlds (dwarf planets, asteroids, comets, meteors). Owns: Pluto is a dwarf planet (since 2006); Ceres; the asteroid belt lies between Mars and Jupiter; comet tails point away from the Sun; shooting stars are meteors.

There is no spacecraft or space-exploration round. The Moon landing lives in Round 4.

### Ownership rules

1. An answer belongs to the round that owns that object. Any round may mention an object in a question, but only its owning round may use it as an answer.
2. Planet names are answers only in Round 2 or Round 3. Moon names are answers only in Round 4. Pluto, Ceres and the asteroid belt are answers only in Round 5. "The Sun" is an answer only in Round 1.
3. No Answer string appears twice anywhere in the pack. PACK lists all 20 answers and checks each one against the others, against its owning round, and against the facts.

### Reserved answers per round

Only the round named may use these as an Answer. Writers pick their 4 answers from their own list (or from other facts their round owns); nothing from another round's list.

| Round | Reserved answers |
|---|---|
| Round 1 | the Sun; a star; hydrogen; helium; about 8 minutes; eight (planets) |
| Round 2 | Mercury; Venus; Earth; Mars; Olympus Mons; rusty dust |
| Round 3 | Jupiter; Saturn; Uranus; Neptune; Great Red Spot; rings |
| Round 4 | the Moon; phases; 1969; Ganymede; Titan; Io; Europa; Phobos |
| Round 5 | Pluto; Ceres; dwarf planet; asteroid belt; comet; meteor (shooting star) |

Watch-out: a Round 5 answer such as "away from the Sun" is a different string from "the Sun", but word it so the answer is the direction, not the Sun itself.

### Superlative owners

Each "most" or "biggest" fact has one owner. Do not ask it in any other round.

| Superlative | Owner |
|---|---|
| Biggest thing in the solar system (the Sun) | Round 1 |
| Hottest planet (Venus) | Round 2 |
| Closest planet to the Sun (Mercury) | Round 2 |
| Biggest planet (Jupiter) | Round 3 |
| Farthest planet (Neptune) | Round 3 |
| Biggest moon (Ganymede) | Round 4 |
| Biggest dwarf planet (Pluto) | Round 5 |

### Fact traps (never write these)

- Mercury as the hottest planet. It is closest, but Venus is hottest.
- Moon counts for any planet. The numbers keep changing.
- Pluto as a planet. Call it a dwarf planet everywhere, in every round.
- "The far side of the Moon" is fine; never call it the "dark side", because it gets sunlight too.

## Difficulty

Every round climbs the same ladder:

| Question | Level | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Easy | Something most 8-year-olds have heard. |
| Q2 | Easy | Still familiar, one small step further. |
| Q3 | Medium | Needs a reason or a less famous name. |
| Q4 | Stretch | A wow fact a curious 10-year-old enjoys learning. |

- Every question is answerable from a good kids' book.
- Numbers come in two kinds, with two rules:
  - Measurements (distances, times, sizes, temperatures) are never precise. Round them and say "about" (about 8 minutes; about 1,300 Earths fit inside Jupiter). No question needs a precise measurement.
  - Dates and counts are exact and exempt from the "about" rule, because they have one true value: the first Moon landing was in 1969; Pluto has been a dwarf planet since 2006; there are eight planets. Write them exactly, never with "about". Only textbook-stable counts qualify (never moon counts; see Fact traps).
- Only textbook-stable facts. No hedged or disputed facts, and no numbers that keep changing.
- The writer notes a source for every number in the order notes.
- Never mock a wrong guess.

## Why-line voice

- One sentence, at most 20 words.
- A friendly, curious guide. It may speak to "you".
- Give the reason; do not just repeat the answer.
- Compare to everyday things: "like a blanket", "like rust on an old bike".
- Plain words a 9-year-old can read alone. If a hard word is needed, explain it the first time it appears.
- No jokes that could confuse.

## Banned words

These words must not appear anywhere in a round, not even inside a longer phrase. They are spelled out letter by letter here so this sheet itself stays clean:

- s-t-u-p-i-d
- d-u-m-b
- k-i-l-l (also "k-i-l-l-s")
- d-e-a-d (also "d-e-a-d star")
- b-l-o-o-d (also "b-l-o-o-d red")
- h-a-t-e

Say instead: "rusty red", "too hot for people", "no air", "no life we know of", "cold".

To be safe, also avoid longer words that contain these letters (for example s-k-i-l-l, d-e-a-d-l-i-n-e, w-h-a-t-e-v-e-r), since a reviewer or a stricter check may flag them.
