## 2. Difficulty, format and the voice of the 'why' line for ages 8-10
Round 1:
- writer-2: Format: open answers, no multiple choice. Choice lists would name other rounds answers and trip the cross-round give-away check, and they add words. Each question is three lines: "**Qn.** question?" then "**Answer:** short canonical name (1-4 words, e.g. Venus, not "It is Venus")" then "**Why:** one sentence". Canonical short answers make the repeat check meaningful. Difficulty for 8-10: per round Q1-Q2 easy (things a child may have heard: the Sun is a star, Mars is red), Q3 medium, Q4 a stretch with a vivid reason (Venus is hotter than Mercury because thick clouds trap heat). Reading ease >= 60: questions <= 14 words, Why <= 20 words, one clause where possible, everyday words; proper nouns like Ganymede are fine but explain nothing with jargon (say "air" not "atmosphere" where it fits). Target about 140-200 words per round, inside the 100-300 limit. Voice of Why: warm and concrete, a picture or comparison a child can hold ("Jupiter is so big that more than 1,000 Earths could fit inside"), never talking down, no "obviously" or "easy". Avoid banned words in natural space phrasing: no "dead star", "dead planet", "kill", or "blood-red" (say "rusty red"). Every Why must be true on its own, not just plausible; writers cite a source (NASA Solar System pages) in builder notes for each Answer.
- writer-1: Format exactly as the orders say: "# Round n: <topic>", then 4 blocks of **Qn.** question / **Answer:** short answer (one to three words, a name or number) / **Why:** one sentence. 100-300 words per round, Flesch reading ease 60+, which means short sentences and everyday words: aim for 10-14 words per sentence, explain any space word in plain terms ("a dwarf planet is a small round world"). Difficulty: Q1-Q2 easy (most 8-year-olds might guess), Q3 medium, Q4 a stretch with a fun surprise; mostly direct questions, at most one multiple choice (A/B/C) per round to vary pace. Numbers rounded and kid-sized ("about 8 minutes", "more than 1,000 Earths"), never long decimals; note "about" so the fact stays true. Voice of the Why line: warm, curious, second person where it helps ("If you could stand on Venus, you would..."), one surprising fact that teaches, never scolds a wrong guess, no scary framing; banned words stupid, dumb, kill, dead, blood, hate, and I suggest also avoiding "die"/"burn up" style phrasing. The Why must not name another round owned answer. Each round self-checks with "node meta.mjs check --order=<O>" before handing in.
