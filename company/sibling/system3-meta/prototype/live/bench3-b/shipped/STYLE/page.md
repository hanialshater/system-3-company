# Style sheet: Solar System Quiz Pack (ages 8-10)

Five rounds, four questions each, twenty questions in all. Every fact must be true and checkable in a standard reference (NASA Solar System Exploration pages are the default source).

## Format

Each question is three lines, numbered within its round (Q1 to Q4):

```
**Q1.** Which planet is closest to the Sun?
**Answer:** Mercury
**Why:** Mercury orbits nearer to the Sun than any other planet.
```

- `**Qn.**`: one question, ending in a question mark, at most 20 words.
- `**Answer:**`: a short answer, one to three words (a name or a number with its unit).
- `**Why:**`: exactly one sentence that explains the answer in words an 8-year-old knows.
- Plain, friendly language. No trick questions, no "all of the above".
- Numbers are rounded the way a children's book would give them (for example "about 8 minutes").

## Rounds

- Round 1: The Sun
- Round 2: The rocky planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars)
- Round 3: The giant planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune)
- Round 4: Moons
- Round 5: Dwarf planets, asteroids and comets

### Facts each round owns

Only the owning round may use these as answers. Pick four from your own list (or add one that fits your topic and no other round's list).

- **Round 1, The Sun:** the Sun is a star; sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth; the Sun is made mostly of hydrogen and helium; the Sun holds about 99.8% of the solar system's mass; sunspots are cooler, darker patches on the Sun's surface.
- **Round 2, Rocky planets:** Mercury is closest to the Sun; Venus is the hottest planet (its thick clouds trap heat); Mars looks red because of rusty iron in its dust; Olympus Mons on Mars is the tallest volcano in the solar system; Earth is the only planet known to have life.
- **Round 3, Giant planets:** Jupiter is the largest planet; the Great Red Spot is a giant storm on Jupiter; Saturn has the brightest, best-known rings; Uranus spins tipped over on its side; Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun.
- **Round 4, Moons:** Ganymede (Jupiter) is the largest moon in the solar system; Titan (Saturn) has a thick atmosphere; Io (Jupiter) has the most volcanoes of any world; Europa (Jupiter) has an ocean under its icy shell; Earth's Moon was visited by astronauts, first in 1969.
- **Round 5, Dwarf planets, asteroids and comets:** Pluto was renamed a dwarf planet in 2006; Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt; the asteroid belt lies between Mars and Jupiter; a comet's tail points away from the Sun; Halley's Comet returns about every 76 years.

### Rules that keep rounds apart

1. **No answer in two rounds.** An answer belongs to the round that owns it above. If you need a new answer, check every other round's page in `work/` first.
2. **No giveaways.** A question may name a body that another round uses (Round 4 may say "Jupiter's moon Io"), but it must not state or hint at another round's owned fact, and its answer must not be another round's answer (so Round 4 may not ask "Ganymede goes around which planet?", because Jupiter is a Round 3 answer). Do not write "Jupiter, the biggest planet" or "Mercury, the closest planet" anywhere in a question or a Why line.
3. If two rounds clash, the round that owns the fact keeps it; the other rewrites. PACK fixes any clash left at assembly.

### Difficulty rule

In every round: Q1 and Q2 are easy (most 8-year-olds could answer), Q3 is medium, Q4 is the hardest (a 10-year-old who likes space could answer). No question needs a calculation or a fact outside the round's topic.
