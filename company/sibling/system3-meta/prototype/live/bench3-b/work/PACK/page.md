# Solar System Quiz Pack

# Round 1: The Sun

**Q1.** The Sun is not a planet. What kind of space object is it?

**Answer:** A star

**Why:** The Sun is a huge ball of hot, glowing gas, just like the stars we see at night, only much closer to us.

**Q2.** About how long does light from the Sun take to reach us here on our planet?

**Answer:** About 8 minutes

**Why:** Sunlight is very fast, but the Sun is so far away that its light needs about 8 minutes to get here.

**Q3.** What are the dark spots that we can sometimes see on the face of the Sun called?

**Answer:** Sunspots

**Why:** Sunspots look dark because they are a bit cooler than the bright parts of the Sun around them.

**Q4.** The Sun is made mostly of two gases. One is helium. What is the other one?

**Answer:** Hydrogen

**Why:** Most of the Sun is hydrogen, and deep in its middle the Sun squeezes hydrogen together to make helium, which gives off light and heat.

# Round 2: The Rocky Planets

**Q1.** Which planet is closest to the Sun?

**Answer:** Mercury

**Why:** Mercury goes around the Sun on the smallest path of all the planets, so no planet is nearer to the Sun.

**Q2.** Which planet is the only one we know of that has living things on it?

**Answer:** Earth

**Why:** Earth has air to breathe, liquid water and just the right warmth, and so far no other planet is known to have life.

**Q3.** Which planet is the hottest of all, even though it is not the closest one to the Sun?

**Answer:** Venus

**Why:** Venus has thick clouds and heavy air that trap the heat like a blanket, so it stays hotter than any other planet.

**Q4.** What is the name of the giant volcano on Mars that is the tallest in the whole solar system?

**Answer:** Olympus Mons

**Why:** Olympus Mons is more than twice as tall as Mount Everest, the tallest mountain on Earth.

# Round 3: The Giant Planets

**Q1.** Which planet is the biggest in our solar system?

**Answer:** Jupiter

**Why:** Jupiter is so big that more than a thousand Earths could fit inside it.

**Q2.** Which planet is famous for its wide, bright rings?

**Answer:** Saturn

**Why:** Other giant planets have thin, faint rings too, but the rings of Saturn are made of bits of ice that shine brightly in sunlight.

**Q3.** What is the name of the huge storm that has swirled on Jupiter for well over a hundred years?

**Answer:** The Great Red Spot

**Why:** This red storm is wider than our whole planet, and people have watched it through telescopes for more than 150 years.

**Q4.** Which planet spins tipped over on its side, so it seems to roll as it goes around?

**Answer:** Uranus

**Why:** Long ago something huge may have bumped Uranus over, and now it spins on its side as it moves on its long path.

# Round 4: Moons

**Q1.** In what year did astronauts first walk on our Moon?

**Answer:** 1969

**Why:** Two astronauts from the Apollo 11 trip stepped onto the dusty ground of the Moon in July 1969.

**Q2.** What is the name of the biggest moon in the whole solar system?

**Answer:** Ganymede

**Why:** Ganymede goes around Jupiter, and it is about one and a half times as wide as our own Moon.

**Q3.** Which moon of Jupiter has more volcanoes that erupt than any other world we know?

**Answer:** Io

**Why:** Io gets squeezed and stretched as it moves on its path, and this makes it hot inside, so lava shoots out of hundreds of volcanoes.

**Q4.** Which moon of Jupiter is covered in a thick shell of ice with a big salty ocean under it?

**Answer:** Europa

**Why:** Scientists think the ocean under the ice on Europa may hold more water than all the seas on Earth.

# Round 5: Dwarf Planets, Asteroids and Comets

**Q1.** Many space rocks circle the Sun in a wide band between Mars and Jupiter. What is this band called?

**Answer:** The asteroid belt

**Why:** Most of the asteroids we know about travel around the Sun in this band, like cars on a huge round track.

**Q2.** In 2006, scientists decided that one small, icy world was not a full planet after all. Which world was it?

**Answer:** Pluto

**Why:** Pluto is round, but it shares its path with many other icy worlds, so it is now called a dwarf planet.

**Q3.** Which famous comet returns to our part of the sky about every 76 years?

**Answer:** Halley's Comet

**Why:** People on Earth last saw it in 1986, and it should be back again in 2061.

**Q4.** What two things are comets mostly made of?

**Answer:** Ice and dust

**Why:** A comet is a frozen lump of ice mixed with dust and bits of rock, a bit like a snowball rolled in dirt.
