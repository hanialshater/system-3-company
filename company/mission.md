# Mission

**Leave people better able to ask the next question than we found them.**

We make interactive lessons of about sixty seconds each, delivered as a vertical feed that people
already know how to use. A swipe feed is a delivery format; we are not in the attention business.
Every card asks for a prediction before it shows anything, lets the learner act on a small world
that answers back, and then explains why. Days later the idea comes back as a single question.

## For whom

Curious adults and older students who have a minute and a phone. The default learner knows
arithmetic and everyday life and has forgotten most of school. Each lesson states its own
knowledge boundary, because "beginner" hides almost everything interesting.

## What we make

- One idea per lesson, chosen because it changes how the learner sees something.
- Lessons across eight domains, each run by a category manager (see `domains/`).
- A public map of what we teach and what we do not teach yet.

## What we refuse to make

- **Engagement for its own sake.** Swipes, dwell and streaks are not our objective. If our
  ecosystem rewards clicks, clickable organisms evolve. See `product/surface-value.md`.
- **Answers without understanding.** We scaffold, we don't substitute: the learner commits
  before we explain.
- **Unchecked claims.** Every factual sentence a learner sees has an address, a source and an
  independent check before it is published.
- **Pretend personalisation.** With no history, we say so ("Fresh picks", not "For you").

## How we know we are working

Not from simulated learners alone. Simulated learners find problems; only people can show that a
lesson teaches. The founder talks to real learners; the analytics steward reports retained recall
on spaced revisits, not views. See the open test in `experiments/EXP-002`.
