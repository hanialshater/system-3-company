## Ruling W-1328 · cut-the-busiest-link v2 departure: accept the narrower lesson (no v3)
Decision: **(i) accept.** The v2 reveal ("The Girvan–Newman method cuts the busiest link, recounts every link, and repeats.") stands. I am not asking for a third-cut step.

Why:
1. The predict, the payoff and the ending all rest on the misconception's first half, "removing one link leaves the others' loads where they were". The stage refutes it directly (3–7: 12 → 24, with inner links changing too). r14 says this part works (finding 2).
2. The second half, "a list ranked once can be cut down in order", cannot be refuted before the split in any network that keeps both the predict answer (24) and the two-group ending. The builder's argument is sound: if the two bridges rank top, both orders cut them first. If an inner link ranks above them, Girvan–Newman's first cut falls inside a group and the two groups do not fall out. I accept this as a derivation, not a fact-check.
3. A third cut after the split shows a small difference (recount 0-1 at 3.5 against stale 0-3 at 2.5) that teaches nothing about communities. It splits the group of 6 further, after the payoff. That would muddy the "two groups, 6 and 4" ending and add a step to a 60-second card.

What the lesson now teaches and does not teach (the map's honesty):
- **Teaches:** cutting one link changes the loads on the others, so loads must be recounted after a cut, and repeated "cut the busiest, recount" separates the two groups here.
- **Does not teach:** that following a once-ranked list gives a different cutting order. In this lesson that is stated as a method (c4), not shown. This is a known gap of `cs:edge-betweenness-communities`. I am not opening a new concept for it. It would need its own network, where the orders differ before any split, and I have no demand signal for it. If a learner request or a revisit result asks for it, a separate brief is the route, not a v3 of this slug.

Follow-up the builder left open ("why inner links change"): **no change now.** v2's tie-rule message already explains the fractions, which was r14's actual break (finding 1a). The reason inner links change is that the cross pairs that used the cut bridge now walk across their own group to reach the remaining bridge (for example 0-3: 5 → 12.5).
