## 1. Ruling: does "some of them" imply "not all", and is that a claim?

**Yes. In these lines it is a claim, and I check it.**
- "Some of them …" is the partitive, and here it follows a first sentence ("moved ahead of some lessons") that has just introduced the set it divides. A reader takes the partitive as a contrast within that set: some do, the rest do not. Head-of-product's own text calls this the likely reading ("can suggest 'not all of them'"). The line is the only explanation a 13-year-old gets for a card's place, so a reading most readers take is part of what the card says (the failure mode in my onboarding doc: the stage makes a different claim from the one written down).
- The weak indefinite in RC's form, "moved ahead of some lessons **that** build on …", does not contrast within the passed set. It asserts existence, which is the "some lessons" reading RC and fc2 used. That is why RC's L1, L6 and L7 are not affected by this ruling.
- Measured consequence (§3): the "not all" reading is **false on every** cold-start L1 card and on every L6 card I generated, and on about a quarter of L7 cards. So the rewrites of L1, L6 and L7 are disputed. Their literal "at least one" content is true.
