## Sensitivity notes (no run; from the text and arithmetic only)

- **P3 and P1 carry the candidate's value.** Step 4's prerequisite factor (1 − 0.15 × (1 − ∏ P(prerequisite known))) moves P(encode) by at most 15%; that bound, times κ(14) 0.484 and 0.66, is the most one prerequisite can add to a lesson's value (≈ 0.048 per lesson). In a domain with few prerequisite edges (batch 5 O4) the term is mostly idle and the order falls back to step 1's domain estimate and the refresh term.
- **P13 makes refreshing a prior-known concept nearly worthless** at base: it loses only 2% over 14 days, against a new concept's 0.66 × 0.484 = 0.32. So q × refresh is small and the value of a lesson is close to (1 − q) × 0.32 × the prerequisite factor: the candidate prefers lessons in domains its step 1 estimate says the learner does not already know. At the outer m = 1 (not on this sheet) the refresh value would be large; the candidate does not know that cell.
- **P14 sets how fast step 1 learns a domain.** At ratios 4.5 / 0.125, one wrong prediction pulls a domain's π from 0.206 to 0.186 and one right to 0.245 (P12 arithmetic). A steeper or rating-dependent world law would make the world's predictions more informative than the candidate assumes.
