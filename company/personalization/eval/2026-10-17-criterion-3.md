### 1.1 Placements (harness3.mjs `runEpisode3`; world3.mjs `PLACEMENTS`, `FEED`)

A session has `slots` card positions (E3 row; criterion 2: 12). A new card takes one. A revisit takes `revisitSlotCost` (E3 row; criterion 2: 1). A card may start while fewer than `slots` positions are used. The learner may stop after any card. The stop hazard after a revisit is × `revisitStopScale` (E3 row; criterion 2 L5).

| Placement | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `first` | The review rule's revisits, in its order, before any new card (AMD-008 F2 as drafted, status proposed). This is criterion 2's placement. | criterion 2 §1.1 |
| **`interleave`** | **Before new card i of the session (i = 0, 1, …), one revisit from the queue when `i % 3 === 1`.** The queue is the review rule's list, at most 12 (Feed.tsx `MAX_REVISITS`). A queued revisit not served in the session is not served then. It stays due and comes back in the next session's queue if the rule returns it. If the catalogue ends (no new lesson left), the rest of the queue is served while positions remain (Feed.tsx appends `due` when `list.next === null`). | src/components/Feed.tsx `items` |

**The fixed schedule in the interleave.** Feed.tsx builds its queue as `Object.keys(progress).filter(revisitDue)`, which is first-completion order (progress key order), and not criterion 2's oldest-last-result order. So in interleave cells, `fixed` serves the same due set as criterion 2's §I.5 in Feed.tsx's order (harness3.mjs `feedDue`, tested). Every other review rule's queue is its own decision, in its order.

At 12 positions and a cost of 1, the interleave serves at most 3 revisits a session (before new cards 1, 4 and 7), against up to 12 in `first`.

**Departures from Feed.tsx that I know of** (for F3; none is modelled):
- D1. As I read it, Feed.tsx's `list.items` is the server's catalogue order, not filtered by local progress, so a returning learner may scroll past lessons they have completed. The simulation counts i over new cards only: default-order and every arm skip completed lessons, as in criteria 2 and phase A.
- D2. Feed.tsx freezes `revisitFrom` at `active + 2` when the metas arrive. The simulation assumes they arrive before the learner moves (active = 0), so the first revisit can come before new card 1.
- D3. The subject filter (chips) and drafts or retired lessons dropping out of the queue are not modelled.
- D4. Feed.tsx has no session length. `slots` is the world's, from E3 (item 1).

### 2.0 The base cell, named before any score: `interleave:base`
Pre-commitment 3 reads the reachable-signal oracle in "criterion 3's base cell", and batch 4 leaves the choice to me. **The base cell is `interleave:base`: the Feed.tsx placement, with every E1 and E3 row at its base.** Reasons, written before any arm but the reference was run in either placement:

For a first visit that is the shipped feed. 2. Rule 3 asks whether personalised ordering is reachable *for the product*. An ordering rule would run in the feed that exists.
3. The placement changes how many new picks an ordering gets, so it changes the size of any ordering gain. I do not know which way it changes the reachable oracle's Δ against default-order, and I have run neither.

### 2.1 O1: the split of oracle-order's gain (hidden-state references, review fixed)

All three read the hidden state, as criterion 2 §2.3's `oracle-order` does. They are greedy (one step), are judged on the same seeds, and differ only in what they may choose. A pick is a **refresh** if the lesson's concept is known (hidden) when it is picked, whether prior-known or learned or refreshed earlier in the episode, and **new** otherwise.

| Arm | May choose | Is |
|---|---|---|
| `oracle-order` | any eligible lesson (criterion 2 §2.3, unchanged, by import) | the full oracle |
| `oracle-new` | when the default order's next eligible lesson is a refresh, that lesson (as default-order would serve it); otherwise the unknown-concept lesson with the largest expected rise in K(T₇) | **new picks only** |
| `oracle-refresh` | the default order's next unknown-concept lesson, or any eligible known-concept lesson, whichever has the largest expected rise in K(T₇) (so it can skip, take or reorder refreshes, but never jumps a new pick) | **refresh choice only** |

**Reported per persona, per cell, with 95% t intervals over paired seeds:** Δ_full, Δ_new and Δ_refresh against `default-order`, and the **interaction** Δ_full − Δ_new − Δ_refresh (per seed, then the interval). The split is measured, not counted. It is not additive by construction, so the interaction is part of the answer. Counted beside it (descriptive): refresh and new picks per episode, with refreshes split into prior-known and "learned before this pick (in the episode or the pre-history)" by whether a completed lesson shares the concept (F3 N12: that is the accurate label of the counter `refreshEpisode`). **K4 (F3 N11):** refreshes **skipped** are counted too (`extra.refreshSkipped`: known-concept lessons passed over when they were the default order's next eligible lesson and never served later in the episode; the reference arm skips none). The readout prints taken vs skipped per N7 cell (base, `priorRateMult-low`, `-high`, `-outer`) and says in words whether Δ_refresh is the value of taking refreshes or of skipping them.

**Limit, stated now.** Lessons map almost one-to-one to concepts (230 lessons, 223 concepts; 7 concepts have two lessons), and completed lessons are not eligible. So "episode-learned" refreshes by ordering exist only through those 7 pairs. The measured split is therefore refresh vs new, and its refresh side is nearly all prior-known. Splitting the refresh side by measurement into prior-known and episode-learned would need a fourth arm with almost nothing to choose. I report the counts instead and do not build that arm.

Readings: wins only, base cell, as criterion 2 §2.3 (HEADROOM if wins in ≥ 4 of 6). They are reported, and nothing reads them by rule.

### 2.2 The reachable-signal oracle (`reachable`, harness/c3/reachable-oracle.mjs; review fixed)

**What it sees, at each new-card pick:** exactly criterion 2 §I.3's object, without `fixedDue` (a review field it does not need), built fresh and deep-frozen by the harness (harness3.mjs `reachableInput`):

| Field | Content | Status (privacy ruling company/reviews/_privacy/2026-10-16-personalization-model-inputs.md §2) |
|---|---|---|
| `now` | the pick's time; used only as `now − completedAt` and `now − recalls[i].at` (test RS4: shifting every timestamp by a constant changes nothing) | clock, not personal |
| `history` | completions, `predictedCorrectly` (null or absent = none), `recalls` cut to the last 30 days | standing-conditions; P1–P4; F1 window |
| `dims` | every lesson's dims (domain, concept, level, prerequisites, successors, status, rating) | not personal |
| `slots` | positions in a session | not personal |

**What it knows:** the world's equations and these population-level values of the cell: learnRate, prediction feedback, prerequisite penalty, slip, guess floor, relearn, the population forgetting rate (E1 base × the cell's forgetting scale, i.e. the persona spread at its median), the forgetting shape, the L9 spread *as a distribution* (it averages over it), the population prior-knowledge mean and SD with their clamp, the N7 rows, and E[pull | rating] under the world's rating law with the persona modifiers averaged (harness3.mjs `reachablePop`, `pullBarByRating`; test RS8: identical for every persona).

**What it does not know:** the persona; any persona's k, prior-knowledge rate, pull modifier or interest; any concept's L9 factor; the hidden prior knowledge or prerequisite state; the day of the episode, the session count or the read time. It reads nothing in model.ts `FORBIDDEN_INPUTS`, no `gapDays` and no `settings` (test RS5 adds each, plus `persona` and `fixedDue`, at two values: nothing changes). It keeps no state between calls (test RS6: a call on another learner's input first changes nothing; RS2 replay; RS3 the input is frozen). Its source imports nothing and names no hidden-state handle (T5c). It derives the default order for its ties from `dims` by the catalogue's own rule (RS7: equal to the catalogue's), so `defaultOrder` is not an extra input.

**The estimate it rebuilds at every call** (reachable-oracle.mjs header, steps 1 to 6):
1. Per domain, the posterior mean of the learner's prior-knowledge rate, on a grid, from the population prior and every first prediction in that domain (P(right) = π(1 − slip) + (1 − π)(1 − E[pull | rating])).
2. Per completed concept, P(known now): the prediction's posterior, then encoding, then a forward filter over its visible recalls with the revisit law (population kept share).
3. Per concept not completed: its domain's estimate.
4. P(encode) = learnRate × (1 + feedback) × (1 − penalty × (1 − ∏ P(prerequisite known))).
5. The value of studying a lesson now, at a **fixed valuation horizon of 14 days** (my choice; about the mean age at the final read of a lesson studied in the episode): (1 − q) × P(encode) × κ(14) + q × refresh, where κ is the population-expected kept share. The refresh value of a prior-known concept uses an **assumed trace age of priorAgeDays + 6.5 days** (my choice: 6.5 is the episode's mean day index, a design average and not this learner's day; after K4's B1 fix priorAgeDays is 0, so the assumed age is 6.5 days in every cell, which F3 N10 finds equal to the world's own mean). For a completed concept it uses the visible time since its last event.
6. Greedy: the largest value, with ties to the default order.

Both constants in step 5 are mine, set now and not tuned. The oracle is one estimator. A better one could exist, so like `oracle-order`, its result is a **lower bound** on what an ordering limited to these inputs could reach in this world. NOT-REACHABLE means "the best rule the eval wrote from the permitted inputs does not move R₇ by δ_min in 4 of 6 personas". It does not mean that no rule could. Pre-commitment 3 reads it as written.

**Reading for pre-commitment 3** (run3.mjs `verdicts.rule3`): in `interleave:base`, **REACHABLE** if `reachable` vs `default-order` wins in at least 4 of 6 personas, where a win is the lower end of the 95% t interval of Δ above 0 and Δ ≥ δ_min = 0.01, as criterion 2 §2.1 item 1. Otherwise **NOT-REACHABLE**. If any validity check on the reachable arm fails at run time (T1 cross-process replay; RS1 to RS6 on its own episodes' inputs), the reading is **INVALID**, and by batch 4's rule an unbuilt or invalid oracle means rule 3 does not fire. I read "criterion 2's rule" for an oracle as criterion 2 read it for its oracles (§2.3, §8 there): **wins only**. Harms and the ordering guardrails G1 to G4 are computed and reported beside the reading, and do not enter it. The arm is a reference, not a candidate. I state this here so it cannot be chosen after the data. Every one-at-a-time cell and `first:base` are reported.

**No input beyond §I.3 is needed. The arm is not stopped, and no privacy-reviewer pre-read is requested.**
