**The condition on the `forgetRate` high bound (new, and it matters for C2 and C4).** Fazio et al.'s single reading gives k ≈ 0.17–0.19 only when the not-read baseline (guessing and prior knowledge, .21–.23 on their items) is subtracted first. On raw cued-recall scores the same data give k ≈ 0.065. The 0.20 bound is therefore sourced **for a learner model that decays what the lesson added on top of a floor set by prior knowledge**. If the eval's learner decays the whole recall probability, prior knowledge included, toward zero, then 0.20 is faster forgetting than this source supports. In that case the source supports roughly 0.07 for a single unreinforced reading. I am not changing the bound, because it depends on the learner's form, which is the eval's code. I will check that form in C2 (W-1533). The two readings are consistent with row 1: free recall of idea units has almost no guessing floor, so Roediger & Karpicke's restudied 0.09 and tested 0.04 sit naturally below a single reading's 0.17–0.19.

On 0.20 versus the data's 0.19: I keep 0.20. It is a rounding at the generous edge of a sweep bound, and it is not fitted to any result. If the eval prefers the measured edge, 0.19 is the sourced value.
