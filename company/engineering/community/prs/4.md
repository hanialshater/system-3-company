- **Provenance reproduced.** I cloned `aourednik/historical-basemaps`, checked out the pinned commit `da7a4b735ecef70aebdc9c73e409d8a2500d50f3`, and ran `node tools/history-maps/generate.mjs artifacts/historical-basemaps` (compare mode, no `--write`). Result: "Verified 100 cases / 300 files from 25 pinned snapshots". The generator checks each snapshot's SHA-256 against `sources.json` and compares every `map.json`, `meta.json`, `Demo.tsx`, `catalog.json` and `sources.json` byte for byte (`generate.mjs` lines 19–40, 127–145). So the shipped data is exactly what the pinned sources produce. Upstream `LICENSE` is byte-identical to `src/lib/history-maps/DATA-LICENSE.txt`. Coastlines come from the Natural Earth land data already in the repo (public domain; ADR-002).
- **The licence is GPL-3.0.** The 100 `map.json` files are SVG paths derived in bulk from GPL-3.0 GeoJSON, and they are bundled into per-lesson client chunks. `company/engineering/best-practices.md` item 11 (from the EDD-004 review, W-145): "Never copy a file, table or program output in bulk from such a source. Anything beyond individual facts needs a decision record." The History category decision (`company/decisions/2026-10-14-history-category.md`, line 47) also says a vendored snapshot needs "a licence review". No such record exists. The repo is `private: true` with no licence of its own.
- **Why it is a merge gate and not only a publish gate:** draft chunks are built into every production build (all lessons are in `loaders.generated.ts`), so the next approved deploy after a merge would distribute the derived data even while every lesson stays draft.
- Questions for the record (not answers; I give no legal view): whether GPL-3.0 on the derived paths is acceptable for us; what attribution each lesson must show (the `/history-maps` footer credits Ourednik, but a lesson page does not, except through its claim sources); whether the generator must be published as corresponding source; whether to use a differently licensed source instead.
