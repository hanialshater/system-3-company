# Retro: 2026-10-13: waves 2–4 (178 → 298 lessons)

owner: chief-of-staff · attendees (by report): 13 builders, 4 fact-check passes, 3 learner evaluators, 1 claim fixer

## Numbers for this wave
- **Lessons added:** 120. **Merge QA failures:** 0. Integrated QA passed 426/426.
- **Tokens per lesson:** about 21k build and 5k check. Builders cost 196k–231k each, for 10 lessons.
- **Claims:** 16 disputed in passes 1–2, all fixed by one Sonnet claim-fix builder (87k tokens for 18 lessons), then re-checked in pass 3. Claims verified are now 90%, up from 77%.
- **Learner verdicts:** 30 launch, 58 revise, 1 fatal. Lessons built after the earn-the-reveal rules launch at about 45%; the earlier physics and math lessons at about 11%.
- **Published:** 2 → 25.
- **Incidents:**
  - A usage-limit COE (8 builders stopped, all resumed).
  - My own `pkill -f` killed the QA shell that ran it.

## What went well (keep doing)
- **Builders correct their briefs.** They fixed 4 or more wrong brief answers or claims (Python list doubling, the 400 W breaker, "Argentina digs to China", Kiribati) instead of building them. The rule "re-compute every number in node" earns its cost.
- **The fact-checker caught a wrong drawing,** not just wrong text: heavy-but-floats mirrored its cap offset. Checking the claim against the stage works.
- **Rules in the builder prompt work.** Folding learner-review findings into the next builder prompt raised the launch rate within one wave.
- **Cheap revision.** A Sonnet claim-fix builder fixed 18 lessons for about 5k tokens each, an order of magnitude cheaper than a new build.

## What hurt (with evidence)
- **Review is the bottleneck,** not building. 209 of 298 lessons have no learner review, and 19 have stale reviews.
- **The gate doesn't check verdicts or versions.** Two chemistry launch verdicts went stale within an hour of being written, when the claim fix bumped those lessons to v2. Only a hand check caught it.
- **Checker-added claims overstate.** 7 of the 11 pass-2 disputes were claims an earlier checker had added. Those claims generalise from the toy to real AI systems.
- **QA lags.** Lessons merge with only their builder's QA run behind them, and the integrated QA record comes later. Four chemistry launches waited on a QA record.
- **Verdict evidence wasn't linked.** The publish gate looks for `evidence.simulatedLearner`, but reviewers write only the file. I linked them by script twice; that should be a mechanism, not a memory.
- **The judge shapes the product.** QA's press-once path pushes builders from sliders to buttons and chips (Goodhart). Each departure is recorded in its build.md, and it is evidence for AMD-001.

## What we will stop doing
- Merging builders faster than review can absorb. The goal is 500 lessons that are reviewed, not 500 lessons.

## Did last retro's changes work?
- **Earn the reveal:** yes. The launch rate rose from about 11% to about 45% for lessons built under the rule. The probe is in the WBR, because reviewer leniency is the competing hypothesis.
- **Second fact-check pass every wave:** yes. It found 11 disputes the first pass missed.
- **Per-agent scratch dirs:** yes. No collisions between builders.
- **Kit defects fixed once:** yes. No kit requests from 13 builders.
