# Correction of Error: 2026-10-15: r7 reached production before the founder approved it

owner: release-manager · recorded-by: chief of staff · reviewers: evaluation-steward, pattern-librarian

## What happened (facts, timestamps, links)
- 12:37Z: the release manager prepared r7 (publish commit e6805f4) and was told not to deploy. It kept the r7 entry out of releases.json and committed it as `company/ops/releases-pending-r7.json` in aefb10a (12:45:58Z). The commit body read: "Held out of releases.json: tests/hq pins r1..r6 and [deploy] commits."
- `vercel.json` `ignoreCommand` builds any commit whose message contains the literal `[deploy]` *anywhere*. Vercel built aefb10a and promoted it to production (GitHub commit status "Vercel: Deployment has completed"). e6805f4 and d8f5f89 show "Canceled by Ignored Build Step", as intended.
- The chief of staff presented r7 to the founder as "not deployed, awaiting approval". The founder replied "yes, deploy r7". Before shipping, the chief of staff searched for `[deploy]` commits and found aefb10a.
- About 13:00Z: the C2 checks were run on https://system-3-company.vercel.app. Five drafts returned 404 twice; one request timed out and returned 404 on retry. /api/meta had only allow-listed keys. coal-came-back and heat-the-ammonia-maker report `published`. So production runs the r7 tree.

## Impact (learners affected, lessons, claims)
- Content: none beyond what was approved. aefb10a = e6805f4 (QA 1930/1930, draft 404s verified on a production-mode build) plus one company/ JSON file. The founder approved this same content about 20 minutes later.
- Governance: a hard rule was broken ("Deploys need the founder's approval, every time"). For about 20 minutes, production ran a release the founder had not approved, while the founder was told nothing had been deployed. One of the 100 daily Vercel deployments was used.
- No second deploy was made after approval. The r7 log entry records aefb10a and this note.

## Five whys
1. Why did production deploy without approval? Vercel built aefb10a because its message contained `[deploy]`.
2. Why did the message contain it? The release manager described the test constraint ("pins ... [deploy] commits") in prose and quoted the token.
3. Why did prose trigger a deploy? The marker is a literal substring match on the whole message, not a structured field. Any mention fires it.
4. Why was nothing in place to catch it? No commit-msg hook or CI check rejects `[deploy]` outside its intended position, and nothing watches for production deploys that lack an approval record. The CLAUDE.md rule asks agents to avoid the string, so it depends on memory.
5. Why was the founder told "not deployed"? The chief of staff took the agent's report as truth and did not check Vercel commit statuses after the release manager's commits.

## What allowed it
- The trigger `grep -qF "[deploy]"` over the full commit message.
- No guard on commit messages.
- No monitor for production deploys that have no approval record.
