# Learner review: widget-marginal-cost
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner (knows total cost vs cost per item and what a fixed cost is; uses the average as "the cost" of an item).
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, phone, 5 frames)

## 1. From the artifact

- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: the prediction, mostly. The hook asserts "the next one costs about $4", and the stem gives both "$2 per widget" and "average $4". The two pull against each other, which is the right tension; a careful reader will spot that $2 is stated, so the trap is mild but real.
- Explore: a falling average-cost curve and a flat "next one +$2" line, with the dot at 50 sitting on $4.00. +1 gives TOTAL $200 → $202, AVERAGE $4.00 → $3.96. Seeing the total move by exactly $2 while the average barely moves is clear and earned.
- The two curves are unlabelled as to what the y-axis is (dollars per widget); the labels on the lines carry it.
- Told-not-shown: "an order for one more at $3 adds $1 of profit here, even though $3 is below the average" is the practical payoff and is told only; the stage has no price line to compare with. It is the most useful sentence for a beginner and it would be easy to show.
- Layout: the reveal shrinks the chart to a small strip (labels tiny). Reveal text fits; no clipping.

## 2. After reading the brief (version only)

- Version 1, 6 claims shown as checked.

## Fix suggested

Add an optional "offer: $3 each" line on the chart (between the $2 and $4 lines) so the profit point is visible; label the y-axis "$ per widget"; keep the chart readable at reveal.
