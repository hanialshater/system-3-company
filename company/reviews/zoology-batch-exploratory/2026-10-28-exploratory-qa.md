# Zoology batch: exploratory browser QA

Date: 2026-10-28. Scope: 35 newly integrated draft lessons: 14 mammals, six birds, five fossils and ten dinosaur/fossil-evidence lessons. The tested slug list and the actual 35 component plus three shared-file Git blob hashes are in `artifacts/zoology-batch-exploration/slugs.json` and `source-hashes.json`. Slugs were selected at commit `52aa6a08` after base `0279bec2`; the shared working tree was dirty. A post-run comparison showed none of the 35 components, Lesson, DemoCard or global CSS changed since input hash capture. Metadata was included in the bundle but was not separately hashed.

## Harness and method

This is exploratory Chromium QA, not the protected Playwright contract or full Next.js application. The local harness bundles the actual 35 components, DemoCard, Lesson, controls and global stylesheet. It supplies each lesson's metadata and stubs the lazy DemoHost and Next Link. A fresh page used the last prediction option for each lesson, then activated enabled stage controls in DOM order (at most 16; range controls received two arrow presses), looking for a changed stage and an enabled reveal. Two viewports: 390×844 and 1280×800, one flow per lesson per viewport. It checked initial predict lock, no early reveal, nonempty controls, observable control changes, reveal gating, text over 120 characters, page/console errors, horizontal overflow and the 24 px minimum control size. After opening reveal, phone geometry also checked that the stage top and reveal bottom fit within the card body.

## Results

35 of 35 lessons completed in both viewports: 70 runs, zero scripted failures, zero captured page/console errors, no horizontal overflow and no measured phone stage-top or reveal-bottom crop. Every lesson had a stage control of 33 px height: this passes the protected contract's 24 px minimum but is below the 44 px touch target used in the revised prototypes. The sampled flows use one prediction per lesson; they do not verify all answer mappings or wrong/correct branches. They do not test keyboard navigation, reload, smaller phones, both display themes, reduced motion, touch gestures, actual Next routing or real feed behavior.

The 35 phone reveal images were captured, but nine were actually opened and visually inspected: `mammal-camel-hump-reserve`, `mammal-elephant-ear-heat`, `mammal-bat-echo-path`, `zoo-bird-feather-contour-down`, `zoo-penguin-wing-water`, `zoo-dino-pterosaur-tree`, `zoo-dino-healed-injury`, `zoo-fossil-ash-bracket`, and `zoo-fossil-offset-fault`. In these nine, the stage and reveal were visibly present. The camel and elephant illustrations are notably abstract: the camel is a simple block with a hump, while the elephant ear is represented by a circle and heat arrows. Visual and learning design should judge whether these illustrations sufficiently identify the animal and demonstrate the claimed mechanism; an operational pass does not establish that the content teaches or that a labeled diagram constitutes independent evidence.
