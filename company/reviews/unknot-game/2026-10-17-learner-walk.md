# Learner review: unknot-game
lesson version: v1
boundary: knows that a knot is a closed loop drawn with over and under crossings (meta.json learner.knows); does not know that one crossing switch can free a knot, why (a strand on top twice in a row slides off), or that any single switch does it on the trefoil and figure-eight (meta.json learner.doesNotKnow). Taken from meta.json as the brief directed; the brief's learner/claims fields were read before walking, build.md and design.md were not.
Screenshots seen: yes (artifacts/walkthroughs/unknot-game/sheet.png, recorded 2026-10-17T00:56:54Z, phone 390x844, built from a10dedf1 into .next-le-unknot) plus my own probe frames at 390x844 on the same build (port 3761; wrong prediction "3", re-tap of one crossing, crossing 1 then crossing 2 without Reset, all 4 figure-eight crossings, reveal)
Verdict: revise

## Findings (written from the walkthrough before reading build.md)

1. **Where understanding first broke: step 1, the first "Plain loop".** I tap crossing 1 and the readout says "Plain loop", but the picture still has three crossings and, to my eye, looks exactly as tangled as before (02-step-1.png). Nothing moves. The only bridge from "tangled picture" to "plain loop" is the blue strand and the caption "On top twice: it slides off". With my boundary (I know over/under, nothing about moves) I can find the blue arc and check that it is the uncut strand at both of its circled crossings; that part is readable. What I cannot do is the next step: picture lifting that arc off ("slides off" what, and to where?) and then see what is left. The reveal adds "and the twist left over falls out", a second move I am told about but never shown; I would have to imagine a one-crossing picture and know that it is just a twist. So I end the lesson able to say the objective sentence ("one switch, a strand on top twice, it slides off") because the caption gave it to me, but I hold "Plain loop" on the readout's authority, not on anything I saw happen. This is the gap the lesson exists to close (it is the doesNotKnow clause), so I rank it first.

2. **The reveal points at a strand that is not on screen (QA path).** On the walkthrough path (trefoil 1, 2, 3, Reset, Figure-eight, Show me why) the reveal opens with the stage on an unswitched figure-eight reading "Still a knot", no strand tinted (07-reveal.png). The reveal says "After each switch, the tinted strand lay on top at two crossings in a row". I look up for the tinted strand and there is none; the last tinted strand I saw was on the trefoil, two taps ago. On my probe path the reveal opened on a switched figure-eight with a tint, while the sentence is about the trefoil ("freed the trefoil on its own: 3 of 3"). Either way the picture beside the "why" sentence is not the one it describes. This is the specific, fixable finding behind the verdict.

3. **What the prediction felt like.** Fair and genuinely uncertain. "The trefoil has 3 crossings" plus options 1/2/3 pulls a beginner toward 3 ("undo each one") or 2, which is the misconception I would bring. Choosing 3 and then seeing "Plain loop" after one tap is a real surprise; the tally (1 of 3, 2 of 3, 3 of 3 with checks) then answers "is there a special crossing?" cleanly. That part works.

4. **Taps replace, silently.** Tapping crossing 2 while crossing 1 is switched restores crossing 1 without any word (probe d-x1-then-x2.png: right crossing back to its old over/under, only the top-left circle bold). Tapping the same crossing twice toggles back to "Still a knot". A learner who predicted 3 ("you must undo every crossing") cannot test their own idea of switching two or all three; they can only see that one is enough. Because one-is-enough is the point, this is a note, not a blocker, but the "then try each of the others" hint does not say the earlier switch is undone, so a careful learner may think they now have two switches in and be confused about what freed it.

5. **Did the reveal answer the question I actually had?** My question after step 1 was "how is that a plain loop? show me". The reveal answers "which crossing?" (none is special: 3 of 3) well, and answers "why?" with a sentence that repeats the caption plus one more untold move ("the twist left over falls out"). The last paragraph (unknotting number; figure-eight is also 1; some knots need more) is new vocabulary and new facts; it reads as fine one-line context, but on the QA path the figure-eight tally is 0 of 4, so "and for the figure-eight too" is told, not seen, for that learner.

6. **Small.** The tap targets are faint grey circles on the drawing; the hint says "Tap a crossing", which is enough at my boundary. Crossing buttons are disabled before Lock it in (probe: 3 buttons, enabled=false), so nothing leaks before the prediction. In my probe reveal frame the stage top sits at the top of the viewport with the panel's top edge just cut; I did not measure the crop (QA's tools/reveal-fit.mjs does).

Verdict reason: the reveal's "why" sentence points at "the tinted strand", which is not visible on the stage when the reveal opens on the QA path (07-reveal.png, unswitched figure-eight), and the slide that turns the switched drawing into a plain loop is never shown, so "Plain loop" rests on the readout's word (findings 1 and 2).

## After reading build.md / meta.json

- Read after the findings above were written: company/reviews/unknot-game/build.md, design.md, 2026-10-16-qa.md (and meta.json learner, objective, claims, which I had read before walking to take the boundary). Nothing above was edited after reading them.
- **Finding 2 is known and argued away, and I disagree.** build.md pre-flight 6 records the same reveal state (4_1 unswitched, no tint) and reads "the tinted strand" as past tense, "what was seen after each switch". QA (2026-10-16-qa.md, line 48) noted its reveal run also had no tinted strand on stage and did not test further. As the learner, the past tense did not help: the sentence is the lesson's one "why", and when I looked up from it there was nothing tinted and the readout said "Still a knot". demo-standard.md (reveal quotes what you saw; every stage element the reveal points at is visible) reads this as a pointer to an absent element. The fix is the builder's (e.g. hold the last trefoil switch or its tint on stage at reveal, or quote it as a trace); I do not prescribe it.
- **Finding 1 is a design choice the learning designer made knowingly.** design.md adds the slide-off tint as the stage's stand-in for the cause and marks "the twist left over falls out" as one told clause of context; it also says that without the tint the lesson would be a likely revise under the told-vs-shown decision. The tint does stand for "on top twice" (I could read it); what stays unseen is the slide and the leftover kink, so "Plain loop" still rests on the readout. I keep this as the first place my understanding broke and as a hypothesis for a human test ("after one switch, can the learner say or sketch what the picture becomes?"), not as a standards breach on its own.
- **Finding 4 matches intent:** one switch at a time is the designer's deliberate change from the brief (design.md Interaction 1), and dropping "switch all three" was ruled out of scope. The silent restore is still unannounced to the learner; a note for the builder, not a blocker.
- **Finding 5:** design.md and build.md both mark "and for the figure-eight too" as claimed context unless 4_1 was visited; consistent with what I saw.
- Intent and experience agree on the prediction and the tally (findings 3): the "no special crossing" half of the misconception is answered on the stage by the 3 of 3 row, as the design note planned.
