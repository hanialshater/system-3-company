## 1. From the artifact

- Hook and predict: "Swap who makes the offers and the outcome changes" frames the tension well. The question is easy to read. Choosing "Both students" is close to a gimme: with students proposing, each sends an offer to a different first choice, so nobody competes. The locked stage shows the four rankings and "First choices: ?". Nothing leaks.
- Explore: tap Ann, then Bea, and arrows go down to X and Y. The stars and "Students proposed: Ann–X ★, Bea–Y ★ · stable" appear. Flip to "Hospitals propose ↑", tap X, then Y, and crossed arrows go up. "Hospitals proposed: X–Bea ★, Y–Ann ★ · stable" appears. The two result lines stack, so the contrast is easy to see. Good: one stage, one switch, and the outcome flips.
- Where understanding first broke: the word "stable". It appears on stage as a tag after each result and carries the reveal ("both matchings were stable, so neither answer was an error"). It is never defined, and nothing on stage shows what makes a matching stable. Nobody checks whether a pair would rather swap. At this boundary "stable" reads as jargon, and "neither answer was an error" asks to be taken on trust.
- The rule on stage, "Receivers keep their best offer", never comes into play. In this 2 × 2 every offer goes to a different receiver, so nobody holds, rejects or moves to a next choice. The learner never sees "deferred" acceptance, which is the procedure the reveal names.
- Step 1 (tap Ann) changes only the arrow. That is fine visually, but the text line stays "First choices: ?" until both have proposed.
- Reveal: it quotes what was seen (both students, then both hospitals). "Deferred acceptance always ends stable and gives the proposing side its best stable partner" is told. Gale–Shapley and the 1998 residency switch are claimed context. It ends on the idea ("a fair-looking procedure can still have a side it favours").
- No FATAL.

## 2. After reading the brief

No earlier learner review exists, so there are no earlier findings to track.

- doesNotKnow 1 (who proposes decides who gets their first choice): **shown**, as the switch flips the stars. It meets the rule.
- doesNotKnow 2 (deferred acceptance always ends stable): **told only**. "Stable" is a tag with nothing on stage standing for it: no blocking-pair check, no "would anyone rather swap?". And no deferral or rejection ever happens in this example. Under "Show the why you promised" this is a **revise**.
- Objective ("the proposing side gets its best *stable* partner") depends on the same undefined word.
- Top finding: the stage must make "stable" mean something the learner can see. For example, after each run, check the one other pairing and show that some pair would rather swap. Ideally the example would also include one held-then-rejected offer, so "receivers keep their best offer" actually happens.
