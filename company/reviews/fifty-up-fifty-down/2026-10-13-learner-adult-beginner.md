## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess; 100 is the intuitive answer, 75 and 125 are plausible foils. The locked stage shows only 100 and a slider at 0%, nothing leaks. Good.
- Where understanding first broke: the diagram at 75% (step 5 screenshot). Four numbers appear at once: a large "43.75", a pink "−131.25" box, a "+75" strip, and "net −56.25" on top, laid out as two rectangles with a dashed outline marked "start 100". I could not tell which number is the price, what the two columns are, or why a "−131.25" is bigger than the starting 100 in a box that looks smaller than the start. The caption below ("Up 75%: 175. Down 75% of 175: 43.75.") is clearer than the picture; I read the sentence and ignored the drawing.
- The panel says "Slide to 50% and compare the shape with the dashed start." The driver moved only between 0 and 75, so I did not see 50%, the case I was asked about. I cannot tell whether the slider offers 50 (likely a driver limit), but the lesson's own question (50 up, 50 down) was not reached by my path.
- Rotating through 0 and 75 showed "back to 100 at 0%" and "43.75 at 75%": the contrast is real but a beginner who sees 75 up and 75 down going to 44 might think "of course, 75% down is a big drop", rather than "it should have cancelled". The asymmetry is explained only in the reveal.
- Reveal: "The rise was 50% of 100, so +50, up to 150. The fall was 50% of 150, so −75. Same percentage, bigger base." That sentence is the real insight and it is clear. But the next paragraph, "(1 + p) × (1 − p) = 1 − p²... The missing corner, p × p", uses algebra the beginner has not met, and refers to a "picture" with "a missing corner" that I could not identify in the drawing. The reveal is earned by arithmetic, not by what the picture showed.
- Reveal gate: appears reachable (Show me why is lit after moving the slider).
- Problems: (1) the diagram's numbers are unlabelled and cluttered; (2) 50% case not on the path; (3) algebraic "p × p corner" invoked but not visible; (4) at 75% the drop looks like a trivial consequence.
- No FATAL; the arithmetic shown is correct.
