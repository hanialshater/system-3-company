# Learner review: split-the-sum
lesson version: 1
boundary: adult beginner; knows single-digit products and area = width × height; does not use splitting as a strategy.
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 3 frames, phone)

## 1. From the artifact

- Where it first lost me: the prediction. "What is 13 × 7?" with 91 / 81 / 101 is an arithmetic test, not a prediction about the idea. Most adults can grind it out (or pick 91 because it "looks right"), so there is nothing to be wrong about and no belief for the stage to overturn. The idea the hook promises ("cut somewhere clever") is never what I commit to.
- Shown well: the 13 × 7 grid with a draggable cut; at 10 the cards read 10×7=70, 3×7=21, together 91. Clean and readable.
- "Show me why" unlocked after one drag. The reveal says "Your cut at 9 gave 63 + 28 = 91 too": true of the drag path, but I only read the cut at 10. "Any cut works" rests on one reading.
- Told-not-shown: "The pieces always fill the same rectangle, so any cut works."
- Layout: fine; reveal fits on phone.
- Reveal earned: the 70 + 21 split, yes; the general idea, told.

## 2. After reading the brief

- doesNotKnow: a hard product splits into easy ones. Delivered as a demonstration, but the prediction never tests it.

## Fix suggested
- Ask about the idea: "Cut 13 × 7 at 8 instead of 10. Does the total change?" (Yes / No / Depends), and require two cut positions before "Show me why".

Verdict: revise
