**Verdict: PASS. Safety class: none (confirmed).** Skip line removed from `company/ops/brief-skips.json`.

## Reasons
- The lesson is mostly a finding (Raymer & Smith 2007), not an action. The only implied action is "clip the two ends together" when storing a cord.
- Careless version 1, powered cables: learners will apply this to charger and extension leads. Clipping the ends of an unplugged cord for storage is harmless. The related habit that does cause harm, using an extension lead while it is still tightly coiled or bunched (it can overheat under load), is not suggested anywhere in the brief. The illustration request already excludes plugs, sockets, chargers and powered cables.
- Careless version 2, loops and children: a closed loop of cord is a strangulation hazard for young children (the reason window-blind cord standards ban loops). A looped cord stored in a box or drawer is low risk, and nothing in the brief or the image involves children.
- No heat, chemicals, health or money claims.

## Required changes
None.

## Conditions I will check at launch review
1. The reveal frames clipping the ends as something you do when you *put a cord away* (in a bag, box or drawer), not as a way to leave cords hanging or lying about.
2. No stage element or illustration shows a powered cable, a coiled extension lead in use, a child, or a looped cord near a child.
