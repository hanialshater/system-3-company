## Findings (non-blocking)

1. N4 (L4, residual). The label is a modern country name applied backwards: in 1850 the United Kingdom included all of Ireland, and "Germany" and "United States" are the dataset's entities too (Germany was not one state before 1871). The lesson does not claim otherwise and the reveal's "United Kingdom" is the dataset's term, so this is not a blocker. A single clause is optional if cm-history wants it, not required. Owner: cm-history, optional.
2. N2/P2 (uncertainty on the land-use clause). The old limit sentence carried the thought that early years are uncertain; the new one drops it. Land-use CO2 for 1850 is the least certain series in the file, and "under 5%" is stated flat. It is safe because the direction (a small UK share) is robust and "under 5%" is not a point estimate, but the fact-checker should confirm that the 4.5% (131.8 of 2,910.9 Mt, claim c8) holds with the dataset's own caveat, and tell cm-history whether a "roughly" is warranted. Owner: fact-checker, then cm-history.
3. Number consistency, routed not decided. Stage at 1800 shows "UK 95%" (walkthrough step 1), while the trust sheet's claim c2 says "1800 UK 96%". Labels at 1800 also sum to 99%. This may be rounding, but a learner who opens "Why trust this?" sees two numbers for one label. I do not verify numbers; fact-checker owns it, and the builder owns the revision.
