# Learner review: river-crossing-time
lesson version: 1
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 5 frames)
boundary: an adult with school physics (speed = distance / time); has not used vectors.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction felt real: "Never" is the hook's bait and 18 s (90 ÷ 5, using the diagonal speed) is the half-remembered-Pythagoras trap. Good set.
- Explore: a top-view river with current arrows, a dashed diagonal path and dots every 10 s. "+10 s" steps show 30 m across and 40 m down each time, so equal across-steps are visible regardless of the drift. This is the right picture.
- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: the reveal says "exactly like the no-current crossing", but I never saw a no-current crossing; the slider started at 2 m/s and I moved it to 4. The key comparison (same 30 s at 0, 2 and 4 m/s) is told, not shown.
- Moving the slider completes the crossing instantly (frame 2 already shows 30 s, 120 m), so the clock never "runs"; "+10 s" helps after a Start over, but a learner who skips it sees only the end state.
- Reveal vs stage: at reveal the stage shows the 10 s state (30 m across), not the finished crossing.
- Layout: the reveal shrinks the river diagram to a thumbnail with unreadable labels; empty top band in explore. No clipping.
- Fix suggested (minor): show ghost paths for 0 and 2 m/s with their clocks (all 30 s), or ask the learner to try current 0 first; snap the stage to the finished crossing at reveal.

## 2. After reading the brief

- Objective: crossing time is set by the boat's own speed whatever the current; the current sets the landing point. Delivered for one current value; 'whatever the current' needs a second current visibly giving the same 30 s.
- Claims: 5, all verified; I cannot vouch for them.

Verdict: launch
