# Learner review: inflation-halves-cash
lesson version: 1
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 7 frames)
boundary: an adult who knows prices rise each year; assumes 3% a year means losing $3 of $100 each year.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction felt real: $28 (24 x $3 = $72 lost) is exactly my sum. The hook names the trap fairly.
- Explore: a 100-square grid of "$1 of today's goods" emptying as years pass is a clear picture; 49 squares left at 24 years makes "about half" visible. Switching 2% / 3% / 5% shows the rate matters.
- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: none badly. But the shrinking yearly loss, which is the whole reason $28 is wrong, is visible only as two numbers in a tile ($2.83 at year 2, $1.48 at year 24). The "24 yrs" button lets me jump past the drag, and the grid does not mark the squares lost this year, so the shrink is read, not seen.
- Reveal vs stage: the reveal quotes "$2.91 in year one", a value I never saw (my first stop was year 2), and the stage at reveal shows the 5% run ($31.01), not the 3% case being explained.
- Told-not-shown: "compounds like interest, in reverse" and "72 ÷ 3 = 24" are told; fine as add-ons. Last caveat line runs under the trust link (clipping, W-135; noted only).
- Layout: grid and tiles fit; no other problems.
- Fix suggested (minor): highlight each year's lost squares in a different colour as the slider moves (so the bite visibly shrinks); quote the learner's own first-year value; snap back to 3% at reveal.

## 2. After reading the brief

- Objective: each year's inflation applies to an already-reduced value, so ~half after 24 years at 3%. The 'about half' result is shown strongly; the 'already-reduced value' mechanism is carried by one tile and is easy to skip.
- Claims: 7, all verified; I cannot vouch for them.

Verdict: launch
