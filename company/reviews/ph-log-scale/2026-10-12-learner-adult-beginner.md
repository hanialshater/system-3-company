*Simulated learner. This review finds problems; it is not evidence that anyone learned.*

Boundary used: remembers basic school arithmetic and everyday life (knows lemon juice is sour, coffee is mildly acidic, has seen "pH" on shampoo bottles); has never studied pH as a logarithmic scale or what a hydrogen ion is.

## 1. From the artifact (written before reading the brief)

Sources: walkthrough transcript and screenshots (phone), QA phone-2 and phone-3 images. The recorded walkthrough barely exercised the slider, so I also drove the lesson myself in a browser (arrow keys on the slider, pH 5 down to 2) and looked at the screens at pH 4, 3 and 2.

**Prediction.** "Same-sized cups: black coffee (about pH 5) and lemon juice (about pH 2). How many times more hydrogen ions does the lemon juice have?" Options: About 2.5 times / 3 times / About 30 times / About 1,000 times. A real guess is possible, and the distractors are the guesses a beginner actually makes: 2.5 (the ratio 5 to 2) and 3 (the difference 5 minus 2) are both sensible naive readings of a number scale. That is a good prediction. But: the hook asks "How much more acid is that?" while the question asks about "hydrogen ions" and neither explains that acid means hydrogen ions or why the lower pH is the stronger acid. A beginner can only guess on the number pattern and may not know which cup is more acidic. The word "pH" is not explained at all.

**Where understanding first broke.** In the recorded path: at the first Explore step. The learner moved the slider "right", and the stage jumped to pH 7 and "1/100 of coffee's hydrogen ions", showing ten empty white squares with a tiny outlined square in the corner and the caption "Now a hundredth of the outlined square". I cannot see a hundredth of anything. The panel told me to slide "down to lemon juice at pH 2", and the first thing I see goes the other way (up, to pure water). The stage then never changes over six actions (the transcript has six identical steps), so the recorded learner saw exactly one state, the one that can't be seen, and then the reveal.

**Explore vs the learner's actual question.** My question: "why would 3 steps be more than 3 times?" Driving it myself gave the answer well: at pH 4 the stage reads "10×" with a thin dashed strip filling a sliver of one square, at pH 3 "100×" with one square filled densely, at pH 2 "1,000×" with all ten squares full. This is a clear and convincing picture, and the big number changes with each step, so sliding one step at a time is a real discovery. The problem is that nothing makes the learner go down. The reveal is available from the first screen; a learner who slides up, or not at all, is told a story about something they didn't see. Also, "STEPS FROM COFFEE" counts 2 at pH 7 and 3 at pH 2, with no sign, so "2" going up (fewer ions) and "2" going down (more) look the same. At pH 3 and 4 the LIQUID card shows just "—", so only 5, 2 and 7 have landmarks.

**Reveal: earned or told?** Earned if the learner slid down to pH 2 (the picture is the key thing and it is what the reveal describes), told otherwise. "a rod of 10, a flat of 100, then ten flats" uses base-ten-block language that the stage never shows by name; on screen it is a strip, then a filled square, then ten filled squares. An adult who has not met "rods and flats" has to map it. "Each pH step down multiplies the hydrogen ions by 10" is shown, not just told, if I slid. "Going up divides by 10. Pure water at pH 7 has 1/100 of coffee's, a sliver too thin to see" is shown. "pH counts powers of ten, so the gap from pH 0 to 14 is a factor of a hundred trillion" is told: the slider only runs from 2 to 7, so 0 and 14 are never on screen, and "powers of ten" is jargon.

**What the lesson never answers.** Why the scale runs the "wrong" way (lower pH, more ions). The learner who picked 2.5 or 3 is told they were wrong but the cause of their error ("pH numbers are exponents, not amounts") is never named in plain words. The closing line "Small pH numbers, enormous differences" is a nice slogan but does not say "each number is ten times the previous".

**Findings as problems**
1. Direction and access: the guidance says slide down, the first thing a learner can do (and what the recorded learner did) goes up to pure water, which is the hardest state to see, and the reveal is reachable without ever seeing 10×/100×/1,000×.
2. At pH 7, "Now a hundredth of the outlined square" with empty squares is unreadable; the learner sees nothing change from "blank".
3. The hook says "acid", the question says "hydrogen ions", and nothing links them or says lower pH means more acid.
4. "pH" is never defined; the reveal's "powers of ten" and "factor of a hundred trillion" are told, not shown.
5. Rod/flat/ten-flats language does not match the stage's visuals by name.
6. "STEPS FROM COFFEE" has no direction; pH 3 and 4 have no label ("—"), so the intermediate steps carry no real-world anchor.
7. The wrong-answer reveal names the right answer but not the likely cause of 2.5 or 3 (reading pH as an ordinary scale).

**FATAL:** none found. No false statement spotted (1,000x, 1/100 and 10^14 all check out by arithmetic), no dead control (slider and keys work, 5 to 2 verified). The recorded-path concern in finding 1 is serious but I judge it a flow problem, since the reveal text says "slide down to pH 2 and you see", which tells the learner what to do rather than claiming they did it.

## 2. After reading the brief

- Intent: dislodge "pH is linear: 2.5x or three units more". The options 2.5 and 3 hit this exactly, and the sliding picture (10x, 100x, 1,000x) is the right instrument; my own drive-through confirms it works.
- The brief's learner already knows lower pH means more acidic and multiplying by 10. I did not assume that, and finding 3 (acid vs hydrogen ions, direction) is weaker under the lesson's own boundary but still unresolved for a true beginner.
- Intent: "step up means one tenth". Shown at pH 7 only as a blank stage, which is where the lesson is weakest (findings 1, 2).
- The recorded walkthrough path (six right-moves, all identical) is a driver artifact, but a real learner could do the same; the reveal's dependency on sliding down is not enforced.
- Claims are source or derivation backed and unverified; nothing I saw contradicts them.

Verdict: revise
