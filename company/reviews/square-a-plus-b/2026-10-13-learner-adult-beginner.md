# Learner review: square-a-plus-b
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner (knows 13 x 13 = 169 or can work it out; half-remembers (a + b)² from school and might write a² + b²).
Screenshots seen: yes

## From the artifact
- Prediction felt empty: the question asks "What is 13 x 13", which any adult computes as 169. The misconception the lesson targets (109 = 10² + 3²) only bites when the learner works from the formula, but the question's wording invites ordinary multiplication. Nobody who reads "13 x 13" picks 109, so there is nothing to be surprised by.
- Shown well: the cut square with 100, 30, 30, 9 areas on a grid; "Only a² + b²" whites out the two strips and labels them red 30 and 30, "Two strips of 30 are uncovered". This is a clean visual proof of the 2ab.
- Told, not shown: "Wherever you cut, the square is a² + 2ab + b²" is supported by the slider (8 + 5 on the locked stage), but the recorded path only looks at 10 + 3; fine.
- Layout: on the reveal frame the square shrinks to a thumbnail; strip labels are tiny.

## Fix suggested
Ask the misconception directly: "Someone says (10 + 3)² = 10² + 3² = 109. How far off are they?" (options 0 / 30 / 60), or "Which is bigger: (10 + 3)² or 10² + 3²? By how much?". Keep the square full size in the reveal.
