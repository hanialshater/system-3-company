# Learner review: minus-times-minus
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who remembers basic school arithmetic and everyday life; has never studied the topic (was told "minus times minus is plus" at school, never why; comfortable with negative numbers on a thermometer).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess. −6 is what many adults feel ("two negatives are doubly bad"), 6 is the remembered rule, −5 is the add-instead slip. The hook ("sounds like a rule someone made up") invites the sceptic. The locked stage already shows the axes and the first point (3 × −2 = −6), which gives the pattern a start without leaking the end. Good.
- Where understanding first broke: the reveal's second paragraph, "(−3) × (−2) plus (−3) × 2 is (−3) × 0 = 0, and (−3) × 2 = −6." That uses the distributive rule, which my beginner has not been shown; it is correct but not earned by anything on the stage.
- What was SHOWN is strong: stepping the first factor 3, 2, 1, 0, −1, −2, −3 draws a straight dotted line with a green "+2" at each step and the dots turn from red (negative) to green (positive) as the line crosses zero. The caption "(−3) × (−2) = 6. The +2 steps never broke." makes the point. I could see the climb rather than being told it.
- A lingering "why must the pattern continue?" question is a real beginner objection: a sceptic can say "the pattern might change after zero". The reveal answers that ("Nothing special happens at zero") by assertion, and then by the algebra I did not follow. The picture does not by itself show why continuing is forced.
- The first factor buttons can be tapped in any order; nothing seems to force passing through 0 before −3 (the reveal enables once −3 appears). A learner could tap 3 then −3 and skip the line's middle. Not a flaw in the lesson as measured here, but the key state "see every step" is not required.
- Reveal earned: the number 6 yes; the "forced" argument only partly.
- Problems: (1) "pattern must continue" is asserted; (2) distributive algebra unmotivated; (3) buttons can skip the middle of the pattern; (4) the stage speaks of a "first factor" only, so the other factor staying at −2 may go unnoticed until the label "First factor, times (−2)".
- No FATAL.

- The objective is the stepping pattern through zero, which the stage delivers; the distributive algebra in the reveal goes beyond the objective and is the part I could not follow.
- Claims verified.
