## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess. 60% is "voting adds nothing", 90% is "five votes, so near certain", 68% is the actual figure and not derivable without binomial arithmetic. The hook ("Does letting them vote help at all?") is neutral. "Independent" in the stem is undefined; the later "Same mistakes" button is the only gloss.
- The locked stage shows an empty 200-square grid. Fine.
- Where understanding first broke: the two numbers per frame. "THIS BATCH 138/200" and "LONG RUN 68.3%" sit side by side; "long run" is never defined. 138 of 200 is 69%, the long run 68.3%, and a learner is left unsure which is the answer. Then at 21 voters the batch is 158/200 and 82.6%. The pair is more confusing than the one number.
- What was SHOWN is strong: green squares increasing as voters go 1, 5, 21, and the "Same mistakes" toggle collapsing 21 voters back to 121/200 and 60.0%. The surprise that 21 voters do no better than one when they share mistakes is on screen.
- Told not shown: "Five voters are wrong only when at least three are wrong on the same question" is the mechanism and is told. The calculation of 68.3% (and 82.6%) is not derivable from the stage. The reveal's "On batch 1 one model got 121 of 200" is read off the Same-mistakes frame, since the learner never pressed "1 model"; the single-model baseline for batch 1 was never shown directly.
- AI claim: "A vote of weak models beats each one only as far as they are wrong in different places" is a sound general statement, here from a toy of independent coin-like models. Real ensemble models are not independent coin flips; the stage is labelled TOY MODELS, which helps.
- Reveal earned: 68% yes (the 138 and 68.3% appear on stage); the mechanism told.
- Problems: (1) "long run" undefined and competes with the batch number; (2) "independent" undefined until the toggle; (3) the 1-model baseline not shown; (4) the 68.3% calculation cannot be reconstructed.
- No FATAL.
