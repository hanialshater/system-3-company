# Learner review: route-explosion
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who uses a phone and a laptop daily and has never programmed (no idea what a variable, array, function or "algorithm" is; comfortable with everyday apps and basic arithmetic).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: "Start at home and visit 9 other cities once each, in any order. How many different orders are there?" is clear in plain English; the three options (81, 362,880, 3,628,800) are a real guess for a beginner: 81 (9 x 9) is the intuitive wrong answer. But 362,880 vs 3,628,800 differ by a factor of 10 with no way of reasoning toward either before the stage; the reveal justifies only afterwards why "home" is excluded. The hook ("A computer is fast. Can it just try every order of 9 cities, or 19?") plants the answer "no" but not the number.
- What was SHOWN: with 3 cities, "Next route" draws one tour and "All routes" draws all six in a grid, captioned "That is every order: 3 x 2 x 1 = 6." That is a lovely demonstration for 3 cities and is the heart of the idea. "Add a city" then moves to 4 cities and says "Draw the routes one by one", with ORDERS "?" again.
- Where understanding first broke: the jump from 3 to 9. The transcript path pressed "Add a city" once and then "Start over": the 24 orders for 4 cities never appeared (the learner has to press Next route or All routes again). So the reveal's "went up to 4 cities: 24 orders" is told, not shown. Then 9 x 8 x ... x 1 = 362,880 is arithmetic; "Pick the first city (9 choices), then the next (8 left)" is a clear explanation, but the learner has never seen the count grow by multiplication on the stage (6 to 24 to 120 would show it). "Home is fixed, so it is not counted" is explained only after the fact.
- Reveal: the 19-city figure "121,645,100,408,832,000 orders: about 3.9 years at a billion checks a second" is a good punchline but is not on the stage and the phrase "home plus 19 cities" mixes with the hook's "19 cities". Note the reveal screenshot is clipped mid-sentence. "Each new city multiplies the count, because it can slot into every old order in several places" is a different argument (insertion) from the "pick first, then next" argument just given: two explanations side by side.
- Reveal earned: the 3-city case yes; the 9-city number only by arithmetic, the huge-number punchline by assertion.
- Problems: (1) the 4+ city growth (24, 120, ...) is not shown unless the learner clicks more; (2) two competing explanations; (3) "home" and the factor of 10 not set up before the question; (4) 3.9 years told; (5) clipped reveal.
- No FATAL.
