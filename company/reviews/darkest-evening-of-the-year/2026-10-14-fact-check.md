**Dispute (c3):** the reveal makes orbital speed the cause and tilt the add-on. By the lesson's own equation-of-time terms the tilt gives 5.9 of the 8.3 minutes of noon drift from 13 to 30 Dec. Wording fix only; every number is right.

| Claim | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| c1 | verified | Re-run of solar.ts in node (type-stripped copy): earliest sunset 13 Dec 15:51 (15:51:20), latest sunrise 30 Dec 08:06, shortest day 21 Dec 7 h 50 min. Independent check with astropy 8.0.1 (Sun altitude -0.833 degrees at 51.5074 N, 0.1278 W): sunset 15:51:18 on both 12 and 13 Dec, latest sunrise 08:06:15 on 30 and 31 Dec, shortest day 21 Dec 7:49:38. Both methods round to the same minutes, so 'around' is apt. Stage note (not blocking): once 2 or more dates are visited, the ring labels 'earliest' and 'latest' mark the extremes among the dates visited so far, so before 13 Dec is visited the stage can say 'earliest 15:53' on 21 Dec. 'earliest seen' would be exact. |
| c2 | verified | Re-run: solar noon 11:54:42 on 13 Dec, shown 11:55; 12:03 on 30 Dec. The astropy maximum-altitude minute is 11:55 and 12:03. Agrees. |
| c3 | disputed | Disputed: the two causes are weighted the wrong way round. The sentence says noon drifts 'because Earth moves fastest in early January and its tilt adds to the drift', which makes speed the cause and tilt a top-up. Splitting the lesson's own equation-of-time formula (solar.ts) from 13 to 30 Dec: of the 8.3 min later drift, the obliquity (tilt) term gives 5.9 min and the eccentricity (orbital speed) term 2.2 min. The tilt is about 70% of it. Both facts in the sentence are right: perihelion is 3 Jan 2027 about 03 UTC (astropy, distance 0.98333 AU), and by Kepler's second law the orbit is fastest there. Fix for demo-builder (wording, in the reveal and in this claim): 'mostly because of Earth's tilt, and partly because Earth moves fastest in early January', or name the tilt first. |
| c4 | verified | Opened: GeoNames via geonamescache (PyPI). London (geonameid 2643743) is at 51.50853 N, 0.12574 W, within about 0.2 km of 51.5074, -0.1278. A 0.2 km shift moves the times by under 1 s. |
| c5 | verified | Opened: IANA tz database (tzdata 2026.5, zoneinfo Europe/London). The UTC offset is 0 from 26 Oct 2026 until 28 Mar 2027 (+1 h on 24 Oct 2026 and 29 Mar 2027), so the clock is GMT throughout 1 Dec - 15 Jan. |

**Result:** disputed: c3 (this blocks publishing). All other claims verified. The fixes are in the notes; demo-builder owns them (board).
