## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess, and a good one: Iceland is the natural pick for someone who has seen the great-circle route on an airline screen; "only open ocean" is the wall-map picture. The hook ("On a wall map, London to LA looks like a hop over the Atlantic. Fly it and look down.") half-gives it away: it says the wall-map route is wrong, so "only open ocean" is out. The question says "Draw the shortest route" but the locked stage draws no route, and shows a polar-looking map centred near the North Pole with London on the right and Los Angeles on the left, a view my beginner has never seen. The caption "Gnomonic map: straight lines are shortest routes. Shows under half the globe." is jargon; the claim in it is the key to the whole lesson and is only a caption.
- Where understanding first broke: the unfamiliar map. London sits at the right of the picture and LA at the lower left, with lines radiating from the pole; I had no sense of where the Atlantic was. Even with the question I could not locate Greenland or Iceland on it without labels (neither is labelled).
- What was SHOWN: sliding "Fly from London" to 3,000 km puts the marker over "Greenland" in the readout BELOW THE ROUTE. Then "Show on Mercator" redraws the same route as a red arch bowing north over southern Greenland (highlighted blue), with a dashed straight line across the Atlantic labelled "DASHED LINE 9,730 km". That Mercator picture is the most helpful frame in the lesson: I can see the shortest route climbing over Greenland while the straight line goes far south.
- The route's own length (8,760 km) appears only in the reveal; the stage shows only the dashed line's 9,730. So the claim that the red arch is shorter than the dashed line is not shown as a number side by side; it is "longer" only in the reveal.
- Reveal vs stage: 9,730 km on the stage; 8,760 km, "compass bearing" and "crosses open Atlantic far to the south" are told (the dashed line on the Mercator picture does cross open water, so that part is visible). "Every great circle comes out straight on a gnomonic map" told.
- Phone legibility: the gnomonic map is legible but the continents are hard to identify and the cropped lower edge cuts off the Gulf; labels are small. The Mercator view is clear. Reveal fully visible in the screenshot (not clipped at the Greenland paragraph; the second paragraph is below).
- Reveal earned: Greenland yes (seen); the reason (great circle) told.
- Problems: (1) the gnomonic map is unreadable for a beginner and unlabelled; (2) the route is not drawn at lock time; (3) the 8,760 vs 9,730 comparison appears only in the reveal; (4) gnomonic, great circle and compass bearing undefined.
- No FATAL.
