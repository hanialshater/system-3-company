# Learner review: nines-without-end
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who remembers basic school arithmetic and everyday life; has never studied the topic (knows decimals and 1/3 = 0.333...; has not met limits; may have heard "0.999... = 1" as a trick).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess, and the options map onto genuine intuitions: "tiny positive gap" (my default), "exactly zero" (the heard-it-somewhere answer), "shrinks but never reaches". The second and third options sound close to each other for a beginner ("never reaches zero" vs "is zero"); the difference only becomes clear in the reveal.
- Where understanding first broke: step 2. I tapped "All of them, forever" after a single "+ one more 9" and the stage snapped to "0.999… = 1 / gap 0" with the caption "With all the nines, the gap is below 0.1, 0.01, 0.001… every one. Only 0 is." Quote: "Only 0 is." That is an argument by assertion. I added one nine, saw the gap shrink by a tenth, then pressed a button that declared the answer. No state showed the gap "running out of room" or the zoom going on without limit; nothing earned the jump from "keeps shrinking" to "is zero". A sceptical adult would say "the button just told me what it wants".
- The part that does work: the zoom. At 0.99 the gap is again "a full tenth of the view" (caption), so no zoom ever makes the gap vanish. That is a good, concrete reason to doubt "tiny positive gap". But it takes more than one zoom to feel, and I had no reason to do more than one.
- The reveal gate opens after "All of them" even if only one nine was added, so a learner can reach the reveal after seeing 0.9 -> 0.99 only.
- Control look: in the final screenshot both "+ one more 9" and "All of them, forever" are greyed and only "Start over" is solid; reads as finished, not dead. The number line also extends right of the dot with no label; I did not know what that part of the line meant.
- Reveal: the first paragraph (gap smaller than every 0.1, 0.01, … so it is 0) is the real argument and is clear. The 1/3 argument ("three times 0.333… is 0.999…") is an extra, quick, and known by many adults; it is the more convincing route for a beginner and it appears only in the last sentence.
- Problems: (1) the jump from "zoom more" to "gap is 0" is asserted by a button; (2) reveal reachable after one zoom; (3) options 2 and 3 are hard to tell apart before the reveal; (4) the stronger 1/3 argument is buried.
- No FATAL: the statements shown are true.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief's objective is the "smaller than every such gap, so zero" argument; that is what the caption and reveal state, but the stage only demonstrates two zooms before the button resolves it. The brief's boundary includes 1/3 = 0.333…, which the lesson uses only at the very end; it could carry more weight for this boundary.
- The misconception (a tiny gap, or a gap that shrinks forever) is covered by options 1 and 3; as flagged, options 2 and 3 blur.
- Claims verified.

Verdict: revise
