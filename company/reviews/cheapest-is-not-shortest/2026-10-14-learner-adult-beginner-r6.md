## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: "the road straight to G costs 10. The other way, S to A to B to G, costs 2 per road. What is the cheapest total cost?" Options 10 / 6 / 3. The question does the sum for me: 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 against 10. Any adult who can add answers 6. "10" catches only someone skimming, and "3" is not a mistake anyone makes. The prediction tests reading, not the misconception (fewest roads = cheapest). The locked stage shows "?" labels, so the stage leaks nothing; the stem does.
- Explore: I tap the towns in order. The line above the map narrates each step ("S settled at 0. G: 0 + 10 = 10; A: 0 + 2 = 2." ... "B settled at 4. G: 4 + 2 = 6 (was 10)."). G's label dropping from 10 to 6 is the one real moment, and it is a good one. The towns turn green as they settle.
- Where understanding first broke: "settle", "label" and "∞" (on B in frame 02). The walkthrough has no frame between the commit and the first tap, so I never saw any definition of them. I tapped in the order the panel gave ("the town with the smallest label") without knowing why that order matters. The towns are not obviously buttons either: they are circles on a map, and only the panel says to tap them.
- Reveal: 10 → 6 and 2 + 2 + 2 were all on stage. "The route with the fewest roads was the dearest" is fair. "Settling the smallest label first is safe: with no negative costs, a route found later can only cost more" is told. On my path the stage showed one instance of it ("Going on to S would cost 16, more than its 0"), and that was about going back to the start, not about a town I had already settled being beaten.
