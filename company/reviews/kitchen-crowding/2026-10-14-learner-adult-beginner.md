# Learner review: kitchen-crowding
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner; believes more workers means proportionally more output; has not heard "diminishing returns".
Screenshots seen: yes

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: 40 / 28 / 20. The question states the rule ("Each added worker adds 2 fewer than the last (8, then 6, then 4…)"), so 28 is arithmetic, not a prediction. A learner who reads it cannot hold the 40 belief; picking 40 means ignoring the question. The hook ("Do four cooks make 40?") sets up a real belief the question then removes.
- Explore: slider to 4 workers stacks +10, +8, +6, +4 = 28 inside a dashed 4 × 10 = 40 outline; adding a 5th adds 2 (30). The shortfall against the dashed outline is a nice picture.
- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: *why* each cook adds less. The stage never shows the oven, the counter, or crowding; the shrinking increments are given, not caused. The reason ("The oven and the counter stay the same size, so each new worker shares them") lives only in the reveal.
- Told-not-shown: the cause (fixed oven) and "worker 6 adds nothing: tops out at 30" (I could add a 6th, but the walkthrough path never did; fine).
- Layout: phone; reveal fits, no clipping. Lots of empty stage above the bar.
- Suggested fix: (1) drop the "adds 2 fewer" rule from the question so the learner actually predicts (e.g. "One cook makes 10 an hour in a one-oven kitchen. Four cooks make about..."); (2) make the fixed input visible and manipulable: an oven/counter graphic that queues cooks, and an "add a second oven" toggle that restores the increments. That would show the cause instead of naming it.

Verdict: revise
