# Learner review (re-review r5): two-colours-enough
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who has heard "four colours are enough for any map"; no proof experience.
Screenshots seen: yes (artifacts/walkthroughs/two-colours-enough/sheet.png)
Verdict: launch

## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: "Colour a page cut by lines", with the hook "How few colours will do?" The question has three options (2 / 3 / 4). Many adults have heard "four colours for any map", so 4 and 3 are real beliefs, and 2 feels too few. The locked stage (a blank page, every readout "?") leaks nothing.
- Explore: "Draw line 1" draws a red dashed line, with CLASH "along line 1" and the caption "neighbours share a colour (red). Flip one side." "Flip one side" turns one side green, and CLASH becomes none. Lines 2 and 3 repeat this: each new line shows its clash only along itself, and each flip clears it, ending with 7 pieces, 2 colours, no clash. The red dashed line marking exactly where the clash is, and then vanishing after one flip, is the mechanism, made visible. The walkthrough learner predicted 2 and watched it hold.
- Reveal: "Each new line made clashes only along itself" was seen. "Flipping... made no new ones, because pieces touching across an older line flipped together" is told, but the picture supports it, since no older line ever turned red. "Works for any number of straight lines" is a generalisation from 3 fixed lines; the parity sentence gives the reason. "Maps with curved borders... up to four" is a fair, told contrast.
- Snag: at each crossing point, opposite pieces share a colour and touch at a corner. The question says "touching pieces always differ" without "along an edge", so a careful beginner looking at the crossing can think the rule is broken. The lines are fixed, so I can't draw my own and test "any lines". In the reveal frame the stage is scrolled and the top is cropped.

## 3. New findings

1. Minor: "touching" should be "sharing a border" (or "along an edge"). At every crossing, same-coloured pieces meet at a point, which contradicts the literal wording.
2. Minor: the learner cannot place their own lines, so "this works for any number of straight lines" rests on the told parity argument and one fixed arrangement. A "random line" button would let them test it.
3. No FATAL. The core idea (each new line's clash is fixed by flipping one side, so 2 colours suffice) comes from the stage.
