## 1. From the artifact (fresh)

- Where understanding first broke, before the prediction: the card title reads "One recipe for sixty", and the question asks "How many different prime recipes can you get?" with options 1 / 2 / 3. The title states the answer in words above the question. A beginner who reads the title has no prediction to make, and "Your prediction held" then means nothing. The hook ("Does where you start matter?") leans the same way.
- Explore: five starts for 60. Each draws a coloured tree, and the shelf collects 2 · 2 · 3 · 5 every time. "Every start ends in 2 · 2 · 3 · 5." is shown, not told, which is good. I only choose the first split, though; the stage makes every split after that for me. So "split any way you like" is true only for the first level.
- "Try 84 instead" opens 84 with its own buttons. On this path I never split it.
- Reveal: "You started 60 as 2 × 30, 3 × 20, 4 × 15, 5 × 12, 6 × 10 ... every entry on your shelf read 2 · 2 · 3 · 5." All of this is on my path. It no longer mentions 84, so it quotes no unvisited state. The general rule ("every whole number above 1 ...") is told, which is fair for a generalisation.

New findings:
- N1 (blocking): the title gives the answer ("One recipe") before the prediction. Retitle so it doesn't give the count away, e.g. "Sixty, split any way you like".
- N2 (minor): only the first split is the learner's choice; the later splits are automatic.
