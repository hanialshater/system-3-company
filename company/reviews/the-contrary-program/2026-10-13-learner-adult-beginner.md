## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: the question is plain once read twice: a checker H says "halts" or "loops forever"; D asks H about D and does the opposite. "Can H be right about D?" is answerable by logic from the text alone, so the choice is easy for a careful reader; the hook ("one of them is out to prove you wrong") already tells me the answer is no. "Yes, if H is clever enough" is the tempting wrong option and is a real guess for a hasty reader. "Halts" is defined inline ("it stops"). The stage shows the three lines of D's code and a "Waiting for H" bar.
- What was SHOWN: I am cast as H. Tapping "H: halts" gives "D loops forever: H was wrong" with a red cross; tapping "H: loops forever" gives "D halts: H was wrong". Both of my answers about D fail in front of me: that is the core moment and it is convincing. Then A ("print hi, stop") and B ("repeat forever: wait") can be judged, and correct answers get ticks. The contrast (ordinary programs can be judged, D cannot) is on the stage.
- Where understanding first broke: the reveal's second paragraph. "What cannot exist is one checker that is right about every program, because from any such H we could build its own D." On the stage, D is simply given as something that asks H; nothing shows that a D can be built for any H, so a beginner can think "fine, H just has to be careful around D-type programs". "Turing proved this in 1936: the halting problem cannot be solved by any program" is told. Also, "H" on the stage is me, a person, not a program, so "No cleverness inside H helps" is asserted.
- Reveal: "On D you said halts and D looped; you said loops and D stopped" matches the stage; "On A and B your verdict was right" matches the ticks (in the transcript path I got A wrong first and B wrong first, so the reveal's "your verdict was right" is true only of the final answers; the stage shows red crosses on A halts-loops and B halts, which is mildly inconsistent).
- Reveal earned: the D-defeats-this-H point yes; the "no program can ever" generalisation no.
- Problems: (1) hook and "out to prove you wrong" leak the answer; (2) the step from "this D beats H" to "no H exists" is told; (3) reveal says my verdicts on A and B were right while the stage recorded my wrong first taps; (4) "program that predicts other programs" is abstract, no everyday anchor.
