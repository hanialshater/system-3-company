## Q1

Q1 ruling: **B (strict).** `held` needs two right answers at least 7 days apart, and both must come after the latest wrong counted recall. A wrong answer resets the pair. It does not reset `recalled`, which comes back on the next right answer.

Why B over A:
1. **The wrong answer is evidence against the span.** Take the draft's own row: right on day 1, wrong on day 4, right on day 11. A shows "Recalled twice, 10 days apart". Each word is true, but the sentence is shown as a *state*, and a learner reads it as "this stayed with me for 10 days". The day-4 answer shows that it did not stay. That is the overclaim AMD-008 M7 forbids ("never more" than was measured) and the one EDD-009 names as its own would-be-wrong (a).
2. **A wrong recall teaches the answer again.** `RevisitCard` (Feed.tsx 279-283) shows the right option and the `why` line after a wrong pick. So the day-11 right answer is 7 days after the learner was last *shown* the answer, not 10 days after they last *knew* it. The honest span starts after the re-teaching, and the strict rule measures it that way.
3. **The draft's cost argument mostly goes away on the real schedule.** `revisitDue` waits `REVISIT_SCHEDULE_DAYS[successes]` after the last recall, and a wrong answer does not add to `successes`. Played through (day 1 right, day 4 wrong): next due day 7, then day 14. A gives `held` on day 14 (13 days from 1 to 14); B gives `held` on day 14 (7 days from 7 to 14). Same day. B costs a learner time only when the slip comes late (see finding F2).
4. **It is not a loss mechanic (M3).** The state drops only because a recall was answered wrong, never because time passed, and the words stay neutral ("worth another look", then "Recalled N days later"). B does not make a drop more likely than A does. It only stops `held` coming back on a pair that the wrong answer splits.

Rests on: AMD-008 **M7** (claim only what was measured), with **M3** satisfied (only a wrong recall lowers, and at once). M1's default `held` ("right on two spaced revisits at least 7 days apart") is silent on a wrong answer in between, so it allows either A or B. This ruling picks the reading that claims less.

## Q3

Q3 ruling: **keep both, with one rule each.** (a) `held` is an acceptable internal id **only while it never reaches learner copy**: no rendered string, aria-label, tooltip, share text or badge may contain "held", "mastered" or "mastery". To a learner, "held" states lasting possession, which nothing here measured. (b) "Recalled twice, {D} days apart" is the right learner sentence, with D computed on `R′` (Q1). It names two events and the gap between them, and that is exactly what was measured. "Twice" refers to the pair that sets D. When `R′` has three or more right answers the sentence says less than happened, but nothing false. I reject a "{k} times" count: it is a number that rises with visits, the shape Q4.2 checks for ("a score to defend") and AMD-008 M5 keeps away from volume.

Rests on: AMD-008 **M7** (a mastery message says what was measured, never more; "You've mastered physics" is M7's own counter-example) and **X2/Q3.4** (the sentence is a recorded claim, true by construction from `R′`). The growth-designer co-owns Q3 and may reword it within (a) and (b). A rewording comes back here only if it adds a word that claims retention ("keep", "know", "remember for").

## Findings outside the two questions (problems, not fixes; for learner-model-engineer to answer in the EDD)

- **F1. "Recalled N days later" after a wrong answer stretches the gap.** N is counted from `completedAt`, but a wrong recall re-teaches the answer (point 2 above). In fixture Q1-a the learner last saw the answer on day 4, and the copy says 11 days. The sentence is literally true ("11 days after playing"), but a learner reads "later" as "later than I last saw it". This is the same principle as Q1, applied to the `recalled` row. I have not ruled on it, because the brief asks for Q1 and Q3 only. If the learner-model-engineer agrees, the fix stays inside existing data (count N from the latest wrong `at` when there is one), and fixture Q1-a's words would become "Recalled 7 days later". Owner: learner-model-engineer, with the growth-designer for the words.
- **F2. Under B, a late slip can cap a lesson at `recalled` for good.** `revisitDue` stops when `successes ≥ 4`, and wrong answers do not reset `successes`. Example: right on days 1, 4 and 11 (`held`), then wrong on day 32. The next revisit is day 53, and if it is right, no further revisit is ever scheduled. `R′` = {53}, so the lesson shows "Recalled 53 days later" from then on, and it can never be `held` again. That sentence is true and it is not guilt copy, so this does not change the ruling. It is a schedule question (should a wrong answer earn the learner further revisits?), and it belongs to whoever owns `revisitDue`'s schedule (learner-model-engineer; `src/kit/progress.ts` is protected, so any change there needs an amendment). It needs no new field.

## Confidence

- Q1 = B: about 80%. The strongest case for A is the cost to a learner of one slip. On the real schedule that cost is zero for an early slip and real only for a late one (F2), and a late slip is when a "10 days apart" sentence would mislead most.
- Q3: about 70% that the sentence survives as written. The doubt is how learners read it, not whether it is true. Q4.2 (learner-evaluator) is the test that would show it reads as a score.
