# Learner review (round 2): below-the-threshold-crash
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult beginner who knows endangered species can recover with protection; assumes a small population just grows back more slowly.
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 7 frames, phone viewport). Re-review after the kit fix for clipped reveals (W-135); written fresh from this walkthrough.

## 1. From the artifact

- Where understanding first broke: the question gives the answer. "Animals need a group of at least 50 to find mates; below that, numbers fall. Two groups start with 40 and 60 ... After many years:" With the rule stated, "40 dies out; 60 grows" is read off the sentence. The misconception the hook raises ("Do small groups always grow back?") cannot be committed to by a learner who reads the stem; choosing "Both grow to 1,000" (the walkthrough's pick) means not reading, not believing.
- Explore: releasing 40 draws a red line sinking to ✕ ("dies out ... by year 54"); 60 climbs green to 1,000; 20 and 80 confirm. The dashed "50: mates found" line on a log scale splits the fates cleanly. A strong picture of a threshold.
- Reveal: lists 20, 40, 60, 80 with their outcomes, all of which I released. No unvisited values. "Small does not always mean slow recovery: below the threshold it means none" is the right line.
- Reveal frame mismatch: on my path I tapped "Clear" last, so when the reveal opens the chart is empty while the text describes four lines. Minor, path-dependent.
- Phone clipping (the reason for round 2): fixed. Full reveal including the "Toy model" caveat and trust link visible.
- Reveal earned: yes for the two fates; but the prediction never tested my belief.

Fixes (owner: builder):
1. Hide the mechanism from the stem: e.g. "These animals need mates nearby to breed. Two groups start with 40 and 60 ... After many years:" and let the dashed 50 line appear only after the commit (it is also on the locked stage).
2. Optionally keep the drawn lines when the reveal opens even if the learner cleared them, or redraw 40 and 60.

- Misconception: "any population, however small, will grow back if left alone". The stem states the opposite rule, so the prediction tests reading. The explore and reveal deliver the objective (40 shrinks, 60 grows to 1,000) well.
- The recall question has the same structure (rule given, consequence asked), so it also tests reading of the rule more than the idea.
