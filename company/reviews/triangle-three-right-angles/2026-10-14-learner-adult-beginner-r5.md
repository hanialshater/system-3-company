## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: "A triangle drawn on the Earth", with the hook "You learned a triangle's angles add to 180°. Draw one big enough on a globe and measure." The question (North Pole, 0°,0°, 90°E,0°) is concrete. The four options span the 180° belief up to 360°. The locked stage shows the three corner dots on a globe (the third at 60°E, not the question's 90°E), with no triangle and "?" readouts. Nothing leaks. Two lines of small print, though ("Excess = angle sum − 180°, in radians. Excess × R²... is the area."), use "radians" and "R²", which are beyond an adult who remembers school maths only loosely.
- Explore: the panel says "Slide the third corner east along the equator to 90°E." I do, and a shaded triangle appears with three "90°" labels: ANGLE SUM 270°, AREA 63.8 M km² · 12.5% of Earth, EXCESS × R² 63.8 M km². Seeing three right angles on one triangle is a striking, honest picture, and the 180° belief breaks on stage. But the gate opens after that single move, and the panel told me exactly where to go. So I saw exactly one triangle, the answer. I never saw a smaller one, so I never saw the sum change with size.
- Reveal: "the walk turns 90° three times and is home" adds a nice second reading of the picture. "On a ball the sides bulge outward, and the angles add up to more" is told; the picture supports it. "Excess in radians × R² is exactly the area" was shown only as two equal numbers at one setting. One coincidence is not a rule, and "radians" is never explained. "Tiny triangles have almost no excess" is told; I never made a tiny one.

## 2. Earlier findings (2026-10-13 review, v1, verdict revise)

1. The question gives the answer away by its own geometry: **not fixed**, now minor. A reader who pictures the pole-to-equator walk can deduce 270°. Many beginners won't, and the walkthrough learner chose 180°.
2. The locked stage showed a different triangle (60°E) and an area before the question's case: **fixed for the leak**. The readouts are now "?" and no triangle is drawn. The third-corner dot still starts at 60°E, which is harmless.
3. "EXCESS (RAD) × R²" unreadable: **partly fixed**. The tile is now "EXCESS × R²", with a two-line caption, but the caption still leans on "radians" and "R²" undefined.
4. Area rule, small triangles and 510 M km² told: **not fixed**. This is still the top finding: the gated path shows one triangle only.
5. Reveal clipped: **fixed**.

## 3. New findings

1. **Revise:** the second idea (excess measures area, and small triangles have almost none) rests on a single slider position, uses radians without explanation, and is told in the reveal. The panel and gate send the learner straight to 90°E and stop there. Options: require one more position (for example 30°E, where the sum is 210° and the area is a third as big), so the sum visibly tracks the size; or cut the excess/area paragraph and the radians small print for this boundary. The builder chooses.
2. Minor: the panel names the destination ("to 90°E"), so exploring amounts to following an instruction. "Slide the third corner east and watch the angle sum" would leave the discovery to the learner.
3. Minor: "EXCESS × R²" next to "AREA" with the same number is opaque to this boundary (no idea what R² is in km²).
4. Routed, not mine: the trust link reads 4/5 claims checked (fact-checker); the 70 s chip (product-owner).
5. No FATAL. The headline idea (a big triangle on a globe can have three right angles, 270°) comes from the stage.
