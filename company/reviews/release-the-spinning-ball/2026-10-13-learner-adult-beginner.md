Boundary: an adult who remembers basic school arithmetic and everyday life; has never studied physics beyond school.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess and a very good trap. Anyone who has swung a bucket or a conker on a string feels it pull outward, so "straight east, away from the centre" is the natural answer; "curving round" is the second most common. The setup is clear: a top-view circle, arrows showing the direction, a compass N and E, and the ball marked at the east point. "Anticlockwise" is stated and the arrows show it.
- Explore: three cuts (east, north-west, south) each leave a dotted straight trail that stays on screen: north, south-west, east. The trails remain, so by the third cut I can see three straight lines leaving the circle, each along the edge, none pointing outward. The compass-point slider and the "Cut the string" button are simple to use. This is strong, direct evidence that earns the reveal.
- Where understanding first broke: nowhere sharply in the explore. The reveal's "Nothing ever flings it outward" runs against my own bodily memory of the outward pull when I whirl a bucket; the lesson never names or explains that feeling, so I could accept the screen and still think "but it does feel like it's pulled out".
- Reveal earned? Yes for "the ball leaves along the edge, in a straight line": three trails on screen show it. The explanation ("the string pulls toward the centre, bending its path; cut it and no sideways force is left") is told, not shown: there is no arrow for the string's pull.
- Problems:
  - No force arrow: the string's pull toward the centre, the reveal's key claim, is only in words.
  - The outward-pull feeling of whirling something is not addressed, so the likely residual misconception survives under the reveal.
  - "Anticlockwise" plus compass letters plus "moving north" is three frames at once; a learner who mixes up clockwise has to rely on the arrows.
  - The ball sits still in the predict screenshot; whether it whirls before the cut (and how fast) cannot be told from the stills, so "keeps its speed" has no visible speed.
  - The final screen shows the SE point with the button live again, after the cuts, with a caption that contradicts nothing but also confirms nothing ("Top view, frictionless table. Ball at the south-east point"): the last thing I see before the reveal is a position, not the outcome.
- FATAL: none found from the artifact.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief's misconception matches my prediction exactly, and the three persisting trails are a sharp answer to it. The objective's first half (the string only pulls inward) is the less-shown half.
- Of the lessons I reviewed in this batch, this one needed the fewest reservations; the main reservations are the missing force arrow and the unaddressed outward feeling.

Verdict: launch
