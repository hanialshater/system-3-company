# Build note: iss-gravity-still-there

builder: demo-builder · 2026-10-13 · for evaluators to read after their findings

- Design intent: Earth and orbits on one true scale out to geostationary height (Earth is 17 px in radius), so the ISS orbit visibly hugs the planet. A gravity bar (100% = ground) and a readout show (R / (R + h))^2.
- Height buttons instead of the brief's log slider: 0, 400, 6,371, 20,200, 35,786 km. Buttons put both key states on the QA path (QA moves a slider only two steps). The current height's button is disabled.
- Earned reveal: milestones 'iss' (400 km) and 'far' (20,200 km or more). Bar and readout hidden until the prediction.
- After screenshots: a gravity arrow at the satellite was either longer than Earth's radius (ground) or only an arrowhead (geostationary). Replaced with the bar under the picture.
- Geostationary altitude 35,786 km is measured from the equatorial radius (6,378 km); the stage uses R = 6371 km throughout, a 0.02% difference in r (noted in c3).

## Pre-flight v3

Changes for r6 (adult-beginner re-review, revise). The top finding was that free fall was illegible at phone size. The zoomed-in view (ground and ISS height) now keeps Earth's radius and every angle true but stretches height above the ground about 6×, so the 400 km gap is 30 px. The stage says so: "Heights stretched 6×" (c6 reopened). Every throw stays drawn, with the current one solid and earlier ones faded, and a slow throw ends at a red ×. The station is a hollow 14 px square with the ball as a filled dot inside it, and the key reads "□ station · ● ball · × hit ground". The readout now says "0 min: falling, hits ground at 5.5 min" right after a slow throw, and "hit the ground 961 km away" once stepped there. That answers the "should fix": the reveal's "hit the ground" now matches the readout. The height buttons now come before the throw buttons in DOM order, so the QA path ends on the ISS picture with all three paths. A "Seen:" row keeps the far stops' values. The reveal quotes only far stops that were visited. Not built, typechecked or QA'd (batch instruction). Status was already "review" before this revision. Whether it should drop back to draft until QA re-runs is a QA-tester / product-owner call, and I did not change it.

1. **Title and hook.** "Is there gravity on the space station?" / "Astronauts float on the ISS. So gravity must be nearly gone up there. Is it?" No: the hook voices the misconception and gives no figure.
2. **Options.** "0% (none)", "90%", "50%": all percentages, same precision.
3. **Locked stage.** Ground stop, zoomed-in Earth with the ISS ring, gauge background with no fill, "gravity ?", readouts "?" and "–". No value or line matches 90%.
4. **Reveal values.** 88.5% at 400 km: (b) milestone iss; the gauge and readout at the final ISS stop. "5.7% at GPS height, 2.3% at geostationary": (a) only the far stops in `seen.stops`, which is on the required path (milestone far), and they stay on the "Seen:" row. 25.0% one radius up: (a) printed only if stop 2 was visited, and it is on the "Seen:" row. "Your 3 km/s and 7 km/s throws … hit the ground (the × marks)": (a) `seen.throws`, with milestone fell-short guaranteeing at least one; the faded paths and × marks stay drawn at the ISS stop. "7.67 km/s … kept missing": (b) milestone orbit-throw; its path is the solid ring.
5. **QA path.** 0 km (disabled), 400 km (iss), 6,371 km, 20,200 km (far), 35,786 km, Throw 3 km/s (fell-short; jumps to the ISS stop), 7 km/s, 7.67 km/s (orbit-throw), +3 min (station and ball move together). All four milestones; no animation. When the reveal opens, the ISS picture shows all three paths, both × marks, and the "Seen:" row with 25.0%, 5.7% and 2.3%.
6. **Reset.** There is no Reset control. Reveal on the QA path (traced from code, not run): "At 400 km the gravity bar barely shrank: 88.5% of its ground value. Only far out did it fade: 5.7% at GPS height, 2.3% at geostationary. / One Earth radius up you were twice as far from Earth's centre, and gravity read 25.0%: a quarter. It weakens with the square of that distance, and 400 km adds only 6% to it. / Your 3 km/s and 7 km/s throws curved down and hit the ground (the × marks). The 7.67 km/s throw fell just as hard but kept missing the ground, on the station's orbit. Station and astronauts move at that speed, so they fall together. That is why they float." Matches the final stage.
