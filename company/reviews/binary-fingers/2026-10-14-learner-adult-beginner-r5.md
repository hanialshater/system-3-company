# Learner review (re-review r5): binary-fingers
lesson version: v2
boundary: adult beginner; knows base-ten place value and doubling; counts on fingers to ten; has never met binary.
Screenshots seen: yes (artifacts/walkthroughs/binary-fingers/sheet.png, 13 frames)
Verdict: revise

## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: the locked stage now shows ten blank fingers with no values, and the hook is neutral. "10" is an honest gut answer and "100" a plausible middle. This is a real prediction now.
- Explore: the first tap reveals the values (512 ... 1) and the running sum ("768 = 512 + 256", later "8 fingers up: 1020"). Ten up gives 1023 and "All down" gives 0. It is tactile and quickly kills "10" and "100": one finger alone shows 512.
- **Where it loses me (main finding):** the question is how *many different* numbers, but the stage only shows the biggest. The step "every number from 0 to 1023 has exactly one pattern, so 1023 + 1 = 1024" is the whole answer, and it is told. Along my path I made 512, 768, 896 ... 1023: big jumps with huge gaps between them. Nothing on the stage shows that 5, or 700, or 1000 can be made, or that no two patterns give the same number. A careful beginner can reasonably think "max is 1023, but surely some numbers are skipped".
- "Adding a finger doubles the count" is a good argument, but it appears only in the reveal text.
- "You made 10 different numbers on the way" matches my taps, but it rather supports my wrong answer of 10.
- Layout: a large empty band under the controls on every explore frame.

## 3. New findings

1. Main: give the stage a way to fill the range. Options: a "+1" button that ticks the fingers through 0, 1, 2, 3 ...; a target ("make 5", "make 700") before the gate opens; or a live "patterns so far: 2, 4, 8 ..." counter as fingers become available. Any of these would show coverage rather than tell it.
2. Minor: use the empty band for that counter or for a short row of reachable numbers.
