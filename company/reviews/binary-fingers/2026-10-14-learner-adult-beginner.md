# Learner review: binary-fingers
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner; knows base-ten place value and doubling; has never met binary.
Screenshots seen: yes

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: 10 / 100 / 1024. The finger values (512 ... 1) are already printed on the locked stage, so a quick learner can add them up; still, "10" was my honest gut.
- Explore: raising fingers left to right builds a running sum (512, 768, ... 1023). Clear and satisfying; the sum readout "512 + 256 + 128" is shown, then switches to "6 fingers up: 1008" for the rest.
- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: the reveal. The question was "how many different numbers", but the stage only showed me the biggest (1023). The jump from "max is 1023" to "every number from 0 to 1023 has exactly one pattern" is the whole lesson, and it is told. I never saw that, say, 5 or 700 can be made, or that no two patterns collide.
- Told-not-shown: "Adding a finger doubles the count" (down = all old numbers, up = all of them again) is a nice argument but only in prose. "You made 10 different numbers on the way" is a weak substitute for seeing the range filled.
- Layout: phone; reveal fits, no clipping. Large empty stage under the controls.
- Suggested fix: add a target ("make 5", "make 700") or a "+1" counter that ticks the fingers through 0, 1, 2, 3 ... so the learner sees every number gets a pattern; or show the count doubling as fingers are added (1 finger: 2 numbers, 2 fingers: 4 ...).

Verdict: revise
