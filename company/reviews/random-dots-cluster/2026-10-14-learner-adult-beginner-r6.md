# Learner review (re-review r6): random-dots-cluster
lesson version: v2
boundary: adult-beginner. Thinks "random" means evenly spread; does not know how often pure chance makes lumps (meta.json learner field).
Screenshots seen: yes (artifacts/walkthroughs/random-dots-cluster/sheet.png, all 5 frames)
Verdict: launch

## 1. From the artifact

- Hook and predict: "40 random dots. Any crowded squares?" / "Will every square stay almost empty?" sets the tension, and "about 15%" is the natural low guess. The locked grid is empty and the tallies read "?", so nothing leaks.
- Explore: "Drop 40 dots" scatters a batch, rings any square with 3 or more and prints the count in it (here a "4"). "Drop 10 more batches" and "Drop 100 more" grow the tally: 1 of 1, 6 of 11, 60 of 111. The learner watches about half the scatters come up crowded. The answer, about 56%, is reached on stage by the learner's own tally.
- **Where I wobble (moderate): "crowded" is never anchored.** 3 dots in one square only reads as a lump if you know the average is 0.4 per square (40 over 100), and the stage never says or shows that. A beginner looking at the scatters sees "a square with 3 dots" next to plenty of neighbours with 1 or 2. Without the 0.4 anchor the ringed square looks a bit unremarkable, and the take-away "lumps are ordinary" lands softer than it could.
- The why behind the frequency (with 100 squares there are many chances for one of them to collect 3) is not on stage or in the reveal. The reveal states the consequence ("lumps are an ordinary part of random scatter", "weak evidence that something attracts the dots") and points at the tally, not at a reason.
- Reveal: it matches the tally (60 of 111, against 55.5% exact) and is short.
- Minor: the grid is small on a phone, with squares of about 16 px. The count digit inside a ringed square is hard to read, and two dots in one square can look like one.

## 2. Earlier findings (v1 review, 2026-10-14)

1. The ringed square's 3 dots overlapped into one blob, so "crowded" did not look crowded: **fixed**. Each ringed square now carries its count ("3", "4"), so the learner can read it even where dots overlap. The digit is small (minor, above).
2. Dots on grid lines and the outer edge, so squares could not be counted: **partly fixed**. Most dots now sit inside cells, but in frames 02 and 03 a few still sit on the grid's outer edge or just outside it.
3. "An evenly spread pattern is the unusual one" was told with no contrast shown: **fixed**. The reveal now compares the two kinds of batch (44.5% vs about half), and both kinds were seen in the tally.
4. "Not evidence" was stronger than the stage supports: **fixed** ("A lump on its own is weak evidence").

## 3. After reading the brief

- The doesNotKnow item ("how often pure chance makes lumps") is the tally the learner builds: 60 of 111. That meets "Show the why you promised" for what the brief promises. The deeper reason (many squares, many chances) is not in the brief's gap, so it does not block.
- The misconception ("no spot gets more than a couple") is answered by the ringed "3" and "4" squares. The 0.4-per-square anchor would make it land harder (moderate, above).
- Minors: small digits and grid, a few edge dots. No FATAL.
