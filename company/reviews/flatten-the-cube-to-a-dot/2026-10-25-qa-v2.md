## 4. W-2065: is "thumb" at 1280 a real defect?

**Verdict: a quirk of the tool, not a defect in the lesson.** Evidence measured at the 1280 reveal:
- The stage is 438 px wide. The main picture is the 300x174 svg, drawn 340x165 with `data-reveal-fit="197"` (the card shrank it on purpose, 84 percent of 197). reveal-fit skips fitted svgs by design, so it keeps looking and picks the next largest svg with a viewBox: the "total after each removal" strip, `viewBox 0 0 284 20`, `width=284 height=20`, `aria-hidden`.
- reveal-fit then compares the strip's scale with `(stageWidth - 20) / 284` = 1.47. The strip is a fixed-size 284 px readout (CSS `max-width:100%`, never scaled up), drawn at 1.0, so 0.68 and under 200 px: "thumb". At 390 the stage is narrower, and the main picture is not reveal-fitted there, so the tool measures the main picture (167 px at 1.07), so nothing flags (by the tool's arithmetic the strip flags whenever the stage is wider than about 400 px, the 0.75 threshold).
- The screenshot (`artifacts/explore-ftc/D-reveal-1280.png`, viewed) shows the whole stage visible (417 of 417 px), the 12-cell strip legible at about 284 px, the dot-and-square picture and the equation readable, no crop. Nothing is squeezed by height. The tool's own words for thumb ("height-squeezed into a thumbnail") do not describe this.
- The flag is the same on v1 (board note), so it is not a v2 regression.

Proposal (not done; I may not edit tools): reveal-fit should ignore svgs that are `aria-hidden`, under 60 px tall, or fixed-size (explicit `width` attribute), and should not fall back to a smaller svg when the largest one carries `data-reveal-fit`. Owner platform-engineer. Until then the 1280 `pass: 0` for this lesson should be read as a false positive; the gate evidence is this record.

One real reading from the same output: at the Shrink reveal the "picture" is just a dot and a square in a 165 px svg, with empty space around it. That is the true end state, not a defect (O3).
