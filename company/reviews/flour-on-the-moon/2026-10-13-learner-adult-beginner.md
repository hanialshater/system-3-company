## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess, but a vocabulary trap rather than a physics trap. In everyday speech "weight" is what the bathroom scale says in kg, so for me "mass" and "weight" are the same word. I pick "its mass" or "both" without any idea there are two quantities. The question tests whether I know a school definition I may never have been taught.
- Explore: tapping Earth / Moon / Mars / Jupiter changes the spring scale and gravity numbers and leaves the balance "level". The contrast between a moving spring and a level balance is a clear, repeatable demonstration.
- Where understanding first broke: the two readouts SPRING SCALE "3.71 N" and GRAVITY "3.71 m/s²" show the same digits on every world. As a beginner I would read them as one thing measured twice; I do not know what N or m/s² mean, and the coincidence of numbers (true only for a 1 kg bag) hides that they are different quantities.
- Reveal earned? Mostly yes for "something changed (the spring) and something did not (the balance)". "The balance ... compares the flour with a 1 kg mass and gravity pulls both equally" is told, not shown: the picture shows a flour pan and a 1 kg block but nothing that makes me see the two pulls cancel. The reveal's formula W = m g arrives with no worked number.
- Problems:
  - The caption "On Moon: the spring changed. The balance did not." is the same sentence on every world, so it is more a label than an observation.
  - The last line, "A bathroom scale is a spring scale: on the Moon it would read about a sixth", clashes with the main message that 1 kg stays 1 kg: my own scale says "kg", so I am told my "kg" reading would be a sixth after I was told mass stays 1 kg. The step from "scale shows kg" to "scale really measures a pull" is missing.
  - "Weight" is defined in the reveal as "how hard a world pulls on the flour", but the everyday use of the word (what the scale says in kg) is never named as the source of the confusion.
  - Newtons are never explained (is 9.81 N heavy?), and the title asks about "kg" while the spring is calibrated in N, so there is no common unit to compare Earth's reading with the 1 kg label.
  - The explore panel invites me to "watch both instruments" but only the spring ever moves; the learner may stop looking at the balance, which is where the claim "mass stays the same" is carried.
- FATAL: none found from the artifact.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief names the misconception exactly as I experienced it (mass and weight are one word). The lesson's response, two instruments side by side, fits it; the weak points are the units (the brief itself says newtons are unknown) and the bathroom-scale line.
- Intent includes "a spring scale measures weight while a balance compares masses"; the caption in step 4 says it in one line, and the reveal repeats it. Shown by the screen, but stated rather than discovered.
