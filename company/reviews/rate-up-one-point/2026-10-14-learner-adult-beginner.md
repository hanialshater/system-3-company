- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: the prediction, as intended. "4% to 5% is up 1%" is exactly what I'd say. The question "by what percent did the rate itself rise" is clear enough to make me hesitate.
- Explore shows it very well: two bars built of 1-point slices; the new slice is red with "+1 pt". At 4 → 5 the red slice is one of four, at 1 → 2 it doubles the bar, at 8 → 9 it is a sliver. The same red block looking big or small depending on the bar under it is the whole idea, shown.
- Tiles CHANGE IN POINTS (+1 every time) vs CHANGE IN PERCENT (+100/+50/+25/+12.5%) nail the contrast.
- Told-not-shown: "careful writers say 'one percentage point'" is told; fine as a usage note.
- Layout: the stage resizes between frames (the 8% frame is taller, the reveal shrinks it to a tiny chart whose labels are unreadable). No reveal clipping; reveal text fits.
- Hook says "That is a 1% rise." as a flat statement before "Or is it?". Harmless rhetoric, but it is a false sentence on screen.
