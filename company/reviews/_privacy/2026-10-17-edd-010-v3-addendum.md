## Q1. `passed-known`, and the lesson-to-lesson comparison

### The facts and where they come from

Facts: `finishedOnPassedConcepts`, the slugs of the learner's own finished pilot lessons on the concepts of the cards that the moved card passed (§16.2). Slugs only, no number. That is Q-P5 class (a) (a named lesson the learner completed) and, through the glue's count, class (b). The relation "it moved ahead of other lessons" is a catalog position, not a fact about the learner. The learner's own history appears in the facts only as completions they can already see in the feed. Nothing here is a tally of predictions, a probability, a trait, a gap in days, or a comparison to other learners. Those were what Q-P5 refused, and none is present.

### Ruling on the copy shape

- **"you've finished lessons on the ideas it moved ahead of" / "moved ahead of some lessons on ideas you have already finished": allowed.** It states completions (class a/b) next to a relative position among "some lessons" (the frame §16.1 already fixes). It claims no ability ("you know", denied), no absolute position, no control and no choice.
- **"needs fewer unfinished lessons first than some it moved ahead of": allowed, in this form only.** It compares **two lessons** by catalog structure (their prerequisites) and by the learner's completions. It does not compare the learner with anyone, and it grades nothing. Q-P5's ceiling is about what a card says of the learner (performance, ability, comparison to others, pressure). A comparison between lessons is catalog-relation class (c). The privacy risk in the sentence is only that "unfinished" could read as a deficit. The condition below keeps the lesson, not the learner, as the subject.

### Conditions

- **V1. Slots, not facts.** `passed-known` adds exactly one key, `earlier:passed-known`, to the key set that `reason-copy.ts` must cover (C7f, T6, T18: **seven** keys, not six; the EDD's "six keys" text is stale after §16.2). The copy layer still receives only `{ key, title?, count?, concept? }` (E10). `finishedOnPassedConcepts` reaches the copy layer only as titles of the learner's **own finished** lessons and as a `count` the glue computes from progress. The titles of the **passed** (unfinished) cards, or of any other row, are not a slot. Test: T23's allow-list stays the four slots, and a fixture proves a line that interpolates a passed card's title fails.
- **V2. Whitelist enforced on the new field.** T24b's static scan adds `finishedOnPassedConcepts` and `versus` to the reads the glue may make only through the whitelist mapping. It also forbids the glue reading `recallsRight`, `recallsWrong`, `pKnown` and any other numeric field from a move or reason object. (The unfrozen v3 still declares `recallsRight`, `recallsWrong` and `pKnown` in its `ReasonFacts` type on `concept-known`, lines 49, 171 and 172. §15.1 says v3 is ungraded, so I expect zeros or removal. Asking the builder: remove them from the move output, so there is nothing to leak. A recommendation, not a condition. If they stay, V2's scan is what keeps them out.)
- **V3. Comparison copy has no quantity and the lesson is the subject.** The "fewer unfinished lessons first" line may not carry a number or a quantity word ("2 fewer", "half as many", "much fewer", "far fewer"), must say "some" (N1 form), and must be about the lesson ("it needs fewer unfinished lessons first"), not about the learner ("you have fewer to do", "you are behind on"). The deny-list in T23 gains "unfinished" **except** in this exact frame, plus "fewer" followed by a digit or a quantity word. A different use of "unfinished" fails. The learner-facing meaning of "unfinished" is "the learner has not completed it", which is a completion fact, so the allow is a narrow one.
- **V4. Ceiling statements stay in force for the new code.** Nothing in a `passed-known` line may say or imply: what the learner knows, is good at, "already mastered", a count of right or wrong predictions, a time gap, that the order was chosen or can be changed (SS3), or an absolute position ("earlier than usual", "your Nth card"; §16.1). "already finished" is a completion fact and is allowed. "already know" is denied. Test: T23 deny-list as in the design read, applied to the new key's line.
- **V5. Truth stays with F7 and RC; I set only the ceiling.** I have not judged whether `passed-known` is causal for each card (that is F7's truth test, §16.4 item 5), and my ruling does not say the line is true. If F7 blocks the code, there is no privacy fallback to offer: a line that rests on a non-completion fact or on a prediction result is over my ceiling (Q-P5). I cannot propose a fifth code; a refusal here would close the website part for M1 by §15.1, and I do not ask anyone to relax G7.

Confidence that `passed-known` copy can be written inside V1 to V4: high. Confidence that the first build meets V1 to V5 without a finding: about 70%, since the glue and the copy do not exist yet.

## Q2. The T4 / Q-P7 control reverses for reachable-v3

**Confirmed.** My design read asked for the control "flipping one `predictedCorrectly` must change the order" (Q-P7), because v2 reads predictions. v3 is ungraded (§15.1 (ii) 1; the unfrozen header says "only `slug` and `completedAt` are read; never `predictedCorrectly`, never `recalls`"). For v3 the right control is the opposite, and that is a **better** privacy state: fewer learner fields are read, and the one that was a performance signal (right or wrong) no longer moves anything. The reversal is accepted as the new Q-P7 condition for a flag-on build, and it does not weaken any ruling I have made.

Conditions (all in `npm run check`, in T4):

- **V6. The reversal, stated as a test.** For the candidate named `reachable-v3`: flipping `predictedCorrectly` on any history item (true, false, `null`, absent: all four values pairwise), and adding, removing or flipping any recall, leaves **the display list, every reason line, `moves` (every entry and every fact) and the memo key** byte-identical. Changing one `completedAt`, or adding or removing a completion, **does** change the order (positive control, so the test cannot be blind to everything). The test reads which control to apply from the imported candidate's `name`, as EDD §12 A2 says. While the flag-off merge carries v2, v2's control applies (flipping a prediction changes the order), and a flag-on build refuses v2 (T18).
- **V7. Do not hand v3 what it must not read.** Minimisation: the glue builds `history` for v3 with `slug` and `completedAt` only. If the candidate's type still requires `predictedCorrectly` and `recalls`, the glue passes inert placeholders (not the learner's values), and T4 asserts that the learner's values never appear in the call arguments (spy on the candidate). This is stronger than "v3 ignores them", and it keeps the property true if a later v3 edit starts to read a field. A recommendation, becomes a condition if the type allows it.
- **V8. The memo key follows.** C8's memo key is `completedAt` per pilot slug for v3 (as EDD says). A test shows that a recall or prediction write to `s3c:progress:v1` does not invalidate it. Reason: it also proves that no code path recomputes on a graded field.

Remaining question I noticed and do not rule on: v3's `refresh` code depends on how long ago a lesson was finished (`completedAt` against the single `now`). The order input is allowed (a single `now`, N3). The **copy** for `refresh` is exposed to the time-gap ban in E10, as §15.1 already says; F7 and RC decide it. A line that cannot be written without a gap in days or "a while ago" fails E10, and I do not relax that.

## Q3. The on-device `shown` list under Q-P1

**Ruling: acceptable. It is the same class as the pins and reason lines in Q-P1 (E1): page-life memory, not stored, not sent.**

What I checked: §16.5 item 1 says `shown` is slugs the display has already shown (pinned slots before `h`), a set, absent equals `[]`, and `rank` never reads it. The pins `{ slug, line }` and `h` already live in a ref (EDD C7d/C7e, E1). `shown` is the slug half of those pins handed to `moves` as an argument. It adds no new datum, no storage and no request, and it carries no time or dwell value (N3 forbids those; the `shown` set has neither). The order of cards `rank` returns is unchanged by it (§16.1: byte-identical).

Two things I watched for and found no problem with: (1) `shown` depends on scroll depth, which is behaviour. But the pin set already does (C7b/C7c) and `rank` does not read it; `moves` uses it only to decide which codes and directions apply to the unshown cards. So scroll depth reaches the order only through the glue's existing pin, and reaches `moves` only as "these slugs are no longer ahead". (2) The fact that a card was shown could be inferred by anyone watching the slug-bearing requests (Q-P2). That residual exists today for `/api/meta` and beacons, and `shown` does not add a request (E4).

Conditions:

- **V9. Not a second store.** `shown` is derived from the pin ref each time `moves` is called (a parameter), or is the pin ref itself. It is not a separate persisted set, and it is never in storage, a cookie, the URL, `history.state`, `window.name`, a service worker or any global (E1), nor in the DOM (`data-*`, aria; E2), nor in an error path (E3). T19/T20/T13b already test these for the pins; add `shown` to their seeded assertions by name (a slug in `shown` must not appear in any storage diff, attribute value or error payload other than as the visible card itself).
- **V10. Invariance and scope.** T-level tests from §16.5 stay: `rank`'s output `JSON.stringify`-identical for absent, `[]`, and any `shown` (§16.5 item 2); permutation and duplicate invariance; slugs not in dims or already in the history ignored. Add to T4: `shown` is not an input to `rank`'s memo key, and a `shown` containing non-pilot slugs or slugs not on the device changes nothing.
- **V11. Not an event.** Nothing reports `shown`, `h` or a pin to `track()`, the beacon, `/api/events` or any `exp` payload. The existing events-intake allow-list test (E5) stays green; T3 asserts the ordering path sends nothing. If analytics-steward wants the scroll-depth signal, it is a new read, and my earlier finding 1 (N1 refuses a client-reported derived value) applies.
