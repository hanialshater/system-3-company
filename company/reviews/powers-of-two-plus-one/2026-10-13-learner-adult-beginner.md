# Learner review: powers-of-two-plus-one
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner (knows doubling 1, 2, 4, 8; can add; has never seen why the sum is one less than a power of two).
Screenshots seen: yes

## From the artifact
- First needed an idea I didn't have: none. The prediction is mostly arithmetic: an adult can add eight numbers to 255 in under a minute. The locked stage also shows a 16 x 16 square with one dashed empty cell, and the hook says the total "hides right next to a number you know", so 255 is strongly cued.
- Shown well: the extra unit (green) joins 1 to make 2, then the green block matches each next block (4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128) and the pairs merge until the square is one green 256. "The pile is 16, the same as the next block. Join it." makes each doubling visible. This is a genuine shown proof.
- Told, not shown: the general formula 2^n - 1 is a one-line tell; fine.
- Layout: on phone the first steps happen in cells a few pixels wide (the 1, 2, 4 blocks in the corner) and are hard to see; a zoom on the corner for the early joins would help. Reveal stage reverts to the small unfilled square, so the full 256 is not on screen next to the reveal text.

## Fix suggested
Ask a question the sum cannot answer quickly (e.g. "1 + 2 + ... + 1024?"), or hide the empty cell until after lock-in; enlarge the corner during the first three joins.
