# Learner review: shrinking-degree
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who has used maps and a globe and knows the continents; has never studied geography or map projections.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess for the beginner, though the question carries a lot: "One degree of longitude is about 111 km wide at the equator. How wide at 60°N?" "Longitude" is used with no definition, and the locked stage shows a small square crop of Africa (not the globe) with a yellow box on the equator. 111 km is the lazy answer; 56 and 28 are the halving traps. The hook ("A degree of longitude sounds like a fixed distance. Carry one north and measure it.") signals "not fixed" without a number.
- Where understanding first broke: "longitude" and "degree" as distances. My beginner knows degrees from angles and temperature; "1° of longitude" and "1° of latitude" tiles appear side by side without saying what either line is. The caption "Orthographic projection (a globe)" is jargon, though the parenthesis helps.
- What was SHOWN: sliding the box or tapping 60°N moves a yellow box on the picture to Scandinavia (Oslo and Helsinki labelled) and it visibly narrows to a trapezoid, while the dashed equator box shows its original width; the readout goes 111.2 to 55.6 km. At 80°N the box is a thin sliver (19.3 km) near the pole. The latitude tile stays 111 km throughout. That is a convincing visual of "the same degrees get narrower".
- Reveal vs stage: 55.6 and 19.3 km are on the stage; "half at 60°" matches the readout. NOT shown: "zero at the pole" (the slider stops at 80°N), the cosine rule, and "lines of longitude all meet at the poles" is told; though the converging meridian lines are drawn on the globe, nothing points to them.
- A real subtlety: the box is drawn 10° by 10°, but the readout speaks of 1°. A beginner has to scale in their head.
- Phone legibility: good; the globe/crop fills the card, labels are readable, the box is clear. In the locked state the map crop looks like a flat Africa map, not a globe. Reveal clipped at the bottom in the screenshot.
- Reveal earned: yes for the number and the shrinking; the "why" (meridians converge) is told.
- Problems: (1) "longitude" and "latitude" never defined (the reveal assumes both); (2) 10 degree box versus 1 degree readout; (3) pole and cosine told; (4) projection jargon.
- No FATAL.


## 2. After reading the brief

- Brief assumes the learner knows degrees of latitude/longitude; the stage does not define them, but the narrowing box and fixed 111 km latitude tile deliver the objective. Pole and cosine are told only.
- Brief misconception: A degree is a fixed distance everywhere.

Verdict: launch
