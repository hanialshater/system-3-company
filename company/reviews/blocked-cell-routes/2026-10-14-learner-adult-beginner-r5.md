## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: the question is concrete, and the three options are plausible to a beginner (I guessed 8 by a loose sketch). The locked grid shows start, the ✕ block and end. Nothing leaks.
- Explore: each tap fills a cell and the caption spells out the addition ("2 from the left + 2 from above = 4"). The first edge tap fills the whole top row, explained by "Edge cells have only one way in". The blocked cell turns into a red 0 with "it passes on 0". You can see the 0 propagate: row 2 column 3 reads "0 from the left + 1 from above = 1". The corner reads 8.
- Where it is thinnest: the learner taps, and the stage does the adding. I never had to produce a number, so I watched the rule rather than used it. The reason the rule works (every route into a cell arrives from the left or from above) is first stated in the reveal. The panel's "routes from the left plus routes from above" carries it adequately before then.
- "Start: count it as 1 route" is a small leap for a beginner: why is standing still a route?
- The stage invites "Remove the block to see how many it cut", but the gate opens without it, and the reveal never gives the unblocked 20 or the 12 that were cut. A learner who picked 20 or 12 (the natural distractors) never sees where their number came from.
- Reveal: "Your prediction held". The text restates the learner's own grid and names dynamic programming. It answers the hook's question ("count without listing?") with yes, but the lesson never shows how much work listing would have been.
