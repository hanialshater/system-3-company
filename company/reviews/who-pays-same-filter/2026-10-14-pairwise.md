Related ideas: both are toy-market lessons with a "made-up numbers" chip. A is the Coase theorem (who holds the right changes who pays, not whether the filter goes up); B is a price ceiling (a cap cuts the quantity sold).

## Give a first-time learner: B, because one graph and three counters carry the whole idea. A teaches the subtler idea well, but asks the learner to track about twenty dollar labels at once.

- **B's single moving picture.** "Cap at $4" moves the price line and the counters read Want 60 / Baked 20 / Sold 20. The reveal is the counters in words ("you can only buy a loaf someone bakes"). Learners do not have to hold anything in memory.
- **B's problems:** at the reveal the chart top is cropped (the "where the lines cross" the reveal names is partly off screen) and the reveal is about 115 words: the longest in today's set, and the last paragraph (queues, black markets, quality) is caveat, not explanation.
- **A's mirrored panels are the right structure** for "same outcome, different payer": two rows, one per legal right, and the reveal sends the learner to the two Totals (-$100 both times). The "both gain" band between $100 and $300 is a good picture of the bargaining range.
- **A fits the phone frame by shrinking, not by cutting.** At the reveal the top panel ("Right to pollute") is redrawn at a size where its labels are a few pixels high, and the reveal's first sentence points at that panel's shaded band. Everything is on screen; not all of it is readable.
- **A's offers fail silently.** "Offer $50" puts a red circled x under $50 with no words; a first-timer has to infer "the factory refused".
- **A frame shows a "DRAFT" badge** while meta.json says review; flagging it, not judging it.

Recommendation (witness): keep both. Neither replaces the other. For A, the problem to solve: at the reveal learners cannot read the panel the reveal points at.
