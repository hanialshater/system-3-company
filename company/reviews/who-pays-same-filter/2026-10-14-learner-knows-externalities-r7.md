## Findings (written from the walkthrough, before the brief)

Walked the phone transcript and sheet.png (7 frames). Chose option 1 ("No, the factory has the right").

1. **The title gives the answer away.** The title is "Who pays, same filter". Read before the question, it says the filter is fitted in both cases and the only thing at issue is who pays. That rules out option 1 ("No, the factory has the right") and option 3 ("Only if a court orders it"), and points straight at option 2 ("Yes, the laundry pays for it"), the only option that is about paying. The hook, "The factory may pollute. Does the smoke have to stay?", is a rhetorical question that also leans towards "no, it doesn't". This breaks "No answer before the commit": the title must not state or point to the answer. On this path the walkthrough still chose option 1, but a learner who reads the title is not making a real prediction.
2. **The stage shows the why well.** In the "Right to pollute" panel the laundry's offers land on a $0 to $300 line: $50 is a red cross below the $100 filter, $350 is a red cross labelled "worse than smoke", and $200 lands in a shaded band from $100 to $300 marked "both gain", with an arrow from laundry to factory and a filter appearing on the chimney. That band is the cause: any payment between the filter's cost and the smoke's cost makes both sides better off. In the "Right to clean air" panel, paying $300 damages vs fitting the $100 filter shows the liable factory choosing the filter itself. The two "Total −$100" readouts side by side show "the right decided only who pays".
3. **Small print on a phone.** The labels inside the panels ("filter $100 a year", "smoke $300 a year", "both gain", the axis values) are very small at phone width. The arithmetic the reveal rests on is in that small print. The two panels are two places to watch, but they are stacked and each is used in turn, so it works.
4. **The reveal quotes what I saw.** $200 in the shaded band, $100 filter, $300 smoke, the liable factory's $100 vs $300, and the two Totals of −$100 are on the stage when the reveal opens. About 95 words. "This is the Coase theorem" names it; the final line gives the limit (free bargaining). Ends on the idea plus caveat.
5. **One thing I could not check on this path:** the reveal says "your $200 offer". The path pressed all three offers; I did not see what the reveal says if a learner never offers $200.

Where understanding first broke: before the question. The title told me the answer; the stage then explained it well.
