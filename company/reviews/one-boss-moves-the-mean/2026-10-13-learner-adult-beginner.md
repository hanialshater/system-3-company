## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: the options only work if I know what "mean" and "median" are. Nothing before the prediction defines either. As an everyday adult I know "average" but not "median"; "Both" is the safe, non-committal pick (the walkthrough took it, and so would a beginner who does not know which is which). So the guess is closer to a coin toss on vocabulary than a judgement about data.
- Where understanding first broke: the predict stage itself, "mean 40 / median 40", two labelled lines already on the same height with no explanation of how each is found. I could not tell why the same number appears twice. Quote from the explore caption: "The median has not moved." I learn only now that the median is the green highlighted bar (40, the middle one), by inference from the colour.
- What works: the chart. The final screenshot shows four small bars, one huge 500 bar, a red mean line at 130 floating above four of the five bars, and a green dashed median line staying on the middle bar. Dragging the slider (200, 350, 500) and watching mean 70, 100, 130 while the median sits at 40 shows the contrast concretely, and "The mean is now above four of the five salaries" is a sharp caption. 
- Small layout problem: the "median 40" label sits on top of the red mean line near the left, so the two labels crowd at 100/40 on the scale.
- Reveal earned: yes for "only the mean changes"; the explanation of why ("the mean adds up sizes... the median only asks who is in the middle") gives the definitions late, after the answer. A beginner needed that before predicting.
- Problems: (1) mean and median undefined before predict; (2) "Both" works as a default guess when vocabulary is missing; (3) crowded labels; (4) the slider can be dragged back and forth without any step that asks the learner to notice the median never moves, apart from the caption.
- No FATAL.
