# Learner review (re-review r6): same-count-different-weight
lesson version: v2
boundary: adult who remembers basic school science (matter is atoms and molecules, atoms differ in mass, grams); has never studied chemistry or met the mole.
Screenshots seen: yes (artifacts/walkthroughs/same-count-different-weight/sheet.png)
Verdict: launch

## 1. From the artifact

- Hook and predict: "Water weighs more than carbon here. Does heavier mean more particles?" names the tension without pushing an answer. The question now defines a particle ("count each carbon atom and each water molecule as one particle"). The locked stage shows 6 g of carbon at 50% of a dashed "full box". It hints that 12 g would fill it, but it says nothing about water, so the answer does not leak.
- Explore: the reveal stays locked ("Try it first") until both "12 g carbon" and "18 g water" are pressed. Both show the same picture: a green-outlined full box, 6.02 × 10²³, 100%. This is the payoff, and the gate requires it. Switching elements keeps the mass: 18 g carbon gives 150%, aluminium 67%, iron 32%. That reinforces the idea that the same grams give different counts.
- Where understanding could break: not on stage. In the reveal, "18 against 12, in the same units" is the one vague spot, because the units are never named. A beginner may still wonder what the 18 is.
- The why (each water molecule is heavier): it is not drawn as a heavier dot, since the dots look the same for every substance. It does stand on stage as the scale reading: the same full box reads 12 g for carbon and 18 g for water. The reveal points straight at it ("You filled the same box with 12 g of carbon and with 18 g of water"). Same count with more grams means each particle is heavier, and the learner made that happen.
- Minor: at 150% (18 g carbon) the third row of dots overlaps the "full box" label.
- Minor: the dots sit in a thin strip at the bottom, under a large empty stage area. The picture could be larger.
- No FATAL.

## 2. After reading the brief: earlier findings (v1 review, 2026-10-13)

1. The 12 g / 18 g in the question were not what the learner had to visit: **fixed** (both are first-row buttons, and the gate needs both).
2. "Mole" and "mass units" used before they were defined: **mostly fixed**. The stage says "full box" and the reveal names it "one mole". "Mass units" became "in the same units", which is still unnamed (minor).
3. Reveal text clipped: **fixed**.
4. Atoms and molecules mixed as "particles": **fixed** (the question defines a particle).

Brief check (show the why): the doesNotKnow items are the mole as a count (shown: the full box, the same count for both) and molar mass as the mass of one mole (shown: the mass reading when the box is full, 12 g and 18 g). The objective's "heavier because each molecule is heavier" is shown by the same box at different masses, and the reveal points at it. Launch is acceptable under the told-vs-shown rule. A size or weight cue on the water dots would make it stronger, but it is not a blocker.
