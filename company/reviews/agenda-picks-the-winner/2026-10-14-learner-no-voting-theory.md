# Learner review: Who sets the order wins (boundary: no-voting-theory)

Version reviewed: walkthrough recorded 2026-10-14T08:42Z (phone, option 1 = "A"), sheet.png seen (all 5 frames), Demo.tsx read for the branches the script does not show.

Boundary used (mine, chosen before reading the brief): knows majority rule and can compare two rankings; has never heard of a Condorcet cycle, agenda control, or sincere versus strategic voting.

## Findings (artifact only)

1. **Nothing is required that I lack before predicting.** The three rankings are on the card, and the question is answerable by counting: A vs B (voters 1 and 3 prefer A), then A vs C (voters 2 and 3 prefer C), so C. The prediction is derivable from visible facts, so it is a genuine prediction rather than a guess. This is the lesson's strength.
2. **Answer not visible before predicting.** The locked stage shows rankings and unlabelled agenda chips (disabled), vote lines "?" and a dashed triangle. The chosen agenda's result is hidden. Hook ("Change only the order of the votes") says order matters but not who wins.
3. **My wrong pick (A) was natural:** A is listed first and ranked first by voter 1. The lesson's first agenda is the one in the question, and the first step produces "C" for it; I could see the mistake at once and trace it in the vote lines ("A vs C: C 2-1").
4. **Shown, not told, for the headline.** Tapping all three chips gives three different winners (C, A, B) with the same rankings and the vote-by-vote tallies on screen. The triangle fills in one arrow per newly seen pair and ends as a cycle (A to B to C to A). The reveal's sentence "the option held back to the last vote won every time" is checkable against my three runs. Good.
5. **Reveal quotes only states reached.** Yes: all three agendas are required to unlock, and all three results are quoted. The "beats" relations are the arrows I watched appear.
6. **The central idea, "the group is circular though each voter is not", is not said.** I see a cycle and read "no option is the majority favourite" but nothing tells me each voter's own ranking is consistent while the group's pairwise preference loops. A learner can leave thinking the voters are confused.
7. **"Whoever sets the order of the votes picks the winner" is a strong generalisation** taken from one three-voter example, with a caveat only about sincere voting ("when every voter backs the option they rank higher"). I tested no other electorate, and only 3 of the 6 possible agendas appear. The phrase is placed at the end of a long sentence, so the condition reads as an afterthought.
8. **Wording:** step 2 prints "B vs A: A 2-1" for the second vote while the chip says "then A" (winner listed first); I read this as an inconsistency for a moment. The caption "2 of 3 pairs seen" is helpful and the legend ("Arrow: beats, 2-1. Filled: winner.") is clear but tiny.
9. **Layout:** after tapping a chip, there is plenty of unused space in the stage on the phone; in the reveal frame the stage is clipped at the top. No dead controls.

Confidence: fairly high. Strongest: findings 1, 4, 6.

## Verdict for the current version: **launch** (with notes: findings 6 and 7 are worth a look, neither blocks)

Top finding: the prediction is derivable from the card and the three agendas show the outcome changing, but the lesson never states the surprising part that each voter is consistent while the group's majority preferences loop.
