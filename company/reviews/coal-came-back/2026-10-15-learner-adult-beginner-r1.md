## Findings (written from the walkthrough before reading build.md or meta.json)

1. **Where understanding first broke.** Nowhere seriously. The one hard read is the stacked share chart at 1968 and 2005. Coal is the bottom band and oil the band above it, so "which is bigger" means comparing the heights of two stacked bands, and that is hard by eye (41 vs 43, 39 vs 37). The right-edge labels do the work, and the small "oil ahead, 1968-2004" bracket under the axis is the only mark of the lead changing hands. It is easy to miss on a phone.
2. **What the prediction felt like.** It felt fair, with a mild twist cue. With my boundary ("the world moved to oil") "Oil" is the honest pick. But a lesson that states "oil passed coal in 1968" and then asks about 2024 implies something changed, and a feed-savvy learner will pick coal for that reason. The title "Coal or oil in 2024?" is an open either/or, which is better than a yes/no. The options are equal and the locked stage is empty.
3. **Did the reveal answer the question I actually had?** Yes. After 2024's "Coal 41% / Oil 32%" my question was "did coal burn more, or did oil burn less?" The "Tonnes, not shares" step answers it on the stage (coal's dark band keeps rising), and the reveal names it: "3 times those of 1968: a fuel can lose first place and still burn more." That is the idea, shown, not told.
4. **Small wording notes.** The readout "COAL SINCE 1968 ×3 tonnes" reads awkwardly ("×3 tonnes" sounds like three tonnes). And "the file is revised every year" in the limit: what file? A beginner would say "these estimates are revised every year".

Verdict reason: no finding blocks a first-time learner. The stacked-band comparison and the wording are notes.

Concrete fixes (notes):
- Make the lead change visible on the stage: draw a thin marker line where coal and oil cross (1968, 2005), or let a tap on the share chart show coal and oil as two unstacked lines.
- Readout: "coal tonnes, 2024 vs 1968: ×3".
- Limit: "these are estimates rebuilt from records of fuel use, revised every year."
