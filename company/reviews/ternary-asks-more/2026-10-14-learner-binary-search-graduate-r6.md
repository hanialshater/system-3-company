## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: the hook "do you find things sooner?" and the title "Thirds sound faster than halves" set up the misconception well. But the third option reads "More: 13". That is the only option with an exact number, and it looks like the answer key. A learner who suspects a trick is steered to it. Suggest "More" with no number, or give all three options a number.
- Explore: "Ask one question" gives Halves "500? · Left: 500" and Thirds "334? · Left: 666". That is the key moment and it is on stage: one thirds question keeps two thirds, not one third. "Ask until found" gives 10 vs 13 on 721. "Try all 1,000 targets" fills the table: worst 10 / 13, average 9.0 / 11.2. My "Fewer than 10" broke on the table.
- **Reveal points at a state I never visited (main finding):** "Look at 'left' after two questions: halving had 249 numbers left, thirds 333." On this path I asked one question, then jumped to found. I never saw a two-question state, so the cause in the objective (a ternary round costs two questions to keep a third; two halvings keep a quarter) arrives as told text with numbers I have to trust. Either gate the reveal on a second "Ask one question" (and put that control first so QA reaches it), or have the reveal quote the state the learner actually reached: after one question, 500 left vs 666.
- Legibility: after "Ask until found" both bars switch to a "701 · zoomed · 740" strip of tick marks. It is hard to see which tick is which question. The "Targets by questions" histograms (labels "10: 489", "13: 272") are tiny at phone size, with no axis I can read. They add a third view on top of the strip and the table. Two of these could go (one representation on a phone).
- The "smooth hill" sentence is told context and is fine if claimed. The trust link reads 0/9 claims checked (fact-checker lane).
