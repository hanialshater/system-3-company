## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: the question is a wall of text: two formulas, "(100 - q) / 10" and "(q + 20) / 10", plus smoke on neighbours, then "How many loaves match the true total cost?". That last phrase is ambiguous (match it how?). A beginner may guess without having parsed the model. Options 40, 35, 0 are plausible, and 40 is the "market is right" belief. The hook ("Who pays for the smoke?") hints the neighbours.
- Where understanding first broke: at the question itself, before the stage. I could not tell what "true total cost" means until the stage drew a red dashed "true cost" line above the bakers' line.
- What I saw: two lines cross at 40 (buyers vs bakers). Add smoke harm $1: a dashed true-cost line appears, and its crossing with the buyers' line is at 35; "TRUE COST SAYS 35 loaves". Then "Put $1 cost on bakers" moves the market to 35 too. The slider to $2 gives 30. The crossing points are readable.
- Terms and labels: the chart labels this line "bakers + $1 tax". The button says "$1 cost on bakers" and the reveal says "a $1 cost on bakers". "Tax" is never introduced; it suggests a policy, which the reveal then disclaims ("not a policy claim"). The labels "true cost" and "bakers + $1 tax" also overlap each other on the chart in the middle frames, so the two lines are hard to tell apart.
- The reveal says "Loaves 36 to 40 are worth less to buyers than they truly cost." That is the heart of the lesson and it is told: nothing on the stage shades loaves 36 to 40.
- Values in the reveal (40, 35, 36 to 40, $1) were on the stage except the 36-to-40 range.
- Reveal earned: the 35 yes; the "too many loaves" meaning only by assertion.
- Problems: (1) dense model sentence and ambiguous question; (2) "tax" label vs "cost on bakers"; (3) overlapping chart labels; (4) 36 to 40 not shown.
- No FATAL.
