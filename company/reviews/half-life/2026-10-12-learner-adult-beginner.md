Boundary used: remembers basic school arithmetic and everyday life; has never studied radioactivity, half-life or probability in any formal way.

## 1. From the artifact (written before reading the brief)

Sources: walkthrough transcript and screenshots (phone), QA phone-3-reveal.png. I did not drive the lesson myself.

**Prediction.** "200 radioactive atoms. After 3 half-lives about 25 are left. During the 4th half-life, what share of those 25 survivors decays?" Options: About half / More than half: they're overdue / Fewer than half: they're the tough ones. The hook ("Half the atoms have decayed. Are the ones still here overdue?") sets up a real intuitive tension, and the two wrong options are both things an everyday adult might believe (the "due" feeling, the "survival of the fittest" feeling). So a real guess is possible. But "half-life" is never defined on the screen before the question. I can only infer it from "After 3 half-lives about 25 are left" (200 -> 25), and a learner who reads "half-life" as "half of the life" has a different picture. The neutral-sounding "About half" is also the answer an adult picks on the pattern "the lesson is going to say half". The answer is guessable from the word "half" in the title of the topic.

**Where understanding first broke.** "Share decayed in each half-life (dashes mark 50%)" with four empty bars. I do not know what a half-life step is until I tap Wait. After one tap: "This half-life: 95 of 200 decayed (48%)." That works. The break comes at the reveal: "Your run, step by step: 48% decayed." I made one step, not "step by step", and the screen has quietly reset to 200 fresh atoms behind the reveal text.

**Explore vs the learner's actual question.** My question: "do the survivors at the end behave differently from the early ones?" The panel says "Tap Wait four times. Compare the share that decays at each step." The instruction is right, but nothing enforces it. The recorded learner tapped Wait once, then New run, three times (steps 1-6 show 48%, 49%, 48% at half-life 1 only). The "Show me why" button was available throughout. So the screens I was given never show half-life 2, 3 or 4, which is where the whole question lives (the prediction is about the 4th half-life, from 25 survivors). In step 3's screenshot the first bar sits just under the dashed 50% line, which in isolation looks like a slight "fewer than half", mildly supporting the wrong option, and nothing on the screen explains that 48% of 200 is noise.

**Reveal: earned or told?** Told. "Each half-life, roughly half of whatever was left decays, even the long survivors" and "Atoms do not age. Every undecayed atom has the same 50% chance each half-life, however long it has lasted" are assertions. The "even the long survivors" part is exactly what I was never shown in the recorded path. "Nothing pushes it toward 'overdue'" is the right idea, but it is the reveal's job to show it, and for me it is only said. The paragraph "a new run keeps different ones" connects to the "Fresh atoms. Different ones will survive this time." message, which is the strongest piece of evidence that survival is not predetermined, but it is not linked clearly to "atoms do not age". The phrase "instead of running out after two half-lives" is a reference to a misconception (two halves make a whole) that I was never asked about, so it lands oddly.

**Reveal tone.** The QA screenshot for a wrong choice reads "Not quite: it was 'About half'." followed immediately by "Your run, step by step: 49% decayed." The mismatch of "Not quite" and a 49% result I might read as supportive of "fewer than half". Also the phrase "49% decayed" was one half-life from the full 200 atoms, not the survivors the question asked about.

**Findings as problems**
1. "Half-life" is never defined before the prediction question that uses it; the learner has to infer it.
2. The reveal is available after one Wait; the screenshot path never shows half-lives 2-4, i.e. the survivors the question is about. The key evidence ("survivors still decay about half") is not required and not seen.
3. "Your run, step by step: 48% decayed." misdescribes what the learner did (one step, or none) and appears on a stage that has been reset to 200 fresh atoms, erasing the run it describes.
4. 48-49% from 200 atoms reads as "slightly under half" with no on-screen reading of noise; the noise explanation comes only afterward in text.
5. "Atoms do not age" is a bare statement; no contrast is shown between a "tough" atom and a fresh one (for instance, which atoms survived, then tracked across the run).
6. The 25-survivor setup is only a premise in text; the stage never lets the learner start from 25 or watch a small group decay, so the exact question asked is never run.
7. "Instead of running out after two half-lives" introduces a second misconception without setting it up.
8. The sentence "Carbon-14 does this with a half-life of about 5,700 years." is decorative and, for a learner who has not been told what half-life means, adds a number without meaning.

**FATAL:** The reveal can be reached without seeing the key thing, and it describes a run the learner did not make. Under the lesson's own question (what happens to the survivors in the 4th half-life), a learner can lock in, tap Wait once, hit "Show me why", and be told "Your run, step by step: 48% decayed ... even the long survivors". No false physics, but a learner-facing statement about their own run that is not true of their run, plus a reveal that "holds" a prediction never tested. (Borderline: the same reveal text is accurate if the learner does tap four times; the flaw is that it does not require it and misreports when not.)

## 2. After reading the brief

- Intent: dislodge "atoms age like a timer; survivors are overdue or tougher". The two wrong options map directly onto this; the prediction does allow a real guess, as I found.
- Intent: the "two half-lives leave nothing" corollary is deliberate; the reveal line I called out of nowhere (finding 7) is the answer to a corollary the prediction never poses.
- The brief's learner "knows halving a number", not the term half-life; the lesson never defines it (finding 1), so the lesson's own boundary does not rescue it.
- Objective ("survivors keep halving") needs the learner to watch several half-lives; the lesson asks for four Waits but does not require them (findings 2, 3). This is where my FATAL flag comes from.
- Claims are source/derivation-backed but all unverified; the physics I saw is consistent with them. My issue is with what the learner is shown, not with correctness.

Verdict: fatal
