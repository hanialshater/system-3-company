## 1. From the artifact

Screenshots seen: predict, step 2 ("Wait to half-life 4"), reveal (all viewed).

- Prediction: a real three-way guess with the two wrong answers phrased as the beliefs a person actually holds ("they're overdue", "they're the tough ones"). The question defines half-life in its first sentence in plain words and gives the starting numbers (200, about 25 left after three). The hook ("Half the atoms have decayed. Are the ones still here overdue?") voices the gambler's-fallacy reading rather than answering it. The stage shows 200 pink dots and four empty bars.
- Where understanding first broke: nowhere hard. The one friction is "Wait 1 half-life" and "Wait to half-life 4" being two different buttons with the same cue; after "Wait to half-life 4" the stage says "Few left now: start a new run", which is clear.
- What was SHOWN: the dots thin out, dashed rings mark the atoms that just went, and four small bars show the share decayed each half-life (49%, 46%, 55%, 48%) against a dashed 50% line. Seeing the bars all sit near the dashes, including the last one with only 25 atoms, is the answer to "overdue?". The shares are on the stage as numbers, not only in the reveal.
- Values in the reveal never on the stage: only "Carbon-14 ... about 5,700 years" and "a quarter after two, an eighth after three" (simple halving, but not drawn).
- Reveal earned: yes. "Atoms do not age. Every undecayed atom has the same 50% chance each half-life" is stated rather than demonstrated, but the bars make it believable. The honest sentence "with only 25 atoms the share wobbles by chance" matters: one run of 25 atoms cannot distinguish 48% from 55%, and a learner who landed on 55% might still half-believe "overdue". The "New run keeps different survivors" idea is a nice second point.
- Terms: "half-life", "decay", "radioactive" are all fine for the boundary.
- Problems: (1) one 25-atom run is thin evidence for "nothing pushes it"; (2) "atoms do not age" is told.
