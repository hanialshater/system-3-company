## Findings

- F1 PASS. The question is the shape of the record (disease against wounds), not who was right in the war.
- F2 PASS. No view on the war.
- F4 FIX. "After a sanitary commission arrived" (held claim 3) plus Nightingale's name invites a great-woman story and a cause the chart cannot carry (refuse list: one person credited with a collective outcome). The note's main risk, and I agree.
- F5 / P1 FIX. The limit sentence does not say whose record this is. The table is "deaths ... in the Hospitals of the Army in the East" (vega datapackage, opened) and the chart was made to argue for army medical reform (HistData doc). The reveal's first sentence ("Disease killed 14,476 men and wounds 1,758") reads as all deaths in the war.
- P2 FIX (mine, beyond the note). "Died of wounds" in a hospital table does not include men killed on the field. The 8-to-1 therefore compares hospital deaths by disease with hospital deaths from wounds. Left unsaid, it reads as "battle hardly killed anyone", which is the minimising reading the note warns about (its item 4). The question and the reveal must say "in army hospitals".
- N3 FIX. "In January 1855 disease deaths ran at an annual rate of 1,023 per 1,000, more than the whole army in a year" uses the death toll as the surprise and can be misread as a count (it is one month scaled to a year). Drop the clause. I do not accept the note's alternative ("the whole army would have died within a year"): it is a counterfactual extrapolation (F3).
- P2 PASS with a condition: "British" appears in the stage question and the reveal; no sentence may slide to "in the Crimean War".
- L1/L2 FIX (small). "Disease" is our simplification of the period's "zymotic" (preventable infectious) diseases. The legend needs one line saying so.
- I1/I2/I5 PASS. Bars and patterns; soldiers counted, not pictured; nothing gamified.
- R-list: no hit.

## Exact changes

1. Stage question (23 words): "British army hospitals in the East, April 1854 to March 1856: for every man who died of wounds, how many died of disease?" Options unchanged.
2. Legend: "Disease (infectious diseases the period called zymotic)" at 11 px; "Other causes" for the third stack.
3. Reveal, replacing the draft (the builder recounts to 90 words; drafted at about 83):
   "In British army hospitals in the East, disease killed 14,476 men and wounds 1,758: about 8 to one. In January 1855 the rate, scaled to a year, was 1,023 deaths per 1,000 men. From April 1855 they averaged 277 a month, against 930 in the first year. Where it stops: these are deaths recorded in the hospitals, in figures published to argue for army medical reform; weather and army size changed too, so they cannot show what caused the fall."
4. Held claim 3: use its fallback as the default text ("From April 1855 ..." with no cause). Do not name Nightingale or the commission anywhere in the reveal. A caption naming the source (table, 1859, compiled by William Farr per the vega package) is fine because it names the record, not a hero.
5. The clause "published to argue for army medical reform" is a claim: the HistData doc says the diagrams were made for medical reform (opened). Fact-checker marks it; fallback is to cut it, leaving "these are deaths recorded in the hospitals".
6. Recall item: keep.
