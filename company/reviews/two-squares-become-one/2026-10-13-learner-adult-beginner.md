# Learner review: two-squares-become-one
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who remembers basic school arithmetic and everyday life; has never studied the topic (may recall "a squared plus b squared" or the 3-4-5 triangle from school, with no idea why it works).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a weak guess for an adult who remembers 3-4-5. "5" is a common half-memory; 7 is the "just add" slip, 3.5 filler. The hook itself says "Pythagoras ... a formula to memorise", which names the topic, so those who remember it will be right without the lesson. For those who do not, 7 versus 5 is a real contest.
- Where understanding first broke: the "corners" screenshot. The caption reads "The same empty space: c × c = 25, so c = 5." and the tilted white square is already labelled 25. Where does 25 come from? Nothing shows it: the faint grid in the tilted square is too faint to count and I did not subtract anything. The big 25 is a given. In the other state ("pairs": a 9 square and a 16 square, both gridded, caption "Same empty space, now two squares: 9 + 16 = 25") the 9 and 16 are countable from the grid, so the equality "tilted square's area = 9 + 16" is what I am meant to take; but because 25 is stated on the tilted square too, the lesson tells me the area rather than making me find it.
- What works: the slider (labelled "corners" at one end and "pairs" at the other, a word instead of a number) really shifts the triangles, and the gap left in the big square changes from a tilted square to two upright squares. A learner who watches this sees that the empty space is conserved by sliding. That is the heart of the proof, and the picture communicates it.
- Count mismatch: the hook says "Slide four triangles", the reveal says "You moved three triangles without turning them". Looking at the two states I cannot tell how many triangles moved; four or three, whichever is true, the two wordings disagree.
- Controls: only the slider shows what is moving; the readout "pairs / corners" is not a number and gives no sense of where the in-between positions are. I went all the way and back as asked.
- Reveal earned: mostly. "Nothing here used 3 and 4 in particular. Any right triangle ... packs the same way" is asserted from one case; fine for a short lesson but unshown.
- Problems: (1) the area 25 is stated, not found; (2) "four triangles" vs "three triangles" wording; (3) option 5 is a common half-memory so the prediction only tests recall for many adults; (4) the generalisation to any right triangle is asserted.
- No FATAL.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief's objective is the conserved empty area (same area as one tilted square or as a² and b²); the slider delivers that and the brief explicitly says the learner does not know any proof, which fits. The "area 25 is stated" gap sits exactly where the objective needs the learner to see the equality.
- The brief's boundary (area of a square = side x side) is enough for the 9 and 16 squares; it is not enough to derive 25 for the tilted one, which is why the lesson states it.
- Claims verified.

Verdict: revise
