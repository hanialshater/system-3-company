### bk-checklist-series-reliability

lesson version: v3
Draft eligibility: eligible-draft

- **Question/prediction:** “Three checkpoints each retain 95% of complete jobs” gives a usable numerical task. It asks for survival through all three, rather than leaking the product. “95.00” is a plausible first guess for someone who has not yet encountered repeated retention.
- **Inspectable cause:** A changes the multiplier to 0.9 and ends at 72.9; B changes it to 0.95 and ends at 85.74, with three rounds in both. The descending steps and repeated multiplier support the comparison. The intermediate values are not labelled, so I can see the pattern more readily than audit each individual step.
- **Reveal:** “Each checkpoint multiplies what remains” answers the mechanism behind the prediction. The two totals are readable and the assumption/measurement boundary is explicit.
- **Transfer:** The portable idea is that successive retention percentages act on what is left, rather than on the original total. The artifact does not demonstrate a real checklist effect.
- **Visible issue:** `× 0.95 + 0` includes an unexplained `+ 0`; it adds notation without helping this task. This is a minor clarity issue, not a first-break finding.

### bk-choosing-information-budget

lesson version: v3
Draft eligibility: eligible-draft

- **Question/prediction:** The 30-minute budget, four-minute inspection, one-minute setup, and complete-options rule make “total inspection time” understandable. Nothing in the locked picture supplies 24.

### bk-habit-cue-policy

lesson version: v3
Draft eligibility: eligible-draft, narrowly as a programmed trigger illustration

- **Question/prediction:** The prompt defines the toy cue by what it does: two actions each round for four rounds. The numeric answer is hidden, though the arithmetic is deliberately simple.
- **Inspectable cause:** Cue present shows `+2`, four rounds, and `0 → 8`; absent shows `+0` and `0 → 0`. The removed stream stays zero in both. I can identify the changed input and resulting count without knowing habit theory.

### bk-irrational-decoy-dominance

lesson version: v3
Draft eligibility: eligible-draft

- **Question/prediction:** The free option's chances and signed point outcomes are given, and the question asks for a long-run mean per entry. The result stays hidden. A beginner unfamiliar with expected value may reasonably guess from the win amount.
- **Inspectable cause:** The two branches explicitly show probability times payoff and then add the contributions. A has `2.4 + (-2.4) = 0`; B has `4 + (-1) = 3`. This supplies the arithmetic needed to compare more than the “free” label.
- **Reveal:** It states that the outcomes are net of fees and that differing chances/consequences drive the difference. It clearly avoids claiming a demonstrated psychological zero-price effect.
- **Transfer:** Compare the chance-weighted net consequences of alternatives, including the cost, rather than treating “free” as sufficient evidence of value.
- **Visible issue:** The starting circle remains a `?` even after both results are known. A newcomer may wonder whether an input is still missing. Label it as the entry/choice, or show the expected value there if that is its purpose. The complete arithmetic below is still sufficient, so this is a minor clarity issue.

### bk-emyth-owner-bottleneck

lesson version: v3
Draft eligibility: revise
Verdict: revise

- **Question/prediction:** “B has capacities 6, 5 and 10 jobs/hour in sequence. What is B's toy result?” never says what “result” means. I can compare the numbers, but I have to supply both the throughput interpretation and the rule connecting three capacities. This is the first point where the new mechanism is required without being made concrete.
- **Inspectable cause:** A displays Owner 3, Handoff 10, Finish 10, with the owner outlined; B displays 6, 5, 10 with Handoff outlined. This suggests a limiting station, and the result changes from 3 to 5. B also has an unlabeled `1` and a small mark before the handoff. Neither the picture nor the prose explains what those marks measure.
- **Reveal:** “Creates a handoff limited to 5” supplies the selected bottleneck, but “Serial steady flow only” is specialist language for this boundary. It never plainly states that every finished job must pass all three stations, so no more than five jobs per hour can finish even though the owner can pass six.
- **Transfer:** The intended portable idea appears to be that relieving one constraint can expose another. I can infer it, but the beginner currently has to infer the reason for the minimum rule and decode the unlabeled `1` at the same time.
- **Required revision / owner:** The lesson builder should ask for completed jobs per hour explicitly, state that every job passes through all three stations, and name or remove the `1`/mark. The explanation should connect the handoff's five-job capacity to the maximum completed flow in ordinary language. Re-review the updated B and reveal frames from artifacts.
