## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: the hook ("A could walk away with $0.40 but never does. Does that $0.40 still change the deal?") is a good tension, and it targets the misconception head-on. The question: "Under Nash bargaining, A gets?" $0.50 / $0.70 / $0.40.
- **Where it broke (top finding): the locked stage prints the rule.** Under the $1 bar, the two halves are labelled "A's fallback, then half the rest" and "B: half the rest". The rule is the lesson's whole doesNotKnow item. With the stem's $0.40, anyone who reads the labels computes $0.40 + $0.30 = $0.70 before committing. A learner who does not read them is guessing at a term ("Nash bargaining") the stem never defines. Either way the prediction does not test the belief "an option only matters if you use it". That belief would make a learner pick $0.50, and the labels talk them out of it before they commit. (W-154 pattern.)
- Explore: presets $0.00 / $0.20 / $0.40 / $0.60 and a slider to $0.80. The bar splits into a dark "fallback" block plus two equal halves of the rest. "No deal: A $0.40, B $0.00" sits above "Deal: A $0.70, B $0.30", and the A GETS / B GETS readouts update. Stepping through the presets, every $0.20 on A's option moves $0.10 to A. This is clear and live, and the mechanism is easy to see once you are exploring.
- "Never takes it": the stage shows deal vs no-deal side by side, so A's $0.70 in the deal beats the $0.40 outside. That is enough of a stand-in for "the option is never used". The hint "watch ... whether anyone walks away" promises a moment that never comes, though: nobody walks away anywhere on the slider (it stops at $0.80).
- Reveal: points at what was seen ($0.70 / $0.30, the deal still happened, $0.60 left split evenly, 10 cents → 5 cents). The model line (money payoffs, deal sure to happen) is fair context.
- Minor: at $0.80 the two $0.10 labels overlap ("$0.10$0.10").

Told vs shown check (brief): doesNotKnow (outside option first, rest split equally) is shown by the bar segments once exploring. The objective's "explain that the option moves the split without being used" is shown by the deal line staying above the no-deal line. No told-only cause. The block is the prediction, not the stage.
