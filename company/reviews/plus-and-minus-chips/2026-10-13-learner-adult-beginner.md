# Learner review: plus-and-minus-chips
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who remembers basic school arithmetic and everyday life; has never studied the topic (can compute 5 - 3 without thinking; knows debt and overdraft; has no formal view of negative numbers).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: barely a guess. The question states "A plus and a minus cancel", then asks for 5 plus-chips and 3 minus-chips. An adult just does 5 - 3 = 2. Option 8 is a slip for the careless only. The hook asks something deeper ("Is a negative number just a positive one wearing a minus sign?") but the prediction asks something every adult can already do, so there is no tension to resolve.
- Where understanding first broke: it did not break because I already had the answer; the problem is that nothing said the lesson was about anything beyond this sum. Quote, step 1: "1 pair cancelled to zero. The total did not move." The TOTAL box shows 2 from the first cancel, before I have cancelled everything, so the answer appears immediately and the explore is a confirmation of arithmetic I could do in my head.
- What works visually: chips are green plus and red minus; the final screenshot shows 4 plus, 2 minus, and a shelf below with one overlapping green/red token labelled "0" under "1 cancelled pair, each worth 0". That makes "a pair is worth nothing" concrete. The free "+ chip" and "- chip" buttons let me try other totals, and adding a minus chip to 3 plus chips gave 2, consistent with the rule.
- Reveal earned? For "2" yes. The reveal then says "A negative number is not a positive one with a sign painted on. It is the thing that cancels a positive, like a debt against cash." That is the lesson's real claim, and nothing on the stage tested it: no step contrasts a negative with "a positive with a sign" (e.g. why 3 minus chips do not make +3 or what happens when minus outnumbers plus). It is stated, not shown. The reveal also says "That is why 5 + (−3) = 5 − 3 = 2", which introduces notation the stage never showed.
- Reveal gate: opens after one cancel and "Cancel every pair"; reachable.
- Problems: (1) prediction answerable by plain subtraction; (2) the answer appears in TOTAL after the first cancel; (3) the central idea (negative = the thing that cancels) is asserted, not demonstrated; (4) no case where minus chips outnumber plus chips, where a negative total would appear.
- No FATAL.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief's boundary (counts and subtracts whole numbers) matches mine, and its objective is just "5 plus-chips and 3 minus-chips total 2, and 5 + (-3) = 5 - 3". The lesson delivers that. My complaint is that for this boundary the prediction is solvable by the very subtraction the lesson wants to explain, so the misconception it targets ("a negative is a sign painted on") is not what the options probe.
