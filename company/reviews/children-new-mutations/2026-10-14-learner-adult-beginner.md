# Learner review: children-new-mutations
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner (knows DNA is a long string of letters copied parent to child, mutations are copying errors; no feel for genome size).
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, phone, 5 frames)

## 1. From the artifact

- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: the stem. "1.2 letters in every 100 million" times "3.1 billion, two copies" is a multiplication across powers of ten I would not do in my head, so I guessed "About 7" on the feel that "rare" means few. That is the intended trap and it felt fair.
- Explore shows it well: 62 squares (one per 100 million letters), red ticks scattered across them; 67 at the real rate, visibly fewer at 0.6, visibly denser at 2.4. Seeing roughly one tick per square makes "about 70" land without arithmetic.
- Confusion: every rate change is labelled "Child 1" (67, then 35, then 140). Is it the same child re-born at a new rate? A beginner may think halving the rate edits an existing child. Calling them "Child 2/3" or "a child at 0.6" would avoid it.
- Told-not-shown: "Studies ... find about 60 to 70" is told (fine, it's a fact, sourced in the trust sheet). "A few dozen letters new" undersells 67 and sits oddly next to "60 to 70".
- Layout: clean; reveal fits on phone.

## 2. After reading the brief (version only)

- Version 1, 5 claims shown as checked.

## Fix suggested

Number the children per press, or name them by rate; replace "a few dozen" with "about 70".
