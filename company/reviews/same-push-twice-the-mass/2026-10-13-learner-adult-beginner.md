## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess, and the question gives me enough to reason with: 10 N on 2 kg gives 10 m/s after 2 s; what for 4 kg? "Same push, same speed" (10 m/s) is the naive answer; 5 and 2.5 are plausible "heavier is slower" answers, and half versus quarter is a genuine toss-up for a beginner. "N" is just a unit I take on trust.
- Explore: sliding the cart mass up and the push up redraws a speed-against-time line against a dashed 2 kg reference. At 5 kg and 19 N the line is below the dashed line (7.6 m/s versus 10 m/s). The readouts "SPEED AT 2 S" and "SPEEDS UP BY ... each second" move with the sliders, so I can see more push means a steeper line and more mass means a shallower one. It is a clear, clean picture.
- Where understanding first broke: "SPEEDS UP BY 3.80 m/s each second". I know speed; "speeding up by 3.8 m/s each second" is acceleration, a new idea named only as a phrase, and it is the quantity the whole lesson turns on. The reveal then gives it a name ("acceleration") and a formula in one sentence.
- Reveal earned? Partly. The pattern (more mass, shallower line; more push, steeper) is shown. But the question's exact case, 4 kg, was never visited in the recorded run (masses 5 and 8 kg, pushes 13, 16, 19 N), so the reveal's "the 4 kg cart reached 5 m/s" is a number I did not see. It looks as though the mass slider steps do not include 4 kg, though the prompt tells me to "Set the cart to 4 kg"; I cannot be sure from the screenshots.
- Problems:
  - The prompt says "Set the cart to 4 kg" but the recorded slider stops are 5 and 8 kg; if the slider cannot land on 4 kg the instruction cannot be followed.
  - There is no cart or push on screen at all; the only picture is a graph, and "steady push" and "frictionless" are words I have to hold in my head.

  - "A steady push does not give a steady speed" is a strong claim contradicting a common belief, but it comes in the second paragraph after the numbers; it is the heart of the lesson and reads as a side note.
  - "Newton's second law: a = F / m" is stated in grey as a footer; "a" and "F" and "m" are never defined for me.
