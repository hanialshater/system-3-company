# Learner review: A good product can still fail (boundary: no-network-effects)

*Simulated learner. This review finds problems; it is not evidence that anyone learned.*

Version reviewed: walkthrough recorded 2026-10-14T08:42Z (phone, option 1 = "Both reach about 80%"), sheet.png seen (all 7 frames), Demo.tsx read for the branches the script does not show.

Boundary used (mine, chosen before reading the brief): comfortable with percentages and simple graphs; has not met network effects, fixed points, cobweb plots or the word "tipping point".

## Findings (artifact only)

1. **First required idea I lack: the model sentence.** "Its value to person x (x from 0 to 1 of the market) is (1 - x) times the share already using it." I do not know what x is (a person's position in a list sorted by enthusiasm?), why value falls with x, or what "0 to 1 of the market" means. I cannot compute the outcome from it. The prediction is therefore not derivable by a newcomer; it is a guess among three stories.
2. **The answer is visible before I predict.** Hook: "Same product, same price: one launch dies, one takes off." Options: both reach 80%, both die, or one dies and one reaches 80%. Only the third matches the hook, and the title "A good product can still fail" confirms it. A learner who picks anything else is ignoring the hook; the prediction tests reading, not reasoning. (The scripted choice was wrong, but a careful reader would pick option 3 at once.)
3. **The locked stage is nearly empty** (tiny "next", "now", a blank chart, "?" readouts) and the bottom controls are cut off in frame 01. Nothing is there to predict from.
4. **The chart is not explained.** Axes "next" and "now", a dashed diagonal, a curve, and a stair path. I understood the line of text ("Round 6: 25 -> 36 -> 56 -> 71 -> 78 -> 79 -> 80%") and ignored the picture until the reveal named "the diagonal". The stair/cobweb is decoration to me; the text row does the teaching.
5. **What I experienced:** 18% -> 11 -> 0 (dies) and 25% -> 80% settles, then slider at 27% -> 41, 61, 74, 78, 80, 80. That matches the reveal's example (18% dies in 2 rounds; 27% reaches 80% in 6). The reveal quotes states reached. Good.
6. **The tipping point at 20% is asserted, not found.** The explore hint says "slide the start toward 20%", but nothing requires me to go there; I stopped at 27%. I never saw 19% versus 21%, which is the only direct evidence for "just below it the share shrinks each round, just above it grows". The reveal's sentence is told.
7. **Reveal has a doubled "Not quite."** The heading already reads "Not quite: it was ..." and the first paragraph begins "Not quite." again; also the heading repeats the whole option text, which is long.
8. **"Below this critical mass, even a product worth buying fails."** I never learned in what sense it is "worth buying": the model gives every person value (1 - x) times share, which at 18% is at most 0.18, close to the 0.16 price. The "good product" in the title is never shown to be good (e.g. what happens at 100%?), and the price is never visible on the stage.
9. **Mechanism of "why users matter" is only in the stage chip** ("value grows with users") in tiny text; the lesson's real idea, "my willingness to join depends on others joining", is never stated plainly until "critical mass" in the reveal's last line.
10. **Controls:** presets and slider and Step / To the end all worked; Step/To the end disable after a run ends so the learner must move the slider to re-run. Layout: reveal frame clips the top of the chart.

Confidence: moderate. Strongest: findings 1, 2, 6.

## Verdict for the current version: **revise** (no fatal flaw found)

Top finding: the question cannot be reasoned to without unexplained notation (finding 1) and its answer is given away by the hook (finding 2), while the tipping point itself is told in the reveal and never found by the learner at 19 vs 21 (finding 6).
