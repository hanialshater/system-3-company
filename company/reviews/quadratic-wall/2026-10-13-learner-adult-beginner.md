# Learner review: quadratic-wall
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who uses a phone and a laptop daily and has never programmed (no idea what a variable, array, function or "algorithm" is; comfortable with everyday apps and basic arithmetic).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: concrete and relatable (files in a folder). "Checking every pair among 10 files takes 45 comparisons" is a given I must trust; the stage shows 10 dots joined by a web of lines labelled "Dot = file, line = one comparison", which makes 45 believable. The three options (4,500; 499,500; 45,000,000) are a real guess: 4,500 is the natural "100 times the files, 100 times the work" instinct, which is the misconception being tested. Good prediction. The hook ("What happens when the folder holds 1,000?") is neutral.
- What was SHOWN: buttons for 5, 10, 20 files: 10, 45, 190 lines, with a running chain "5 -> 10 . 10 -> 45 . 20 -> 190". At 20 the web is dense; "1,000 files" shows a plain circle with "499,500 pairs: too many to draw", which is honest and a good moment. A slider also exists (I set it to 18: 153 pairs).
- Where understanding first broke: the 1,000 step. The number 499,500 simply appears; it is a lookup, not a pattern I could see. Nothing on the stage shows why pairs grow like this: the web gets denser, but the rule "each new file is compared with every file already there" is told only in the reveal. In the stage after tapping "1,000 files", the slider still reads 20 and the circle is empty, a small mismatch that could confuse.
- Reveal: "5 -> 10, 10 -> 45, 20 -> 190, 1,000 -> 499,500" matches the stage chain. "Doubling from 10 to 20 multiplied the pairs by 4.2, not by 2" is computed from the stage numbers (190/45): fine, but the learner has to take 4.2 on trust; "11,100 times the work for 100 times the files" is arithmetic (499,500/45) that was never on the stage; "n x (n - 1) / 2" introduces algebra with n undefined. The last sentence ("grows with the square of the input") uses "square" without showing it. Reveal text is clipped in the screenshot.
- Reveal earned: the conclusion that 1,000 files gives about half a million checks, yes. The formula and the "wall" statement, partly.
- Problems: (1) the 1,000-file answer is a lookup, not a picture; (2) "each new file checked against every file already there" never shown on the stage; (3) n x (n-1) / 2 and "square" jargon; (4) slider/circle mismatch at 1,000.
- No FATAL.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief's learner knows counting steps and multiplication; matches. The objective (n(n-1)/2, doubling quadruples, 1,000 files 499,500, 11,100 times the work for 100 times the files) is delivered by the 10, 45, 190 chain and the 499,500 step; the formula and 11,100 are told. The misconception (100 times the items means 100 times the work) is hit by option 4,500.
- Claims: not assessed here (fact-checker's job); my notes about claims are limited to wording a learner reads.
