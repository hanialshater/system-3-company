## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess. "About 90%" is what a beginner might say if they think of the continents bunching up; "just under half" is a close alternative; the third option "29%, same as the whole planet" is a trap for someone who thinks any half has the average. The hook ("Earth is mostly water, but land bunches up. Can one half of the globe be mostly land?") sets up the question without giving the number. The locked stage shows a globe centred in the Atlantic that already looks half water, a hint that 90% is high.
- Where understanding first broke: the control labels. "Centre latitude" and "Centre longitude" sliders and the caption "Orthographic: the disc is exactly half the Earth" are jargon for a beginner; I understood the globe turns, and the preset buttons made the sliders optional.
- What was SHOWN: "Western Europe" gives 47% and sets MOST YOU FOUND to 47%; "North Pole" gives 40%; "Mid-Pacific" gives 21%. The land is shaded blue on a white globe so the proportion is easy to eyeball, and the 47% globe is visibly half white. The "MOST YOU FOUND" counter is a good device: I can keep trying to beat it.
- Problem with the exploration: the first preset hands over the answer. "Western Europe" jumps straight to the landiest half, so the panel's invitation to "turn the globe to find the most land you can fit" is replaced by one tap. A learner never has to search, so "47% is the best anyone can do" rests on trust, not on my own failed attempts.
- Reveal vs stage: 47% and 42°N, 2°E are on the stage; 40% for the Northern Hemisphere matches the North Pole preset (though a beginner might not equate "centred on the pole" with "the Northern Hemisphere"). NOT shown: "the whole planet is 29% land" (never on the stage) and "the emptiest half near New Zealand is only 11%" (the lowest I saw was 21% in the mid-Pacific).
- Phone legibility: the globe is clear; the locked globe is smaller than the open one. Reveal clipped at the bottom in the screenshot.
- Reveal earned: partly: "just under half" is seen; "every half is mostly water" follows only if I believe 47% is a true maximum.
- Problems: (1) the preset gives the maximum immediately; (2) 29% and 11% told; (3) latitude/longitude/orthographic jargon.
