# Learner review: ruler-on-mercator
lesson version: v?
boundary: an adult who has used maps and a globe and knows the continents; has never studied geography or map projections.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess; the question gives the setting precisely ("the yellow bar spans 2,000 km at the equator. Slide the same bar to 60°N"). The options 1,000 / 2,000 / 4,000 km: a naive learner who believes a scale bar is a scale bar picks 2,000; a learner who knows "maps stretch near the poles" picks 1,000. The hook ("Maps come with a scale bar. Slide one north...") does not leak. The locked stage shows the bar at the equator over East Africa: yellow bar 2,000 km, "MAP STRETCH 1.00x". The map in the locked state is oddly framed (a narrow crop of Europe, Africa and Asia) with the bar tiny at the bottom, so little is visible.
- Where understanding first broke: "Mercator" and "projection" are used in the question without definition; "MAP STRETCH 1.00x" is a new term, though its meaning is guessable. A beginner who has not heard "Mercator" would still follow because the question says "this Mercator map" as a label.
- What was SHOWN: the slider and buttons give 1,999 km at 2°N, 1,732 km at 30°N (stretch 1.15x) and 1,000 km at 60°N (stretch 2.00x). The yellow bar keeps its on-screen length while the green dashed bar (a true 2,000 km) grows longer, twice as long at 60°N, and sits just below the yellow bar. That is a direct visual: the same screen length covers different distances. Good.
- Reveal vs stage: 1,000 km and 2x are on the stage. NOT shown: "2.9 at 70°", and the formula 1/cos(latitude); "same north-south as east-west, which is why shapes look right while sizes do not" is told (the stage shows only a horizontal bar, so a beginner never sees the north-south direction). "A single scale bar on a world map is only true along one line" is a good takeaway but told.
- A real confusion: the green bar is longer than the yellow bar at 60°N and the reader must decide which one is "wrong"; the caption says green is "a true 2,000 km here", which resolves it, but the colour legend is small and pale. Also the map seems to shrink between states (different crop), so the bar's position relative to land shifts.
- Phone legibility: the map and bars are readable once at 60°N; the bars are thin; the caption is small. Reveal clipped at the bottom in the screenshot.
- Reveal earned: yes for the number and the bar picture; the north-south point is asserted.
- Problems: (1) Mercator/projection undefined; (2) 70° and 1/cos told; (3) north-south = east-west told with a horizontal bar only; (4) locked map crop is odd and the bar is tiny.
- No FATAL.
