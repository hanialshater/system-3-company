*Simulated learner. This review finds problems; it is not evidence that anyone learned.*

lesson version: v1

Boundary: an adult who remembers basic school arithmetic and everyday life; has never studied physics beyond school.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess. Everyone has waited for thunder, but no one has a feel for "6 s = how far". The three options span two orders of magnitude, so I would pick on gut feel; 200 m is attractive to someone who thinks of lightning as "very close". Honest guess, no giveaway.
- Explore: the slider moves a series of arcs from the strike toward me, one per second, and STRIKE DISTANCE updates. The mapping count -> distance is visible and I can test other counts. That earns the main idea.
- Where understanding first broke: the FLASH TOOK readout ("6.9 µs", "17.1 µs"). "µs" is never explained, and a beginner will not know whether 17.1 µs is long or short, or why a flash has a travel time at all before the reveal explains it.
- Reveal earned? Yes for "sound is slow, counting seconds gives distance". The "divide your count by 3" rule is a nice takeaway but is introduced only in text, after the 343 m figure; I was not led to find it myself.
- Problems:
  - The reveal talks about the 6 s case ("2.06 km away") while the screen beside it is stuck on 15 s / 5.14 km; the text and picture disagree (likely a driver effect since the run ended at 15 s, but a real learner who ends at another count would see the same mismatch).
  - "Arcs: where the thunder was each second" does not say each arc is 343 m; the number only appears in the reveal, so the scale is not readable from the arcs.
  - "µs" and "millionths of a second" are introduced in the same breath; the reveal gives one of them but the readout shows the other.
  - The lightning is drawn as a cloud and bolt standing on the ground at the far end, with the learner at 0 km; the flash visually "arrives" nowhere, so "light covers the same distance in 6.9 µs" has no picture.
  - "Same event, two messengers" is a good line but arrives as the closing aside, not as something shown.
- FATAL: none found from the artifact.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The objective is the 1 km per 3 s estimate. The slider supports the mapping and the reveal states the rule, but the rule is never exercised by the learner (no "now estimate this one" step), so the estimating skill itself is only described.
- The brief says the learner does not know light is near-instant. The FLASH TOOK readout carries that idea but with a unit (µs) outside the stated boundary.
- The misconception (the delay means nothing) is not really held by an adult, who has likely heard "count the seconds"; the lesson's real work is the scale, which it does handle.

Verdict: revise
