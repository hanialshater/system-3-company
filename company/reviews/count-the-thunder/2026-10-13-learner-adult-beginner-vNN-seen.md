# Learner review: count-the-thunder
lesson version: vNN
boundary: an adult with school-level maths and science, new to the topic (has counted "one Mississippi" after lightning without knowing the rule; knows sound is slower than light as a fact). Screenshots seen (sheet.png, all 3 frames).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess; 200 m (thunder feels very near), 2 km, 20 km are well-spread orders of magnitude. The hook ("The gap is a ruler") is a nudge, not a leak. The locked stage already states "Each arc: one second of the thunder's trip, 343 m", which an adult can use to work it out; that is a fair help, not a leak, since the learner has to multiply 6 arcs.
- Where understanding first broke: the reveal's "FLASH TOOK 0.0000069 s". On the stage this appears as a readout (the label is a "?" until the slider moves) and its meaning, the time light takes to cross the same distance, is stated only in the reveal. The speed of light is never mentioned, so 6.9 millionths of a second is a number to accept. It does support "the delay is almost all the thunder's trip", but the beginner cannot check it.
- What was SHOWN is strong: one slider to 6 s draws six arcs reaching the 2 km mark, with "Count ÷ 3 = 2" in plain words and the strike distance 2.06 km. The learner sees why 3 s is about 1 km (three arcs of 343 m, the 1 km tick). Payoff is on the stage. Single interaction, enough here since the claim is one point.
- Weak point: the slider is only the delay; there is no way to feel that the flash itself arrives instantly (no light arc). "Same event, two messengers" is told.
- "2.06 km" vs "about 2 km": the rounding is fine. 343 m/s at 20 °C is stated on the stage.
- Reveal earned: about 2 km, yes; the rule ÷3, yes.
- Problems: (1) light speed never shown or stated, 6.9 µs unverifiable; (2) no visual for the flash messenger.
- No FATAL.
