# Learner review: twenty-dollar-basket
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner (prices and simple multiplication; knows a budget is a fixed amount; tends to think "it still fits, so it's free").
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, phone, 5 frames)

## 1. From the artifact

- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: nowhere, and that is the problem. The hook raises the interesting idea ("it still fits in the $20, so adding a loaf costs nothing else. Right?"), but the prediction is plain arithmetic: $20 − 3 × $4 = $8, $8 ÷ $2 = 4 apples. I got it right without meeting the idea the hook promised.
- The stage is a good picture: a $20 cart of ten $2 slots; each $4 loaf takes two slots, each apple one. That a loaf "takes the room of 2 apples" is visible.
- But I never saw the trade-off happen. My path was: 3 loaves, fill with apples (4), remove an apple. I never added a 4th loaf to a full cart and watched 2 apples leave. "One more loaf always means 2 fewer apples" is told in the reveal, not shown or tested.
- The reveal's "Your prediction held" makes it feel like a sum I got right, not an idea I had wrong.
- Layout: clean; reveal fits on phone.

## 2. After reading the brief (version only)

- Version 1, 4 claims shown as checked.

## Fix suggested

Make the prediction target the hook: "Your cart is full: 3 loaves and 4 apples. You add one more loaf. What happens to the apples?" (none / 1 fewer / 2 fewer). In explore, let the loaf slider push apples out of a full cart so the cost is seen.

Verdict: revise
