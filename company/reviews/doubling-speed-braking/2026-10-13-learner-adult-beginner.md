## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess, and a tempting wrong one. "Twice the speed, twice the distance" (14 m) is what most adults would say. The three options are an evenly spaced ladder (14, 21, 28), so "21" reads as the safe middle compromise. Good, honest prediction.
- Explore: the block picture is the strongest part. Each block is one 30 km/h stop; at 75 km/h I count 6.25, at 120 km/h 16. Seeing the road fill with blocks makes "much more than double" visible.
- Where understanding first broke: the reveal's "Why: twice the speed carries four times the movement energy (it goes as speed squared), and the same brakes remove the same energy per metre." Both halves are asserted; neither is on screen. I accept "four times" because the blocks showed it, not because of the energy sentence.
- Reveal earned? Mostly. 16 blocks at 4x speed earns "square". But the run stops only at 75 and 120 km/h; the 60 km/h case the question asks about (and the reveal quotes as 27.8 m / four blocks) was never displayed in the recorded steps, so the prediction was never directly compared to its own answer. Possibly a slider-step or driver effect; I cannot tell from the screenshots.
- Problems:
  - The prompt says "Raise the speed to 60 km/h" but the recorded slider jumps (75, 120) skip 60; if the slider cannot land on 60, the instruction cannot be followed.
  - Blocks alternate two shades of orange; at 16 blocks they look like a striped bar, and the label "16 blocks" is what I rely on rather than counting.
  - "Speed vs 30 km/h 4.0x" sits next to "Braking distance 111.1 m" without saying that 4.0x speed gives 16x distance; the lesson's key comparison (speed multiple vs block count) is left to me to compute.
  - Mixing "movement energy" in the explanation with "speed squared" in a bracket is jargon dense for a one-line justification.
  - "Reaction time adds more road on top" is a new idea in the last line with no number.
- FATAL: none found from the artifact.
