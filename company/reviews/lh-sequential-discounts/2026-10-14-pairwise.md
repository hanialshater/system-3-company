Same idea: stacked percentage discounts multiply (0.8 x 0.9 = 0.72), so 20% then 10% is 28% off, not 30%.

## Give a first-time learner: B, because the second discount's smaller base is on screen.

- **B shows the mechanism; A states it.** In B, the bar loses $20, then a visibly shorter $8 chunk, with the line "10% of $80 = $8 off". The learner sees *why* the second cut is smaller before the reveal says it. In A, the receipts are blank outlines with dashes and a dot; the only information is two totals (EUR 72, EUR 70) and their formulas. The reveal's key sentence ("the second percentage acts on what remains") points at nothing drawn: the EUR 8 vs EUR 10 is never shown.
- **B's swap answers the next question learners ask** ("does the order matter?") with a second recorded run ($10 then $18, still $72). A's slider moves the original from 100 to 110, which shows the gap scales with price but adds a second set of numbers the reveal must juggle ("At the prediction's 100EUR ... Your stage now uses 110EUR").
- **A has stage-text problems a first-timer will trip on:** "Full scale:EUR108" (no space, and "full scale" is chart jargon with no visible scale), "100EUR" next to "EUR72" (two currency conventions), "EUR79.2" (unpadded cents).
- **A's genuine strength:** its question is a real shopping decision (stacked coupon vs one 30% offer, which is cheaper?) rather than "what is the total discount". That is the right life-hack framing and B lacks it.
- **B's problem:** the reveal frame shows the stage after Reset ($100, 0%) while the text talks about $72; the Seen lines carry it, but the bar the learner watched is gone.

Recommendation (witness, not decision): keep B as the concept's lesson. A is a duplicate that teaches less; if Life Hacks wants the "which offer is cheaper" framing, it belongs as a stage variant of B's mechanism, not as a separate template lesson.
