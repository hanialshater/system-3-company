# Learner review: sorting-without-comparing
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner; knows sorting by comparing two items at a time and that a whole number can label a position; has not heard of counting sort.
Screenshots seen: yes

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: 0 / 9 / 33. The question describes the method ("tallying how many of each score occur and writing them out"), so a careful reader can get 0 from the wording; a learner who believes "sorting = comparing" picks 9 or 33. Acceptable tension.
- Explore: ten taps drop scores into buckets 0 to 5 (dots fill), then six taps read buckets out into a sorted row 0 0 1 2 2 3 3 4 5 5. The COMPARISONS counter sits at 0 throughout. Clear and honest.
- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: what would a comparing sort have cost? The 9 and 33 options are never shown or explained, so the 0 has nothing to be surprising against. The counter that never moves is a weak signal on its own.
- Told-not-shown: "With a huge range you would need a bucket for every possible value" is told; no control widens the range to show the buckets multiplying.
- Layout: phone; reveal fits, no clipping. 16 taps is long for a 60-second card; the score chips shrink to dots once dropped, which is fine.
- Suggested fix (optional): a side counter or a one-tap "sort by comparing" run on the same ten scores showing its comparison count, and a range toggle (0 to 5 vs 0 to 100) that shows the bucket row explode. A "drop all" button after the first few taps would save time.

Verdict: launch
