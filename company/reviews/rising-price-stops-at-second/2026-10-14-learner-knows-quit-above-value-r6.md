## 1. From the artifact

- **Where it loses me (main finding):** the hook reads "The lamp is worth $70 to the top bidder. So why does she pay less?" That sentence answers the prediction's main wrong option before I commit. The misconception is "the winner pays close to her own value", which is option 1, $70. The hook tells me she pays less, so $70 is ruled out and the choice shrinks to $56 vs $41. The walkthrough chose $70 anyway, but a reader who takes the hook at its word never gets to be wrong in the way the lesson exists to correct.
- Predict: otherwise good. Values $70 / $55 / $40, the price rises $1 at a time, and the options $70 / $56 / $41 are each a precise, defensible story ($41 = "stops when the first bidder drops" is a nice distractor). The locked stage shows three value bars and a price line at $30. No leak on the stage.
- Explore: Raise $1 and Raise $5 move the price line up across the bars ("3 still bidding at $36"). "Run to the end" stopped with the line just over Ben's bar. Ben's and Cal's bars turn into dashed outlines labelled "quit at $56" and "quit at $41", Ana's bar reads "wins at $56", and the caption says "Only Ana is left. She pays $56." The price line sitting just above the runner-up's bar is the why, on the stage: the runner-up's value set the price.
- Reveal: it quotes $56, Ben at $55, and "$14 of her $70" (derivable from the stage). "Her own value decided who won; the runner-up's value set the price" points at the bars. "A sealed second-price auction would charge $55" is told context outside the brief's gap, in one sentence. The reveal frame shows the reset stage at $30 (minor; the QA path pressed Reset).
- "Run to the end" jumps rather than letting me watch Cal drop and then Ben drop. The Raise buttons do let me walk it, and the labels record both quit prices, so it is fine.
