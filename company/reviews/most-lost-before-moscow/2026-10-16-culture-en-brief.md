## Findings

- F1 PASS. The question is where in the column the men were lost.
- F4 FIX (the main one). The stage question begins "Napoleon's main column ..." and the learner's misconception is "the winter destroyed Napoleon's army". Charter: "'Napoleon lost his army' is a phrase; the lesson is about the column's numbers." The possessive makes him the agent. Name the column by its source.
- F2/F3 FIX. The reveal's "winter finished an army that was already mostly gone" states a cause. The file records survivors, not causes, and a contemporary account (note section 4, PMC5106729, medium) puts hard cold from 20 to 25 October, about two weeks before Minard's first sub-zero reading on 9 November. "Before winter" (option B) claims more than the record supports.
- N1/N3 FIX. "422,000 out, 10,000 back, about 1 in 42" rests on a file where group 2's 28,000 men disappear at 28.3 degrees E and are not added to group 1 (note section 3). It is a death toll used as a surprise on an artefact. Drop that preset.
- P1/P2 FIX. The limit sentence leaves out Russian losses and the multinational column. "Lost counts deaths, desertion, capture and garrisons" rests on Friendly (2002), unopened; use the fallback.
- L4 FIX. Place labels "Kowno" and "Moscow": "Kowno" is the source's name for Kaunas. Give both: "Kowno (Kaunas)".
