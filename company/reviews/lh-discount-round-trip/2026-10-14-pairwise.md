Same idea: equal percentage changes down and up do not cancel, because the second acts on a different base.

## Give a first-time learner: B, because the lost amount is a shape you can point at.

- **B makes the asymmetry visible.** One slider; the square grows by +50, then a band of -75 is cut across the whole risen width, and the "lost 25" corner is red and labelled. The reveal names that corner and generalises (10% up then 10% down ends at 99). The learner's eye and the reveal point at the same thing.
- **A's stage carries no amounts.** Two blank receipts; "Test A" reveals the unchanged original (EUR 100), which is a tap that teaches nothing; "Test B" reveals EUR 96 with "(100x0.8x1.2)". The EUR 20 off and EUR 16 back, which *are* the idea, never appear. The reveal tells it ("the rise starts from the smaller base").
- **A's prediction is the easier one.** "Below / at / above original" with three choices gives a guessing learner a fair chance and an answer key of direction only. B asks for the number (100 / 75 / 125), which forces a commitment the picture then corrects.
- **B's problem:** the reveal is about 90 words with two paragraphs; it is the longer one. I prefer it despite the length, not because of it: its extra sentences point at the drawn corner.
- **A strength:** the store-discount-then-markup context is closer to where learners meet this than B's abstract "price of 100".

Recommendation (witness): keep B; A duplicates it with less. Same template finding as lh-sequential-discounts (see the ritual record).
