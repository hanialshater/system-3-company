## Findings (written from the walkthrough before reading the brief or build.md)

Path walked: chose "Ordered cause chain"; tapped Cover, Swap 1↔2, Swap 2↔3, Check; opened reveal.

1. **The prediction is a giveaway.** "Which test preserves the explanatory links? Ordered cause chain / Alphabetical words." Nobody thinks alphabetising preserves a cause chain. And the alphabetical option never appears on stage, so I could not have checked it anyway.
2. **Where it first broke: I was shown the answer, then asked to reorder it.** Before Cover, the stage shows "cause: cloud, event: rain, result: puddle" above my pre-filled attempt (puddle, cloud, rain). After Cover I rearranged those same three words with two swap buttons. I did not explain or remember a link; I copied an order I had just seen. This is the same activity as the cover-before-recall lesson with different words.
3. **The chain is too easy to test anything.** Cloud, rain, puddle can be ordered by common sense without remembering anything. So even a fresh learner would score 3/3; the check cannot tell "remembered the explanation" from "knows weather".
4. **Reveal versus my question.** The hook ("a list of words is not yet an explanation") is a good idea: knowing the terms is not knowing how they connect. The reveal then says only "This checks the stated chain, not whether every cloud produces rain." It never says why remembering links beats remembering words, or how to do it when studying (say why each step leads to the next).

Fixes (for the builder):
- Use a chain where the order is not guessable (a short process the learner has just read), and have them rebuild it after it is hidden, without a pre-filled set.
- Compare with the alternative the prediction names: show what remembering only the word list loses.
- State the payoff in the reveal (as a sourced claim if it is a learning-science claim).

Verdict reason: the activity has the learner reorder three words they were just shown, in an order guessable from common sense, so neither recall nor "links" is tested, and the reveal does not give the payoff.
