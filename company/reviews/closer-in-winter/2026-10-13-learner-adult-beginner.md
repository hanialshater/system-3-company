# Learner review: closer-in-winter
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who has used maps and a globe and knows the continents; has never studied geography or map projections.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess. The hook ("Summer feels like being nearer the Sun") names my everyday misconception and does not give the answer. Locked stage shows 149.0 million km at 21 Mar, a start value with nothing to compare against yet. Good.
- Where understanding first broke: the status line under the readouts. On 1 Jun it says "North: spring · South: autumn", and on 1 Sep "North: summer". As a beginner, 1 June is summer, and the lesson tells me it is spring. I do not know what scheme of seasons it uses and nothing on the stage says (no mention of solstices). This undercuts trust in the whole panel at the moment I need it.
- Second break: "NOON SUN OVERHEAD 23.0°S / 22.1°N" is used with no definition of what "overhead at 23°S" means for a beginner; I can guess "the latitude where the Sun is straight up" but never used latitude before.
- What was SHOWN: jumping 1 Jan, 1 Jun, 1 Sep gives 147.1, 151.7, 151.0 million km. The Earth is closest in January, when the stage says it is northern winter. That is a clear, quick refutation of "closer = summer". The orbit looks nearly circular and the Sun is near the centre, so "the distance barely changes" is visible.
- Reveal vs stage: the reveal names 152.1 million km "early July" and "about 3% different". I never saw July or 152.1 (my largest was 151.7 on 1 Jun); no month button for July. The 3% is told. The tilt of 23.4° is told: the stage's arrow "north pole leans this way all year" is there but nothing shows the lean toward the Sun in June; the closest I got is the overhead latitude readout.
- Hook says "Drag the Earth around its orbit": I dragged a date slider, not the Earth.
- Phone legibility: orbit diagram is large and clear; arrow and Earth dot are tiny; the arrow caption is very small and pale. Reveal text clipped at the card bottom.
- Reveal earned: the "not distance" half yes; "so the tilt is the cause" is asserted.
- Problems: (1) season label on 1 Jun reads "spring", unexplained; (2) July / 152.1 / 3% not shown; (3) overhead-latitude readout undefined; (4) tilt payoff told; (5) "drag the Earth" does not match the control.
- No FATAL.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief's misconception (summer = closer) is hit by the hook and the January result; the stage delivers the distance half of the objective. The "3%" and "opposite seasons, so tilt" half is told, not shown, and the 1 Jun "North: spring" label works against the beginner who knows June is summer. The brief assumes the learner knows when the Earth is where in its orbit; the stage never explains its season labelling.
- Claims verified, but claim c4's "seasons bounded by equinoxes and solstices" is a convention the learner is not told.
- Revise: relabel or explain the seasons shown, let the learner reach July/152.1, show the tilt effect.
