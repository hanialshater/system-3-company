## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: the stem gives every number (able produce $100k, others $50k; pay $100k for holders; cost $20k vs $60k). A careful learner can do the subtraction: 80 > 50, but 40 < 50, so "Only the able". But the hook and stem both say "the degree teaches nothing", and a learner who holds the misconception is pulled straight to "No one" without doing any arithmetic. "Everyone" ("it pays $100k, of course") is the other naive pull. So the choice is real for this boundary, even though it can be solved from the stem. The locked stage shows white bars with "?". No leak.
- Explore:
  - Others' cost $40k: both "degree" bars go green with "gets it", and "Others get it too: firms pay $100k for $50k workers."
  - Others' cost $60k: others' degree bar turns grey/red and reads "skips it", and "Only the able get it: the degree reveals ability."
  - Able's cost $60k: both skip, "No one gets it: it tells firms nothing."
  - The sliders then let me push both costs to $80k.
  - The red cost slab sitting on top of the net bar, against the dashed $50k line, made "worth it or not" readable without arithmetic.
- What made it click: the three buttons in sequence. Cheap for everyone means everyone buys it and it means nothing. Dear for others only means it separates. Dear for everyone means no one buys it. The cause (a cost *difference*) is the thing I changed.
- Reveal: $80k vs $50k and $40k vs $50k were on stage. "When you made it cheap for the others ... firms paid $100k for $50k workers" was seen, word for word. "It signals ability only because it costs the able less to earn" is stood for by the Able's-cost $60k press. The reveal does not point back at that press ("when the able paid $60k too, no one bought it"). It would land harder if it did.
- Minor findings:
  - The bar value labels (−$20k, $80k, $50k) are about 6 px on a phone.
  - In frames 04–06 the "none" bars turn green and the "degree" bars grey, so the colour now means "chosen". That is not labelled.
  - The cost sliders under the buttons duplicate them. QA pushed both to $80k, which teaches nothing new.
  - On the locked stage the second slider is cropped at the bottom.
  - Pay stays at $100k even when no one, or everyone, holds a degree. The "Model:" tag covers this, but an alert learner will ask what firms would actually pay.
