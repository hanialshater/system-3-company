# Learner review (round 2): cradle-two-in-two-out
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult beginner who has played with a Newton's cradle; has heard of momentum but could not define energy of motion.
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 5 frames, phone viewport). Re-review after the kit fix for clipped reveals (W-135); written fresh from this walkthrough.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: lift two, release; the far end swings two balls at the starting speed / one at double speed / one at the starting speed. Many adults have seen a cradle and will pick "two" from memory; the interesting question (why not one ball at double speed?) is the explore's job. I chose two.
- Explore: each outcome draws the balls and two bar pairs, Momentum (m v) and Energy (½ m v²), before vs after, with "same" in green or "differs" in red, plus MOMENTUM KEPT? / ENERGY KEPT? Two balls: Yes/Yes. One at double: Yes/No (energy 2 vs 1). One at starting: No/No. The "one at double speed keeps momentum but doubles energy" case is the insight and it is shown, not told. Good.
- Reveal: lists the three outcomes' tallies, all of which I tried; no unvisited values. "Two constraints leave one way to move" slightly overclaims: they leave one of the three options shown. A beginner will not notice; a curious one might ask "what about other splits?".
- Units: the energy bar is labelled ½ m v² but the reveal says "units m v and m v²"; "energy 1" for two balls only makes sense if you work the ½ through. Minor friction for a beginner.
- Phone clipping (the reason for round 2): fixed. Full reveal and trust link visible.
- Where understanding first broke: nowhere material.
- Reveal earned: yes.

Fixes (owner: builder, optional): "so of these three, only one keeps both" instead of "one way to move"; give the energy unit once on the stage ("energy in units of m v²").

## 2. After reading the brief

- Objective: "two balls out at the starting speed is the only outcome that keeps both momentum and kinetic energy". The stage shows it is the only one of three; the reveal phrases it as the only way. For identical balls and a simple split of outgoing balls that is a defensible claim, but the lesson does not show the general case. Flag for the fact-checker on wording, not blocking.
