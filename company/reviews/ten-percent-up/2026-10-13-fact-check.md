Checker: fact-checker (independent). Inline node arithmetic. Stage text from the walkthrough transcript; Demo.tsx read (energy at level i = 10,000 x (eff/100)^i, square side = 140 x sqrt(E/10,000)).

| Claim | Result | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| c1 | verified | 10,000 -> 1,000 -> 100 -> 10 -> 1 over four links. |
| c2 | verified | Respiration, heat, uneaten and undigested material account for the energy not passed up (Lindeman 1942, Ecology 23(4): 399-418, the trophic-dynamic aspect; standard ecology). |
| c3 | verified | Few links are sustainable because energy shrinks geometrically; Elton's pyramid of numbers (1927) and the energy-flux explanation of short food chains are standard. The claim is hedged ("tends to"). |
| c4 | verified | Mean transfer efficiency about 10% is attributed to Pauly & Christensen (1995), Nature 374: 255-257, and the 5-20% range to Lindeman-era and later estimates; individual systems fall outside it (one Portuguese trawl-fishery model reports 22%; lake studies 5-10%). The lesson says "roughly 5 to 20%" and "a rule of thumb", which is a fair summary. I could not open the Nature paper for its exact range. |
| c5 | verified | 10,000 x 0.2^4 = 16; 10,000 x 0.05^4 = 0.0625 (stage shows 0.06). |
| c6 | verified (convention) | Square area proportional to energy (side proportional to the square root), stated on the stage ("Square area = energy"). The reveal's "hawk square was a dot beside the plant square": side ratio is 1/100. |
