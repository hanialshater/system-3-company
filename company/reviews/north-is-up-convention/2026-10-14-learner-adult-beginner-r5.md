## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: the Hereford map (England, about 1300): which direction is at the top (North / South / East)? This is a recall question. A newcomer can only guess, but "North" is the live default and the hook sets it up. The locked stage shows a modern globe centred on Jerusalem, north up, with a compass and "Top of the map: North". It says plainly that this is not the medieval map. Nothing leaks.
- Explore: the panel gives the Hereford layout in words (Asia on top, Europe lower left, Africa lower right) and asks me to turn the globe to match. Turn left 90° puts Asia on top and the compass needle sideways: "Top of the map: East. Matches the Hereford layout." Turn 180° gives Africa on top: South. Matching a described layout to a compass reading is real work for the learner, and it lands.
- Reveal: East, with the reason (Sun rises; Orient = rising) and al-Idrisi's south-up map, then "a ball with no top, so up is a choice". That last idea is felt through the turning.
- Finding (minor): the Hereford layout itself is told in the panel, and no image of the medieval map is shown. The learner trusts the text for the one historical fact; the stage only converts it into a compass direction. A small thumbnail or outline of the Hereford layout would let the learner match against a picture, not a sentence.
- Finding (minor): the reveal says "Turning twice gave south at the top", but on this path I tapped "Turn 180°" once. The text describes a path I didn't take; "Turning it upside down" would fit either path.
- Finding (minor): the chip says 6/8 claims checked.
