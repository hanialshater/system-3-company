# Learner review: uphill-finds-a-peak
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult beginner who has heard that binary search halves a sorted list; assumes halving needs order.
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 7 frames, phone viewport)

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: 7 face-down cards, turn the middle and its right neighbour, keep the bigger side. "At most how many cards do you turn over?" 3 / 5 / 7. I chose 3 (halving 7 three times). The question is long but the definitions are needed.
- Explore: turning cards 4 and 5 (4, 7) and trying "Keep left half" is refused with a reason ("4 < 7: the left side is downhill"): a good corrective. "Keep right half" greys the left. "Finish by the rule" turns 3 and 6 and ends "Only 6 is left: a peak."
- Where understanding first wobbled: at the end I could see 7 face-up, sitting between 4 and 3, which is obviously a peak, and the method walked past it to 6. The reveal answers this ("6 is a peak even though 7 is bigger: a peak only has to beat its neighbours"), but the question a beginner actually has is "why did it throw away a peak it had already seen?" That is not answered; it is a side effect of the rule only looking at the middle pair.
- The "at most 5" is told ("Over all 5,040 orders ... never needs more than 5"). I saw 4, which at least rules out my 3 and fits 5. "About 2 per halving" lets me rebuild 2 + 2 + 1. Acceptable.
- Reveal values (6, 4 of 7, 3 face down) were all on the stage. No unvisited values.
- Phone: reveal fully visible, no clipping.
- Reveal earned: "halving works without order if one look tells you the side" yes; the worst-case 5 partly told.

Fixes (owner: builder, optional): one line when the range moves past a visible peak ("7 was a peak too; the rule only needs one, and it guarantees one on the uphill side").

## 2. After reading the brief

- Objective (middle pair shows the uphill half; discard the other unseen; at most 5 on 7 cards) is delivered by the walked path, with the bound told. Misconception ("halving only works on sorted lists") is directly broken by the shuffled cards.
