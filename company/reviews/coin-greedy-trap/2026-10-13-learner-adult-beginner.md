# Learner review: coin-greedy-trap
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who uses a phone and a laptop daily and has never programmed (no idea what a variable, array, function or "algorithm" is; comfortable with everyday apps and basic arithmetic).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: the hook ("Grab the biggest coin each time and you get the fewest coins. Usually.") plants the idea and the "Usually" flags the trap. The question asks "the fewest coins that add up to exactly 6" with coins 1, 3, 4. A beginner just does the arithmetic: 3 + 3 = 2 coins. So option 2 is an easy correct answer by reasoning, not a prediction that tests the misconception (that biggest-first is best). The learner who gets it right here learns nothing yet, and the reveal still says "Your prediction held". The locked stage shows rows "You / Greedy / Fewest" with no explanation of "Greedy" until it is faded in.
- What was SHOWN: tapping "Greedy's way" fills the Greedy row with 4, 1, 1 = 3 (in red) and "Fewest possible" fills 3, 3 = 2 (in green) under my own row. Seeing them stacked is clear and makes the point without words.
- Where understanding first broke: switching the target to 8 and 10. In the transcript path the stage showed an empty board after tapping 8 and 10 (the two buttons "Greedy's way" and "Fewest possible" must be pressed again); the reveal then says "for 8 it takes 4 + 4" and "10: 4 + 4 + 1 + 1 against 4 + 3 + 3". Those numbers were not displayed unless the learner presses those buttons: a learner who only taps the target buttons sees nothing.
- "With everyday coins like 1, 5, 10 and 25, greedy happens to be always right" is told, not shown, and "happens to be" is vague. The closing line ("The best-looking step is not always part of the best answer") is a good one-sentence takeaway, earned by the 4, 1, 1 vs 3, 3 picture.
- The "Show me why" button is lit after a single coin tap; nothing forces trying to make 6.
- Reveal earned: for 6, yes. For 8, 10 and the real-coin claim, no.
- Problems: (1) the prediction is solvable by arithmetic and does not test greedy; (2) hook flags the trap; (3) targets 8 and 10 show nothing until the learner presses two more buttons; (4) the everyday-coins claim is told only.
- No FATAL.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief's learner knows making change and adding; matches. The objective is that greedy fails at 6 with coins 1, 3, 4 and works for 8; the stage shows 6 well. But the misconception (grabbing the best-looking option always wins) is not actually tested by the prediction, because 3 + 3 is obvious arithmetic, and the hook 'Usually' flags the trap. The 8 and 10 cases in the reveal need two extra button presses the default path did not make.
