*Simulated learner. This review finds problems; it is not evidence that anyone learned.*

Boundary used: an adult who remembers school arithmetic and everyday life; has met the word "evolution" but has never studied natural selection or population genetics.

## 1. From the artifact (written before reading the brief)

**Where understanding first broke.** The reveal: "The dark-to-pale ratio grows 1.1× per generation: 1.1^100 is about 14,000. By generation 100, about 99 in 100 are dark." A beginner hears "ratio" and "14,000" and then "99 in 100". How a ratio of 14,000 (to what starting ratio?) becomes 99 of 100 is not bridged; at the start the lesson says "1 in 100", so the learner has to know that the starting ratio is 1 to 99 and that 14,000 times that is about 140 to 1. Nothing on screen builds that. A second, earlier break: the question says dark beetles "leave 10% more young than pale ones", while the stage is always exactly 100 beetles. Where the extra young go is never explained, so the grid does not obviously match the sentence.

**Did the prediction allow a real guess?** Yes, as a gut guess; the four options span "barely moves" to "takes over". Most beginners reason linearly (10% more per generation, 100 generations, so maybe 10 or 50) or by "1% doesn't become anything" (About 2). The walkthrough learner chose "About 2" and this was a believable choice. The sooty-bark setup is clear, and "young keep their parent's colour" is a useful stated rule.

**Did exploring answer the learner's actual question?** The hook asks how a population changes if no beetle changes colour. The caption "Every beetle here is new; none changed colour." answers it by assertion each time the slider moves; nothing on the grid shows parents and young, so "new" is told, not seen. The walkthrough learner:
- never got past generation 14 (5.3% dark, 5 beetles of 100), although the panel said "Drag the generations to 100". At 14 generations the picture is still compatible with "about 2" or "about 10", so nothing on the learner's screen contradicts their guess;
- raised the edge to 13% instead of shrinking it as the panel suggested, and the readout stayed on small numbers;
- hit "Start over" and re-dragged to 3, i.e. the exploring ended with 1 or 2 dark beetles on screen.
So the take-off, the key thing, is never seen. The slider to 100 is available, but the learner is not pulled to it, and "Show me why" is available throughout.

Layout issues visible on the phone screenshots: the "DARK" readout chip (the number the whole lesson is about) is cropped in half at the bottom of the stage ("DARK" with the value cut off) in both the walkthrough and the QA phone shots; the caption under the grid is also clipped in the reveal ("new; none changed colour." is cut). The last paragraph of the reveal ("Real case: near Manchester, dark peppered moths") is cut off at the card edge in the reveal screenshot. I can only say what the screenshot shows, not whether it scrolls.

**Reveal earned or told?** Told. "For 25 generations dark beetles stay under 10%" is a good line (it explains why 14 generations tell you nothing), but the learner has not seen generation 25, 49 or 100. The 1% edge result ("only reach about 3%") is likewise told; the panel asked the learner to try it. The moth case (1848 to 98% by 1895) is a strong real anchor, but in the screenshot it is cut off, and without it the 99 is only a number from a model.

**Findings (problems):**
1. The exploration can end at generation 14 or less; the take-off to 99 is told, not seen, and what the learner did see supports a wrong guess.
2. The jump from "ratio grows 1.1x" and "14,000" to "99 in 100" is not bridged for someone who has not met odds or ratios.
3. "10% more young" does not match a stage that is always 100 beetles; population size is never stated as fixed.
4. "Every beetle here is new; none changed colour" is an assertion; the grid does not show parent to young.
5. The DARK readout chip is clipped on the phone stage, and the reveal's last lines and caption are clipped in the screenshot.
6. The edge slider moves in single percent steps and the learner can drive it up (13%) instead of down; the panel's "shrink the edge" is a request, not something the stage steers.
7. Why dark beetles leave more young (birds, sooty bark) is implied by "sooty bark" but never said; a beginner may read "edge" as a vague advantage.

**FATAL:** none declared, but flagged as borderline for the steward: a learner can reach the reveal having seen only 14 generations (5% dark), and then be told 99. That is "reveal reached without seeing the key thing" in a mild form; I did not mark it FATAL because the panel names the missing step and the control to take it is obvious, so a motivated learner can still see it.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief names two misconceptions: a small edge makes a small difference, and individuals adapt. The experience works on the first only if the learner reaches high generations; the second is carried by one repeated caption and the reveal's "No beetle ever changed colour", so it is told, not shown.
- The brief lists "exponential growth of odds" as something the learner does not know. The reveal relies on exactly that (ratio times 1.1 each generation, then 14,000). The brief's own gap is the lesson's weakest step.
- The brief's boundary says "predators eat prey they can see"; the experience never mentions predators, so the one piece of prior knowledge that would explain the edge is not used.
- The recall (plant A, 5% edge) asks about compounding in words; it fits what a learner who saw the take-off would carry, and fits poorly what the walkthrough learner saw (14 generations).

Verdict: revise
