# Learner review: snowshoe-pressure
lesson version: 1
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 4 frames)
boundary: an adult with school science; knows snowshoes work but not why; kPa, N and m² are half-remembered units.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: the question gives both areas and the boot pressure, so 10x area → 1/10 pressure is a one-step ratio. I chose 17 kPa (same weight, same pressure), which is a real misconception, so the trap exists. Fine.
- Where understanding first broke: I never saw a boot sink. The locked stage shows the boot standing on the surface; after unlocking I tapped Snowshoes (as the panel invites) and "Show me why" opened. The reveal then says "the foot stopped sinking" and "In boots it was 17.2 kPa", neither of which I saw on the live stage. The contrast that answers the hook ("How can that keep you on top of the snow?") is skippable.
- The pressure gauge ("snow limit" with a small green bar, "kPa") is tiny and unlabelled with numbers; I could not tell whether 1.72 or 2.15 kPa was under or over the limit until the reveal said "3 kPa".
- Told-not-shown: "Pressure is force divided by area: 687 N ÷ 0.40 m²" is told; "687 N" for a 70 kg hiker is unexplained (weight in newtons). Acceptable with a gloss.
- Good: the honest caveat that the 3 kPa limit and sinking depths are made up.
- Layout: the stage shrinks at reveal so the foot and gauge are unreadable; empty top band. No clipping.
- Fix suggested: start explore in boots with the foot visibly sunk and the gauge over the limit, and require both Boots and Snowshoes before unlocking; make the gauge a labelled bar with the 3 kPa line; gloss 687 N ("70 kg pulls down with 687 newtons").

## 2. After reading the brief

- Objective: pressure = weight / area; 10x area gives 1/10 pressure; the snow holds. The intended learner knows newtons (so the 687 N finding is minor for them). 'The snow holds' depends on a visible contrast with sinking, which the artifact does not guarantee.
- Claims: 5, all verified; I cannot vouch for them.

Verdict: revise
