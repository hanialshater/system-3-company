# Learner review: shortest-way-to-tokyo
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who has used maps and a globe and knows the continents; has never studied geography or map projections.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess and a good one: Hawaii and Mexico look natural on a flat map for a Pacific trip, and "Northern Alaska" is the counter-intuitive correct answer. The hook ("On a flat map the straight line looks shortest. Fly it on a globe and see.") tells me the straight line is not shortest, which half gives it away, but "which place" is still open. The locked stage shows the straight blue line from New York to Tokyo across the map (12,820 km) and tick labels for the three places.
- Where understanding first broke: the readouts. "VS STRAIGHT LINE —" then "+1,380 km" etc. are fine. The stage does not say what "Straight on the map" length means: 12,820 km is "measured over the globe" (caption) for a line that is straight on this flat map, which is a subtle idea; and the "Via ..." routes are each a curve through a stated point, not obviously "the shortest path". A learner could easily read "Via northern Alaska" as just one of three detours that happens to be shortest of three.
- What was SHOWN: tapping Via northern Alaska draws a blue arch up over Alaska, 10,880 km, -1,940 km vs the straight line. Via Mexico City 14,680 km (+1,860) and Via Hawaii 14,200 km (+1,380). So I can see numerically that the northern arch is shortest of the options. The "Shortest you tried: via northern Alaska" line is a useful nudge. In the final screenshot a green dashed arch appears labelled "the shortest path" and the Mexico route is drawn as a different curve.
- Why it is short is not shown: the stage never shows a globe. A beginner sees an arch on a flat map that looks longer, and the reveal explains "on a ball, the shortest path is a great circle: the line a string takes when pulled tight across a globe", but the stage has no string, no globe. "Fly it on a globe" in the hook is not delivered by the stage.
- Reveal vs stage: 10,880 and 12,820 km on the stage. NOT shown: "it bows north, up to about 70°N", "about 18% longer", and "Mercator stretches the north, so the shortest path looks bent". The "shortest" claim is only "shortest you tried" on the stage; that the green path is the shortest possible is told. The Hawaii route being 14,200 km (longer than straight) is a surprise to my beginner, since on the map it is a detour.
- Terms: "Mercator projection" in the caption undefined; "great circle" defined in the reveal; "compass bearing" used undefined.
- Phone legibility: map is readable; labels are small; the Mexico City and Hawaii dots are tiny pale yellow. The reveal is clipped at the bottom in the screenshot.
- Reveal earned: the answer (northern Alaska, shortest tried) yes; the reason (great circle) is told.
- Problems: (1) no globe, so "why" is told; (2) "straight line on a map" measured over the globe is subtle; (3) 70°N and 18% told; (4) Mercator and compass bearing undefined.
- No FATAL.


## 2. After reading the brief

- Brief's misconception (the straight map line is shortest) is hit by the hook. The objective, that the shortest path is a great circle, is told; the stage has no globe and only shows 'shortest you tried'.
- Brief misconception: The shortest route is the straight line you draw on the map.
