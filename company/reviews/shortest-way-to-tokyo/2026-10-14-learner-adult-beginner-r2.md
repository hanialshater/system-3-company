lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult beginner who has flown long-haul and seen curved routes on seat-back maps without knowing why; trusts the flat map's straight line.
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 5 frames, phone viewport). Re-review after the kit fix for clipped reveals (W-135); written fresh from this walkthrough.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: New York to Tokyo, shortest path over the globe; which place does it pass closest to? Hawaii / Northern Alaska / Mexico. I chose Hawaii (it lies "between" on the flat map). A real misconception test.
- Explore: four route buttons with THIS ROUTE and VS STRAIGHT LINE. Straight on the map 12,820 km; via Hawaii 14,200 (+1,380); via northern Alaska 10,880 (−1,940); via Mexico City 14,680. The route that looks longest on the Mercator map is shortest: that contrast is the lesson and it lands hard. "Shortest you tried: via northern Alaska" is a fair summary.
- Reveal: all four numbers quoted were seen. "About 18% longer" and "up to about 70°N" are told. "The line a string takes when pulled tight across a globe" is a good beginner image.
- Phone clipping (the reason for round 2): the reveal text is fully visible. On the reveal frame the map is scrolled up so the green dashed great circle the caption mentions ("Green dashes: the shortest path") is mostly off-screen at the top; I can scroll back, but it is the picture the reveal is about.
- Small logic gap a careful adult may notice: I only compared three detours, so "shortest you tried" is not "the shortest". The green great-circle line is what closes that gap, which is why its visibility matters.
- Where understanding first broke: nowhere.
- Reveal earned: yes.

Fixes (owner: builder, optional): keep the great-circle line in view when the reveal opens (e.g. scroll the stage so the map top is visible, or draw it in the stage before the reveal).
