# Learner review: pcr-not-quite-doubling
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner (knows repeated doubling; does not know what PCR is or how to read a log axis).
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, phone, 7 frames)

## 1. From the artifact

- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: reading the chart. The axis is "copies (log scale)" labelled 1, 10^3, 10^6, 10^9. I don't read log scales, and on this one the 90% line sits just under the dashed perfect line, a sliver apart. My eye says "almost the same", which is the misconception the lesson is meant to break. The picture argues for my wrong answer; only the caption ("21% of perfect") argues against it.
- At 80% (4% of perfect) the lines are still visibly close. The stage never makes "a fifth of perfect" look like a fifth.
- "PCR" is never explained beyond "copying DNA"; fine for the idea, but "Toy PCR" in the stem is a jargon first line.
- Prediction is good: 90% is the natural answer and the options span the trap.
- Reveal is the clearest part: "×1.9 instead of ×2, which is 0.95 of a perfect cycle ... 0.95 thirty times is 0.21". That sentence teaches; the stage does not.
- Layout: reveal fits on phone; after the slider the stage shows 98%, not the 90% case the reveal discusses.

## 2. After reading the brief (version only)

- Version 1, 3 claims shown as checked.

## Fix suggested

Add a linear readout the eye can compare: e.g. two end-of-run bars (perfect vs chosen) on a linear scale, or a "fraction of perfect" bar that drops 100 → 21%. Keep the log curve as secondary or drop it. Snap back to 90% at reveal.
