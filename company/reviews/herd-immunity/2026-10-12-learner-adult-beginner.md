*Simulated learner. This review finds problems; it is not evidence that anyone learned.*

Boundary used: an adult who remembers school arithmetic and everyday life; has never studied epidemiology, R0 or herd immunity.

## 1. From the artifact (written before reading the brief)

**Where understanding first broke.** In the reveal: "The tipping point is where a case replaces itself exactly once: immune share 1 – 1/R0 = 75% here." "R0" has never been introduced anywhere on screen; the question says "each case infects about 4 others". A beginner has to guess that R0 means that 4, and the formula is dropped in without a step. A little earlier: "Each case meets 4 people, but at 80% immune only about 0.8 of them can catch it." The arithmetic (4 times the unprotected share, 0.2) is not shown; the learner is given 0.8 as a fact. The stage readout "EACH CASE INFECTS ≈ 0.2" at 95% is the only clue, and it is a bare number with no "of 4".

**Did the prediction allow a real guess?** Mostly. The question is long (400 people, 4 others, 80% immune, 80 unprotected, one falls sick) but it is concrete, and the three options are distinct. A beginner will pick "Nearly all 80" because "each case infects 4" sounds explosive; that is a fair intuition to test. The word "Usually" is doing work that a learner won't notice (typical vs. any one run).

**Did exploring answer the learner's actual question?** The hook asks "can everyone else's immunity shield them?". The learner in the walkthrough pushed the slider up to 95%, then 90%, and watched "1 of 20", "2 of 40" typical results. They never visited the question's own setting (80%), and never did what the panel asked ("drop immunity to 50% and run it again"). So what they saw was only the case that confirms "just a handful"; the case where the outbreak takes off, which is what gives the shield its meaning (below the tipping point there is no shield), was never on their screen. Other issues:
- "Over. 1 of 20 unprotected caught it." after a single "Next step", while the stat chip says "TYPICAL, 200 RUNS 1 of 20": the same number appears as the result of this run and as the typical value, so it reads as one coincidence or as the same thing.
- "sick now" vs "had it" are two near-identical pinks; in the step screenshot a line joins a strong-red dot to a pale one with no cue which infected which.
- The chip labels "1 of 20 / 2 of 40" mean "of the unprotected": the denominator moves with the slider and is not labelled.
- The "Step 1: 1 new case." message is the only way to see the chain; with so few unprotected, the visual is a mostly green grid with one line. The idea "immune people are blocking the lines" is not visible.

**Reveal earned or told?** Largely told. "You saw 1 caught it at 95% immune" refers to the learner's run (honest), but the claims that follow concern 80% and 50%, which the learner may not have seen: "typically about 4 of the 80", "At 50% each case makes about 2 more, and most of the unprotected catch it." The 75% threshold is told by formula. The line that answers the hook ("Above it, the immune shield people who have no immunity at all") is the last sentence of the second paragraph; the rest is mechanics. The closing caveat (random mixing, perfect immunity) is honest and appropriate. In the screenshot the reveal text is cut off at the bottom of the card ("In real towns, unprotected"), which hides the caveat unless the learner scrolls (noting only what the screenshot shows).

**Findings (problems):**
1. "R0" and "1 - 1/R0" appear in the reveal with no introduction; the number 4 in the question is never named.
2. The learner can finish exploring without visiting 80% or 50%; the below-threshold outbreak is never shown, so the tipping point is told, not seen.
3. The step from "each case infects 4" to "0.8" at 80% immune is not shown; the readout "EACH CASE INFECTS ≈ 0.2" has no "out of 4" or "times 0.05".
4. Run result and "typical" figure are visually the same kind of number and can coincide ("1 of 20" twice), blurring the single-run vs. average distinction.
5. "Sick now" and "had it" are hard to tell apart; the line between two dots has no explanation.
6. The reveal's statement that answers the hook is buried, and the closing caveat is clipped in the screenshot.

**FATAL:** none found. No false statement or dead control seen, and the guess "nearly all 80" is contradicted by what the learner sees at 95%. The missing 80%/50% visit is a serious gap (finding 2) but the learner still sees results that contradict the wrong guess.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief names the misconception as "protection grows gradually rather than tipping at a threshold". The experience can end without a single step across the threshold (the walkthrough learner only went up from 80%), so the tipping is the one thing in the objective that is only told.
- The brief says the learner does not know R0, yet the reveal and the recall both rely on the term; the objective's "R0 times the unprotected share" is never built on screen, only the readout "each case infects".
- The brief aims at "which also shields people who are not immune", the hook's question. The reveal does say it, but as the tail of a mechanics paragraph.
- The recall (R0 = 5, 80%) needs the formula the lesson only states once, so a learner would have to remember a formula, not reuse a picture.

Verdict: revise
