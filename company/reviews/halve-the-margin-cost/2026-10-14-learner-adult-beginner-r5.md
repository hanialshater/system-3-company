# Learner review (re-review r5): halve-the-margin-cost
lesson version: v2
boundary: adult who reads poll headlines, knows polls report a plus-or-minus and that bigger samples wobble less; assumes the error halves when the sample doubles.
Screenshots seen: yes (artifacts/walkthroughs/halve-the-margin-cost/sheet.png)
Verdict: revise

## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: 2,000 is the doubling instinct, and the three options separate ×2 / ×4 / ×8. The locked stage shows the 1,000-person state (±3.1, 95 of 100 inside band), which is the question's given and doesn't leak.
- Explore: "Double the people" gives 2,000: ±2.2 points, 97 of 100, with a dashed line marking the earlier band. Good: the 2,000 step is now on stage, and I could see that doubling didn't halve. Then the walkthrough dragged the slider to the end, **8,000 (±1.1), skipping 4,000, and "Show me why" unlocked.** I never saw the state the answer is about.
- Where understanding broke: the reveal says "It took 4,000, four times as many, to reach ±1.5: the band was half as wide." On this path I saw 1,000 → ±3.1, 2,000 → ±2.2, 8,000 → ±1.1. I can work out that 4,000 sits somewhere between, but "×4 people = half the margin" wasn't shown. 8,000 → ±1.1 shows that ×8 is more than enough, not that ×4 is exactly right.
- The dashed earlier-band lines are a good addition. At 8,000 several dashed pairs crowd together and are hard to tell apart.
- Reveal: the square-root rule is told. That's fine as a name, if the 1× / 4× / half pattern had been seen.

1. Gate let the learner skip 2,000: **fixed** ("Double the people" lands on 2,000 first).

- Minor: label the ghost bands (1,000 / 2,000) so "half as wide" can be read against the 1,000 band directly.
