# Learner review: halve-the-margin-cost
lesson version: 1
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 3 frames)
boundary: an adult who reads poll headlines and knows "margin of error" as a phrase; assumes error halves when sample doubles.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction felt real: 2,000 is the doubling instinct. Good.
- Where understanding first broke: the gate. One slider move straight from 1,000 to 4,000 unlocked "Show me why". I never stopped at 2,000. The reveal then opens with "Doubling to 2,000 people only took the margin from ±3.1 to ±2.2 points", a number I never saw. The key contrast (2,000 is not enough) is told, not shown, for any learner who drags past it.
- Stage: the dot strip and band narrowing from 1,000 to 4,000 is clear, and "95 of 100 inside band" staying constant helps. But the band narrows by eye only modestly at this scale; side-by-side widths (1,000 / 2,000 / 4,000) would make "half as wide" visible.
- Told-not-shown: "shrinks with the square root of the sample size" is told; fine as a name once the 1x/4x/half pattern is seen, but here the pattern was not seen.
- Good: the last line (margin covers only sampling luck, not refusals or biased lists) is a useful honest caveat.
- Layout: empty top band in explore; no clipping.
- Fix suggested: require a stop at 2,000 (preset buttons 1,000 / 2,000 / 4,000, or a gate that needs both), and keep a ghost of each band so widths can be compared.

## 2. After reading the brief

- Objective names the 2,000 -> 2.2 point step explicitly; the artifact lets a learner skip it entirely, so the middle of the objective can arrive only as told text.
- Claims: 4, all verified; I cannot vouch for them.
