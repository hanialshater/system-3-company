## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: the rules are in the question (one disk at a time, never bigger on smaller), and the stage shows three pegs with three stacked disks, which makes the puzzle concrete. "3 disks need 7 moves" gives me one data point. 12 (adding), 36 (multiplying) and 63 are a real guess, and the hook ("add a few more, or something worse?") signals that it is worse, so 12 looks like the trap.
- What was SHOWN: the "Shortcut" buttons move the top two disks together and the move counter jumps by 3 ("+3: the 2-disk puzzle, done as one block"). After two shortcuts and one single-disk move the counter reads 7, matching the 7 in the question. That makes the structure visible (block, big disk, block). But the "+3" is declared, not demonstrated: I never see how 2 disks take 3 moves, so I must take the counter on trust.
- Where understanding first broke: the screenshot at the end of the transcript path shows the stage reset to 3 disks and 0 moves; the learner never used "Add a disk", the one control that shows the doubling. The reveal's 4 disks take 15, 5 take 31 and 6 take 63 therefore appear for the first time in the reveal text. A learner who does not press "Add a disk" sees only the 3-disk case and 7 moves and reaches the reveal told, not shown. Nothing requires pressing it.
- The reveal is a good explanation (move the stack off the big disk, move it, put the stack back = "twice plus one") and matches the shortcut idea, but "2^n - 1", "10 disks need 1,023" and "64 disks ... 18 quintillion" are told only.
- Terms: "shortcut" and "block" are not explained; the red-yellow colours of disks are not labelled.
- Reveal earned: the structure for 3 disks yes; the doubling to 63 only if the learner chooses to press Add a disk.
- Problems: (1) the doubling control is optional, so the key numbers may never be seen; (2) "+3" shortcut cost is asserted; (3) hook hints "worse"; (4) 2^n and 18 quintillion told.
- No FATAL.
