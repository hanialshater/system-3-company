## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess only in part. The question itself hands over "61° of longitude, roughly 4 hours of the Sun's travel", so "4" (and "5") is what arithmetic suggests and "1" is the surprise. My beginner picks 5 or 4. The hook ("as wide as four hours of sunlight") does not give away the answer. The locked stage ("? / ?") is clear about what will be read out.
- Where understanding first broke: not at the map. It is the readout labels "KASHGAR: CLOCK / SUN" with no definition: I had to infer from the numbers that CLOCK is the official time on the wall and SUN is local sun time (12:00 = sun highest). "World time (UTC)" is used before it is defined; I treated it as "some reference time".
- What was SHOWN is good. Tapping "Noon Sun at Kashgar" moves the yellow line to Kashgar and the readout says 14:56 / 12:00, so the sun is highest at nearly 3 p.m. on the same clock that says 11:54 in Shanghai. The reveal's numbers (11:54, 14:56) are exactly the ones I saw on the stage.
- Not shown on the stage: "4.1 hours" and the idea that the dashed 15° bands are "what geometry alone would suggest". The dashed lines are never explained during exploring (caption only says "15° bands"), so the claim that zones would be four is not demonstrated; I take it on trust.
- Jargon: "Equirectangular projection" in the caption is meaningless to me and not needed; "UTC+8", "Beijing time" arrive in the reveal undefined but are harmless.
- Phone legibility: map readable, city labels fine, dashed lines faint. After a button is used it greys out, which looks the same as a disabled button (I could not tell if I was supposed to tap again).
- Reveal text is clipped at the bottom of the screenshot (third paragraph fades under the card edge); I read it in the transcript only. If that is a scroll area, nothing signals it.
- Reveal earned: yes for "one clock, sun disagrees by ~3 h"; partly for "decision made by law".
- Problems: (1) CLOCK/SUN labels undefined; (2) 15° bands and 4.1 h told, not shown; (3) "UTC" and "projection" undefined; (4) used buttons look disabled.
- No FATAL.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief expects the learner to know time zones exist and that the Sun is highest near noon; my beginner matches. The misconception (a wide country has several zones, clock tracks the Sun) is hit by option 5/4 and by the 14:56 / 12:00 readout. The stage delivers the objective's key number (nearly 3 p.m. in Kashgar) by action.
- The objective's second half ("time zones are decisions, not geometry") is only asserted in the reveal; the dashed 15° bands are the only hint and are unexplained. Not blocking.
- Claims verified (6/6 shown on the stage). Launch with fixes noted: define CLOCK/SUN, drop "equirectangular".
