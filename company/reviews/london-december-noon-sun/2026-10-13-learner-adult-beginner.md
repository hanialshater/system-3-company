# Learner review: london-december-noon-sun
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who has used maps and a globe and knows the continents; has never studied geography or map projections.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess, somewhat nudged by the hook ("Everyone knows the winter Sun is lower. Guess how low...") toward the lowest option, 15°, which turns out right. The three options 15, 38, 62 are spread across an angle most adults have no feel for. The locked stage already draws the sky as a half circle with the Sun on the arc at "21 June" and an equinox marker, but the readout "NOON SUN HEIGHT ?" and no angle arc, so June's height is not given. The question gives London's latitude (51.5°N) without saying what it is for.
- Where understanding first broke: "±23.4° tilt" and "equinox" labels sit on the arc with no explanation. The panel says "Step the date from June through the equinox to December", but the control is a slider and only one step (to 21 December) was taken in my walk, so I did not see the equinox position or June's height as a number.
- What was SHOWN: at 21 December the Sun sits low in the south with a red angle arc "15.1°" and the readout "90° − 51.5° − 23.4° = 15.1°". That formula is on screen: I can see how the number is made. The faint gold band along the arc and two dotted circles show the Sun's range through the year.
- Reveal vs stage: 15.1° and the formula are on the stage. The reveal introduces 38.5° (equinox) and 61.9° (June) and "46.9° lower than summer, twice the tilt": June's 61.9 was never shown as a number in my walk (only the unmarked starting Sun position). "A low Sun spreads the same light over more ground, and its light passes through more air" is told with nothing on the stage. 
- A beginner may not follow why "subtract latitude" works (90 - 51.5): the picture has no globe, no London on a sphere, only a horizon and a Sun, so the formula is accepted rather than understood.
- Phone legibility: the half circle is big and clear; labels N, S, you London are readable; the "equinox" and "tilt" labels are small and the Sun partly overlaps "equinox". Reveal fully visible in the screenshot, not clipped.
- Reveal earned: the 15° yes; the 38.5 and 61.9 and the "twice the tilt" insight partly, since only one date was visited.
- Problems: (1) hook nudges to the lowest option; (2) latitude's role never shown, formula taken on trust; (3) only the December state shown, so June/equinox numbers are told; (4) light spreading and air told; (5) "tilt" and "equinox" unexplained.
- No FATAL.


## 2. After reading the brief

- Brief expects the learner to know the Sun is lower in winter; the 90-51.5-23.4 formula on screen delivers the December case. June and equinox numbers (objective) were not shown in my walk, and why subtracting latitude works is not shown.
- Brief misconception: The winter Sun in a mid-latitude city is only a little lower than at the equinox.

Verdict: revise
