## Findings (written from the walkthrough before reading build.md)

Path walked: chose "It rose"; tapped "Total: getting worse", "Each month: getting worse", "Total: getting better"; opened reveal.

1. **The prediction is genuine.** "It rose" is the rising-chart reflex; "The total can't tell you" is the target. Four options, one of them a meta-answer, which is fine here.
2. **The month-by-month bars are the payoff.** 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5% in red under "joined that month" while the total still climbs to 450: clear and surprising for my boundary.
3. **The pictures contradict the reveal's general rule.** The "worse" total bends over (concave); the "better" total bends up (convex). Both end at 450, but their shapes are visibly different on the same chart (04-step-3, 05-reveal). The reveal then says "A running total can't fall, so it can't tell you which way you are going." From my seat the stage shows that it can: the bend of the total is the monthly rate. A learner who notices this is told the opposite of what they see; a learner who does not may leave with "cumulative charts are useless" instead of "look at the steps, not the height". Either way the take-away is muddied. The question's narrow claim (knowing only that the total rose, you can't tell) is right; the reveal's broader sentence over-reaches.
4. **The locked stage already shows the "worse" curve.** In 01-predict the total is drawn bending over. A careful reader could answer "It fell" from the picture, and would then be marked wrong by "The total can't tell you". The prediction is not quite fair to the attentive learner.
5. **Preset names spoil the explore.** "Total: getting worse" / "Total: getting better" tell me which world I am in before I look; the point that the totals look alike is weakened when the button names say they differ.
6. "The Lean Startup calls numbers like this vanity metrics" and "One trial with 116 startups ..." are real-world claims; the trust sheet lists 7, none checked yet (fact-checker's lane).

Verdict reason: the core sentence of the reveal is contradicted by the lesson's own curves, and the locked picture lets a careful learner answer a "wrong" option correctly. Not a block: the narrow claim is true and the per-month bars teach the right habit.

Fixes (hypotheses for the builder, not tested):
- Narrow the reveal: "Rising every month tells you nothing about each month: only the size of each step does" (and point at the bend, or the bars).
- Locked stage: show the total as plain rising points without a visible bend, or say "you only know it rose".
- Neutral preset names ("Total A", "Total B", "Each month of A").
