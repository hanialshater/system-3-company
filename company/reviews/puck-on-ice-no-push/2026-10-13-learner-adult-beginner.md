- Prediction: a real guess and a good one. "A steady push forward" is the intuition of almost every adult ("things stop when you stop pushing"); "no push" is the surprise. Honest.
- Explore: four lanes (High, Medium, Low, None friction) and a puck that slides on whichever I tap. The dots (once a second) mark the puck's trail; on the frictionless lane the dots are evenly spaced, 2 m apart, and on the high-friction lane the puck stalls within a metre or two. That contrast is the whole point and it is visible.
- Where understanding first broke: the screenshot at the end shows the Low-friction puck at 14.8 m and the None puck at 14.1 m, so the lane with a little friction is ahead of the lane with none. The pucks were started at different moments (each tap launches one more puck, all running at once), but nothing says so; I see a puck on a "Low" lane beating the "None" lane, which is the opposite of what the lesson tells me.
- Reveal earned? Not assessable (never reached).
- Problems:
  - Pucks started at different times run side by side, so the picture at any moment compares unequal start times; the "None" puck can look slower than "Low".
  - "Even dot spacing = constant speed" is not said anywhere on screen ("Dots: once a second" is the only hint); a beginner must infer that equal spacing means no slowing.
  - The caption "Each puck starts at 2 m/s. Nobody pushes after that." never changes, and carries the lesson's answer ("nobody pushes") before I have seen the frictionless lane.
  - The lane labels (High, Medium, Low, None) look like row headings but are buttons ("Slide the puck: ..."); a learner may not realise they must be tapped.
  - Most of the stage is empty grey; the four lanes occupy a band in the middle and the 20 m scale is hard to read.
  - The "None" lane has a different (pinkish, ruled) background from the other three; it is a hint, not explained.
- FATAL: none established; the reveal gate could not be assessed from this artifact (driver limit). The Low-vs-None ordering is a presentation problem, not a false statement, but should be looked at.
