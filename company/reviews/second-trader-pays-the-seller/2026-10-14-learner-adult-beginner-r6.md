## 1. From the artifact

- **The title gives the answer.** The card title, visible above the question while I choose, is "A second trader pays the seller". The hook asks "Do they split the dollar?" and option 2 is "The seller". A learner who reads the title before the options is told what to pick, so the "split it" belief (the one the lesson exists to break) never gets committed to. The standard asks for a title that is a question or tension, and the prediction is what the reveal answers.
- Predict otherwise: the question is short. "Who gets it?" leaves "it" (the dollar of profit) a little vague. The locked stage shows S, T1 and B1, plus T2 and B2 dashed, with "?" readouts. The stage itself does not leak.
- Explore: "Add T2" lights both routes ("Both can resell for $1. Who outbids?"). "Outbid 1¢" moves the item to T2 (T1 keeps $0.00, T2 keeps $0.99, seller $0.01: "still worth it"). "10 outbids" goes to $0.11. "To the end" goes to $1.00, where everyone keeps $0.00 and "outbidding would lose money". "Remove T2" goes back to "Alone, T1 bids $0 and sells for $1". The why is on stage: at every step the losing trader would rather outbid by a cent, and the KEEPS readouts show the profit draining to the seller.
- Minor: the bar under the graph fills with the bid, but it has no label. I took it to be the bid level only after "To the end".
- Minor: the outbidding back and forth between T1 and T2 is visible only at the first step. "10 outbids" and "To the end" skip the alternation. The leader label is enough, though.
- Reveal: it quotes the path (T1 alone at $0, the bid climbing to $1.00, the seller taking $1). It ends on the idea ("competition hands it to the seller"). The model is named and disclosed.
- Minor: trust link 0/4 claims checked (draft; fact-checker's lane).
- No FATAL.
