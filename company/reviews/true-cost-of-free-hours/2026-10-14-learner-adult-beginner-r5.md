# Learner review (re-review r5): true-cost-of-free-hours
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult beginner who thinks a price tag is the whole cost.
Screenshots seen: yes (artifacts/walkthroughs/true-cost-of-free-hours/sheet.png)
Verdict: launch

## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: the stem sets up the forgone pay ("you could have earned $20 an hour"), so this is a real choice between the price tag ($50), the pay alone ($60) and both ($110). "$50" is the honest gut answer at my boundary. The locked stage shows only the fee block and "REAL COST ?". Nothing leaks.
- Explore: moving the slider to 3 h stacks a second block labelled "3 h × $20", and the bar reads $110. Switching to "Else: nothing paid" turns that block into a dashed empty outline and REAL COST drops to $50. That contrast is the core idea, and it is on stage: the extra cost exists only if the alternative was real.
- Where it is thinnest: the second block is labelled with arithmetic ("3 h × $20"), not with meaning. Nothing on stage names it as "pay you gave up". A learner who said $50 sees the bar jump, but the stage asserts why that block belongs in "cost" rather than showing it. The reveal supplies the reason ("what you give up to make it"). For a definitional idea like opportunity cost, that is probably the best a stage can do, but a label would carry it.
- Contradiction: the pill "Toy numbers: paid work was on offer" stays on screen after switching to "Else: nothing paid".
- The slider starts at 1 h, and the gate needs 3 h. This is harmless.
- Reveal: it retells the learner's own $110 and $50 and answers the hook's question ("Is that all you pay?").
