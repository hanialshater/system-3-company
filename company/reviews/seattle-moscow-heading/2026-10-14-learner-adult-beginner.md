- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: the map itself. "Straight lines from it are shortest routes" is a caption I must take on trust; this learner has never seen a map centred on a city and does not know why a straight line on it means shortest. The reveal is supposed to answer that.

Lost: the entire explanation, "both are far north, and on a ball the shortest way climbs over the top: across Canada, Greenland, the Arctic ...". Here the clip carries the explanation, so the learner sees the answer without the why.
- Told-not-shown: the "over the top" path is never traced on a familiar picture (e.g. a globe or the flat map with the same route curving north).
- Suggested fix: (1) shorten the reveal so the "over the top" sentence survives the fold; (2) add a toggle to show the same route on an ordinary flat map, where it bends north, so the learner links "straight here" with "curved there"; (3) consider a hook that does not name north.
