## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: the stem states the coin is 60% heads, the test bar (32 of 50) and what that bar means for a fair coin (3.2%). It asks how often you catch the bias: 90 / 60 / 34%. The locked stage shows an empty histogram axis (40%–80% heads) with a dashed "bar: 32 heads of 50" line. Nothing leaks. 90% ("it IS rigged, so a test will find it") is the honest beginner pick.
- Explore: "Run 10 experiments" gives 6 of 10 caught, which briefly made 60% look right. "Run all 100" gives 28 of 100: a pile of red (missed) bars, mostly left of the line, and a shorter green tail to the right. "Use 200 flips" squeezes the pile narrower, the bar moves to 113 of 200, and 80 of 100 are caught. Watching the pile narrow and slide across the line is the idea, on stage: with few flips a real bias usually hides inside the noise. I got it before the reveal.
- What I had to work out myself: why the pile sits mostly left of the line at 50 flips. The coin's true rate (60%) is not marked on the axis. Only the midpoint between "40% heads" and "80% heads" hints at it. With it marked, I would see that the 50-flip bar (64%) sits above the coin's real rate while the 200-flip bar (56.5%) sits below it. That is the cleanest reason one test fails and the other works.
- Reveal: 28 of 100, the bar of 32, 80 of 100 and the bar of 113 were all seen. The exact 33.6% / 86.0% figures are labelled as exact, beside my sampled ones. "'No significant result' from a small test is not 'no effect'" is the right takeaway, and the stage earned it.
