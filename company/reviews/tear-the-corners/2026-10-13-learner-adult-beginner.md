# Learner review: tear-the-corners
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who remembers basic school arithmetic and everyday life; has never studied the topic (may half-remember "angles in a triangle add to 180" without a reason; knows a right angle is a square corner).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess. The question frames a physical act (tear off three corners and fit them together), and options "a right angle / a straight line / it depends" are all imaginable. Someone who half-remembers 180 degrees can pick "straight line" without understanding; someone who does not will go with "it depends" or "right angle". The hook's "A fat triangle looks like it has more turn than a thin one" points toward "it depends" as the intuition to test. Good.
- Where understanding first broke: the first explore step. I tapped "Tear off the corners" and the log shows the corners "lined up at the top" with "70° + 65° + 45° = 180°: the corners fill a straight line." In the final screenshot the three coloured wedges (blue, yellow, green) sit as a small half-disc hugging the top corner of the triangle under a long dashed horizontal line. I never saw corners being torn off and moved; they are simply at the top. It is not clear to me that those three wedges are the same pieces as the three corners of the triangle below (the yellow one is the apex corner itself, and it never leaves its place). The sentence supplies the 180 degrees; the picture, small and at the top of a large empty stage, carries it only weakly.
- The three shape buttons (Tall, Flat, Lopsided) work and the captions change (70/65/45, 25/30/125, 40/110/30), always totalling 180. Repetition across three shapes is shown and does address "a fat triangle has more turn".
- Reveal earned? The result ("straight line, every time") yes. The "Why" paragraph ("the dashed line runs through the top corner, parallel to the base. Each bottom corner fits exactly in the gap between that line and a side") relies on parallel-line angle facts I do not have; the picture does not show the bottom corners moving into those gaps. This is told, not shown.
- Reveal gate: reachable after a tear and a shape change.
- Problems: (1) the tearing and moving is not shown, only the end state; (2) the wedges are small and the stage mostly empty; (3) the "why" needs parallel-line facts the learner has not met; (4) the right-angle option may be chosen by learners who confuse a corner with 90 degrees, and the stage never speaks to that.
- No FATAL.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief lists "the parallel line through the top corner" as something the learner does not know, and the objective stops at "fit together into a straight line"; the reveal's why-paragraph goes beyond the objective and is the part I found unearned. The objective itself (any triangle's corners fill a straight line; growing one shrinks the others) is served by the three shapes.
- The brief assumes "a straight line is a half turn"; that is how a "straight line" answer connects to 180, and the lesson never names the half turn, so the link to "turn" in the hook is implicit.
- Claims verified.

Verdict: revise
