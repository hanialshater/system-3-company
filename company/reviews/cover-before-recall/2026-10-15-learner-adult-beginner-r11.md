## Findings (written from the walkthrough before reading the brief or build.md)

Path walked: chose "Answer covered"; tapped Cover, Swap 1↔2, Swap 2↔3, Check; opened reveal.

1. **The prediction is a giveaway.** "Which check asks you to retrieve an answer? Answer covered / Answer beside the cue." Retrieving something you can see is not retrieving. No belief at stake.
2. **Where it first broke: I never retrieved anything.** Before I tapped Cover, the stage showed the answers (bell: lake, kite: plum, leaf: door) right above a pre-filled attempt (door, lake, plum). Then Cover hid the top row and I rearranged three words I had just been shown, with two swap buttons. That is recognising and sorting a given set, seconds after seeing the key. The lesson's own point ("seeing the answer and retrieving it are different") is undercut by the activity it uses.
3. **The swap mechanics took my attention.** Swap 1↔2 and Swap 2↔3 are a puzzle about positions, not about memory. I spent my effort on which swap to press.
4. **Reveal versus my question.** "Covering the answer removes the direct reading route. This reconstruction records one attempt, not a long-term memory score." True and careful, but the question a learner has is "does covering actually help me remember better than rereading?" The lesson does not say, even in words, that self-testing tends to beat rereading (if that is the claim, it needs to be stated and sourced; if not, the lesson has no payoff).

Fixes (for the builder):
- Show the pairs, then cover, then ask the learner to produce (pick from a larger set, or type) the answer for each cue. Do not pre-fill the attempt with exactly the three answers.
- State the payoff in the reveal (what covering does for later recall), with its claim and source.

Verdict reason: the activity shows the answers and then has the learner reorder those same three words, so it demonstrates recognition, not retrieval, and the reveal gives no reason covering is worth doing.

## After reading the brief (meta.json)

- Objective: 'Perform a closed-answer check before using feedback.' The stage does have a closed check (cover, then Check), so the procedure is delivered, but the attempt is pre-filled with the three answers, so it is a reordering, not a retrieval (finding 2).
- Fix route: demo-builder (activity); fact-checker if a benefit claim for self-testing is added to the reveal.
