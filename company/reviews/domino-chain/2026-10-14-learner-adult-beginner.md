# Learner review: domino-chain
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner; knows whole numbers go on forever and what "true for one case" means; has never heard of induction.
Screenshots seen: yes

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: Only the first few / All of them / Only the even ones. The question itself states the answer ("any falling domino knocks over the next"), so "All of them" is just reading the premise back. "Only the even ones" is not a belief anyone holds. There is no real surprise to resolve.
- Explore: "Push domino 1" drops all 12; "Move domino 6 out of reach" leaves 6 to 12 standing. Clear visuals; the broken-link case is the best part (it shows why the second fact is needed).
- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: the reveal. The hook's real question is "how could we *know* the millionth falls without pushing it?", but the stage only ever shows 12 physical dominoes falling, which is exactly the "watching it happen" the hook says we can't do. The step from 12 to every number, and the line "Dominoes picture it; they do not prove it", are told.
- Told-not-shown: the name and form of induction ("prove it for 1, prove true for n means true for n + 1"); the two-facts structure is only shown via domino 6.
- Layout: phone; reveal fits, no clipping. Large empty stage area.
- Suggested fix: (1) make the prediction carry tension, e.g. "Domino 1 falls; you have checked 1 knocks 2, 2 knocks 3 ... up to 100. Does the millionth fall?" with options that separate "checked cases" from "the rule for every n"; (2) add a "jump to domino 1,000,000" view that shows only the rule (n falls → n+1 falls) rather than more falling dominoes.

Verdict: revise
