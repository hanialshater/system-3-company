# Learner review (round 2): two-bets-split
lesson version: v2
boundary: an adult beginner with savings; has heard "don't put all your eggs in one basket" but thinks splitting just lowers returns.
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 3 frames, phone viewport). Re-review after the kit fix for clipped reveals (W-135); written fresh from this walkthrough.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: two independent bets, each +20% or −10%, half the money in each; chance of losing money? 50% / 25% / 0%. A good question; I chose 50% ("each bet loses half the time").
- Explore: one slider, "Bets the money is split across". On my path the slider moved from 1 to 3 in one go, and "Show me why" unlocked immediately. I never saw 2 bets, which is the case the question asks about. At 3 bets I saw 12.5% / 37.5% / 37.5% / 12.5% bars, "CHANCE OF A LOSS 12.5%", average +5%.
- Where understanding first broke: the reveal answers a case I never visited. "With two bets you lose only if both fall: ½ × ½ = 25%. Half the time one rises and one falls, for +5%." None of the 2-bet stage was shown to me; the reveal's key numbers (25%, the +5% middle bar) are quoted from a state I skipped. It also quotes "with 4 bets it is 31.3%", also unvisited. This is the known "reveal quotes values the learner never visited" pattern in its plain form.
- Even read cold, the reveal is clear and the caveat ("Real investments often fall together. Not advice.") is right.
- "WORST CASE −10%, 12.5%" is cryptic (a return and its chance in one box with a comma).
- Phone clipping (the reason for round 2): fixed. The full reveal including the caveat and trust link is visible; the top of the chart is scrolled off on the reveal frame, fine.
- Reveal earned: "average stays +5%" yes (I saw it at 3); "25% with two" no, told.

Fixes (owner: builder):
1. Require the 2-bet state before the reveal unlocks (e.g. make the slider snap through 2, or unlock only after the learner has seen 2), or have the reveal quote the bet counts the learner actually visited.
2. Show the 31.3% at 4 only if visited, or phrase it as a pointer ("try 4: the chance of some loss goes up again").
3. Split WORST CASE into "worst result −10%" and "its chance 12.5%".
