## Findings

- N4 / F5 FIX (the main one). The hook reads "How was the world's CO2 split between them?" with no "fossil-fuel". The brief's stage question and reveal say "fossil fuels", but the learner meets the hook and the title first. On all CO2 including land-use change, the UK's 1850 share is 4.5%, not 62% (note section 3, the same dataset, the same source). The lesson picks the measure that makes one industrial country dominant and erases clearing for farms. It is correct as stated and misleading as titled.
- N2 / P2 FIX. The current limit sentence ("rebuilt from records of coal and other fuels, and early years are the roughest") is not supported by what the research note opened, and it leaves out the largest omission: land-use emissions. Replace it.
- L4 / N4 FIX. The data's entity is "United Kingdom", a modern country code applied backwards (in 1850 the UK included all of Ireland, from memory); the stage says "Britain". Use "the United Kingdom" on labels and in the question, or say "Britain (the dataset's United Kingdom)" once.
- F2 / R3 PASS with a condition. "Britain produced 62%" is allowed as a measured quantity. The care line holds: no blame, no "who owes what", no today's policy. No moral word near the number, and the including-land-use number is a sentence, not a toggle (a second toggle would make the lesson an argument about whose emissions count, which is the "who owes what" ground).
- R1 PASS. Comparison only as a measured quantity with its uncertainty.
- Slug FIX (route, not wording). The slug and URL say "britain-made-most-co2": that is false on all CO2 and visible to a learner. Renaming a published-path slug is the chief of staff's and cm-history's call; I flag it, and a title fix alone leaves the URL. Recorded as an objection below.
- Master-branch pin: a publish risk, not a framing one. Re-read shares if OWID updates the file.
