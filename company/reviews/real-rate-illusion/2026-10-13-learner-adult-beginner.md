# Learner review: real-rate-illusion
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner; has a savings account, knows prices go up; has never compared the two rates.
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 4 frames)
Verdict: revise

## 1. From the artifact

- No missing idea at predict. "More" is the natural pull ("my balance goes up"). Good question.
- Explore shows it: a number line with "your $100 after a year: $103" (pin) and "today's $100 basket costs $105.00" (marker). At 1% the pin is green, "+2.0%", "buys more"; at 5% it turns red, "−1.9%", "buys less". Seeing the basket marker pass the pin is the lesson.
- Main finding (false "you saw"): the reveal says "You saw the colour flip exactly where prices rise 3%, the same as your interest." In this path I went 1% then tapped 5%; I never saw the flip, let alone at 3%. A reveal that tells the learner what they saw, when they did not, undermines trust in the rest. Either only say it if the slider passed 3% with a pause, or ask the learner to find the flip point.
- Told-not-shown: "(1.03 ÷ 1.05) − 1" and "roughly interest minus inflation" are told; acceptable as the closing rule.
- Layout: the "5%" quick chip floats to the right of the slider and looks like a label rather than a button. Large empty band between the line and the caption in explore frames; in the reveal the line shrinks to tiny labels.

## 2. After reading the brief

- Learner field (knows inflation erodes cash; savings pay interest) matches. Intent delivered apart from the misreported observation.
- Suggested fix: condition the "you saw the flip at 3%" sentence on the learner's actual path, or add a task "find where the colour flips"; style the 5% chip as a button.
