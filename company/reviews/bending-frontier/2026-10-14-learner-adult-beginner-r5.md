# Learner review (re-review r5): bending-frontier
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult beginner who knows opportunity cost and how to read a curve, and assumes swapping one good for another has a fixed price.
Screenshots seen: yes (artifacts/walkthroughs/bending-frontier/sheet.png)
Verdict: revise

## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: the stem gives "corn = 100 − wheat²", so the answer is arithmetic (100 − 81 = 19). A formula-shy learner guesses; a formula-comfortable one does not need the stage. The hook ("So does the tenth. Right?") hints that the answer is no.
- Explore: pushing wheat to 10 draws a red staircase under the curve: tiny steps near the top, long ones at the right, "−19" and "LAST TON COST 19 corn"; one step back shows 17. That costs rise is seen.
- Reveal: "1, 3, 5, ... 19" is a list the stage only partly labels (−17, −19). **The cause ("land is not all alike ... the last wheat takes the best corn fields") is told only.** Nothing on stage is a field; the curve is a formula. A learner leaves knowing that it bends, and has to take the reason on trust. The brief's "why a production frontier bows outward" is the part not earned.
- Layout: the reveal frame scrolls the chart so its top is cut off; readouts stay visible.

## 2. Earlier findings (v1 review; lesson unchanged at v1)

1. Prediction is algebra, not intuition: **not fixed**.
2. Why it bends (unequal land) told, not shown: **not fixed**.
3. Chart shrinks or crops at reveal: **not fixed** (now cropped rather than shrunk).

The earlier review gave launch with these as notes. I give revise because the "why" is one of the two things the learner is meant not to know, and the stage gives no hold on it.

## 3. New findings

- **Revise:** put the reason on stage, for example by tinting each wheat ton by the kind of field it takes (poor for corn, then best corn land) so the growing steps line up with visibly better corn land.
- Minor: drop the formula from the stem, show the first drop of 1 on stage, and ask about the last ton, so the prediction tests the fixed-price intuition.
- No FATAL.
