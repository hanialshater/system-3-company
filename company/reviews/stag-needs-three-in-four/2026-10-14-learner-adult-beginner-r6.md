## 1. From the artifact

- **Where it loses me (top): the title gives the answer away.** "The stag needs three in four" sits above the question "How sure must you be your partner hunts stag?", and one option is "At least 75%". A first-time learner who reads the title before the options has been told the answer, and the prediction is no longer a guess. Board pattern W-154 (locked stage or framing leaks the answer). The hook line itself ("Both hunters prefer the stag. So why might both settle for a hare?") is a good tension and could carry the card on its own.
- Predict: the stem is compact and complete (4 / 0 / 3). The locked chart is empty with "?" readouts, so the stage does not leak.
- Explore: the 50 / 75 / 100% buttons and the slider move a dot along the stag line against the flat "hare 3" line, with a shaded "stag better" zone right of 75%. The readouts give stag 2 vs hare 3 (hare) at 50%, 3 vs 3 (tie) at 75%, 4 vs 3 at 100%, and 3.6 vs 3 at 90%. The crossing is seen and the threshold is felt. The "why" of the threshold (stag = 4 × confidence, against a sure 3) is visible as the straight line from 0 to 4 meeting 3.
- **The second half of the reveal is told.** "Both 'both stag' and 'both hare' are stable: each is the best reply to itself ... doubt alone can lock them into hare" is the answer to the hook's question ("why might both settle for a hare?"). The stage shows one hunter's best reply against their belief about one partner. It never shows the two-player picture, where if I expect hare, hare is my best reply, and the partner reasons the same way. A learner gets "I need 75% confidence" from the stage and "so both can get stuck at hare" from trust.
- Minor: "worth" means expected payoff and is never named. It works for a beginner from the line, but "4 × your confidence" is the first time the multiply is said.
- Minor: "Why trust this? (0/4 claims checked)" (fact-checker item, DRAFT). At the reveal the stage is scrolled and the model chip is cropped.

## 3. After reading the brief

- The doesNotKnow item, the 75% trust threshold, is shown well by the crossing lines. But the title states it before the prediction, so the learner never gets to be wrong about it. Top finding: the title leaks the answer.
- The objective also asks the learner to explain "that both-stag and both-hare are each stable, so doubt can lock in the worse outcome". That is the misconception's direct answer ("if both prefer stag, both will hunt stag"), and it is one told sentence. Under "Show the why you promised" it needs a stand-in on the existing chart, for example marking that at low confidence your best reply is hare, which is the partner's reason to doubt you in turn, and the reveal pointing at it. A told sentence alone is a revise. How to show it is the builder's call.
