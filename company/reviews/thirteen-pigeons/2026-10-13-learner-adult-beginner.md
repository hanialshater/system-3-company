# Learner review: thirteen-pigeons
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who remembers basic school arithmetic and everyday life; has never studied the topic (knows there are 12 months; has not met the pigeonhole principle).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess between 12 and 13 (24 is filler). "12" is the tempting answer ("12 months, 12 people") and the hook's word "certain" nudges a careful person to 13. The locked stage shows twelve empty month tiles with "SEATED ?" and "MONTHS USED ?", so nothing leaks. Good tension for the walkthrough's pick of 12.
- Where understanding first broke: the panel says "Seat people so that nobody shares a month, for as long as you can." I had to work out that tapping a month tile is how a person is seated; the tiles are not labelled as buttons. The first tap gave "Person 2 waiting. 11 empty months left." which then made it clear. A small badge appears on each used month (1, 2, 3 ...). I read it as the person's number, not a head-count in that month; if a month got a second person I do not know what the badge would show, which I could not tell from this path.
- What works: the caption "Person 7 waiting. 6 empty months left." (final screenshot) changes with each tap, so the countdown of free months is visible, and the counters "SEATED 6 / MONTHS USED 6 of 12" move in step. The learner is invited to try to avoid collisions, which is the pigeonhole idea as a game.
- The path to the key state is long: all 12 months must be filled before the 13th person forces a repeat. The reveal stayed locked ("Try it first", greyed) after 6 seatings; no message says how many are needed or that the lock is waiting for a 13th. In this walkthrough that is a driver limit, not a lesson flaw. For a real learner it means about 13 taps in a 60-second lesson and a button that gives no hint why it is locked.
- Roughly the lower half of the stage is empty.
- Reveal: not seen, so I cannot say whether the answer 13 and the "why" are earned. 
- Problems: (1) tapping a tile as "seating a person" is not signposted; (2) the badge's meaning is ambiguous; (3) the lock gives no hint about what unlocks it; (4) many taps before the point lands; (5) empty lower half of the stage.
- No FATAL.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The brief's misconception ("sharing is only likely, never certain") is exactly what the hook's "certain" addresses; the stage's game format (avoid sharing as long as you can) targets it.
- The objective hinges on the 13th person, who is only reached after twelve taps; the brief does not say how the learner is expected to know to go on past twelve. My finding (3) about the unexplained lock is the practical version of that.
- Boundary matches; no extra vocabulary assumed.
- Claims verified.

Verdict: revise
