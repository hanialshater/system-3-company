## Findings

**F1. Reveal-fit at 360x740: the top of the stage is cut off. It does not block.** (known limit, design-lead's call)
Measured on every possible last switch (6₁ c1-c6, 5₂ c1-c5) with `fit.mjs`, comparing the stage and drawing against the top of the scrolling `.card-body` after the reveal opens:

| Viewport | Stage top cut | Drawing curve cut | Ring |
|---|---|---|---|
| 360x740 | 42 px (name 3₁ or 4₁), 62 px (name 0₁, longer reveal text) | 14-15 px, or 34-35 px for 0₁ | never fully hidden; partly clipped (11 px on 6₁ c3, 17 px on 5₂ c3, ring is about 37 px tall) |
| 390x844 | 0 | 0 | whole |
| 360x640 (outside the ask) | 137-157 px | 109-131 px | fully hidden for 6 of 11 switches |

So the builder's 62 px reproduces, and is the 0₁ case; the common 4₁ and 3₁ cases cut 42. The card body is already scrolled to its maximum (scrollTop 42 or 62 of 42 or 62), so the content above is one swipe away (`360x740-scrolltop.png`). Readout, Seen line, chips and the reveal text are fully visible; the cut part is the top loop of the knot and the top of the box, plus, on crossing 3 of either knot, a corner of the ring. Why I say it does not block at 360x740: the learner can still see the ring's centre, the readout and the sentence, and the drawing's other crossings and its count stay countable. It is a weaker picture, not a wrong one. The design review (`company/design/reviews/2026-10-16-draw-a-table-knot-s4.md`, line 38) records 62 px (21 to 102 across variants) at 360x740 before D3 and a reveal crop "down by 20 px" after it; my 42-62 px after D3 is inside that spread, so D3 did not remove the crop, and I could not tell from the record what the reveal-phase target was. At 360x640 the same cause hides the ring on most paths; that viewport is outside this brief, so I record the numbers and leave the call to design-lead.

**F2. The stage stays live after the reveal opens, so it can disagree with the reveal sentence. Low.**
At reveal 8 of 9 stage buttons are enabled (only the active chip is disabled). From a reveal on 6₁ c1 (name 4₁): tapping crossing 1 again switches it back, so the readout says "Name: 6₁" while the sentence and the Seen line still say 4₁; Reset does the same; the 5₂ chip shows a 5₂ drawing under a sentence about 6₁. Tapping a different crossing that gives the same name leaves stage and sentence in agreement; a different name was not tried. This needs a deliberate tap after the reveal and the Seen line stays right, so I rate it low. The kit passes `locked` only as `phase === "predict"` (`src/kit/Lesson.tsx` line 82), so the stage is never locked at reveal; whether that is intended is for the builder and design-lead.

**F3. Observation, not a failure.** The reveal button reads "Try it first" while locked, with no hint that a switch is required; a learner who only changes chips sees a dead-looking reveal. Already known (memory: same pattern on NCM lessons); here the explore hint above the stage does say "Tap a crossing".

## What this does not show

A passing browser run does not tell us whether a learner understands that the first number is the fewest crossings. Feed swipe-away and `feed.spec.ts` were not exercised. I did not test a real touch drag across the feed, and did not test 320 px widths. The stage-at-reveal check was done by reading the DOM (ring, aria, readouts) plus screenshots at 390 and 360x740 for A and the three colour/motion combinations; the other 24-run screenshots exist in `artifacts/explore17/` but I did not view each.
