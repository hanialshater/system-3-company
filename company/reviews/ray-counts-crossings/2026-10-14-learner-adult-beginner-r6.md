## 1. From the artifact

These findings were formed from the transcript and sheet before I opened meta.json.

- **Where it loses me (top): there is no tension in the prediction.** The locked stage draws the U shaded and the point at (7, 4), with the caption "(7, 4): 0 crossings, even, so outside." That caption states the parity rule before I commit. The correct option repeats it ("Outside: it crosses an even number of edges"), so the rule's words point to the answer. The shaded picture also shows the gap at (3, 4) is plainly unshaded, so looking gives the right answer too, while the hook says "Count, don't look". A first-time learner has no reason to think the point is inside. The wrong guess the reveal is built to answer is unlikely to happen, and the one I made in this run ("Inside: it is 1 crossing") felt like a guess against both the picture and the caption.
- Explore: five position buttons. Each moves the point, draws the ray, numbers each crossing (1, 2, 3) on thick green edges, and updates CROSSINGS and VERDICT. (1, 4) gives 3 and inside, (3, 4) gives 2 and outside, (5, 4) and (3, 1) give 1 and inside, (7, 4) gives 0 and outside. The counting is clear and responds at once.
- **The why is told, not shown.** The reveal's reason is "every crossing flips inside and outside, and far right is always outside". Nothing on stage stands for the flip: the ray is one dashed line from end to end, and nothing marks which stretches lie inside the shape and which lie outside. The learner sees counts and a verdict, which is the rule, not why the rule works. So "odd means inside" is taken on trust.
- Reveal: it matches the counts seen (2 at x = 4 and x = 6, 3 from (1, 4)). The corner tie-rule sentence is fine as context. At the reveal the stage is back at (7, 4) and scrolled, with the top cropped, so the 2-crossing picture the reveal describes is not on screen.
- Minor: "ray crosses 1 edges" in the picture alt text.

## 3. After reading the brief

- Misconception: "a point between the arms is inside because it is near the middle". The shaded stage already shows that point is outside, so the lesson does not provoke this belief. A shape where looking misleads (a spiral or a deep notch where the point looks enclosed), or an unshaded outline, would give "count, don't look" something to do. That is the builder's call, not mine.
- Objective: "explain that the ray flips inside/outside at every boundary crossing". Under "Show the why you promised" the flip must stand on stage, for example as ray stretches tinted in or out, alternating at each numbered crossing, with the reveal pointing at them. Today it is one told sentence. That alone is a revise.
