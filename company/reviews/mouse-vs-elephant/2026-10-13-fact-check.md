# Fact-check: mouse-vs-elephant (2026-10-13)

Checker: fact-checker (independent). Inline node arithmetic. Stage text from the walkthrough transcript; Demo.tsx read (total = ratio^0.75, per gram = ratio^-0.25).

| Claim | Result | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| c1 | verified | Kleiber (1932), Hilgardia 6: 315-353, and West, Brown & Enquist (1997), Science 276: 122-126, give the 3/4 scaling of basal metabolic rate with body mass. The lesson calls it a rough rule. |
| c2 | verified | 20^4 = 160,000, so 160,000^0.25 = 20 exactly, 160,000^0.75 = 8,000, per gram 1/20. 3,200 kg / 20 g = 160,000. |
| c3 | verified | 10^0.75 = 5.623. Stage values for masses 1, 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000, 160,000 x mouse: 1, 5.6, 31.6 (32), 177.8 (178), 1,000, 8,000. |
| c4 | verified | White & Seymour (2003), PNAS 100: 4046-4049 ("Mammalian basal metabolic rate is proportional to body mass^2/3", exponent about 0.686) and Dodds, Rothman & Weitz (2001), J. Theor. Biol. 209: 9-27 (re-examination of the 3/4 law) support "debated, nearer 2/3". For reference: with an exponent of 2/3 the elephant would use 2,947 times a mouse's energy instead of 8,000, so the lesson's number depends on the rule (it says so). |
| c5 | verified (convention) | House mice are roughly 15-30 g; 3,200 kg is a modest elephant (adults range from about 2,000 to 6,000 kg). Rounded masses are labelled on the stage. |
