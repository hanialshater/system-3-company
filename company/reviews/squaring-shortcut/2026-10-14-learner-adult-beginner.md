# Learner review: squaring-shortcut
lesson version: v1
boundary: adult beginner (knows a power is repeated multiplication; has not thought about reusing results).
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, phone, 6 frames)

## 1. From the artifact

- Where I first needed an idea I didn't have: that "multiply any two numbers you already have" lets me multiply 9 by 9. The stem says it, but my default is 15 (one more 3 each time). The options 4/8/15 frame it well.
- Explore shows it: arcs on a 1–16 line that double in width (1→2→4→8→16), four of them, against 15 small scallops underneath for ×3. The visual contrast of 4 big jumps vs 15 small ones is the lesson, and it is clear.
- Needed-but-unstated: the number line is the exponent k, not the value. "3^k means k threes multiplied together" appears only on the locked stage; after that the line is just 1..16.
- Told-not-shown: "no step can do more than double it, so 3 steps reach at most 3^8" is the proof that 4 is the fewest. It is told and is the right sentence; nothing on stage tries 3 steps and falls short.
- Stale caption: after "×3 once" the stage still reads "3^8 × 3^8 = 3^16" while the ×3 tile says "at 3^2".
- Layout: the final word ("millions") is just clipped on phone (W-135); not load-bearing.

## 2. After reading the brief (version only)

- Version 1, 5 claims shown as checked.

## Fix suggested

Label the line "exponent k (3^k)"; update the caption on ×3 presses ("3^1 × 3 = 3^2").
