## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess and an honest trap. "Twice as far, half as bright" (50 lux) is what I would say; 25 and 12.5 are both plausible to someone who half-remembers "squared". "Lux" is just a unit on a number; I take 100 lux as "100 brightness".
- Explore: the 3 m view is the best image in the lesson. A lamp, a cone of light, and a grid of 9 squares with the one-square "page" outlined makes "same light, more squares" visible. The readouts LIGHT SPREAD OVER 9 squares / 11.1 lux go together.
- Where understanding first broke: the hook, "How much of its light does it lose?", versus the caption "Same light, spread over more squares." The hook says light is lost; the explore says none is lost, it is spread. I had to reconcile two pictures of what happens to the light on my own.
- Reveal earned? Yes for 3 m (9 squares, 11.1 lux). The 2 m case, which is what the question and the reveal's first line are about, is not in the recorded steps (2.5, 3, 1.5 m), so "At 2 m the page got 25 lux ... covers 4 squares" is a number I did not see. Likely a slider-step or driver effect, not shown to be a lesson fault.
- Problems:
  - Hook wording ("lose") invites the wrong picture (light disappearing) that the lesson then has to undo; "lose" is never addressed in the reveal.
  - The prompt says "Move the page to 2 m and count the squares" but the recorded slider stops skip exactly 2 m; if the slider cannot land on 2 m the instruction cannot be followed.
  - The reveal says the beam is "twice as wide and twice as tall" at 2 m; the picture is a perspective drawing and I cannot check this by eye (the 1 m / 2 m / 3 m labels at the bottom do not line up with where the page sits in perspective).
  - "Brightness falls as one over distance squared" is introduced as a formula in words (one over distance squared) after a lot of numbers; a beginner may read it, nod, and not connect it to "4 squares -> a quarter".
  - "Inverse-square law, for a small source with nothing reflecting" adds a qualification (small, nothing reflecting) that I cannot relate to anything shown; it may sound like a hedge that lets the rule fail in my kitchen.
- FATAL: none found from the artifact.
