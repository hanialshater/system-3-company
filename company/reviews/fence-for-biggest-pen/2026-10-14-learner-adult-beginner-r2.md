# Learner review (round 2): fence-for-biggest-pen
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult beginner who knows area = length × width; half-believes the same fence always encloses the same area.
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 11 frames, phone viewport). Re-review after the kit fix for clipped reveals (W-135); written fresh from this walkthrough.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: 20 m of fence, rectangular pen; which shape holds the most? 1 × 9 / 5 × 5 / 4 × 6. Fine, quick. Note: the hook asks "Does every pen hold the same?", but "they are all the same" is not an option, so the belief the hook raises cannot be chosen. A learner who holds it has to pick something else.
- Explore: nine size buttons; each draws the pen on a 1 m grid with its area and the fence sum ("Fence: 4 + 6 + 4 + 6 = 20 m"). Area climbs 9, 16, 21, 24, 25 and falls back symmetrically; the 5 × 5 pen turns green. Seeing the long thin 1 × 9 strip next to the fat square is convincing. Good.
- Small display overlap: at 1 × 9 the area label "9 m²" collides with the side label "9 m" ("9 m²9 m"). Cosmetic.
- Reveal: 9 m² and 25 m² were both seen; "4 × 6 = 25 − 1 = 24" matches. No unvisited values. "Area is 25 minus the square of the distance from 5" is told, and checkable against 21 (25 − 4) and 16 (25 − 9), which I saw.
- Phone clipping (the reason for round 2): fixed. Full reveal and trust link visible.
- Where understanding first broke: nowhere.
- Reveal earned: yes.

Fixes (owner: builder, optional): add "All hold the same" as an option so the hook's misconception can be committed to; nudge the 1 × 9 label so the two numbers do not touch.

- Objective (area peaks at 25 for 5 × 5, falls by the square of the distance from 5) is delivered.
