## The look
The brief compares quarters of 86 French departments by recorded property crime per 100,000 people in 1825-1830 against literacy, and reads one correlation (r = 0.37). It is a one-period cross-section of places measured by the state, not a trend in crime or violence, and no people or group is compared: departments are administrative units and none is named (care line). So it stays off the route (R8 is not hit; R1 is not hit). The risks are in the reading:
1. **Recorded is not committed.** The figures are crimes brought to court (stated in the limit sentence), which also depend on policing and reporting. Wealthier, more literate departments may simply have had more to steal and more cases pursued. The recall item covers confounding; the reveal's "In this record, more schooling did not go with less crime" is accurate but can be quoted against schooling.
2. **Ecological reading.** A relation across departments says nothing about literate individuals, and a learner can slide from "departments" to "literate people commit more crime".

## Changes (cm-history)
1. Limit sentence: "Where it stops: these are cases brought to court in 1825 to 1830, which depend on policing as well as crime; they compare departments, not people, and two numbers moving together say nothing, alone, about what caused what." (Adds "depend on policing" and "compare departments, not people"; recount to the cap.)
2. Reveal's middle sentence: "In this record, the more literate departments did not have fewer cases brought to court." replaces "In this record, more schooling did not go with less crime", which reads as a verdict on schooling.
