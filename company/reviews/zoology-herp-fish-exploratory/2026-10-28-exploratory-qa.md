# Reptile, amphibian and fish batch: exploratory browser QA

Date: 2026-10-28. Scope: 25 new drafts—13 `zoo-herp-*` lessons and 12 `fish-*` lessons. The 25 exact slugs are in `artifacts/zoology-herp-fish-exploration/slugs.json`. Tested 25 lesson components, their 25 metadata files, and four shared Lesson, controls, DemoCard and stylesheet files; `source-hashes.json` in the same directory records their Git blob hashes, which were unchanged when checked after the run. The branch HEAD at selection was `54668ef203622059ef061984c9c8c3739b458138`; the shared working tree was dirty.

## Method and limits

A Chromium component harness bundles the actual lesson components, DemoCard, Lesson, controls and CSS, supplies the lesson metadata, and stubs DemoHost and Next Link. It is not a Next.js application or the protected QA contract. For each lesson on 390×844 and 1280×800, a fresh page chose the last prediction option, then activated enabled controls in DOM order (up to 16; range controls received two right-arrow presses). Checks covered initial prediction lock, no initial reveal, reveal gating, stage change for exercised controls, ability to reveal, explanation over 120 characters, console/page errors, horizontal document overflow and the 24 px control-size contract minimum. The 390×844 reveal geometry was compared with the card body for top-stage and bottom-reveal cropping.

## Results

25 lessons × 2 viewports = 50 runs, zero scripted failures, zero captured page/console errors, no horizontal document overflow and no measured 390×844 stage/reveal crop. Every lesson had a 33 px-high stage control, meeting the existing 24 px contract minimum but smaller than the 44 px target used for the revised prototypes. This observation is a touch-target improvement to review, not a claim of contract failure.

All 25 phone-reveal screenshots were captured; only these nine were actually opened and visually inspected: `fish-countercurrent-oxygen`, `fish-lateral-line-water-motion`, `fish-parrotfish-scrape`, `fish-swim-bladder-buoyancy`, `zoo-herp-crocodile-bird-branches`, `zoo-herp-frog-tadpole-change`, `zoo-herp-snake-tongue-chemicals`, `zoo-herp-turtle-ribs-shell`, and `zoo-herp-lizard-sun-shade`. Their stage and reveal were visible in the sampled screenshots. Several animal depictions are very abstract: the snake tongue diagram uses a fish-shaped oval, the lizard's sun/shade scene uses green ovals without legs, and the parrotfish scraping example uses a generic lens shape. The diagrams show a simplified relationship, but visual and learning design should decide whether the animal is recognizable enough for the lesson. This browser run does not establish that the pictured causal mechanism is scientifically accurate or that learners understand it.

Evidence: `artifacts/zoology-herp-fish-exploration/results.json`, `probe.log`, `probe.mjs`, `server.mjs`, `entry.tsx`, `host.tsx`, `slugs.json`, `source-hashes.json` and `<slug>-phone.png` in that directory. Only one prediction option per lesson was exercised; answer mapping across all choices, keyboard, reload, smaller phones, theme changes, feed interactions, full routes, comprehensive accessibility, independent science review and learner evidence were not tested. No launch verdict.
