# Learner review (re-review r5): ramp-same-work
lesson version: v2
boundary: an adult with school-level maths and science, new to the topic (knows ramps make lifting easier; does not know a 50 kg crate weighs about 490 N).
Screenshots seen: yes (artifacts/walkthroughs/ramp-same-work/sheet.png, 5 frames)
Verdict: revise

## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: the title and hook ask about work ("Does a ramp save you work? ... So it must save effort. Does it?"), but the question asks about the push force (490 / 123 / 31 N). These are different misconceptions. A learner who correctly picks 123 N (the ramp makes the push smaller, true) gets "prediction held" while still believing the ramp saves work. The question the title raises is never predicted. This is where the lesson first lost me. I came in for "does it save work?", and the commitment I was asked to make was about force.
- Leak (screen-reader path): the locked stage's picture description reads "Ramp 2 m long, push 245 N". That is half of 490 for a 2 m ramp, which points straight at 123 N for 4 m. It is not visible on screen, but it is read aloud.
- Explore: the slider at 4 m shows PUSH 122.6 N at once. "Push it up" runs the work counter to 490.5 J and logs "4 m: 123 N × 4 m = 490.5 J". "Lift straight up" logs "1 m: 491 N × 1 m = 490.5 J". The equal-work comparison is on stage, side by side. Good.
- Arithmetic snag: "123 N × 4 m = 490.5 J" does not multiply out (123 × 4 = 492). A beginner who checks will stumble. Show 122.6 N in the log, as the reveal does.
- Layout: the PUSH and WORK DONE chips are too narrow. "122.6 N" and "490.5 J" wrap onto two lines.
- Reveal: it answers the title's question with on-stage numbers. "Weight × height ÷ length" is told, which is fine.

1. "50 kg weighs about 490 N" was missing: **fixed** (it is in the question).
2. The hook asks about work but the prediction asks about the push: **not fixed**. This is now the top finding.

## 3. New findings

- Blocking, top (carried): make the prediction ask about work, or put a work question alongside the force one. For example: "Compared with lifting it straight up, the work you do on the 4 m ramp is: less / the same / more". As it stands, a correct force guess counts as "prediction held" while the "ramp saves work" belief is never challenged.
- Should fix: the locked picture description "push 245 N" leaks the halving pattern to screen-reader users.
- Minor: log "122.6 N × 4 m = 490.5 J" so the arithmetic checks, and widen the readout chips.
