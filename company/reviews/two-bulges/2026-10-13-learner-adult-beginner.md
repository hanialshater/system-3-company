## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: not a real guess. The hook says "So why does the sea rise twice a day?" and the title is "Why two tides a day?", which hands over the answer to the question "how many high tides in one day?" before I touch anything. Anyone who reads the hook picks 2. The options "1" and "4" are decoys with no pull. The locked stage shows the Earth seen from the North Pole as a plain ring of water (no bulges yet), with the Moon at right and a dot for "your coast". It is a fair model of the setup, but "from above the North Pole" is a viewpoint the beginner has to infer from the label "North Pole" in the middle.
- Where understanding first broke: the reason, not the count. The hook's real question is why, and the stage shows two bulges, but nothing on it says why the far side bulges. The reveal's explanation, "the Moon pulls the near-side water a little harder than the Earth's centre, and the centre a little harder than the far-side water; what matters is that difference", is the part I could not follow without a picture of differing pull arrows; the stage has no force arrows.
- What was SHOWN: the ring becomes an oval with two bulges lined up with the Moon (the picture at 12 h 25 min shows my dot on the left, on the bulge away from the Moon: High tide). "Coast faces the Moon" gives High tide at 0 h and "Coast faces away" gives High tide at 12 h 25 min. So I can see high tide on both sides. But the bulges were already drawn the moment I opened the explore; nothing builds them up, and only High tide is ever shown: I never saw a low tide, so I never watched the water at my coast go down and up.
- Reveal vs stage: 12 h 25 min and "two per day" are on the stage. "24 h 50 min lunar day" is told (the slider tops at about 24.8 h, not labelled). "Some coasts get only one tide a day" is told with no support. "Exaggerated" is flagged in the caption, which is honest.
- The slider "Time" runs in hours of a "lunar day" with no definition.
- Is this geography? It sits in the geography domain, and it has no map; fine, but the title card says Geography & Maps.
- Phone legibility: the diagram is large and clear; the Moon is small and grey; the red dot is easy to see. Reveal clipped at the bottom in the screenshot, so the explanation of the far bulge was cut off mid-paragraph.

- Problems: (1) hook and title give away the answer; (2) why the far bulge exists is told, not shown; (3) no low tide ever shown; (4) "lunar day" undefined; (5) the claim of one tide on some coasts is unsupported on the stage.
