## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a guess on pure intuition: the numbers (5, 50, 500 km) have nothing to hang on. The question is also heavy: "frictionless spinning platform like the Earth at 45°N", "unsteered", "100 m/s (360 km/h)", "straight-line aim". A beginner will struggle to hold the setup. The hook ("The Earth turns under anything that moves...") does not give the number. Locked stage: a small, nearly empty box (about a third of the card width) with an "aim" dashed line, "start", and a "500 km" scale bar in tiny text.
- Where understanding first broke: the premise. "Spinning platform like the Earth at 45°N" is never explained: why would a platform at 45°N drift east? The idea that a north-moving object keeps its eastward speed from the equatorial side... nothing in the question or stage tells me. I accepted the toy as a black box.
- What was SHOWN: "Send it 1,000 km" gives 472 km (a long, bent red path with the drift labelled); 100 km gives 5.2 km; 10 km gives 52 m; moving latitude to 75°N at 10 km gives 70 m. The scale bar changes with each send (500 km, 50 km, 5 km), so the drawn path always looks the same size, which hides how different the drifts are; the numbers do it instead. A learner may think the picture shows the same drift each time.
- Reveal vs stage: 472 km, 5.2 km and 52 m are on the stage. The reveal says "About 470 km" for the same thing. NOT shown: "the drift grows with the square of the time", "9,000 times as much (a bit under 100^2)", "zero at the equator and strongest at the poles" (the stage's 75°N run gave 70 m, versus 52 m at 45°N; the equator was never tried), and "shapes winds and ocean currents". "Coriolis effect" is named at the very end, undefined. The line "this toy cannot show whether it steers a draining sink" is honest.
- The question's own option "About 500 km" is the right answer while the unit story (1,000 km at 360 km/h is nearly 3 hours) is never mentioned; the learner is never told the trip takes about 2.8 hours, so "the ground turns under it" has no time to attach to.
- Phone legibility: the locked stage is nearly unreadable (tiny fonts, tiny box); the open stage is clear with large labels. Reveal clipped at the bottom in the screenshot (the squared-time line is cut).
- Reveal earned: the 470 km figure yes; the physics of why (square of time, latitude dependence) is told, not shown.
- Problems: (1) the setup cannot be held without prior physics; (2) autoscaled scale bar hides the size differences; (3) square law, equator, poles, winds told; (4) "Coriolis" appears undefined; (5) travel time never stated.
- Fatal? No false statement found, but the lesson is hard to follow for this beginner. Not FATAL; strongly revise.
