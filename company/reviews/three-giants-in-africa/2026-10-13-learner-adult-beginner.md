# Learner review: three-giants-in-africa
lesson version: v?
boundary: an adult who has used maps and a globe and knows the continents; has never studied geography or map projections.

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess, and the hook ("On most world maps Africa looks like one big country. Try fitting the giants inside it.") does not say the result, though it invites the surprise. Most beginners know Africa is big but not that big; "fit with room to spare" is the answer the hook seems to suggest, and it is correct, so a reader tuned to the hook picks it. The locked stage shows Africa (blue) alone on an unfamiliar tilted map, with three buttons and "?" readouts. The caption "Equal Earth map: drawn areas are true areas" is the only hint of why the picture can be trusted.
- Where understanding first broke: nowhere in the main idea. Each button drops a coloured country over Africa, the bar fills and the percentage rises: US 31%, US plus China 63%, plus India 73%, with "ROOM LEFT 8.0 M km²". With all three placed I can see blue still showing at the west coast, Madagascar and the south, so it is visible that Africa is not full.
- Honesty problems: the pieces are shown rotated and stretched to fit: the US appears with Alaska pointing up and sits at an angle, China and the US overlap along a visible seam and India overlaps the US; and some shapes poke past Africa's coast (China's western bulge at the Atlantic). The caption admits "about 3% of the pieces overlap a coast or each other", in tiny print at the bottom. Without the caption a beginner could suspect cheating. The overlap also makes it hard to judge "room left" by eye: the visible blue is more like a quarter, so it roughly agrees.
- Reveal vs stage: 22.0 M km², 73%, 30.0 M km² and 8.0 M km² are on the stage (22.0 is not shown as a number; only 73% and 8.0, but the figure is implied). "Bigger than the three largest you moved put together" is a slip: the US, China and India are not the three largest countries (Russia and Canada are larger than the US and India), so "the three largest" is false as written. This is a content error a beginner who knows Russia is the biggest country could catch. NOT shown: the Mercator inflation of the US and China ("sit farther from the equator than most of Africa and get inflated more"), told. 
- Another unshown statement: "You moved each country across the globe without stretching it": the stage does not show a globe, only a flat map.
- Phone legibility: good: large map, bold labels, bright colours, a clear progress bar; the caption and its important 3% note are tiny. Reveal clipped at the bottom in the screenshot (the false-looking "three largest" sentence ends off-screen).
- Reveal earned: the numbers yes; the Mercator explanation told; one sentence wrong.
- Problems: (1) "the three largest" is false (US, China, India are not the three largest countries); (2) overlap and rotation reduce trust, flagged only in tiny print; (3) Mercator inflation told, not shown; (4) "without stretching" claimed about a flat map.
- Not FATAL on the main idea (the 73% result is shown and true), but one reveal sentence states something false and must be fixed before launch.
