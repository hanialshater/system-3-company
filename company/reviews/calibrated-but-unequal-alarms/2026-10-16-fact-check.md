## Counterexample used for c4 (re-runnable)

Score levels 0.1, 0.3 (below the cut-off) and 0.6, 0.9 (flagged). In both groups each level's reoffend rate equals its score, so the score is calibrated within each group. Group 1 has 100, 100, 100 and 100 people at the four levels. Group 2 has 0, 320, 90 and 340.
- G1: positives 10+30+60+90 = 190 of 400 (47.5%); FP = 40+10 = 50 of 210 negatives (23.81%); FN = 40 of 190 (21.05%).
- G2: positives 0+96+54+306 = 456 of 750 (60.8%); FP = 36+34 = 70 of 294 (23.81%); FN = 96 of 456 (21.05%).

How I found it: with two flagged and two unflagged levels there is a one-parameter family that keeps FNR and FPR fixed. With a single unflagged level and two flagged levels, the two equalities fix the mix, so the base rate is fixed and no counterexample exists. The lesson's two-level case is the extreme of that: equal errors force equal base rates unless the score is perfect.
