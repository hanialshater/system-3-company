## Findings (written from the walkthrough before reading build.md or meta.json)

1. **Where understanding first broke.** At the question: "high risk (80% reoffend) or low (20%), true in both groups." For this mind "true in both groups" is the hard clause; it is not obvious that "true" means "of the people labelled high, 8 in 10 really reoffend, in A and in B". The hook says "equally accurate", the question says "true", the reveal says "honest", and the last reveal sentence says "calibrated". Four words for one idea, and "calibrated", the word that carries the generalisation, is never tied to the other three on screen. A beginner reading "no score that makes mistakes is calibrated with equal errors" may read "calibrated" as a new technical condition. Non-blocking: the toy numbers carry the idea without the word; one bridge ("honest, or calibrated: ...") would close it. Note to the CM.
2. **What the prediction felt like.** A real choice, but tilted. The locked stage already prints "A: 50 ringed" and "B: 20 ringed" over two 100-dot grids. A learner who just counts rings ("A has more flagged") lands on "Higher" without reasoning about non-reoffenders, which is the right answer for a shallow reason. Conversely the learner who trusts "equally accurate" picks "The same", which is the misconception the lesson wants, so the item still discriminates. Note, not blocking. Small legibility snag: on the locked stage B's count reads only "32" with no "reoffend" label (A's reads "50 reoffend"); the slider label "Reoffenders in B 32" explains it a line lower.
3. **Explore.** "Show outcomes" fills the grids: black = reoffend, red ring = false alarm, purple ring = high risk; hollow dots (non-reoffenders) have no legend entry but are inferable. The arithmetic printed (A 10/50 = 20% false alarms, B 4/68 = 6%; misses 10/50 = 20% vs 16/32 = 50%) is consistent with 80/20 labels at 50 and 32 reoffenders (I re-derived it). Moving the slider to 50 makes B match A on both errors: a clean, visible contrast. **Confusion point:** after the slider move the status line reads "B at 32: false alarms 6%, misses 50%." while the slider shows 50 and B's grid shows 20%/20%. It is a memo of the earlier state, but nothing says so ("Earlier, at 32: ..." would). A beginner checking the line against the grid sees a contradiction. Note to the CM, not blocking because the grid numbers right under it are correct and the reveal restates both states.
4. **Whether the reveal answered the question the learner actually had.** Mostly yes. The learner who said "same" wants "how can an equally accurate score flag A's innocent people more?" The reveal gives the mechanism: "A has more reoffenders, so more of A is ringed, guilty or not", and "the slider changed B's people, not the score". That answers it, if compressed: the step "a ringed group is always 2 in 10 innocent, so more rings means more innocent people ringed, out of fewer innocents" is left to the learner. The final sentence ("Otherwise no score that makes mistakes is calibrated with equal errors") is a general theorem stated after a two-point demo (32 and 50); the learner takes it on trust. That is acceptable for a 60-second reveal but it is a claim and should be on the ledger (checked below).
5. **Phone legibility.** In the reveal frame the grids shrink and the red/black counts under them ("false alarms 10/50 = 20%", "10/50 = 20%") look near or under the 10 px floor in the screenshot. I cannot measure computed font size from the image: a question for qa-tester, not a finding.
