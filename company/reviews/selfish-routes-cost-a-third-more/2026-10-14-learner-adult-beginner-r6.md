lesson version: v2
boundary: adult-beginner (meta.json learner field). Knows that drivers pick the faster road and that a road slows as more drivers use it. Does not know that the selfish equilibrium can be worse on average than a planner's split (up to 4/3 with delays proportional to traffic).
Screenshots seen: yes (artifacts/walkthroughs/selfish-routes-cost-a-third-more/sheet.png)
Verdict: revise

## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: the hook and title pose the tension. The stem states both delay rules, so the arithmetic is there. **But the locked stage already draws the whole average-trip curve, with its clear bottom at 45 above 50%, and the y-axis is marked 30/45/60.** The answer can be read off the picture before I commit. I chose "No, 60 is best" without reading the graph. A learner who looks for two seconds picks 45 from the curve, not from thinking. (W-154 pattern: the locked stage leaks the answer.)
- Explore: the slider moves the dot along the curve, and the three readouts (A, B, average) update. The caption names the pull at each point ("B takes 54 min, A takes 60, so drivers on A want to switch"). At 50% it says the same. That shows why the 45 split does not hold. "All on B" shows the selfish rest point, "no driver gains by switching". Good.
- Where it thinned: the reveal's cause, "each driver who joins B slows everyone already on it, a cost that driver never feels", is not on stage. The readouts show B's time per driver, but nothing shows the extra minutes one joining driver adds to all the others. That is the difference between what the driver feels and what the average pays. I watched that the average is lower at 50%, but not why selfish choice misses it.
- Reveal: 60 / 45 = 4/3 matches what I saw. "That is the worst it can ever be" (with affine delays) is told context. The reveal stage crops the top of the chart.

## 2. After reading the brief

- The objective asks the learner to explain the 45 "*because* each driver joining B slows everyone already on it". On stage that cause is **told only**: one reveal sentence, with nothing on stage standing for the cost a joining driver puts on others. Under "Show the why you promised" that is a revise. Possible stand-in on the existing stage (the builder's call): at the current split, show what one more driver on B feels (B's time) next to what the group pays (the rise in total minutes).
- The doesNotKnow item (selfish can be worse than planned) is **shown**: 60 versus 45 on the curve. But the locked curve shows it before the prediction.
