# Learner review: amps-times-hours-is-atoms
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult with school-level maths and science, new to the topic (knows a current is moving charge and a mole is a counted amount; assumes current alone sets how much copper forms).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess backed by a calculation. The question hands over every constant (2.0 A, 1.0 h, 2 electrons per atom, 96,485 C per mole of electrons). 1.2 g is what you get by forgetting the 2 electrons or the full hour; 4.7 g is double; 2.4 g is the answer. A beginner may be unable to run the sums in their head, but the stage runs them. The hook ("How much copper forms?") does not leak.
- What I saw is the lesson's strength: a five-line chain on the stage, "2.0 A x 3,600 s = 7,200 C", "/ 96,485 C/mol = 0.0746 mol electrons", "/ 2 per atom = 0.0373 mol Cu", "x 63.55 g/mol = 2.37 g", with a bar that fills. 15 min gives 0.59 g, 30 min 1.19 g (my wrong pick of 1.2 g is exactly this half-hour value, which made my error obvious), 1 hour 2.37 g, and 1.0 A for the hour gives 1.19 g again. Every number in the reveal was on the stage.
- Where understanding first broke: minor. "63.55 g/mol" (copper's molar mass) is not explained, but a school-level learner has seen molar masses. "Each copper atom needs a fixed two" is told; the stage prints "/ 2 per atom" but offers no way to see what changes if it were 1 or 3.
- The question's "1 mole of electrons carries 96,485 C" is a big number dropped before the learner has been told why such a constant exists; given the chain on the stage, it is acceptable.
- The button "1 hour" greys after being tapped and the previous selection is shown; harmless.
- Reveal earned: yes.
- Problems: (1) 63.55 and the "2 per atom" are given, not shown; (2) the brief's claim "halving the current or the time halves the copper" is shown by 1.0 A and 15 / 30 min, but never stated in the reveal.
- No FATAL.
