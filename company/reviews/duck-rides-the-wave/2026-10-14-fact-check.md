**Ruling on c4 (W-581), asked for by the dispatcher:** the builder's objection is right. In deep water w^2 = gk, so the group velocity is dw/dk = c/2 = 3.12 m/s, and a wave train's front and its energy travel at that speed (the energy flux is E c_g). The buoy 100 m away starts to bob at about **32 s**, not 16 s. A dispersive-packet simulation (wj-econ-phys-math-geo/duck.mjs) confirms it: the envelope moves 125 m in 40 s. The stage repeats the error, because Demo.tsx moves the train's front at C. c4 is disputed until the stage, the Seen row, the reveal and the claim say about 32 s.

| c4 | disputed | Ruling: the builder's objection is right; 16 s is wrong. In deep water w = sqrt(gk), so the group velocity is dw/dk = c/2 = 3.12 m/s, and the energy flux is E c_g. Individual crests run at c through the group and die out at its front, so no crest arrives before the group. Re-checked by simulation: a Gaussian packet with carrier k = w^2/g, propagated with the exact dispersion, has its envelope peak at 125 m after 40 s and 250 m after 80 s (3.12 m/s). The stage makes the same error twice: Demo.tsx draws the front at C*t, and the buoy label and Seen row say 16 s. Optional: draw the 1-wave and 10-wave presets as a steady train already filling the sea, so c1's '250 m' does not show a crest outrunning a front that has only reached 125 m. {S}duck.mjs. |
