## Ruling: option (c) with a derivation example in place of the canyon. The Grand Canyon leaves the lesson.

**(a) is rejected on pedagogy, not on facts.** Pass 44's wording is accurate, and every part of it is in the three opened sources. But after the colon it no longer shows what c6 promises. What the sentence now describes is the river *joining* older stretches into one path. That is a drainage change, and nothing in it says how much rock was cut in the 6-5 Ma window. The large quantity, 1.8 km of depth, is now explicitly not tied to the window. "How old those stretches are is debated" then tells the learner that the large thing may be older and its timing is unknown. So the colon would introduce an example that, read carefully, does not show "a slow process did a large thing in the time shown". A careless reading gives the old whole-depth error back. Tightening the wording cannot fix this, because the problem is the example itself. Three fact-check passes in one day on one sentence is also a signal: the example's real history (paleocanyons, integration, contested ages) is too tangled to carry a one-line point.

**(b) is rejected for now.** Every real slow-process example I could name (the Atlantic opening, Himalayan uplift, a dated erosion rate) is a class-H source sentence. The reveal already carries its one allowed held citation (c2, Homo sapiens). Adding a second H sentence breaks D4(b), and section 5 says the next H example would need its own fallback anyway. That fallback is the derivation below. So start from the fallback.

**(c), as ruled:** keep c6's sentence and its colon. Replace the example with a derivation that sits on a stage event the learner has already found, the non-bird dinosaurs' end at 23:39 (c3, verified, a milestone). Remove the canyon event from the stage. The example becomes class D: rate × time, from c3's date and simple arithmetic. It cannot be disputed on sources, and it still gives learner finding 4 (r10) a stage referent, a dot the learner reached with the slider.
