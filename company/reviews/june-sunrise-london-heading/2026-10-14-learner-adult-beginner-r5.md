## 1. From the artifact

- Predict: "Where does the Sun rise in June?" with the hook "The Sun rises in the east. In London in June, does it?" The "does it?" signals that due east is wrong, though the learner here still chose it. The question gives the compass convention (N 0°, E 90°), which helps. The three options (east / north-east 50° / almost north 10°) are well spread. The locked stage (empty compass, date 15 Apr, bearing "?") leaks nothing.
- Explore: "21 June" draws a rise mark at 50° north-east and a set mark at 310°. "21 December" swings it to 130° south-east. "21 March" gives 90° east, and the slider to 23 Mar gives 89°. Seeing the rise point jump across the east line between June and December is the whole idea, and it is visual. The set mark mirroring it is a nice extra.
- Reveal: "On 21 June... 50°, north-east; on 21 December at 130°, south-east." Seen. "Due east only when day and night are equal, in March and September": March was seen, September was not, and the "day and night equal" link is told. "Swings about 79° between the two": I saw 50° and 130°, an 80° gap, so "about 79°" reads like a small mismatch with my own numbers. Why the point moves (the Earth's tilt) is never shown or said. For this boundary that is acceptable, because the question is "where", not "why".
- In the reveal frame the stage shows 23 Mar (the last slider position), not June. Harmless.

## 3. New findings

1. Minor: "swings about 79°" vs the 50° and 130° shown (an 80° gap). Say "about 80°", or show the bearings to one decimal so 79 follows from what was seen.
2. Minor: the hook's "does it?" pre-rejects due east.
3. Minor: September as a second due-east date is told; the slider can reach it, but the gate doesn't send the learner there.
4. No FATAL. The core idea (in June, London's sunrise is well north of east, and it swings with the seasons) comes from the stage.
