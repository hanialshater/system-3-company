# Learner review: half-adder-wiring
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult who uses a phone and a laptop daily and has never programmed (no idea what a variable, array, function or "algorithm" is; comfortable with everyday apps and basic arithmetic).

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: "one-bit numbers", "carry" and "sum" are not words I know in this sense, and the three options are only a guess unless I already know that 1 + 1 is "10" in binary. The locked stage shows a diagram (switches A and B, an OR gate and an XOR gate feeding two circles) which looks like a real circuit, but "gate", "OR", "XOR" are not explained anywhere before the reveal. The hook ("does it need to know arithmetic, or just the right wiring?") is a nice question but gives away that wiring is the answer.
- What was SHOWN: tapping A and B lights wires green and puts a 0 or 1 in the output circles; tapping a gate name swaps the gate and the wires and outputs change; a counter says "ADDS RIGHT ON 4 of 4 inputs" when the pair of gates is right. This is a good puzzle and I did find the working pair (XOR for sum, AND for carry) by trying. But the stage never states what "adds right" means (that 0+0 = 00, 0+1 = 01, 1+1 = 10) and never says what OR, XOR or AND do, so I was solving by trial and error with a score, not by understanding. With only 9 gate pairs that is easy to brute-force without learning.
- Where understanding first broke: the question itself, and then the reveal's first line "Your working circuit gave carry 1, sum 0". In the transcript path the learner pressed Reset before the reveal, so the stage beside the reveal reads OR / XOR, A = 0, B = 0, outputs 0 and 0, "1 of 4 inputs": not a working circuit and not 1 + 1. The reveal speaks of something the stage is no longer showing.
- Reveal: "2 in binary is 10" is told, not shown. XOR and AND are defined here in one clause each ("exactly one input is 1", "only when both are 1"), which is the first time I learned what they do. OR is never defined. "Half adder", "any width" and chaining with a third carry input are told; none was on the stage.
- Reveal earned: partly. The idea "the right gates make the arithmetic" is earned by the 4 of 4 moment; why those gates, and the binary link, are not.
- Problems: (1) binary, carry, gate vocabulary never taught before the question; (2) the stage does not state what correct addition is, so the score is opaque; (3) the reveal can be reached after Reset, and then its "your working circuit" does not match the stage; (4) OR never defined; (5) chaining is told only.
- No FATAL.
