# Learner review: two-pointers-meet
lesson version: v1
boundary: an adult beginner who can add and knows what "sorted" means; would check pairs one by one.
Screenshots seen: yes (sheet.png, 5 frames, phone viewport)

## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: "At most how many sums could this ever take on 8 numbers?" Options 28 / 7 / 3. The question is long (rule, list, target and a worst-case question in one paragraph) and "at most ... ever" is the part a beginner skims. I chose 28.
- Explore: the triangle grid with greyed rows is a good picture: after "16 was too small: row 1 ruled out" a whole row goes grey (7 pairs). Then the right finger move is refused with a clear reason ("18 is too small: a smaller right number only makes it smaller"), which is the key insight shown, not told. "Follow the rule" finds 4 + 15 = 19 in 3 sums, 13 of 28 ruled out.
- Where understanding first broke: the answer. I asked for the worst case; what I saw was a lucky case of 3 sums, and 3 is itself one of the options. The reveal then says "it was 7", a number I never saw on the stage. A beginner who watched it finish in 3 would reasonably think "3" was right. The 7 rests entirely on the told sentence "each sum moves a finger one step inward, so 8 numbers need at most 7 sums". This is the "reveal quotes a value the learner never visited" pattern, in the form of a worst case the stage never produces.
- Reveal values it does quote from my path (3 sums, 13 of 28) match the stage.
- Phone: the reveal text is fully visible. On the reveal frame the stage is scrolled so the number row and fingers are cut off at the top; not blocking.
- Grid reading: columns are not labelled; I had to infer the top number row is the column header. The row labels (1, 3, 4 ... 13) help.
- Reveal earned: "a too-small sum rules out a whole row" yes; "at most 7" no, it is told.

Fixes (owner: builder):
1. Let the learner see a 7-sum run: e.g. a second target (or "worst case" button) whose search takes all 7 steps, or a step counter showing "fingers are 7 apart: each sum closes the gap by 1".
2. Or change the question to what the stage shows (how many sums for 19 here?) and keep 7 as the generalisation.

## 2. After reading the brief

- Objective: "at most n - 1 sums (7 for 8 numbers) instead of 28". The artifact shows the row-elimination well but never shows a 7-sum run, so the headline number is told. The recall (100 numbers, 99) depends on exactly the part I found told.
- Claim c2 is disputed by the fact-checker: the reveal's "13 of the 28 pairs, never looked at" counts the two sums the learner did look at (11 were never looked at). I did not notice that as a beginner, which is the problem: a learner would take the false number on trust. It must be fixed before launch regardless of my findings.
- Other 4 claims verified by others; I cannot vouch for them.
