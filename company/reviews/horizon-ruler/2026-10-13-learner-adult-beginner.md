## 1. From the artifact

- Prediction: a real guess. 1.5 km, 4.7 km and 15 km are all believable for someone standing on a beach; most adults overshoot (15) or undershoot (1.5). The hook ("Ships vanish over the horizon long before your eyes give up") does not give the number. The locked stage already draws a cross-section with a sight line grazing the curve at an eye height of 30 m, not 1.7 m, and the horizon readout is "?". That is a fair model of the question without answering it.
- Where understanding first broke: nowhere in the core idea. Tapping "Your eyes, 1.7 m" gives 4.7 km, "100x higher, 170 m" gives 47 km (a 10x increase, visible in the picture where the sight line is much longer), and "Burj Khalifa, 828 m" gives 103 km. The grazing point is marked as a green dot labelled "horizon". Seeing that 100x the height gives only 10x the distance is a good surprise and I could do it myself.
- Reveal vs stage: 4.7, 47 and 103 km all appeared on the stage. "Pythagoras on the tangent, the distance is the square root of 2 x Earth's radius x your height" is told as a formula; the stage never shows the right angle or the radius, and I could not follow "tangent" without school geometry. "Air bends light, real horizons a few per cent farther" is told.
- Honest caption: "Distances true. Curve and height stretched 47x. No refraction." is useful; a beginner would otherwise think the real Earth looks that curved. "Refraction" is undefined but it is only a caveat.
- Phone legibility: the diagram is the stage and it is clear, but the horizon label and the "No refraction." caption collide at the bottom edge in the tall-height states, and the "109 km" label is crossed by the sight line itself. At 1.7 m the person is a tiny stub (I opened the 170 m and 933 m states, not the 1.7 m picture, so I cannot comment on its size). The slider tops out at 933 m, not the Burj height, which is a little odd after a 828 m button.
- Reveal earned: yes for the numbers and the 10x/100x point; the square-root formula is asserted.
- Problems: (1) formula and "tangent" told; (2) overlapping labels at tall heights; (3) "refraction" undefined.
